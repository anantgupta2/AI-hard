FAMILY 049: A stable-coordinate counterexample in four variables
SUBJECT: Algebraic and complex geometry
OPENAI SUMMARY: Constructs a polynomial in four complex variables that is not a coordinate but becomes one after adjoining a single variable, disproving the Stable Coordinate conjecture in four variables. Every fiber is affine three-space, yet none of its embeddings is rectifiable, also disproving the Abhyankar–Sathaye conjecture even when all fibers are affine spaces.

=== MANUSCRIPT: A stable coordinate that is not a coordinate in four variables (October-5-2026) [preprints/A-stable-coordinate-that-is-not-a-coordinate-in-four-variables-October-5-2026/build/source/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We construct an explicit degree-five polynomial over $`\mathbf C`$ that is not a coordinate in four variables but becomes one after adjoining a single variable. This gives a counterexample to the stable coordinate conjecture in four variables. Every fiber is isomorphic to affine three-space, yet its embedding in affine four-space is not rectifiable. Thus the example also disproves the Abhyankar–Sathaye embedding conjecture in ambient dimension four.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Let $k=\C$. An element $f\in k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ is a \emph{coordinate} if there are elements $f_2,\ldots,f_n$ such that \[ k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]=k[f,f_2,\ldots,f_n] \] as a polynomial ring. Equivalently, a polynomial automorphism sends $f$ to $x_1$. It is a \emph{one-stable coordinate} if it is a coordinate in $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n,w]$, where $w$ is independent. The stable coordinate conjecture asks whether an element that becomes a coordinate after adjoining finitely many variables was already a coordinate. The one-variable formulation appears in \cite[Conjecture~3]{ShpilrainYu2002}; successive applications give the formulation with finitely many added variables. Over $\C$, the answer is affirmative in two and three variables by \cite[Proposition~1.1(c)]{ShpilrainYu2002}. The case of one original variable is elementary. Thus four variables are the first possible dimension for a counterexample, and one added variable already suffices. [statement] The example also has a geometric consequence. A closed embedding $\A^{n-1}\hookrightarrow\A^n$ is \emph{rectifiable} if an automorphism of the ambient affine space takes its image to a coordinate hyperplane. The Abhyankar--Sathaye embedding conjecture asserts, in characteristic zero, that a polynomial $g$ with $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/(g)\simeq k^{[n-1]}$ is a coordinate; see \cite[p.~8429]{BlancVanSanten2019}. Here $k^{[r]}$ denotes a polynomial ring in $r$ variables. Corollary~\ref{cor:fibers} gives a negative answer to this conjecture in ambient dimension four. In fact, every fiber is affine three-space and its inclusion in $\A^4$ is nonrectifiable. [statement] \begin{proof} Proposition~\ref{prop:fibers} identifies each fiber with $\A^3$; in particular, $(f-\lambda)$ is its prime defining ideal. If an ambient automorphism takes $f^{-1}(\lambda)$ to a coordinate hyperplane, it takes its defining principal ideal to the ideal of a coordinate. The two generators therefore differ by a nonzero constant. This would make $f-\lambda$, and hence $f$, a coordinate, contrary to Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. \end{proof} \subsection*{History and related methods} The coordinate question retains the embedding in affine space: it asks whether a given function can be made into a coordinate projection by an ambient automorphism. Cancellation asks instead whether an affine variety whose product with an affine line is an affine space must itself be an affine space. Makar-Limanov, van Rossum, Shpilrain, and Yu established an implication from the stable coordinate conjecture to cancellation in \cite[Theorem~1.2]{MakarLimanovRossumShpilrainYu2004}. In our example the ambient variety is already $\A^4$, and the obstruction concerns its distinguished polynomial. The low-dimensional proof in \cite[proof of Proposition~1.1(c)]{ShpilrainYu2002} uses curve cancellation and the Abhyankar--Moh embedding theorem in two variables. In three variables it combines affine-surface cancellation, using work of Fujita and Miyanishi--Sugie, with Kaliman's theorem that a polynomial on $\C^3$ with general fiber $\C^2$ is a coordinate \cite[Main Theorem]{Kaliman2002}. The four-variable V\'en\'ereau polynomials have been an important testing ground for these coordinate questions. Lewis proved that the second V\'en\'ereau polynomial is a coordinate \cite{Lewis2013}. Blanc and Poloni developed a description in terms of affine bundles and bivariables, revisited that result, and proved one-stability for a broader family of related polynomials \cite[Proposition~2.3]{BlancPoloni2022}. These results illustrate the distinction between exhibiting polynomial coordinates after stabilization and proving that a polynomial cannot occur in any original coordinate system. For the polynomial in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, the first problem is settled by explicit substitutions; the second requires a derivation obstruction. \subsection*{The construction and the obstruction} The proof uses two presentations of the same four-variable polynomial rin […]
[theorem] \label{thm:main} In $R=\C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]$, put \begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-counterexample} Q=x_2^2-x_4^2+x_1x_3, \qquad f=x_1-2Q\bigl(Q(x_2+x_4)+x_1x_4\bigr). \end{equation} There is a $\C$-algebra automorphism of $R[w]$ sending $f$ to $x_1$, but there is no such automorphism of $R$.
[corollary] \label{cor:fibers} For every $\lambda\in\C$, the hypersurface $f^{-1}(\lambda)\subset\A^4$ is isomorphic to $\A^3$ and its embedding in $\A^4$ is not rectifiable.

=== MANUSCRIPT: An explicit noncoordinate polynomial with affine three-space zero fibre (September-24-2026) [preprints/An-explicit-noncoordinate-polynomial-with-affine-three-space-zero-fibre-September-24-2026/build/paper.tex]
ABSTRACT: We construct an explicit counterexample to the Abhyankar–Sathaye conjecture: a noncoordinate polynomial in four complex variables whose zero fibre is affine three-space. Adjoining variables gives counterexamples in every ambient dimension at least four.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} The Abhyankar--Sathaye conjecture asks whether a polynomial $f\in\CC[z_1,\ldots,z_n]$ satisfying \[ \CC[z_1,\ldots,z_n]/(f)\cong\CC^{[n-1]} \] must be a coordinate, that is, the first component of a polynomial automorphism of $\Aff^n_{\CC}$. Here $\CC^{[m]}$ denotes a polynomial ring in $m$ independent variables, and the displayed isomorphism is required to fix $\CC$. Geometrically, the question asks whether every hypersurface embedding $\Aff^{n-1}_{\CC}\hookrightarrow\Aff^n_{\CC}$ can be carried to a coordinate hyperplane by an ambient polynomial automorphism. For $n=2$, the question is answered affirmatively by the Abhyankar--Moh--Suzuki Theorem~\cite{AM,Suzuki}. The higher-dimensional problem belongs to a family of questions about polynomial maps with affine-space fibres. Sathaye~\cite[pp.~1--2]{Sathaye} distinguished the coordinate, or epimorphism, question from the weaker question whether an affine-space zero fibre forces all fibres to be affine space. A coordinate polynomial defines a projection after an automorphism, so its fibres are all isomorphic to $\Aff^{n-1}_{\CC}$; an isomorphism of the zero fibre alone supplies no such global trivialization. Kraft~\cite[\S4]{Kraft} surveys the embedding problem and its relation to algebraic group actions. Among the early affirmative results, Sathaye~\cite{Sathaye} proved rectifiability for planes defined by polynomials linear in one of three variables in characteristic zero, and Russell~\cite[Theorem~2.3]{Russell} extended this result to arbitrary fields. In four variables, Kaliman, V\'en\'ereau and Zaidenberg~\cite[Theorem~4.2(a)]{KVZ} and Maubach~\cite[Corollaries~4.2--4.3]{Maubach} established affirmative results for equations $a(x)y+b(x,z,t)$ with $a\ne0$: an affine-three-space zero fibre can be straightened by an ambient automorphism. Here $x,y,z,t$ are independent variables, and the coefficient of $y$ depends on only one of them. Further algebraic treatments appear in work of El~Kahoui, Essamaoui and Ouali~\cite{EEO} and Ghosh, Gupta and Pal~\cite{GGPthreefold}. Ghosh, Gupta and Pal also study linear hypersurfaces with multivariable coefficients under additional hypotheses on the coefficients and their divisors~\cite[Theorem~4.11]{GGP}. Ghosh~\cite[Theorem~1.4]{Ghosh} proves an affirmative embedding theorem in characteristic zero for $a(x_m)b(x_1,\ldots,x_{m-1})y+f(z,t)+x_m$, where the displayed variables are independent, $b\ne0$, and $a$ has at least two distinct roots over the algebraic closure. These results illustrate the role of restrictions on the defining equation; an abstract polynomial presentation of the quotient does not itself provide an automorphism of the ambient polynomial ring. We give a counterexample in ambient dimension four, and hence in every ambient dimension at least four. The three-variable case is not addressed. The construction starts from the cusp parametrization $(u^3,-u^2)$ and perturbs its two entries by independent multiples of $h$. In $R=\CC[h,u,v,w]$, put \begin{equation}\label{eq:xyz} \begin{aligned} x&=u^3+hv,\qquad y=-u^2+hw,\\ s&=S(h,u,v,w) :=2u^3v+3u^4w+h(v^2-3u^2w^2)+h^2w^3. \end{aligned} \end{equation} Since $x^2+y^3$ vanishes modulo $h$, it is divisible by $h$ in $R$. The displayed polynomial $s$ is its quotient, so $x^2+y^3=hs$. Define $p$ and $F$ by \begin{equation} p(x,y,s)=-2s^2x+3sy^2-3s^3y, \qquad F=h-p(x,y,s)-1. \label{eq:F} \end{equation} Thus $F$ is a polynomial in the four independent variables $h,u,v,w$. [statement] The critical point $P=(2,0,-1/2,1/2)$ lies on the fibre $F=-1$. The zero fibre is smooth, since it is isomorphic to affine three-space. [statement] Shpilrain and Yu~\cite[\S1]{SY} used the ambient critical locus to distinguish isomorphic hypersurfaces with inequivalent embeddings. They asked whether the gradient must be nowhere zero when the zero fibre is isomorphic to a coordinate hyperplane. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives a negative answer in ambient dimension four, and Corollary~\ref{ […]
[theorem] \label{thm:main} The polynomial $F$ defined in \eqref{eq:xyz}--\eqref{eq:F} satisfies \[ R/(F)\cong\CC^{[3]}, \qquad \grad F(2,0,-1/2,1/2)=0. \] Consequently $F$ is not a coordinate of $R$.
[corollary] \label{cor:dimension} For every integer $n\ge4$, there is a polynomial $f\in\CC[z_1,\ldots,z_n]$ such that $\CC[z_1,\ldots,z_n]/(f)\cong\CC^{[n-1]}$ and $f$ is not a coordinate.
