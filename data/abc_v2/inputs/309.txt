SUBJECT: Topology

=== MANUSCRIPT: The Kervaire invariant problem at the prime three (September-24-2026) [preprints/The-Kervaire-Invariant-Problem-at-the-Prime-Three-September-24-2026/build/paper.tex]
ABSTRACT: We solve the Kervaire invariant problem at the prime three for the standard Kervaire classes in the mod-three Adams spectral sequence. These classes survive precisely at indices 0, 2, and 3, and each surviving detection coset contains an element of additive order three. In particular, there is an order-three Kervaire element in stem 322.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} The odd-primary Kervaire invariant problem asks which distinguished classes on the Adams two-line represent stable homotopy classes of spheres. Its strong form asks for representatives killed by the prime. These are different questions: permanence determines an associated-graded class, while the order of a representative also depends on an extension in actual homotopy. We solve both questions for the standard family at the prime three. Write \[ E_2^{s,t}=\Ext_{\calA_3}^{s,t}(\F_3,\F_3) \Longrightarrow (\pi^S_{t-s})^{\wedge}_3, \qquad b_j\in E_2^{2,\,4\cdot3^{j+1}}\quad(j\geq0), \] where $\calA_3$ is the mod-three Steenrod algebra and $b_j$ is the standard Kervaire class. The spectral-sequence and family conventions are those of \cite[Theorems~2.1.1 and~3.4.4]{Ravenel}. Define \begin{align*} K&=\{j\geq0:b_j\text{ survives nontrivially to }E_\infty\},\\ K_3&=\{j\in K:\text{some class detected by }b_j \text{ has additive order }3\}. \end{align*} The second definition concerns existence within a detection coset. It does not require every representative of that coset to have the same additive order. [statement] Positive stable stems are finite \cite[Theorem~1.1.8]{Ravenel}. Consequently, the assertion is unchanged if the sphere is taken to be $3$-local instead of $3$-complete. We use the local sphere for the Moore-spectrum construction and complete coefficient rings for Morava $E$-theory. The problem here is the standard odd-primary Adams-family problem; the classical prime-two Kervaire invariant one problem has a different family and dimension convention. \subsection{Unstable consequences} Throughout this subsection all spaces, maps, fibres, and homotopy equivalences are localized at three. [theorem] \label{thm:classification} At the prime three, \[ K=K_3=\{0,2,3\}. \] Thus the surviving classes occur in stems $10$, $106$, and $322$, and each admits an additive-order-three representative. The class $b_1$ and every $b_j$ with $j\geq4$ fail to survive.
[corollary] \label{intro:unstable-consequences} \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}] \item There are homotopy equivalences of H-spaces \[ \Omega S^{163}\{3\}\simeq T^{163}(3)\times\Omega T^{487}(3), \qquad BW_{81}\simeq\Omega T^{487}(3). \] Consequently, $W_{81}\simeq\Omega^2T^{487}(3)$. \item For every integer $n>1$, the underlying space $\Omega S^{2n+1}\{3\}$ has a nontrivial product decomposition if and only if $n\in\{3,27,81\}$. For every other $n>1$ it is mod-three atomic, hence indecomposable. Here a product is nontrivial when both factors are noncontractible. \item For every integer $n>1$, the homotopy type $T^{2n+1}(3)$ admits a homotopy-associative H-space multiplication if and only if $n\in\{3,27,81\}$. The multiplication on $T^{163}(3)$ supplied by the displayed product is homotopy associative and homotopy commutative. For this multiplication, the third-power map on $T^{163}(3)$ is null-homotopic, as is the third-power map on the loop H-space $\Omega T^{487}(3)$. \end{enumerate}
