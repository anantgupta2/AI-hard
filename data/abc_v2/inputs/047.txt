FAMILY 047: Zariski cancellation and affine fibrations over the complex numbers
SUBJECT: Algebraic and complex geometry
OPENAI SUMMARY: Constructs an integral complex affine fourfold $`X\not\cong\mathbb A^4`$ with $`X\times\mathbb A^1\cong\mathbb A^5`$, disproving affine-space cancellation over ℂ in dimension four. It also disproves the Dolgachev–Weisfeiler affine-fibration conjecture: smooth surjections $`X\to\mathbb A^1`$ and $`\mathbb A^5\to\mathbb A^2`$ have every residue-field fiber isomorphic to affine three-space but are not Zariski-locally trivial.

=== MANUSCRIPT: An explicit failure of complex affine-space cancellation (September-23-2026) [preprints/An-explicit-failure-of-complex-affine-space-cancellation-September-23-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We construct an explicit integral complex affine fourfold X with $`X\times\mathbb A^1\cong\mathbb A^5`$ but $`X\not\cong\mathbb A^4`$. This gives a negative answer to Zariski's affine-space cancellation problem over ℂ in dimension four. The same construction disproves the Stable Coordinate Conjecture in ambient dimension five and yields smooth 𝔸3-fibrations over 𝔸1 and 𝔸2 that are not Zariski-locally trivial. These fibrations disprove the Dolgachev–Weisfeiler affine-fibration conjecture over these bases.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} The Zariski cancellation problem asks whether an affine variety is determined by its cylinder when that cylinder is affine space. Over the complex numbers, its algebraic formulation is the implication \begin{equation} A[w]\simeq \mathbb C\poly{n+1} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad A\simeq\mathbb C\poly n, \label{eq:cancellation-question} \end{equation} where $A$ is a finitely generated commutative unital complex algebra, $w$ is an independent polynomial variable, and all isomorphisms fix the complex scalars. Here $C\poly r$ denotes a polynomial ring in $r$ variables over a ring $C$. The hypothesis says that adjoining one variable turns $A$ into a polynomial ring; the question is whether that variable can be cancelled. An isomorphism of cylinders need not respect the given projection to the affine line, so its existence alone does not identify polynomial coordinates on $A$. Cancellation for the affine line follows from the work of Abhyankar, Heinzer, and Eakin~\cite[Corollary~(2.8)]{AEH}. Cancellation for the complex affine plane was established through the work of Fujita, Miyanishi, and Sugie~\cite{Fujita,MiyanishiSugie}, using the structure of affine surfaces and cylinderlike open sets. In positive characteristic, Asanuma's work on polynomial fiber rings supplied threefolds whose one-variable cylinders are affine four-space~\cite{Asanuma}. Gupta proved that suitable members of this family are not affine three-space over their ground field~\cite{GuptaThreefold}, then established failure of cancellation in every dimension at least three over every field of positive characteristic~\cite{GuptaPositive}. The latter result uses a separate family of examples: adjoining variables to a threefold whose first cylinder is already polynomial would not produce higher-dimensional counterexamples. The characteristic-zero problem in dimensions at least three is still listed as open in Gaifullin--Petrov's July 2026 preprint~\cite[p.~1]{GaifullinPetrov}. Gupta's survey~\cite{GuptaSurvey} explains its history and its connections with coordinates, polynomial fibrations, and the recognition of affine space. We give a negative answer to \eqref{eq:cancellation-question} over $\mathbb C$ for $n=4$. The defining polynomial also gives a negative answer to the Stable Coordinate Conjecture over $\kk$ in ambient dimension five, already after adjoining one variable. Let $p,s,u,F,J$ be algebraically independent over $\kk$, and set \begin{equation} \begin{split} P&=\kk[p,s,u,F,J],\qquad x=s^2+u^3+p^2F,\\ H&=x^2F-(1+2sx)J-p^2J^2-pu,\qquad A=P/(H). \end{split} \label{eq:example} \end{equation} Thus $x$ is an abbreviation for the displayed polynomial, and $H$ is a polynomial in exactly the five indicated variables. [statement] \paragraph{The polynomial cylinder.} The stabilization belongs to the theory of residual and stable coordinates. A polynomial in $\kk[p]\poly4$ is a \emph{residual coordinate} if its image is a polynomial coordinate over the residue field of every prime of $\kk[p]$; it is \emph{one-stably a coordinate} if it becomes a coordinate after adjoining one variable. Our $H$ is a residual coordinate: it is a coordinate after inverting $p$, while a linear change in $F,J$ makes its reduction at $p=0$ a coordinate. Its one-stability therefore follows from the theorem of El Kahoui--Ouali~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{ElKahouiOuali}, generalized by Dutta--Lahiri~\cite[Theorem~3.4]{DuttaLahiri}. The Stable Coordinate Conjecture asks whether a polynomial $f\in\kk\poly n$ that becomes a coordinate in $\kk\poly{n+1}$ after adjoining one variable must already be a coordinate in $\kk\poly n$ \cite[p.~342]{MakarLimanovVanRossumShpilrainYu}. The assertion concerns polynomial automorphisms of the ambient spaces. [theorem] \label{thm:main} The algebra $A$ in \eqref{eq:example} is an integral complex algebra of dimension four. For an independent variable $w$, \[ A[w]\simeq\kk\poly5, \qquad A\not\simeq\kk\poly4. \] Consequently the Zariski cancellation problem over the complex numbers has a negative answer in dimension four.
[corollary] [Failure of the Stable Coordinate Conjecture] \label{cor:stable-coordinate} The polynomial $H$ in \eqref{eq:example} is not a coordinate of $P=\kk[p,s,u,F,J]$, but it is a coordinate of $P[w]$ for one independent variable $w$. Thus the Stable Coordinate Conjecture over $\kk$ fails in ambient dimension five after one stabilization.
