=== MANUSCRIPT: Weak pure infiniteness and O-infinity absorption (September-25-2026) [preprints/Weak-pure-infiniteness-and-O-infinity-absorption-September-25-2026/build/manuscript.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove that every complex C∗-algebra in which each positive element is properly infinite is strongly purely infinite. This answers the ordinary-to-strong part of Kirchberg–Rørdam's comparison question. For exact algebras, we also prove that proper infiniteness of one fixed finite amplification of every positive element implies proper infiniteness of each positive element. Consequently, every separable nuclear algebra with this fixed-amplification property absorbs $`\mathcal O_\infty`$, without assumptions of unitality or simplicity.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Pure infiniteness expresses the ability of positive elements of a \(C^*\)-algebra to contain several copies of themselves in Cuntz comparison. For a nonsimple algebra, an individual comparison and the simultaneous comparison of two elements are different demands: the latter must also control a mixed entry. We prove that proper infiniteness of every positive element already provides this compatibility. We then give two ways to remove a fixed finite amplification, one for exact algebras and an independent construction for nuclear algebras. In the separable nuclear case the result yields absorption of the Cuntz algebra \(\Oinf\). \subsection{Infiniteness notions and main results} All \(C^*\)-algebras are complex and may be nonunital. Let \(\Kc\) denote the compact operators on an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space. For \(u,v\in(C\otimes\Kc)_+\), write \(u\precsim v\) if some sequence \(x_j\in C\otimes\Kc\) satisfies \(x_j^*vx_j\to u\) in norm, and write \(u\CuEq v\) if comparison holds in both directions. This is stable Cuntz comparison \cite{Cuntz1978}. Finite matrices over \(C\) occupy the standard corners of \(C\otimes\Kc\); comparisons between different sizes use zero padding in the other coordinates. Write \(h^{\oplus n}\) for the direct sum of \(n\) copies of \(h\). A positive element \(h\) is \emph{properly infinite} if \(h\oplus h\precsim h\). We include zero as properly infinite. For \(h\in C_+\), let \[ \Her(h)=\overline{hCh},\qquad \Id(h)=\text{the closed two-sided ideal of \(C\) generated by \(h\)}. \] We will use the following two comparison conditions: \begin{align} &h\text{ is properly infinite for every }h\in C_+, \tag{P}\label{eq:property-P}\\ &h^{\oplus n}\text{ is properly infinite for every }h\in C_+, \quad\text{for one fixed integer }n\ge1. \tag{\(F_n\)}\label{eq:property-F} \end{align} In \(\propF\), the same \(n\) is chosen before all positive elements are tested. It therefore applies to every scalar spectral cutoff used later. The premise concerns scalar positives in \(C\), with their indicated amplifications. Condition \(\propP\) is equivalent to pure infiniteness in the nonsimple sense of Kirchberg--R{\o}rdam \cite[Theorem~4.16]{KR2000}; the equivalence is also recalled in \cite[Section~21]{STW99problems}. The existence of an \(n\) satisfying \(\propF\) is \emph{weak pure infiniteness} in the fixed-amplification sense. We use the following formulation of \emph{strong pure infiniteness}, valid for arbitrary \(C^*\)-algebras \cite[Definition~3.1]{KS}: for every \(x,y\in C_+\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), there are \(s,t\in C\) such that \begin{equation}\label{eq:SPI-definition} \|s^*x^2s-x^2\|<\varepsilon,\qquad \|t^*y^2t-y^2\|<\varepsilon,\qquad \|s^*xy t\|<\varepsilon. \end{equation} The coefficients in this formulation have no prescribed norm bound. Kirchberg--R{\o}rdam introduced the weak and strong notions in \cite{KR2002}. Strong pure infiniteness implies ordinary pure infiniteness, and \(\propP\) implies \(\propF\) with \(n=1\). The reverse implications form the comparison question in Kirchberg--R{\o}rdam's Question~9.5, as recalled in \cite[Section~21, Problem~LXXII]{STW99problems}. In version~2 of that problem collection, the question asks whether the three notions agree in general and in the nuclear setting. Our first theorem proves the implication from ordinary to strong pure infiniteness for arbitrary complex \(C^*\)-algebras, in the following more flexible form. [statement] The cross term \(c\) is arbitrary and need not be a product of the diagonal inputs. Taking \((a,b,c)=(x^2,y^2,xy)\) gives the stated consequence. Arbitrary-entry diagonalization is a known equivalent form of strong pure infiniteness \cite[Remarks~3.2(i)--(ii)]{KS}; our proof obtains it directly from \(\propP\). It also gives coefficient bounds for positive contraction inputs that are independent of the requested mixed-entry accuracy; see Proposition~\ref{prop:bounded-diagonalization}. Recall tha […]
[theorem] \label{thm:individual-to-strong} Let \(C\) be a complex \(C^*\)-algebra satisfying \(\propP\). For all \(a,b\in C_+\), \(c\in C\), and \(\varepsilon>0\), there are \(s,t\in C\) such that \begin{equation}\label{eq:main-diagonalization} \|s^*as-a\|<\varepsilon,\qquad \|t^*bt-b\|<\varepsilon,\qquad \|s^*ct\|<\varepsilon. \end{equation} Consequently \(C\) is strongly purely infinite.
[theorem] \label{thm:exact-infiniteness} Let \(C\) be an exact complex \(C^*\)-algebra. Suppose that there is one integer \(n\ge1\) such that \(h^{\oplus n}\) is properly infinite for every \(h\in C_+\). Then every positive element of \(C\) is properly infinite. Consequently \(C\) is strongly purely infinite.
[corollary] \label{cor:absorption} Let \(A\) be a separable nuclear complex \(C^*\)-algebra. If there is one integer \(n\ge1\) such that \(h^{\oplus n}\) is properly infinite for every \(h\in A_+\), then \[ A\cong A\otimes_{\min}\Oinf. \]
