=== MANUSCRIPT: Bloch's Law for Finite-Range Heisenberg Ferromagnets in Three Dimensions (October-5-2026) [preprints/Blochs-Law-for-Finite-Range-Heisenberg-Ferromagnets-in-Three-Dimensions-October-5-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove Bloch's T3/2 law for the spontaneous magnetization of three-dimensional quantum Heisenberg ferromagnets at every fixed positive quantum spin. The result holds for every nonnegative symmetric finite-range interaction whose support generates ℤ3, including spatially anisotropic couplings. The leading magnetization deficit has the exact coefficient determined by the determinant of the quadratic one-magnon dispersion. The thermodynamic limit is taken before the right field derivative at zero, and the low-temperature limit is taken last.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Bloch's law predicts that the spontaneous magnetization of a three-dimensional ferromagnet falls below its fully polarized value by an amount proportional to $T^{3/2}$ at low temperature. We prove this law for the quantum Heisenberg ferromagnet at every fixed spin, allowing any nonnegative symmetric finite-range interaction whose support generates the lattice. The interaction may be spatially anisotropic and need not contain the ordinary nearest-neighbor bonds. Its quadratic dispersion determines the leading coefficient. \subsection{The model and the result} Fix $S\in\{\tfrac12,1,\tfrac32,\ldots\}$. The spin space at each site is $\mathfrak h_S=\C^{2S+1}$, with orthonormal basis $(e_m)_{m=-S}^S$ and operators \[ S^ze_m=me_m,\qquad S^+e_m=\sqrt{S(S+1)-m(m+1)}\,e_{m+1}\quad(m<S),\qquad S^+e_S=0. \] Set $S^-=(S^+)^*$, $S^x=(S^++S^-)/2$, and $S^y=(S^+-S^-)/(2i)$. A subscript specifies the site where an operator acts. Let $J:\Z^3\to[0,\infty)$ have finite support and satisfy \begin{equation} J(0)=0,\qquad J(z)=J(-z),\qquad \langle z:J(z)>0\rangle_{\Z}=\Z^3. \label{eq:interaction} \end{equation} On $\Lambda_N=[-N,N]^3\cap\Z^3$, with free boundary, define \begin{equation} H^J_{S,N}=-\frac12\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_N} J(y-x)\boldsymbol S_x\cdot\boldsymbol S_y, \qquad M_{S,N}=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_N}S_x^z. \label{eq:hamiltonian} \end{equation} The sum counts each unordered bond twice; the factor $1/2$ removes that duplication. We set the lattice spacing, Boltzmann constant, and $\hbar$ equal to one, so $T=\beta^{-1}$. For $\beta>0$ and $h\in\R$, put \begin{equation} p_\beta(h)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1{\beta|\Lambda_N|} \log\Tr e^{-\beta(H^J_{S,N}-hM_{S,N})}, \qquad m_{S,J}(\beta)=\partial_h^+p_\beta(0). \label{eq:pressure-magnetization} \end{equation} The finite-range pressure limit exists and is convex and $S$-Lipschitz; Section~\ref{sec:pressure} recalls the proof. Rotation by $\pi$ about the $x$-axis makes the pressure even, so $m_{S,J}(\beta)\in[0,S]$. The derivative selects the magnetization in a field tending to zero through positive values after the thermodynamic limit. Define the positive definite matrix \begin{equation} D_{S,J}=\frac S2\sum_{z\in\Z^3}J(z)zz^{\mathsf T}. \label{eq:diffusion-matrix} \end{equation} It is the quadratic coefficient of the one-magnon dispersion: \begin{equation} \varepsilon_{S,J}(k)=S\sum_zJ(z)(1-\cos(k\cdot z)) =k^{\mathsf T}D_{S,J}k+O(|k|^4). \label{eq:dispersion} \end{equation} Positive definiteness follows because the interaction support spans $\R^3$. The bosonic representation of Holstein and Primakoff \cite{HolsteinPrimakoff1940} and Dyson's analysis of spin-wave interactions \cite{Dyson1956a,Dyson1956b} developed the low-temperature expansion. In particular, Dyson identified the interaction contribution to the magnetization at order $T^4$; his estimates of the remaining higher-order terms were not a rigorous fixed-spin proof of the asymptotic law \cite[Section~9]{Dyson1956b}. The issue is to justify this coefficient for the interacting spin system and the field derivative in \eqref{eq:pressure-magnetization}. At each site $x$ let $\boldsymbol S_x=(S_x^1,S_x^2,S_x^3)$ be the spin-$S$ operators on $\mathbb C^{2S+1}$. With free boundary conditions, the Hamiltonian and total $z$-spin are \[ H_{S,N}=-\sum_{\substack{\{x,y\}\subset\Lambda_N\\ |x-y|_1=1}} \boldsymbol S_x\cdot\boldsymbol S_y, \qquad M_N=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_N}S_x^3. \] Every unordered lattice edge is counted once. ABSTRACT: We prove the spherical magnetization law for the three-dimensional nearest-neighbor isotropic quantum Heisenberg ferromagnet at every fixed positive quantum spin and every sufficiently low fixed positive temperature. As the even periodic cubes grow, the symmetric zero-field magnetization converges in moments to a uniform direction with a deterministic positive magnitude. This magnitude equals the right derivative at zero field of the infinite-volume pressure. The law is expressed through self-adjoint linear combinations of the spin components and requires no joint measurement of noncommuting observables.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} An isotropic ferromagnet has no preferred direction in zero field. In a finite periodic volume, its Gibbs state is therefore rotation invariant even at temperatures where magnetized equilibrium states exist. The natural description of the ordered phase is a magnetization of fixed length and uniformly distributed direction. Rotation invariance alone does not establish this description: it allows a mixture of different lengths. The question addressed here is whether the symmetric finite-volume state selects a single length, and whether that length is the magnetization selected by an infinitesimal field. \subsection{Model and result} Fix $S\in\{\tfrac12,1,\tfrac32,\ldots\}$. ABSTRACT: For every dimension d ≥ 3 and every spin $`S\in\{\frac12,1,\frac32,\ldots\}`$, we prove that the nearest-neighbor isotropic quantum Heisenberg ferromagnet has a translation-invariant, spontaneously magnetized equilibrium state at every sufficiently low positive temperature. The same state satisfies the KMS condition for the zero-field dynamics and has magnetization at least $`S/4`$. This resolves the low-temperature ordering problem in the spontaneous-magnetization formulation.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} The nearest-neighbor quantum Heisenberg ferromagnet assigns a quantum spin to each lattice site and favors parallel neighboring spins. Its Hamiltonian is invariant under simultaneous rotations of all spins. Spontaneous magnetization asks whether, at zero applied field, an infinite-volume equilibrium state can nevertheless select a direction and have a nonzero one-site spin expectation. The distinction between an individual ordered state and its rotation-invariant average is essential. \subsection{The model and the result}\label{sec:model} Fix an integer $d\geq3$ and a spin $S\in\{\frac12,1,\frac32,\ldots\}$. At each site use $\C^{2S+1}$ with orthonormal basis $(e_m)_{m=-S}^{S}$ and operators \[ S^z e_m=m e_m,\qquad S^+e_m=\sqrt{S(S+1)-m(m+1)}\,e_{m+1},\qquad S^+e_S=0. \] Set $S^-=(S^+)^*$, $S^x=(S^++S^-)/2$ and $S^y=(S^+-S^-)/(2\mathrm i)$, and write $S_x^a$ for the corresponding operator at a site $x$. For finite $\Lambda\subset\Z^d$, the free-boundary Hamiltonian at coupling one is \begin{equation}\label{eq:hamiltonian} H_\Lambda =-\sum_{\substack{\{x,y\}\subset\Lambda\\|x-y|_1=1}} \sum_{a\in\{x,y,z\}} S_x^aS_y^a. \end{equation} The outer sum counts each unordered edge once; in its inner sum the letters $a=x,y,z$ designate spin components. For a finite set $F\subset\Z^d$, let $\mathcal A_F=\bigotimes_{x\in F}M_{2S+1}(\C)$, with the usual embeddings obtained by adjoining identity factors. The quasi-local algebra $\mathcal A$ is the norm completion of their union. The bounded finite-range interaction in \eqref{eq:hamiltonian} defines a dynamics $\tau_t$ by the norm limit, on local observables, of \[ \tau_t^\Lambda(A)=e^{\mathrm itH_\Lambda}A e^{-\mathrm itH_\Lambda} \] along increasing boxes. This limit is independent of the boxes. We use the resulting norm-continuous orbit $t\mapsto\tau_t(A)$ for every $A\in\mathcal A$. The locality estimates underlying this construction originate in Lieb and Robinson~\cite{LiebRobinson1972}. Existence and independence of the exhausting boxes follow from \cite[Theorem~2.2]{NachtergaeleOgataSims2006}; its hypotheses are checked when we use this dynamics in Section~\ref{subsec:deep-dynamics}. A state is a positive linear functional $\omega$ on $\mathcal A$ with $\omega(1)=1$. For $0<\beta<\infty$, it is a $\beta$-KMS state for $\tau$ if, for every $A,B\in\mathcal A$, there is a bounded function $F_{A,B}$, continuous on the closed strip $0\leq\operatorname{Im}z\leq\beta$ and analytic in its interior, such that \begin{equation}\label{eq:kms-definition} F_{A,B}(t)=\omega(A\tau_t(B)),\qquad F_{A,B}(t+\mathrm i\beta)=\omega(\tau_t(B)A) \quad(t\in\R). \end{equation} This is the algebraic equilibrium condition developed by Haag, Hugenholtz and Winnink~\cite{HaagHugenholtzWinnink1967}. We call such a state an equilibrium state at inverse temperature $\beta$. If $T_x$ denotes translation by $x\in\Z^d$, translation invariance means $\omega\circ T_x=\omega$ for every $x$. [statement] Thus the zero-field model has spontaneously magnetized equilibrium states at every sufficiently low positive temperature, for each allowed spin.
