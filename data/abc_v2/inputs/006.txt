=== MANUSCRIPT: Goldfeld's analytic density conjecture and the 2-converse for elliptic curves (October-7-2026) [preprints/Goldfelds-analytic-density-conjecture-and-the-2-converse-for-elliptic-curves-October-7-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove Goldfeld's analytic density conjecture: for every elliptic curve E over ℚ, the quadratic twists of E with analytic rank zero and one each have density 1/2 among signed squarefree twist parameters ordered by absolute value. We also prove the low-corank 2-converse: if the $`2^\infty`$-Selmer corank of E is zero or one, then it equals the analytic and Mordell–Weil ranks, and the Tate–Shafarevich group is finite.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Let $E$ be an elliptic curve over $\Q$. Write \[ a(E)=\ord_{s=1}L(E,s),\qquad r(E)=\rank_{\Z}E(\Q),\qquad c_2(E)=\corank_{\Ztwo}\Sel_{2^\infty}(E/\Q). \] The Selmer group uses the local Kummer conditions at every place. Its corank includes both the Mordell--Weil rank and any divisible $2$-primary contribution from the Tate--Shafarevich group. Modularity supplies the analytic continuation and functional equation of $L(E,s)$ \citep{BreuilConradDiamondTaylor2001}. [statement] There is no restriction on reduction type, complex multiplication, rational torsion, or rational isogenies. The hypothesis concerns the full $2$-power Selmer corank; a Mordell--Weil rank bound alone does not give it. For a nonzero squarefree integer $d$, let $E^{(d)}$ denote the quadratic twist of $E$, and set \[ \mathcal D(X)=\{d\in\Z:0<|d|\le X,\ d\text{ squarefree}\}. \] Thus positive and negative parameters are counted together and ordered by absolute value. A residue class can fix the sign of the functional equation; the half-density statement below concerns the full set $\mathcal D(X)$. [statement] The theorem follows by combining the pointwise $2$-converse with Smith's distribution of full $2$-power Selmer coranks \citep[Theorem~1.1]{Smith2025}. The proof uses the alternating Cassels--Tate pairing to pass from $d_2(E)$ to the full Selmer corank, and is given in Section~\ref{sec:conclusions}. The density theorem resolves the frequency assertion for analytic ranks zero and one. The mean analytic-rank assertion requires additional control: a set of density zero can still contribute to the rank-weighted average. The separate companion \citet[Theorems~1.1 and~1.2]{OpenAIMeanAnalyticRank2026} proves the required analytic tail estimate and, together with Theorem~\ref{thm:main-density}, obtains mean analytic rank $1/2$ in the same signed squarefree ordering. Its proof uses the $2$-converse and analytic twist densities established here. The rank and finiteness assertions are distinct from the Birch--Swinnerton-Dyer leading-coefficient formula. Their common analytic and Mordell--Weil ranks are zero and one with density $1/2$ each. [theorem] [The $2$-converse]\label{thm:main-converse} For every elliptic curve $E/\Q$ with $c_2(E)\in\{0,1\}$, \[ a(E)=r(E)=c_2(E),\qquad \Sha(E/\Q)\text{ is finite}. \]
[theorem] [Goldfeld's analytic density conjecture]\label{thm:main-density} For every elliptic curve $E/\Q$ and each $j\in\{0,1\}$, \[ \lim_{X\to\infty} \frac{\#\{d\in\mathcal D(X):a(E^{(d)})=j\}}{\#\mathcal D(X)} =\frac12. \] Consequently the twists of analytic rank at least two have density zero. For a density-one set of signed squarefree parameters $d$, the analytic and Mordell--Weil ranks of $E^{(d)}$ agree and its whole Tate--Shafarevich group is finite.
[corollary] [A finite Selmer criterion]\label{cor:finite-descent} If $d_2(E)\in\{0,1\}$, then \[ a(E)=r(E)=c_2(E)=d_2(E),\qquad \Sha(E/\Q)\text{ is finite},\qquad \Sha(E/\Q)[2^\infty]=0. \]

=== MANUSCRIPT: The mean analytic rank of quadratic twists of elliptic curves (October-6-2026) [preprints/The-mean-analytic-rank-of-quadratic-twists-of-elliptic-curves-October-6-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: For every elliptic curve over ℚ, we prove that the average analytic rank of its quadratic twists tends to 1/2 when signed squarefree twist parameters are ordered by absolute value. This resolves Goldfeld's mean analytic-rank conjecture in this counting convention.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Goldfeld's mean-rank conjecture predicts that the average central order of vanishing in a quadratic-twist family is \(1/2\) \cite[p.~113, Conjecture~(B)]{Goldfeld1979}. The issue addressed here is the contribution of rare twists of large analytic rank: knowing the densities of ranks zero and one does not determine the mean without control of this contribution. Let \(E/\Q\) be an elliptic curve. For a nonzero squarefree integer \(d\), write \(E^{(d)}\) for its quadratic twist, and put \[ \mathcal D(Y)=\{d\in\Z:0<|d|\le Y,\ d\text{ squarefree}\}, \qquad a(E^{(d)})=\ord_{s=1}L(E^{(d)},s). \] Both signs of \(d\) are included. We call \(a\) the analytic rank and write \(r(E^{(d)})=\operatorname{rank}_{\Z}E^{(d)}(\Q)\) for the algebraic or Mordell--Weil rank. Outside the explicitly algebraic statements below, rank means analytic order of vanishing. [statement] Theorem~\ref{thm:mean} resolves positively Goldfeld's mean analytic-rank conjecture in this signed squarefree counting convention. It applies without restrictions on complex multiplication, rational torsion, rational isogenies, or reduction type. The analytic density theorem \cite[Theorem~1.2]{OpenAIAnalyticDensityV2} states that, for this same family and every \(E/\Q\), \begin{equation}\label{eq:density-input} \frac{\#\{d\in\mathcal D(Y):a(E^{(d)})=j\}}{\#\mathcal D(Y)} \longrightarrow\frac12 \qquad(j=0,1). \end{equation} For the analytic mean theorem, \eqref{eq:density-input} is the only input from the companion manuscript identified in the bibliography. A zero-density exceptional family can still contribute to a rank-weighted average. The new assertion needed for the mean is the following tail estimate, whose proof is independent of \eqref{eq:density-input}. [statement] Together, \eqref{eq:density-input} and Theorem~\ref{thm:tail} show that \[ \sum_{\substack{d\in\mathcal D(Y)\\a(E^{(d)})\ge2}}a(E^{(d)})=o_E(Y). \] For each fixed sufficiently large integer \(R\), \eqref{eq:density-input} makes the complement of ranks zero and one have density zero, so ranks \(2\) through \(R\) contribute \(o_{E,R}(Y)\). For the remaining ranks, use the height limsup in Theorem~\ref{thm:tail} and then let \(R\) tend to infinity. This order of limits will be made explicit in Section~\ref{sec:tail}. \subsection{Algebraic-rank moments} The same signed squarefree family has the following algebraic-rank moment limits. This consequence uses the companion's density conclusions and its density-one equality of analytic and algebraic ranks, together with the exponential-moment bound of Koymans and Smith. It does not use the analytic tail estimate of Theorem~\ref{thm:tail}. [statement] The proof appears in Section~\ref{sec:algebraic}. All parameters \(t\) and moment orders \(m\) in Corollary~\ref{cor:algebraic-moments} are fixed before \(Y\to\infty\). The corollary concerns algebraic ranks only: it gives no higher analytic-rank moments, no uniformity for parameters varying with \(Y\), and no moment limit for thin polynomial subfamilies. \subsection{History and significance} Goldfeld's original question concerns the change of rank when a fixed elliptic curve is viewed over quadratic fields. Under Birch--Swinnerton-Dyer, that change is the central order of the associated quadratic twist. His Conjecture~(B) formulates the average directly in terms of this analytic order, using quadratic-field discriminants ordered by absolute value \cite[pp.~113--114]{Goldfeld1979}. Here the counting parameter is instead the signed squarefree integer \(d\) in \(\mathcal D(Y)\). The rank-zero/rank-one density prediction describes the smallest orders allowed by the two functional-equation signs. The mean prediction also requires the larger orders, however sparse, to have negligible total contribution. Conditional analytic estimates illustrate both aspects of the problem. [theorem] \label{thm:mean} For every elliptic curve \(E/\Q\), \[ \lim_{Y\to\infty}\frac{1}{\#\mathcal D(Y)} \sum_{d\in\mathcal D(Y)}a(E^{(d)})=\frac12. \]
[theorem] \label{thm:tail} For every elliptic curve \(E/\Q\), there are constants \(C_E>0\) and an integer \(R_E\ge1\) such that, for every integer \(R\ge R_E\), \[ \limsup_{Y\to\infty}\frac1Y \sum_{\substack{d\in\mathcal D(Y)\\a(E^{(d)})>R}}a(E^{(d)}) \le \frac{C_E}{R}. \]
