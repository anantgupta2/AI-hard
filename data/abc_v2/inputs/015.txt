FAMILY 015: Torus-packet equidistribution in prime, quartic, and sextic degrees
SUBJECT: Number theory
OPENAI SUMMARY: Proves Haar equidistribution without escape of mass for complete volume-weighted torus packets from totally real fields: arbitrary lattices and prescribed local types in fixed prime degree at least five, arbitrary-order Picard packets in primitive quartic fields, and maximal-order ideal-class packets in primitive sextic fields. Here primitive means having no proper intermediate field; the relevant order or field discriminant tends to infinity.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Equidistribution of Prime-Degree Torus Packets with Arbitrary Local Type (September-24-2026) [preprints/Equidistribution-of-Prime-Degree-Torus-Packets-with-Arbitrary-Local-Type-September-24-2026/build/paper.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove the packet form of the higher-dimensional Duke equidistribution problem for totally real fields of any fixed prime degree at least five, allowing arbitrary local homothety types of full lattices. As the multiplier-order discriminant tends to infinity, the volume-weighted packet measures converge to Haar probability measure, with no escape of mass.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:intro} Compact diagonal orbits in the space of unimodular lattices encode ideal classes and units of totally real number fields. The higher-dimensional Duke equidistribution problem asks whether arithmetically complete families of these orbits become uniformly distributed as their discriminants grow. The grouping matters: one local homothety type specifies a complete packet, whereas a multiplier order alone can combine several packets. We prove the prime-degree packet statement for every such local type. Fix a prime $n\ge5$, and write \[ X_n=\SL_n(\Z)\backslash\SL_n(\R),\qquad A_n=\{\diag(e^{u_1},\ldots,e^{u_n}):\textstyle\sum_j u_j=0\}. \] We regard $X_n$ as the space of covolume-one row lattices, with the right action of $A_n$, and let $m_n$ be its $\SL_n(\R)$-invariant probability measure. Haar measure on $A_n$ is $du_1\cdots du_{n-1}$ in the displayed logarithmic coordinates. Let $K$ be a totally real field of degree $n$, let $\sigma=(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_n)$ order its real embeddings, and let $M\subset K$ be a free rank-$n$ $\Z$-submodule spanning $K$ over $\Q$. We call such an $M$ a full lattice. Its multiplier order and discriminant are \[ \cO(M)=\{\alpha\in K:\alpha M\subset M\},\qquad D(M)=|\Disc(K)|[\cO_K:\cO(M)]^2. \] Two such lattices are \emph{locally homothetic} if $M'_\ell=c_\ell M_\ell$ for every rational prime $\ell$, where $c_\ell\in(K\otimes_\Q\Q_\ell)^\times$ and subscripts denote completion. Choose representatives $M'$ for their $K^\times$-homothety classes within the local type of $M$, and normalize \[ \Lambda_{M',\sigma} =\operatorname{covol}(\sigma(M'))^{-1/n}\sigma(M'). \] The packet $\mathcal P_{K,M,\sigma}$ consists of the distinct orbits $\Lambda_{M',\sigma}wA_n$, with \[ w\in W_n=\{\diag(\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_n): \epsilon_j\in\{\pm1\},\ \textstyle\prod_j\epsilon_j=1\}. \] These orbits are compact and their number is finite; the parametrization in Section~\ref{sec:packets} also proves these facts. For an orbit $O$, let $\nu_O$ be its invariant probability and $\vol(O)$ its volume for the specified Haar measure. Set \begin{equation}\label{eq:packet-probability} \mu_{K,M,\sigma} =\frac{\sum_{O\in\mathcal P_{K,M,\sigma}}\vol(O)\nu_O} {\sum_{O\in\mathcal P_{K,M,\sigma}}\vol(O)}. \end{equation} [statement] Theorem~\ref{thm:main} permits nonmaximal orders of unbounded index and arbitrary local module types. The field discriminants may remain bounded or grow independently of those indices. The statement is separate for each local homothety type; it makes no assertion about an individual orbit, a convergence rate, or composite degrees. \subsection*{The maximal-order specialization} Take $M=\cO_K$. Every lattice in its local type is a fractional $\cO_K$-ideal, and its homothety class is an ordinary ideal class. Thus the packet is obtained by embedding one representative of each ideal class, normalizing its covolume to one, and taking the distinct sign translates of its positive diagonal orbit. For every sequence of totally real fields of degree $n$ with $|\Disc(K)|\to\infty$, the volume-weighted measures of these packets converge to $m_n$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. This construction also explains why an individual ideal class is not the object being averaged. For general $M$, the same maximal-order ideal classes remain the global parameters, but each carries a finite set of local module choices. Proposition~\ref{prop:adelic-packet} in Appendix~\ref{subsec:adelic-packet} identifies this construction with the pushforward of adelic torus Haar probability, independently of the rational basis and finite-lattice presentation. The theorem therefore includes all these presentations of the maximal-order specialization, as well as arbitrary noninvertible modules over nonmaximal orders. \subsection*{Context and prior work} Linnik's ergodic approach connected arithmetic distribution problems with dynamics \cite{Linnik1968}. [theorem] \label{thm:main} For every fixed prime $n\ge5$ and every sequence $(K_i,M_i,\sigma_i)$ as above with \mbox{$D(M_i)\to\infty$}, \[ \mu_{K_i,M_i,\sigma_i}\longrightarrow m_n. \] The convergence is weak convergence of probability measures. === MANUSCRIPT: Equidistribution of primitive quartic torus packets for arbitrary orders (October-5-2026) [preprints/Equidistribution-of-Primitive-Quartic-Torus-Packets-for-Arbitrary-Orders-October-5-2026/build/manuscript/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: Let K range over totally real quartic fields with no proper intermediate field, and let $`\mathcal O`$ be any order in K. We prove that the packets of periodic diagonal orbits attached to invertible $`\mathcal O`$-ideal classes, weighted by orbit volume, equidistribute with no escape of mass as $`|\mathop{\mathrm{Disc}}\nolimits (\mathcal O)|`$ tends to infinity. The proof combines measure rigidity with a cubic-resolvent estimate that remains uniform at primes dividing the order index.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Ideal classes in a totally real number field give compact orbits of a diagonal group on a space of lattices. A natural arithmetic question is whether the finite collections of orbits obtained in this way become uniformly distributed as their discriminants grow. We prove this for the complete packets attached to invertible ideals of every order in a primitive totally real quartic field. The order may have arbitrarily large index in the ring of integers. Let \[ X_4=\mathrm{SL}_4(\Z)\backslash\mathrm{SL}_4(\R) \] be the space of covolume-one row lattices in $\R^4$, and let $m_4$ be its invariant probability measure. Put \[ H_4=\{u\in\R^4:u_1+u_2+u_3+u_4=0\},\qquad A_4=\{\diag(e^{u_1},e^{u_2},e^{u_3},e^{u_4}):u\in H_4\}. \] The group $A_4$ acts on $X_4$ by right multiplication. Its Haar measure is $du_1\,du_2\,du_3$, with $u_4=-u_1-u_2-u_3$. A quartic field $K$ is \emph{primitive} if it has no field strictly between $\Q$ and $K$. Let $K$ be primitive and totally real, let $\OO\subseteq\OO_K$ be an order, and fix an ordering $\sigma=(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_4)$ of its real embeddings. Here an order is a full-rank subring containing $1$. A fractional $\OO$-ideal $I$ is \emph{proper} if its multiplier order $\{a\in K:aI\subseteq I\}$ is $\OO$, and it is \emph{invertible} if $IJ=\OO$ for some fractional $\OO$-ideal $J$. The ordinary ideal class group $\Pic(\OO)$ consists of proper invertible fractional $\OO$-ideals modulo $I\sim aI$ for $a\in K^\times$. Using all signs makes the set independent of the chosen ordinary ideal representatives. Every orbit is compact by the unit theorem; Section~\ref{sec:packets} proves these assertions and describes the associated measure. For an orbit $\mathcal Y\in\mathcal P_{\OO,\sigma}$, let $\nu_{\mathcal Y}$ be its invariant probability and let $\vol(\mathcal Y)$ be its volume for the specified Haar measure. [theorem] \label{thm:main} Let $(K_i,\OO_i,\sigma_i)$ be any sequence in which $K_i$ is a primitive totally real quartic field, $\OO_i$ is an order in $K_i$, and $\sigma_i$ is an ordering of its real embeddings. === MANUSCRIPT: Equidistribution of Primitive Sextic Torus Packets (October-5-2026) [preprints/Equidistribution-of-Primitive-Sextic-Torus-Packets-October-5-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove that the complete ideal-class packets of maximal orders in totally real sextic fields with no proper intermediate fields become equidistributed, with no escape of mass, in the space of unimodular lattices as their field discriminants tend to infinity. The limit is Haar probability measure. Each packet includes all coordinate-sign translates and is weighted by diagonal-orbit volume.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} The real embeddings of a totally real number field turn its ideals into lattices. Multiplication by positive diagonal matrices changes the relative sizes of the embedding coordinates while preserving covolume. The units make each resulting diagonal orbit compact, and the ideal classes assemble these orbits into a finite packet. The distribution of such packets connects the arithmetic of number fields with the dynamics of higher-rank diagonal groups. We prove equidistribution for totally real sextic fields having no proper intermediate field. \subsection{Packets and the main theorem} Let \[ X_6=\SL_6(\Z)\backslash\SL_6(\R),\qquad A_6=\{\diag(e^{u_1},\ldots,e^{u_6}):\textstyle\sum_j u_j=0\}. \] We regard $X_6$ as the space of covolume-one lattices in $\R^6$, with matrices acting on row vectors on the right. Let $m_6$ denote its $\SL_6(\R)$-invariant probability. We normalize Haar measure on $A_6$ as $\dd u_1\cdots\dd u_5$, with $u_6=-\sum_{j=1}^5u_j$. A degree-six number field $K$ is \emph{primitive} if there is no field $F$ with $\Q\subsetneq F\subsetneq K$. Suppose $K$ is totally real, choose an ordering $\sigma=(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_6)$ of its real embeddings, and write $\mathcal O_K$ for its ring of integers. For a nonzero fractional $\mathcal O_K$-ideal $I$, set \[ \sigma(I)=\{(\sigma_1(d),\ldots,\sigma_6(d)):d\in I\},\qquad \Lambda_{I,\sigma}=\covol(\sigma(I))^{-1/6}\sigma(I). \] Choose one representative of each ordinary ideal class. Let $\mathcal P_{K,\sigma}$ be the set of distinct compact orbits \[ \Lambda_{I,\sigma}wA_6, \qquad w=\diag(\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_6),\quad \epsilon_j\in\{1,-1\}. \] Here every sign matrix acts on the underlying lattice, including those of determinant $-1$. Dirichlet's unit theorem gives compactness of the orbits. Including all signs makes the packet independent of the chosen ordinary ideal-class representatives. For $\mathcal O\in\mathcal P_{K,\sigma}$, let $m_{\mathcal O}$ be its invariant probability and $\vol_A(\mathcal O)$ its volume for the specified Haar measure. The packet probability is \begin{equation}\label{eq:packet-definition} \mu_{K,\sigma}= \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal O\in\mathcal P_{K,\sigma}} \vol_A(\mathcal O)m_{\mathcal O}} {\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal O\in\mathcal P_{K,\sigma}} \vol_A(\mathcal O)}. \end{equation} [statement] The theorem concerns each complete packet attached to a field. It allows the fields and their embedding orderings to vary freely under the stated hypotheses. The arithmetic role of primitivity is to make every nonrational integer of $K_i$ generate the full field; this will yield uniform separation in the dual lattice used to count thin tubes. \subsection{History and the composite-degree obstruction} For real quadratic fields, the corresponding packets project to packets of closed geodesics on the modular surface. Linnik's ergodic method, including Skubenko's treatment of the one-sheeted hyperboloid, gave equidistribution under an auxiliary splitting condition \cite[Chapter~VI]{Linnik1968}; see also the historical statements in \cite[Theorems~1.1--1.2]{ELMV2012}. Duke removed this condition for fundamental discriminants by analytic methods \cite[Theorem~1]{Duke1988}. Einsiedler, Lindenstrauss, Michel, and Venkatesh later gave a dynamical proof formulated for all positive nonsquare order discriminants~\cite[Theorem~2.3]{ELMV2012}. In higher degree the compact diagonal orbits have dimension greater than one, and the dynamics of the full diagonal group provides additional rigidity. Einsiedler, Katok, and Lindenstrauss proved that, on $\SL_d(\Z)\backslash\SL_d(\R)$ for $d\ge3$, an ergodic probability invariant under the full positive diagonal group and having positive entropy for a diagonal flow is algebraic~\cite[Theorem~1.3]{EKL2006}.
