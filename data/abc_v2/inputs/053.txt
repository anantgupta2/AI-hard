FAMILY 053: A counterexample to Pixton completeness in Chow
SUBJECT: Algebraic and complex geometry
OPENAI SUMMARY: Constructs a tautological relation on a moduli space of stable pointed curves that vanishes in rational Chow, hence in rational cohomology, but lies outside Pixton's original relation span. This disproves the Chow and rational-cohomological forms of his original completeness conjecture.

=== MANUSCRIPT: A high-arity counterexample to Pixton completeness in Chow (September-24-2026) [preprints/A-high-arity-counterexample-to-Pixton-completeness-in-Chow-September-24-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We disprove the Chow and rational-cohomological forms of Pixton's completeness conjecture for his original relation system. We construct a formal tautological class outside the original Pixton relation span whose image is zero in the Chow ring and in rational cohomology. The example has genus $`10^{60}`$ and $`3\binom{10^{60}}3`$ markings.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} The tautological ring of the moduli space of stable curves is generated by cotangent and kappa classes, together with the classes obtained by forgetting markings and gluing nodes. Describing all relations among these natural classes is a central problem in the intersection theory of moduli spaces. Pixton proposed a uniform system of stable-graph relations and conjectured that it is complete. We construct a relation outside that system: an alternating product of corrected small diagonals that vanishes in rational Chow, and therefore in rational cohomology. \subsection{The relation problem} Mumford initiated the systematic study of natural classes in the Chow ring of the moduli space of curves \cite[Sections~4--5]{Mumford1983}. Faber's calculations and geometric constructions on powers of the universal curve led to a proposed description of the tautological ring of the smooth unpointed space \(\mathcal M_g\), including its Gorenstein property \cite[Conjectures~1--2]{Faber1999}. Faber and Zagier later gave explicit hypergeometric formulas for relations among the kappa classes. Pandharipande--Pixton proved these relations in Chow using stable quotients and formulated their completeness on \(\mathcal M_g\) \cite[Theorem~1 and Conjecture~2]{PandharipandePixton2021}. On the compactification, a presentation must also account for boundary strata and their intersections. Arbarello--Cornalba and Graber--Pandharipande developed the forgetful and boundary calculus \cite[Section~1]{ArbarelloCornalba1996}, \cite[Appendix~A]{GraberPandharipande2003}; in genus zero, Keel gave a presentation of the entire Chow ring by boundary divisors \cite[Section~4, Theorem~1]{Keel1992}. Pixton's 2012 graph formula extends the Faber--Zagier system to \(\Mbar_{g,n}\) and proposes a complete set of relations \cite[Sections~1--3, Conjecture~2]{Pixton}. Pandharipande--Pixton--Zvonkine proved the cohomological validity of this system using the \(3\)-spin CohFT \cite{PPZ}; Janda proved its Chow validity using equivariant Gromov--Witten theory of \(\mathbb P^1\) \cite{Janda}. These results establish relations, while completeness asks whether any further relations exist. We fix the original relation space precisely. Let \(\Salg_{g,n}\) be the strata algebra of formal decorated stable graphs, with decoration degree truncated at the dimension of each vertex factor. Its realization is \[ q_{g,n}\colon\Salg_{g,n}\longrightarrow\CH^*(\Mbar_{g,n};\Q), \qquad [\Gamma,\gamma]\longmapsto\xi_{\Gamma*}\gamma. \] The gluing pushforward generators are unnormalized, as in \cite[Section~0.3]{PPZ}. Let \(\Pspan_{g,n}\) be the span obtained by placing one of Pixton's graph-formula relations at a vertex of an outer stable graph, placing arbitrary strata classes at the other vertices, and gluing. Pixton's Proposition~1 shows that this span is already an ideal, stable under the gluing and forgetting pushforwards and pullbacks \cite{Pixton}. Thus it includes these formal consequences from the outset. The smaller \(0/1\)-marking seed system recovers the same span under the operations described in \cite[Section~3.5]{PPZ}. Section~\ref{sec:strata} gives the full recipe and all conventions. Writing \(R^*(\Mbar_{g,n})=\im q_{g,n}\), the Chow completeness question is whether \[ \ker q_{g,n}=\Pspan_{g,n} \] for every stable pair. The known validity theorem gives the inclusion \(\Pspan_{g,n}\subseteq\ker q_{g,n}\). Our example makes it strict. \subsection{The counterexample} The construction uses \(g\) colors and one three-label factor for each increasing triple of colors. Each factor is a product of two rational-tail divisor sums, corrected by a class supported on two-node rational bridges. We multiply these factors and alternate over relabelings that preserve the color of each slot. Definition~\ref{def:candidate} specifies the resulting formal class \(Y\). [theorem] \label{thm:main} Set \[ g=10^{60},\qquad D_*=\binom g3,\qquad n=3D_*. \] The explicitly defined class \(Y\in \Salg_{g,n}^{2D_*}\) of Definition~\ref{def:candidate} satisfies \[ q_{g,n}(Y)=0,\qquad Y\notin\Pspan_{g,n}. \] Consequently Pixton's original relation span does not exhaust the tautological Chow relations: the equality \(\ker q_{g,n}=\Pspan_{g,n}\) fails for this stable pair.
[corollary] [Rational-cohomological incompleteness] \label{cor:cohomological-incompleteness} For the stable pair and class \(Y\) of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, \[ q^H_{g,n}(Y)=0 \quad\text{in }H^{4D_*}\!\left(\Mbar_{g,n}(\C);\Q\right), \qquad Y\notin\Pspan_{g,n}. \] In particular, \(\Pspan_{g,n}\subsetneq\ker q^H_{g,n}\).
