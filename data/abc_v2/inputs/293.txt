ABSTRACT: For every irrational angle, we construct a continuous nonnegative circle weight with exactly one zero and logarithmic integral $`-\infty`$ whose weighted rotation has no nontrivial invariant projection in the associated hyperfinite type II1 factor. This answers negatively the question of Zhu, Fang, and Shi, with the angle prescribed in advance. The resulting operator is nonzero and norm-quasinilpotent.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Let $\theta\in(0,1)$ be irrational. In the tracial representation let $R_\theta$ be the von Neumann algebra generated by unitaries $U,V$ with \[ VU=e^{2\pi i\theta}UV,\qquad \tau(U^mV^n)=0\quad((m,n)\in\Z^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}),\qquad\tau(1)=1. \] This is the hyperfinite factor of type $\mathrm{II}_1$; the concrete representation used below is the group-measure-space representation of irrational rotation. For a continuous function $f$ on the unit circle $\T$, write $T_f=Uf(V)$. A closed invariant subspace is \emph{affiliated with $R_\theta$} when its orthogonal projection belongs to $R_\theta$. A nontrivial such subspace is therefore equivalent to a projection $p\in R_\theta$ satisfying \[ 0<\tau(p)<1,\qquad (1-p)T_fp=0. \] There is no requirement that $p$ commute with $T_f$. Let $m$ denote normalized Haar measure on $\T$. The Fuglede--Kadison determinant of the scalar weight is \[ \Delta(f(V))=\exp\left(\int_\T\log|f(z)|\,dm(z)\right), \] with $\log0=-\infty$ and $\exp(-\infty)=0$. Zhu, Fang, and Shi asked whether $T_f$ must have a nontrivial affiliated invariant subspace if $f$ is nonzero almost everywhere, $|f|$ is nonconstant, and this determinant is zero \citep[p.~264, Question after Proposition~5.7]{zfs}. We answer their question negatively for every prescribed irrational angle. [statement] The angle is fixed before the weight is constructed; no Diophantine condition is imposed. The conclusion concerns projections in the specified containing factor. Arbitrary invariant subspaces of its Hilbert-space representation need not have their projections in that factor. Corollary~\ref{cor:quasinilpotent} also shows directly that the constructed operator is nonzero and norm-quasinilpotent. \subsection{Hyperinvariant subspaces and commutants} For a bounded operator $T$ on a complex Hilbert space $\mathcal H$, write $\{T\}'=\{A\in B(\mathcal H):AT=TA\}$. A closed subspace is \emph{hyperinvariant} for $T$ if every member of $\{T\}'$ leaves it invariant. An operator algebra is \emph{transitive} if its only common closed invariant subspaces are $0$ and $\mathcal H$. \begin{lemma}[Commutant algebra]\label{lem:commutant-algebra} If $T\in B(\mathcal H)$ is nonscalar, then $\{T\}'$ is a proper, strong-operator closed, unital complex algebra. It is transitive exactly when $T$ has no nontrivial hyperinvariant subspace. \end{lemma} \begin{proof} The commutation equation is preserved by addition, multiplication and scalar multiplication, and the identity commutes with $T$. If $A_\lambda\to A$ strongly and $A_\lambda T=TA_\lambda$, then for every $\zeta\in\mathcal H$ both sides converge, giving $AT\zeta=TA\zeta$. Thus the algebra is strongly closed. If every bounded operator commuted with $T$, then every rank-one orthogonal projection would commute with $T$, so every nonzero vector would be an eigenvector. Applying this to $x,y,x+y$ for linearly independent vectors shows that the eigenvalues are equal; hence $T$ would be scalar. Properness follows. The last assertion is precisely the two definitions. \end{proof} The bridge to the containing von Neumann algebra is the elementary observation recorded in \citet[Proposition~2.1]{dykema2005}. \begin{lemma}\label{lem:hyperinvariant-projection} Let $\mathcal M\subset B(\mathcal H)$ be a von Neumann algebra and $T\in\mathcal M$. The orthogonal projection onto any hyperinvariant subspace for $T$ belongs to $\mathcal M$ and is invariant for $T$. \end{lemma} \begin{proof} Every unitary $W\in\mathcal M'$ and its inverse commute with $T$. Hyperinvariance therefore makes the subspace reducing for $W$. Its orthogonal projection commutes with all such unitaries and hence belongs to $\mathcal M''=\mathcal M$. Since $T\in\{T\}'$, its range is invariant under $T$. \end{proof} [statement] \begin{proof} The tracial representation of $R_\theta$ is separable and infinite-dimensional. === MANUSCRIPT: Backward intertwiners and a transitive commutant (September-27-2026) [preprints/Backward-intertwiners-and-a-transitive-commutant-September-27-2026/build/paper.tex]
ABSTRACT: We give a negative answer to the hyperinvariant-subspace problem by constructing, on every infinite-dimensional separable complex Hilbert space, a nonzero bounded norm-quasinilpotent operator with no nonzero proper closed hyperinvariant subspace. Its commutant is a proper strongly closed unital transitive complex operator algebra.
INTRODUCTION (start): Let $H$ be a complex Hilbert space and let $\B(H)$ denote its bounded linear operators. The \emph{commutant} of $S\in\B(H)$ is \[ \{S\}'=\{A\in\B(H):AS=SA\}. \] A closed subspace is \emph{hyperinvariant} for $S$ if every operator in $\{S\}'$ leaves it invariant. An operator algebra is \emph{transitive} if its only closed common invariant subspaces are $\{0\}$ and $H$. Thus $S$ has no nonzero proper closed hyperinvariant subspace precisely when its commutant is transitive. The hyperinvariant-subspace problem asks whether every bounded nonscalar operator on an infinite-dimensional separable complex Hilbert space has a nonzero proper closed hyperinvariant subspace. The construction below gives a negative answer. Its purpose is to exhibit the mechanism explicitly: the ordinary invariant subspaces of each weighted-shift fiber are coordinate tails, and a second, commuting operator moves those tails backward. Invariance under the commutant therefore imposes a strict descent that cannot persist on a probability space. \subsection{The construction theorem} Put $K=\ell^2(\Z)$, with orthonormal basis $(e_j)_{j\in\Z}$, and define \[ \Ktail{h}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{e_j:j\ge h\} \quad(h\in\Z),\qquad \Ktail{-\infty}=K,\qquad \Ktail{+\infty}=\{0\}. \] Let $X=\Z_2$ be the compact additive group of $2$-adic integers, the inverse limit of the groups $\Z/2^m\Z$. Equip it with completed Haar probability $\mu$, put $\theta x=x+1$, and set $H_0=L^2(X,\mu;K)$. For $g\in L^\infty(X)$, the scalar multiplier is $(M_gf)(x)=g(x)f(x)$. [statement] All generators in the last assertion commute with $S$. The explicit power bound implies norm-quasinilpotence, $\lim_{n\to\infty}\|S^n\|^{1/n}=0$, while the first assertion will give many ordinary invariant subspaces of the resulting operator. [statement] Here the strong operator topology is pointwise norm convergence on $H$. Consequently the commutant in the corollary also gives a negative answer to the question whether every unital transitive operator algebra is strongly dense in $\B(H)$. \subsection{Context and relation to the companion} Douglas and Pearcy studied the connection between hyperinvariant subspaces and transitive algebras~\cite{DouglasPearcy1972}. A major positive result is Arveson's density theorem: a transitive algebra on a separable Hilbert space which contains a maximal abelian self-adjoint subalgebra of $\B(H)$ is strongly dense in $\B(H)$ \cite[Theorem~3.3]{Arveson1967}. The scalar multiplier algebra used here has infinite multiplicity. Section~\ref{sec:structure} gives an explicit operator commuting with every such multiplier that is not itself a scalar multiplier, so the maximality hypothesis is absent. Domar's work on invariant subspaces of weighted translation spaces \cite{Domar1981} and Grabiner's work on unicellular shifts \cite{Grabiner1982} provide the classical lattice ideas behind the fibers. Section~\ref{sec:criterion} proves the precise quadratic weighted-translation criterion we need. Its convex-envelope and support-separation argument follows Domar's method. The proof includes both normalized convolution-tail estimates; no general criterion for arbitrary weights is assumed. Our additional task is to link the fibers by a bounded commuting operator that moves their invariant tails backward. Opposite logarithmic changes at the two ends of the bilateral sequence make this possible, and one measurable normalization controls the operator over the whole base. Atzmon and Sodin constructed completely indecomposable bilateral weighted shifts with spectrum the unit circle~\cite{AtzmonSodin1999}; their 1999 paper left open whether those shifts have proper hyperinvariant subspaces. Here the extra commutant action is supplied by transport between different fibers. For a study of operators with shift-type invariant subspaces, see K\'erchy~\cite{Kerchy2026}. [theorem] \label{thm:direct-model} There are measurable positive weights $\beta_j:X\to(0,\infty)$, $j\in\Z$, and a contraction $V\in\B(H_0)$ with the following properties. The shifts \[ S_xe_j=\beta_j(x)e_{j+1},\qquad (Sf)(x)=S_xf(x), \] define a bounded operator $S$ on $H_0$, and: \begin{enumerate} \item For every $x\in X$, the closed invariant subspaces of $S_x$ are exactly $\Ktail{h}$, $h\in\Z\cup\{-\infty,+\infty\}$. \item The operator $S$ is nonzero, $\|S\|=e^{-10}$, and for every integer $n\ge1$, \begin{equation}\label{eq:main-power-bound} \|S^n\|\le \exp\left(-10\left\lfloor\frac{(n+1)^2}{4}\right\rfloor\right). \end{equation} \item The operator $V$ commutes with $S$ and has the form \[ (Vf)(\theta x)=V_xf(x),\qquad V_xe_j=b_j(x)e_{j-1}, \] where $0\le b_j(x)\le1$ are measurable and are positive on a common conull set. On that set, \[ \overline{V_x\Ktail{h}}=\Ktail{h-1}\quad(h\in\Z), \qquad \overline{V_xK}=K. \] \item The unital complex algebra generated by $S$, $V$, and all scalar multipliers $M_g$ is transitive. \end{enumerate}
