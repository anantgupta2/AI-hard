FAMILY 082: Annular variation and dyadic absolute bounds for the triangular Hilbert transform
SUBJECT: Real and complex analysis
OPENAI SUMMARY: Proves maximal and annular r-variation bounds, for every r &gt; 2, from complex $`L^3(\mathbb R^2)\times L^3(\mathbb R^2)`$ to $`L^{3/2}(\mathbb R^2)`$. The maximal estimate controls both hard truncation endpoints and gives almost-everywhere and norm convergence. Pairing with a third input settles the triangular Hilbert transform estimate at the symmetric $`L^3\times L^3\times L^3`$ point.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Annular variation of the triangular Hilbert transform at the symmetric point (October-5-2026) [preprints/Annular-variation-of-the-triangular-Hilbert-transform-at-the-symmetric-point-October-5-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove the annular r-variation estimate for the triangular Hilbert transform from complex $`L^3\times L^3`$ to L3/2 for every r &gt; 2. The partitions may depend on the output point and range over all positive scales. The estimate yields the two-endpoint maximal bound and joint almost-everywhere and norm principal values, and resolves the symmetric scalar triangular Hilbert transform problem.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} For complex functions $F,G\in L^3(\R^2)$ and $0<\varepsilon<R<\infty$, define the annular triangular Hilbert transform by \begin{equation}\label{eq:bilinear-truncation} B_{\varepsilon,R}(F,G)(x,y) =\int_{\varepsilon<|t|<R}F(x+t,y)G(x,y+t)\,\frac{\dd t}{t}. \end{equation} The two inputs are translated along different coordinate directions. Every finite integral is absolutely convergent outside one common null set; we use its usual representative there and set all quantities to zero on the exceptional set. Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-truncations} justifies this convention and continuity in the endpoints. For $r>2$, its annular variation is \begin{equation}\label{eq:variation-definition} V_r(F,G)(x,y) =\sup_{\substack{J\ge1\,,\ 0<t_0<\cdots<t_J\\t_j\in\mathbb Q}} \left(\sum_{j=1}^J |B_{t_{j-1},t_j}(F,G)(x,y)|^r\right)^{1/r}. \end{equation} The supremum is pointwise: the partition may depend on $(x,y)$, and there is no restriction on the number of endpoints within a dyadic scale interval. The use of rational endpoints makes measurability immediate and, by endpoint continuity, does not change the supremum. [statement] Taking a single increment gives the maximal estimate with the supremum over both hard truncation endpoints. In Section~\ref{sec:consequences} we prove that the joint principal value \[ B(F,G)=\lim_{\substack{\varepsilon\downarrow0\\R\uparrow\infty}} B_{\varepsilon,R}(F,G) \] exists almost everywhere and in $L^{3/2}$. In fact, the supremum of $|B_{\varepsilon,R}(F,G)-B(F,G)|$ over $0<\varepsilon<1/m$, $R>m$, tends to zero in $L^{3/2}$. Pairing the bilinear output with a third $L^3$ input gives a scalar form. Its symmetric, or simplex, coordinates are \begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-simplex} \Lambda_{\varepsilon,R}(G_0,G_1,G_2) =\iiint_{\varepsilon<|a+b+c|<R} G_0(a,b)G_1(b,c)G_2(c,a) \frac{\dd a\dd b\dd c}{a+b+c}. \end{equation} The three equal exponents $3$ form the symmetric point of the scalar H\"older relation $1/p_0+1/p_1+1/p_2=1$. We obtain a uniform bound by $C\prod_v\|G_v\|_3$ and the joint scalar principal value for every complex $L^3$ triple. Proposition~\ref{prop:flat-equivalence} gives the norm-preserving coordinate maps and equality of the optimal constants for the flat and simplex scalar formulations. In particular, this proves the symmetric scalar estimate in Thiele's Problem~13 in \cite[Section~9]{GrafakosEtAl2017}. \subsection{History and related work} The flat operator occurs in Demeter and Thiele's study of bilinear averages for two commuting transformations \cite[Section~6]{DemeterThiele2010}. The scalar form \eqref{eq:intro-simplex} is a basic entangled singular integral: each input sees one pair of the three variables, and the pairs form a cycle. This dependence differs from the bipartite configurations in many multilinear estimates. Kova\v{c}, Thiele and Zorin-Kranich established estimates for a Walsh model with two general inputs and a third input of specified structure \cite[Theorem~1.7]{KovacThieleZorinKranich2015}. Their paper also records the cyclic trace formulation and the equivalence of the flat and simplex forms. Quantitative cancellation over a finite range of scales was developed in several stages. Tao proved sublogarithmic bounds for one-dimensional multilinear Hilbert transforms \cite{Tao2016Cancellation}, and Zorin-Kranich obtained sublogarithmic cancellation for simplex Hilbert transforms \cite{ZorinKranich2017}. Durcik, Kova\v{c}, \v{S}kreb and Thiele proved square-function and norm-variation estimates that yield square-root cancellation for the triangular form over finitely many smooth scales \cite[Corollary~5]{DurcikKovacSkrebThiele2019}. Durcik, Kova\v{c} and Thiele obtained power-type cancellation for hard truncations \cite[Theorem~1]{DurcikKovacThiele2019}. Permuting and interpolating their $(4,4,2)$ bounds gives a $\sqrt{\log(R/\varepsilon)}$ bound at the symmetric tuple. These estimates retain a dependence on the scale range. === MANUSCRIPT: An L³ bound for the dyadic triangular Hilbert form (October-5-2026) [preprints/An-L3-bound-for-the-dyadic-triangular-Hilbert-form-October-5-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove a uniform $`L^3\times L^3\times L^3`$ estimate for the dyadic triangular Hilbert form with unrestricted real inputs. The sum of the absolute local contributions over any finite set of scales is bounded by forty times the product of the input norms. In particular, the bound allows coefficients of modulus at most one to vary independently among admissible interval triples.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} The triangular Hilbert form couples three functions of two variables, each pair sharing one variable. We study its dyadic model, in which the intervals are constrained by binary addition without carries. The estimate below imposes no structural condition on any of the three inputs. Write \(\R_+=[0,\infty)\). For \(k\in\Z\), let \[ \cD_k=\{[2^kn,2^k(n+1)):n\in\Z_{\ge0}\}. \] For \(I\in\cD_k\), denote its index by \(n_I\) and set \[ h_I=\mathbf1_{I_{\mathrm{left}}}-\mathbf1_{I_{\mathrm{right}}}. \] Let \(\oplus\) denote bitwise XOR of nonnegative integers, and define the set of admissible triples \[ \cA_k=\{(I_0,I_1,I_2)\in\cD_k^3: n_{I_0}\oplus n_{I_1}\oplus n_{I_2}=0\}. \] For \(\mathbf I\in\cA_k\) and three real functions \(F_v\) on \(\R_+^2\), put \begin{equation}\label{eq:local-form} L_{\mathbf I}(F_0,F_1,F_2) =2^{-k}\int_{I_0\times I_1\times I_2} F_0(x_0,x_1)F_1(x_1,x_2)F_2(x_2,x_0) \prod_{v=0}^2 h_{I_v}(x_v)\dd x_0\dd x_1\dd x_2. \end{equation} The dyadic triangular Hilbert form with a finite set of scales \(S\subset\Z\) and real coefficients \(\abs{\epsilon_k}\le1\) is \begin{equation}\label{eq:given-form} \Lambda^{\mathrm d}_{S,\epsilon}(F_0,F_1,F_2) =\sum_{k\in S}\epsilon_k\sum_{\mathbf I\in\cA_k} L_{\mathbf I}(F_0,F_1,F_2). \end{equation} [statement] In particular, \eqref{eq:given-form} satisfies the same product bound, uniformly in the scales and coefficients. The absolute values in \eqref{eq:main} also allow a separate coefficient of modulus at most one for each admissible triple. The estimate extends to all real \(L^3\) inputs by density, as explained in Section~\ref{sec:summation}. \subsection{Context and relation to earlier work} \subsection{The energy argument} The proof reduces the form to finite matrices by taking inputs constant on sufficiently small dyadic squares. On a fixed admissible triple, each input becomes a matrix between the atom spaces of its two variables. At each variable, we join its two incident matrices into a single row operator \(R\) and assign it the energy \(\tr((R^*R)^{3/2})\). Let \(e(\mathbf I)\) be the sum of these three energies, and let \(\operatorname{ch}(\mathbf I)\) consist of the four admissible triples obtained by halving each interval. The key estimate is \begin{equation}\label{eq:overview-gap} \abs{L_{\mathbf I}(F_0,F_1,F_2)} \le C_0\,2^{2k} \left(\frac14\sum_{\mathbf I'\in\operatorname{ch}(\mathbf I)} e(\mathbf I')-e(\mathbf I)\right), \qquad \mathbf I\in\cA_k, \end{equation} with an absolute constant \(C_0\). At each scale, the weighted total energy \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in\cA_k}2^{2k}e(\mathbf I)\) is bounded by an absolute constant times the sum of the cubed input norms. To prove \eqref{eq:overview-gap}, a matrix trace estimate bounds the triangular product by three Hessian quadratic forms of \(P\mapsto\tr(P^{3/2})\). Conditional on one child half, each neighboring half remains equally likely to be left or right. The two neighbors are correlated, but \(RR^*\) is a sum of the two edge Gram matrices; it contains no product between their multipliers and has the same nonzero spectrum as \(R^*R\). Convexity therefore converts the Hessian bound into an increase of the child energy. Writing the local integrals in normalized interval measures leaves the weight \(2^{2k}\). Each child has one parent and weight \(2^{2(k-1)}\), one quarter of the parent's weight, so these increases telescope. Section~\ref{sec:matrix} establishes the energy inequalities, Section~\ref{sec:trace} proves the trace estimate, Section~\ref{sec:dyadic} supplies the conditional-averaging argument, and Section~\ref{sec:summation} sums the resulting differences and passes to measurable inputs.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} For every finite \(S\subset\Z\) and all real bounded compactly supported measurable functions \(F_0,F_1,F_2\) on \(\R_+^2\), \begin{equation}\label{eq:main} \sum_{k\in S}\sum_{\mathbf I\in\cA_k} \abs{L_{\mathbf I}(F_0,F_1,F_2)} \le 40\prod_{v=0}^2\norm{F_v}_{L^3(\R_+^2)}. \end{equation}

=== MANUSCRIPT: The maximal triangular Hilbert transform at the symmetric point (September-24-2026) [preprints/The-maximal-triangular-Hilbert-transform-at-the-symmetric-point-September-24-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: For arbitrary complex inputs in $`L^3(\mathbb R^2)`$, we prove the pointwise maximal $`L^3\times L^3\to L^{3/2}`$ estimate for the triangular Hilbert transform, with the supremum over both hard truncation endpoints. The estimate yields joint almost-everywhere and L3/2 convergence as the lower endpoint tends to zero and the upper endpoint tends to infinity. This also proves the conjectured scalar estimate at the symmetric point.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} The triangular Hilbert transform couples translations in two different coordinate directions. For complex functions $F,G$ on $\R^2$ and $0<\varepsilon<R<\infty$, write \[ B_{\varepsilon,R}(F,G)(x,y) =\int_{\varepsilon<|t|<R}F(x+t,y)G(x,y+t)\,\frac{\dd t}{t}, \qquad B_*(F,G)=\sup_{0<\varepsilon<R<\infty}|B_{\varepsilon,R}(F,G)|. \] For $L^3$ inputs, the finite integrals are absolutely convergent outside one common null set, and the supremum is measurable; the details are given in Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-truncations}. [statement] The estimate controls a truncation chosen separately at each spatial point. This is stronger than a uniform bound for the norm of each individual truncation: the same $L^{3/2}$ majorant must control every choice of the two endpoints. It also gives the joint principal value \[ B(F,G)=\lim_{\substack{\varepsilon\downarrow0\\R\uparrow\infty}} B_{\varepsilon,R}(F,G) \] almost everywhere and in $L^{3/2}$, with the same norm bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:bilinear-pv}). Pairing the bilinear output with a third $L^3$ function leads to the scalar triangular form. In its symmetric coordinates this is \[ \Lambda_{\varepsilon,R}(G_0,G_1,G_2) =\iiint_{\varepsilon<|a+b+c|<R} G_0(a,b)G_1(b,c)G_2(c,a) \,\frac{\dd a\,\dd b\,\dd c}{a+b+c}. \] The three factors have equal exponent $3$, which is the symmetric point of the scalar H\"older relation $1/p_0+1/p_1+1/p_2=1$. We obtain an endpoint-uniform bound by $C\prod_v\|G_v\|_3$ and a joint scalar principal value for every complex $L^3$ triple. Proposition~\ref{prop:flat-equivalence} gives the explicit determinant-one changes of variables and proves that the flat and simplex scalar estimates have the same optimal constant. In particular, the result resolves positively the symmetric triangular Hilbert transform problem stated by Thiele in~\cite[Section~9, Problem~13]{GrafakosEtAl2017}. \subsection{Previous work} The flat operator appears in Demeter and Thiele's discussion of bilinear averages for two commuting transformations~\cite[Section~6]{DemeterThiele2010}. The three inputs of the scalar form depend on the three pairs of variables, making it a basic example of an entangled singular integral. Kova\v{c}, Thiele and Zorin-Kranich proved estimates for a Walsh model with one structurally restricted input~\cite[Theorem~1.7]{KovacThieleZorinKranich2015}. Tao proved sublogarithmic cancellation for one-dimensional multilinear Hilbert transforms~\cite[Theorem~1.2]{Tao2016Cancellation}. Zorin-Kranich extended this cancellation to simplex Hilbert transforms~\cite[Theorem~1.3]{ZorinKranich2017}. Square-function estimates of Durcik, Kova\v{c}, \v{S}kreb and Thiele gave square-root cancellation for the triangular form across finitely many smooth scales~\cite[Corollary~5]{DurcikKovacSkrebThiele2019}. Durcik, Kova\v{c} and Thiele proved power-type cancellation with hard truncations~\cite[Theorem~1]{DurcikKovacThiele2019}; at the symmetric tuple the bound grows like $\sqrt{\log(R/\varepsilon)}$, by permutation and interpolation of their $(4,4,2)$ estimates. These finite-scale bounds retain a dependence on the scale range. Bounds for averages over direction parameters were obtained by Durcik and Roos~\cite[Theorem~1]{DurcikRoos2021} and, for rough angular weights, by Lin and Slav\'ikov\'a~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{LinSlavikova2026}. For curved translations, Christ, Durcik and Roos proved bounds along $(t,t^2)$~\cite[Theorem~1]{ChristDurcikRoos2021}. Hsu and Lin treat more general curves under geometric hypotheses excluding the flat curve $(t,t)$~\cite[Theorem~1.37]{HsuLin2026}. The curvature in these results provides an additional source of cancellation that is unavailable for the flat curve. Our argument uses a nonnegative matrix energy and Gaussian heat flow. Energy telescoping for entangled forms appears in Kova\v{c}'s twisted-paraproduct argument~\cite[Lemma~2]{Kovac2012Twisted} and his Bellman-function framework~\cite[Section~2]{Kovac2011}.
