FAMILY 041: Hyperkähler SYZ and projective-space bases
SUBJECT: Algebraic and complex geometry
OPENAI SUMMARY: Proves the strong hyperkähler SYZ conjecture: every holomorphic line bundle with nonzero nef isotropic first Chern class on a compact irreducible holomorphic symplectic Kähler manifold is semiample. It also proves that every projective Lagrangian fibration with normal projective base has projective space as its base, in every dimension and deformation type.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Projective-space bases of Lagrangian fibrations (September-23-2026) [preprints/Projective-Space-Bases-of-Lagrangian-Fibrations-September-23-2026/build/source/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove that the normal projective base of a projective Lagrangian fibration from a compact irreducible holomorphic symplectic Kähler manifold is projective space. This resolves the projective-space base conjecture for such fibrations in every dimension and deformation type.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Let \(X\) be a compact irreducible holomorphic symplectic (IHS) Kähler manifold of dimension \(2n\). A nontrivial fibration of \(X\) is a proper surjective holomorphic map \(f:X\to B\) with connected fibers onto a normal complex space, with \(0<\dim B<2n\). In the projective setting, Matsushita proved that \(\dim B=n\) and that a smooth general fiber is an abelian variety \cite{Matsushita1999,Matsushita2001}. His equidimensionality theorem shows that every irreducible component of every fiber is Lagrangian once the general fiber is Lagrangian \cite[Theorem~1]{Matsushita2000}. For compact Kähler IHS total spaces, Greb--Lehn's Proposition~2.2 shows that the base is projective of dimension \(n\) and that every fiber component is Lagrangian \cite{GrebLehn2014}. The projective-space base conjecture asks whether this normal base must be isomorphic to \(\PP^n\). Hwang proved the projective-space conclusion when \(X\) is projective and \(B\) is smooth \cite[Theorem~1.2]{Hwang2008}. Greb and Lehn extended the smooth-base theorem to compact Kähler IHS total spaces \cite[Theorem~1.1]{GrebLehn2014}. These results reduce the base problem to the possible singularities of \(B\). The conclusion is also known for the \(K3^{[n]}\), generalized Kummer, \(\mathrm{OG6}\), and \(\mathrm{OG10}\) deformation types without assuming the base smooth. A common nef formulation of the earlier isotropic-divisor and rational-fibration results is recorded in \cite[Theorem~1.3]{DebarreHuybrechtsMacriVoisin2024}: a nef line bundle \(L\) whose first Chern class is primitive and isotropic for the Beauville--Bogomolov--Fujiki form on such a compact IHS manifold admits a holomorphic Lagrangian fibration \(g:X\to\PP^n\) with \(L\simeq g^*\cO_{\PP^n}(1)\). For an existing fibration \(f:X\to B\), choose an ample line bundle \(H\) on \(B\) and write \(f^*H\simeq L^r\) with \(c_1(L)\) primitive. This class remains nef and isotropic, so the theorem applies to \(L\); connected fibers identify the graded section rings of \(H\), \(L^r\), and \(\cO_{\PP^n}(r)\). Taking their \(\operatorname{Proj}\) identifies \(B\) with \(\PP^n\). In dimension four, Ou reduced the normal surface base of a projective IHS fibration to \(\PP^2\) or the specified Fano surface \(S^{\mathrm n}(E_8)\), with one \(E_8\) singularity and two nodal rational anticanonical curves \cite[Theorem~1.2]{Ou2019}. Huybrechts and Chenyang Xu excluded the second case \cite[Theorem~0.1]{HuybrechtsXu2022}. Müller and Zheng Xu subsequently proved a local theorem: a projective Lagrangian germ from a smooth holomorphic symplectic manifold has smooth base if the normal analytic base has only isolated quotient singularities \cite[Theorem~1.3]{MullerXu2026}. Their Corollary~1.4 treats such germs in total dimension four without a singularity assumption, and their Theorem~1.5 treats terminal threefold bases in total dimension six. Their Example~4.1 exhibits a nonisolated quotient stratum for a noncompact symplectic total space; excluding these strata in the compact setting therefore requires additional global geometry. For projective IHS total spaces, the cohomological constraint is already that of projective space: Shen--Yin compute the rational intersection cohomology of \(B\) as one-dimensional in degrees \(0,2,\ldots,2n\) and zero in odd degrees \cite[Theorem~0.4(a)]{ShenYin2022}. This does not itself establish smoothness. Our proof first establishes smoothness and then applies Hwang's theorem. [theorem] \label{thm:main} Let \(n\geq1\), and let \(X\) be a compact irreducible holomorphic symplectic Kähler manifold of dimension \(2n\), with symplectic form \(\sigma\). Suppose that \[ f:X\longrightarrow B \] is a projective surjective holomorphic morphism with connected fibers, where \(B\) is a normal projective variety of dimension \(n\). Assume that every irreducible component of every fiber has dimension \(n\) and that the restriction of \(\sigma\) to its smooth locus is zero. Then \(B\) is isomorphic to \(\PP^n_{\C}\).

=== MANUSCRIPT: The strong hyperkähler SYZ conjecture (September-23-2026) [preprints/The-Strong-Hyperkahler-SYZ-Conjecture-September-23-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove the strong hyperkähler SYZ conjecture: every holomorphic line bundle with nonzero nef isotropic first Chern class on a compact irreducible holomorphic symplectic Kähler manifold is semiample.
INTRODUCTION (start): A compact irreducible holomorphic symplectic K\"ahler manifold is a simply connected compact K\"ahler manifold $X$ such that $H^0(X,\Omega_X^2)=\C\sigma$ for a holomorphic two-form $\sigma$ which is nondegenerate everywhere. Its complex dimension is even, say $2n$. We write $q=q_X$ for the Beauville--Bogomolov--Fujiki quadratic form, and also for its associated symmetric bilinear form. We denote the K\"ahler cone by $\mathcal K_X$. A line bundle $L$ is \emph{nef} if $c_1(L)$ lies in the closure of the K\"ahler cone, \emph{isotropic} if $q(c_1(L))=0$, and \emph{semiample} if some positive tensor power is generated by its global holomorphic sections. A nonzero nef isotropic class has numerical dimension $n$, by Fujiki's relation: its powers have positive intersection with a K\"ahler class through degree $n$, and zero intersection in higher degrees. The strong hyperk\"ahler SYZ conjecture asks whether every line bundle with such a class is semiample \cite[Conjecture~1.7]{VerbitskySYZ}. This would turn a class on the boundary of the K\"ahler cone into a holomorphic map with half-dimensional fibers. The numerical condition by itself supplies neither sections nor control of their base locus. [statement] Theorem~\ref{thm:main} resolves the strong hyperk\"ahler SYZ conjecture positively. The conclusion holds on the given manifold $X$: a positive tensor power of $L$ has no base points anywhere. No restriction on the dimension, deformation type, or second Betti number is imposed. There is also a consequence for the number of possible smooth manifolds. Here a deformation type means an equivalence class under smooth proper deformations through compact irreducible holomorphic symplectic K\"ahler manifolds. Section~\ref{sec:finiteness} gives the deduction in the smooth category, including the role of the hypothesis $b_2\ge5$. The former asks for semiampleness, while the latter relates the existence of a nef square-zero divisor to the existence of an abelian fibration. Matsushita established the half-dimensional and Lagrangian character of fibrations from projective irreducible symplectic manifolds \cite{Matsushita1999,MatsushitaAddendum}; his equidimensionality theorem also controls every component of every fiber \cite[Theorem~1]{MatsushitaEquidimensional}. [theorem] \label{thm:main} Let $X$ be a compact irreducible holomorphic symplectic K\"ahler manifold of complex dimension $2n$, where $n\ge1$. If a holomorphic line bundle $L$ satisfies \[ c_1(L)\ne0,\qquad c_1(L)\in\overline{\mathcal K_X}, \qquad q_X(c_1(L))=0, \] then $L$ is semiample. More precisely, there exist a positive integer $m$, a normal projective variety $B$, an ample line bundle $A$ on $B$, and a surjective holomorphic map with connected fibers \[ f:X\longrightarrow B,\qquad L^m\simeq f^*A, \] such that $\dim_{\C}B=n$. Every irreducible component of every fiber has complex dimension $n$, and $\sigma$ vanishes on its smooth locus.
[corollary] \label{cor:finiteness} For each integer $n\ge1$, there are only finitely many smooth deformation types of compact irreducible holomorphic symplectic K\"ahler manifolds of complex dimension $2n$ with $b_2\ge5$.
[corollary] [Projective case]\label{cor:projective-base} In Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, assume additionally that $X$ is projective, and let $f:X\to B$ be the associated connected-fiber map furnished there. Then $B\simeq\mathbb P^n_{\C}$. Moreover, there do not exist a prime divisor $D\subset B$, an effective divisor $E$ on $X$, and an integer $r>1$ such that $f^*D=rE$.
