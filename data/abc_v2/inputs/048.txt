FAMILY 048: A characteristic-zero counterexample to Lipman–Zariski
SUBJECT: Algebraic and complex geometry
OPENAI SUMMARY: Constructs a singular normal affine complex surface with free rank-two tangent sheaf, disproving the characteristic-zero Lipman–Zariski conjecture.

=== MANUSCRIPT: A singular normal affine surface with free tangent sheaf (September-23-2026) [preprints/A-singular-normal-affine-surface-with-free-tangent-sheaf-September-23-2026/build/paper.tex]
ABSTRACT: We construct a singular normal affine complex surface whose tangent sheaf is free of rank two. This disproves the Lipman–Zariski conjecture in characteristic zero.
INTRODUCTION (start): For a finitely generated integral $\CC$-algebra $A$, write \[ \Der_{\CC}(A)=\Hom_A(\Omega^1_{A/\CC},A). \] The module $\Der_{\CC}(A)$ consists of the algebraic vector fields on $\Spec A$. On a smooth variety of dimension $d$, the tangent sheaf is locally free of rank $d$. The Lipman--Zariski conjecture asks for the converse: a complex algebraic variety with locally free tangent sheaf should be nonsingular. In affine terms, it asserts that local freeness of $\Der_{\CC}(A)$ forces $A$ to be regular. We prove the following. [statement] Here freeness holds on the entire affine surface, which can be chosen smooth away from the marked point. We first construct an isolated Gorenstein analytic surface singularity. The passage to algebraic geometry preserves its completed local ring, from which tangent freeness descends. \subsection{Context and significance} Lipman's study of free derivation modules established that local freeness of the tangent sheaf forces normality~\cite[Theorem~3]{Lipman1965}. The conjecture thus asks whether the same hypothesis excludes the remaining normal singularities. In positive characteristic the corresponding assertion fails even for hypersurfaces: over a perfect field of characteristic $p$, the singular surface $xy-z^p=0$ has tangent sheaf freely generated by $x\partial_x-y\partial_y$ and $\partial_z$ \cite[\S1]{GrafPositive2022}. The construction here has a different source of tangent freeness and takes place entirely over $\CC$. Several positive results explain both the strength of tangent freeness and the role of surfaces in the problem. Hochster proved that a reduced finitely generated characteristic-zero algebra with a nonnegative grading and degree-zero part equal to the base field is a polynomial algebra if its derivation module is free at the irrelevant maximal ideal \cite[\S1, Theorem]{Hochster1977}. Van Straten and Steenbrink used extension of differential forms to prove the isolated-singularity case in dimension at least three~\cite[Theorem~1.3 and~\S1.6]{VanStratenSteenbrink1985}. Their extension argument leaves the surface case as a distinct problem. Hypersurfaces were treated by Scheja and Storch~\cite[Satz~9.3]{SchejaStorch1972}; K\"allstr\"om established the characteristic-zero algebraic locally complete-intersection case~\cite[Corollary~1.3(1), corrected version]{Kallstrom2011}. Extension theorems for differential forms also give strong results defined by the singularities of the canonical divisor. Greb, Kebekus, Kov\'acs and Peternell proved the conjecture for Kawamata log terminal spaces \cite[Theorem~6.1]{GKKP2011}. The log-canonical case was proved by Druel~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{Druel2014} and by Graf and Kov\'acs \cite[Corollary~1.3]{GrafKovacs2014}. These results relate tangent freeness to the behavior of differential forms on a resolution. Another distinction concerns the Lie bracket: J\"order proved smoothness for a normal complex space whose tangent sheaf locally admits a basis of pairwise commuting vector fields \cite[Theorem~1.1]{Jorder2014}. Thus no tangent frame near our singular point can be chosen commuting. For normal complex surfaces, the cohomology and configuration of the exceptional curves give further criteria. Graf proved smoothness when the geometric genus $p_g$ is at most one, and also when $p_g=2$ and the exceptional divisor is not a tree of rational curves \cite[Theorem~1.1]{GrafLowGenus2021}. Bergner and Graf then obtained the criterion \[ p_g-g-b\le2, \] where $g$ is the sum of the genera of the exceptional curves and $b$ is the first Betti number of the resolution's dual graph \cite[Theorem~1]{BergnerGraf2020}. The analytic singularity constructed here has one exceptional curve of genus $21$ and self-intersection $-10$. Its canonical discrepancy is $-5$, and $p_g\ge33$, so $p_g-g-b\ge12$. [theorem] \label{thm:main} There exist a finitely generated normal integral $\CC$-algebra $A$ of Krull dimension two and a maximal ideal $\m\subset A$ such that \[ \Der_{\CC}(A)\simeq A^{\oplus2}, \qquad A_{\m}\text{ is not regular}. \]
