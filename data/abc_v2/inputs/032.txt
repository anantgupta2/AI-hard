=== MANUSCRIPT: The rational Hodge conjecture for CM abelian varieties (October-6-2026) [preprints/The-rational-Hodge-conjecture-for-CM-abelian-varieties-October-6-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove the rational Hodge conjecture for complex abelian varieties with complex multiplication: every rational Hodge class on such a variety is a rational linear combination of algebraic cycle classes. As consequences, we obtain the generalized Hodge conjecture for CM abelian varieties, the Tate conjecture for abelian varieties over finite fields, and the Hodge standard conjecture for abelian varieties in arbitrary characteristic.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} For a smooth projective complex variety \(X\), the rational Hodge conjecture asks whether every rational cohomology class of type \((p,p)\) is a rational linear combination of codimension-\(p\) algebraic cycle classes. We prove this assertion for abelian varieties with complex multiplication, in every dimension and codimension. A complex abelian variety \(A\) is of \emph{CM type}, or simply \emph{CM}, if \(\End(A)\otimes_{\Z}\Q\) contains a commutative semisimple \(\Q\)-algebra of dimension \(2\dim A\). We include the point by convention. Finite products and powers of CM abelian varieties are again CM. \Needspace{12\baselineskip} [statement] The theorem also has consequences beyond the algebraicity of individual CM Hodge classes. The generalized Hodge conjecture predicts algebraic supports of the codimension allowed by the Hodge types of a cohomology substructure. We obtain it for CM abelian varieties by applying the theorem to correspondences from auxiliary CM varieties, following Hazama and Abdulali \cite{Hazama2003CM,Abdulali2005CM}. Two theorems of Milne then give the Tate conjecture for all abelian varieties over finite fields, which asks for algebraic representatives of Galois-invariant \(\ell\)-adic classes, and the Hodge standard conjecture for abelian varieties in arbitrary characteristic, which asserts positivity of the intersection form on primitive algebraic classes \cite{Milne1999Tate,Milne2002Polarizations}. Section~\ref{cc:section} states these conclusions with their coefficient and support conventions and explains the separate implications. \subsection{Hodge classes on CM abelian varieties} The codimension-one case of the Hodge conjecture is the Lefschetz \((1,1)\) theorem. Hodge formulated the broader problem by asking for an extension of this algebraicity criterion to higher-dimensional cycles \cite[p.~184]{Hodge1952}. Cup products of divisor classes therefore give algebraic Hodge classes in higher degree. For an abelian variety, \(H^*(A,\Q)=\Lambda^*H^1(A,\Q)\), but this description of its cohomology does not make every Hodge class a linear combination of divisor products. The problem is to construct cycles for the remaining classes. Weil classes provide an important instance of this difficulty. Suppose an imaginary quadratic field \(K\) acts on an abelian variety of dimension \(2n\), with its two embeddings occurring with equal multiplicity in \(H^{1,0}\). Then \(\Lambda_K^{2n}H^1(A,\Q)\) supplies a two-dimensional rational space of classes of type \((n,n)\). These determinant classes and their generalization to CM fields are discussed in \cite[Proposition~4.4]{Deligne1982}. Their Hodge type follows from the multiplicity condition; algebraicity requires a geometric construction. Weil classes need not lie in the algebra generated by divisor classes; this occurs already on abelian fourfolds \cite[Theorem~0.1]{MoonenZarhin1999}. Recent work of Markman proves algebraicity of the Weil classes on polarized abelian sixfolds of Weil type for an imaginary quadratic field, with polarization discriminant \(-1\), and deduces the Hodge conjecture for all abelian fourfolds \cite[Theorem~1.5.1 and Corollary~1.6.1]{Markman2025Secant}. The sixfold theorem assumes an imaginary quadratic action, without requiring the full CM condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:cm}. Deligne proved that every Hodge class on an abelian variety is \emph{absolute Hodge}: after transport to a conjugate variety through de Rham cohomology, it remains a Hodge class \cite[Main Theorem~2.11]{Deligne1982}. This establishes an arithmetic compatibility shared by algebraic cycle classes, but does not by itself produce algebraic cycles. CM varieties play a central role in that argument. Their Hodge groups are tori, and their degree-one cohomology splits, after extending coefficients, into lines indexed by field embeddings. [theorem] \label{thm:cm} Let \(A\) be a complex CM abelian variety. For every integer \(p\geq0\), the Betti cycle-class map \[ \operatorname{cl}_B:\CH^p(A)_{\Q} \longrightarrow H^{2p}(A,\Q)\cap H^{p,p}(A) \] is surjective, where \(\CH^p(A)_{\Q}=\CH^p(A)\otimes_{\Z}\Q\) and the intersection is taken in \(H^{2p}(A,\C)\). In particular, the rational Hodge conjecture holds in every codimension on every finite product of complex CM abelian varieties and on every power of such a product.

INTRODUCTION (start): \label{intro:section} The Hodge conjecture asks which cohomology classes on a smooth projective complex variety come from algebraic subvarieties. In its rational form it asserts surjectivity of \[ \cl:\CH^r(X)_{\Q}\longrightarrow \Hdg^r(X):=H^{2r}(X,\Q)\cap H^{r,r}(X) \] for every $r$. The generalized Hodge conjecture asks the corresponding support question for whole Hodge substructures. Write \[ N^pH^j(X,\Q)= \sum_{\substack{B\subset X\text{ closed}\\\operatorname{codim}_X B\ge p}} \ker\bigl(H^j(X,\Q)\longrightarrow H^j(X\setminus B,\Q)\bigr). \] The generalized conjecture says that this equals the largest rational sub-Hodge structure whose types $(a,b)$ satisfy $a,b\ge p$. We use rational coefficients for both conjectures throughout, and abbreviate them as HC and GHC. For a projective K3 surface $S$, let \[ T_S=\NS(S)_{\Q}^{\perp}\subset H^2(S,\Q) \] with its cup-product form $q$. Its extreme Hodge spaces are $T_S^{2,0}=H^{2,0}(S)$ and its conjugate, both one-dimensional. The Hodge index theorem gives signature $(2,20-\rho(S))$. This small Hodge level makes K3 surfaces a natural setting for the generalized conjecture, but their powers contain tensor Hodge classes and substructures that need not be generated by divisor classes alone. Kuga and Satake attached abelian varieties to polarized K3 Hodge structures through Clifford algebras \cite{KS}, and Deligne developed the construction in families \cite{Deligne}. The distinguished correspondence is absolutely Hodge and motivated \cite[Proposition~6.26\textup{(d)} and Corollary~6.27]{DeligneMilne}, \cite[Section~7.1]{Andre}. Algebraicity is the additional assertion needed here: we require a cycle inducing the prescribed Clifford map, not merely some nonzero Hodge embedding into abelian cohomology. \subsection{One exact Kuga--Satake tensor} We specify the Kuga--Satake tensor used throughout the paper. INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} The Kuga--Satake construction associates an abelian variety to a polarized Hodge structure of K3 type. Its original purpose was to place the second cohomology of a K3 surface inside a tensor construction on the first cohomology of an abelian variety \cite{KugaSatake}. The resulting embedding is a Hodge morphism. Its realization by an algebraic correspondence is a further assertion, usually called the algebraicity of the Kuga--Satake correspondence; see \cite[Chapter 4]{Huybrechts} and \cite{vanGeemen}. This assertion is a particular instance of the rational Hodge conjecture. We prove two conditional reductions for this algebraicity problem. At a point where the primitive cohomology has full orthogonal Hodge group, an algebraic correspondence inducing any nonzero map from primitive cohomology to the second cohomology of an abelian variety suffices to recover the \emph{specified} map into the \emph{full} even-Clifford target. Very-general existence of such a map then implies algebraicity of the specified correspondence at every point of the same polarized component. These reductions do not establish the existence of the initial map and therefore do not resolve the unrestricted Kuga--Satake conjecture. The proof uses three devices which may be useful in related algebraicity questions. Independent operations on the two weight-one factors recover every multiplicity, and rational descent handles nonsplit Clifford algebras.
