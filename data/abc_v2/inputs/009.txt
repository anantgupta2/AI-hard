FAMILY 009: Function-field reconstruction from Milnor K-theory and Galois data
SUBJECT: Number theory
OPENAI SUMMARY: Reconstructs function fields of transcendence degree at least two over algebraically closed constants from $`K^{\mathrm M}_1/\ell`$, $`K^{\mathrm M}_2/\ell`$, and their product. These data recover the perfect closure and constants when ℓ differs from the characteristic, and the original field and its named base in equal characteristic. Also proves Bogomolov–Pop reconstruction from abelian-by-central pro-ℓ Galois data away from the characteristic.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Reconstruction of Function Fields from Mod-ℓ Milnor K-Theory (October-5-2026) [preprints/Reconstruction-of-Function-Fields-from-Mod-ell-Milnor-K-Theory-October-5-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove a mod-ℓ Bogomolov–Pop reconstruction theorem for function fields of transcendence degree at least two over arbitrary algebraically closed fields of characteristic different from ℓ. The groups $`K^{\mathrm M}_1/\ell`$ and $`K^{\mathrm M}_2/\ell`$, together with their full bilinear product, determine the perfect closure and its constant field. Every compatible isomorphism of these data is induced by a field isomorphism up to a single scalar in $`\mathbb F_\ell^\times`$, with only Frobenius ambiguity in the field isomorphism in positive characteristic.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Birational anabelian geometry asks how much of a function field can be recovered from its Galois-theoretic invariants. In the Bogomolov program, the field has algebraically closed constants, and the relevant invariants are much smaller than the full absolute Galois group. The present paper proves the reconstruction assertion for the first two Milnor $K$-groups modulo a single prime, with their multiplication. The result includes the isomorphism statement: it identifies every compatible linear isomorphism, as well as the ambiguity in the field isomorphism that induces it. \subsection{The reconstruction theorem} Fix a prime $\ell$ and put $\Lambda=\F_\ell$. For a field $F$ of characteristic different from $\ell$, set \[ V_F=F^\times/(F^\times)^\ell, \qquad R_F=\Span_\Lambda\{[x]\otimes[1-x]:x\in F\setminus\{0,1\}\}. \] We write $V_F$ additively, so $[xy]=[x]+[y]$. Let \[ W_F=(V_F\otimes_\Lambda V_F)/R_F, \qquad m_F:V_F\otimes_\Lambda V_F\longrightarrow W_F. \] Thus $V_F=K^{\mathrm M}_1(F)/\ell$ and $W_F=K^{\mathrm M}_2(F)/\ell$, with $m_F$ the Milnor product. A $\Lambda$-linear isomorphism $\Theta:V_K\to V_L$ is \emph{compatible} if \[ (\Theta\otimes\Theta)(R_K)=R_L. \] Equivalently, there is a unique linear isomorphism $\Theta_2:W_K\to W_L$ intertwining the products. Write $\Isom_{\mathrm M}(V_K,V_L)$ for the set of compatible isomorphisms. Multiplication by $a\in\Lambda^\times$ acts on this set; the induced degree-two map is then multiplied by $a^2$. Let $K/k$ and $L/l$ be finitely generated extensions of algebraically closed fields. Write $F^i$ for the perfect closure of a field $F$: it equals $F$ in characteristic zero and $\bigcup_{r\ge0}F^{1/p^r}$ in characteristic $p>0$. Denote by $\Isom^i(K,L)$ the field isomorphisms $\alpha:K^i\to L^i$ satisfying $\alpha(k)=l$. In positive characteristic, identify two such isomorphisms if they differ by postcomposition with $\Frob_L^n$, $n\in\Z$, where $\Frob_L(z)=z^p$. Denote the resulting set by $\Isom^i_{\mathrm F}(K,L)$; in characteristic zero take no quotient, and if the characteristics differ this set is empty. Purely inseparable extension induces canonical isomorphisms on $V$ and $W$. Indeed, for an extension of exponent at most $r$, inclusion and the $p^r$-power homomorphism compose to the $p^r$-power map on each field. On Milnor groups in degrees one and two this composition is multiplication by $p^r$ and $p^{2r}$, respectively, both invertible modulo $\ell$. Passing to the union proves the assertion for perfect closures. Thus $\alpha$ induces a compatible map $\alpha_1:V_K\to V_L$, and postcomposition by $\Frob_L^n$ multiplies it by $p^n$ in $\Lambda^\times$. There is consequently a canonical map \begin{equation}\label{eq:main-map} \Isom^i_{\mathrm F}(K,L)\longrightarrow \Isom_{\mathrm M}(V_K,V_L)/\Lambda^\times. \end{equation} [statement] Only the two vector spaces and their bilinear product enter this statement. The proof recovers the valuations and rational-subfield images needed for reconstruction from this datum. The scalar is one global element of $\F_\ell^\times$; at $\ell=2$ this ambiguity is trivial. \subsection{Historical context} Bogomolov proposed reconstructing higher-dimensional function fields over algebraically closed constants from the pro-$\ell$ quotient in which commutators are central \cite{Bogomolov1991}. Bogomolov and Tschinkel proved reconstruction for surfaces over algebraic closures of finite fields \cite{BT2008}, and subsequently for higher-dimensional function fields over those constants \cite{BT2011}. Pop independently completed reconstruction in this constant-field setting \cite{PopBI2012}. These results established that a small part of the Galois group can retain the geometry needed to recover a field. Over more general algebraically closed constants, Pop also obtained reconstruction from pro-$\ell$ data endowed with divisorial inertia, in relative transcendence degree greater than two \cite[Theorem~1.1]{Pop2018}. [theorem] \label{thm:main} Let $\ell$ be any prime. Let $K/k$ and $L/l$ be finitely generated extensions of arbitrary algebraically closed fields, with $\charac k,\charac l\ne\ell$ and $\trdeg(K/k),\trdeg(L/l)\ge2$. === MANUSCRIPT: Reconstruction from Milnor K-theory modulo the characteristic (October-5-2026) [preprints/Reconstruction-from-Milnor-K-theory-modulo-the-characteristic-October-5-2026/build/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: Let K and L be finitely generated extensions of transcendence degree at least two over algebraically closed fields of characteristic p. We prove that every isomorphism of their first Milnor K-groups modulo p preserving the degree-two Steinberg relations is a nonzero scalar multiple of the map induced by a unique field isomorphism. The proof recovers projective lines over the subfields of pth powers by an elementary calculation with derivations.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Milnor $K$-theory records multiplication in a field together with relations arising from addition. A reconstruction problem asks whether this information determines the field, and whether isomorphisms of the invariant come from field isomorphisms. We study this question in characteristic $p$ using only the first two Milnor $K$-groups modulo $p$. Fix a prime $p$ and write $\Lambda=\Fp$. For a field $F$ of characteristic $p$, set \[ V_F=F^\times/(F^\times)^p=\KM_1(F)/p. \] We write $[f]$ for the class of $f\in F^\times$ and use additive notation in $V_F$: thus $[f]+[g]=[fg]$, $r[f]=[f^r]$ for $r\in\Z$, and $[1]=0$. Define the Steinberg relation subspace \[ R_F=\Span_{\Lambda} \{[f]\otimes[1-f]:f\in F\setminus\{0,1\}\} \subset V_F\otimes_{\Lambda}V_F. \] The quotient $W_F=(V_F\otimes_{\Lambda}V_F)/R_F$ is $\KM_2(F)/p$ by the tensor presentation of Milnor $K$-theory \cite[Section~1]{Milnor1970}. We denote the image of $[f]\otimes[g]$ in $W_F$ by $\{f,g\}_F$. Let $\Isom_{\mathrm M}(V_K,V_L)$ be the set of $\Lambda$-linear isomorphisms $\Theta:V_K\to V_L$ satisfying \[ (\Theta\otimes\Theta)(R_K)=R_L. \] We call these isomorphisms \emph{compatible}. Equivalently, $\Theta$ extends to an isomorphism of the degree-one and degree-two groups respecting their product pairing. Scalar multiplication by $\Lambda^\times$ acts on this set. For specified subfields $k\subset K$ and $l\subset L$, write $\Isom(K,k;L,l)$ for the field isomorphisms $\alpha:K\to L$ with $\alpha(k)=l$. Such an isomorphism induces $\alpha_*([f])=[\alpha(f)]$ and carries $R_K$ onto $R_L$. [statement] The invariant therefore determines the field and its isomorphisms, with exactly the scalar identification in the displayed target. The argument also recovers the named algebraically closed base from the field itself. \subsection{Context and method} Bogomolov and Tschinkel developed a reconstruction method that recovers projective lines from multiplication and algebraic dependence, and applied it to Milnor $K$-theory in characteristic zero \cite{BogomolovTschinkel2009}. Cadoret and Pirutka extended reconstruction from the multiplicative group modulo constants and algebraic dependence to finitely generated regular extensions of perfect fields \cite[Theorem~4]{CadoretPirutka2021}. For algebraically closed or finite base fields, they also obtained reconstruction from suitable quotients of the Milnor $K$-ring \cite[Corollary~10]{CadoretPirutka2021}. Reconstruction from other reduced forms of $K$-theory has also been studied. For a prime $\ell$ different from the characteristic, Topaz recovers perfect closures from mod-$\ell$ data together with rational subgroups for function fields over algebraically closed bases in transcendence degree at least five \cite[Theorem~B]{Topaz2016}. His work on rational Milnor $K$-theory reconstructs perfect closures for fields of absolute transcendence degree at least five \cite[Main Theorem]{Topaz2023}. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} concerns reduction modulo the characteristic and starts directly from $V_F$ and $R_F$; neither rational subgroups nor algebraic-dependence data are supplied. The geometric framework of our proof has close predecessors. A differential approach to line recovery appears in an argument of Rovinsky recorded by Bogomolov and Tschinkel \cite[Proposition~9]{BogomolovTschinkel2012}. In positive characteristic, Cadoret and Pirutka recover projective lines over the subfield of $p$th powers after a common power correction, using a differential intersection calculation \cite[Section~1.3, Lemma~23, and Proposition~27]{CadoretPirutka2021}. Here the intersection calculation involves only degree-$p$ extensions of the subfield of $p$th powers. === MANUSCRIPT: The Bogomolov-Pop reconstruction theorem (September-23-2026) [preprints/The-Bogomolov-Pop-reconstruction-theorem-September-23-2026/build/paper.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove the Bogomolov–Pop reconstruction conjecture for function fields of transcendence degree at least two over arbitrary algebraically closed fields of characteristic different from ℓ. The pro-ℓ abelian-by-central datum determines the perfect closure and its constant field, with precisely the Frobenius and ℓ-adic unit ambiguities.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Birational anabelian geometry asks how much of a field can be read from its Galois groups. Over algebraically closed constants, the arithmetic Galois action disappears. Bogomolov's program proposes that a particularly small quotient still remembers every function field of dimension at least two: the quotient in which commutators are central. We prove the Isom form of this assertion, including surfaces and the prime $2$. \subsection{The exact statement} Fix a prime $\ell$. A function field $F/\kappa$ in this paper is a finitely generated extension of an algebraically closed field $\kappa$. For $\operatorname{char}F\ne\ell$, let $G_F^{(\ell)}$ be the maximal pro-$\ell$ quotient of the absolute Galois group, and set \[ \Pi_F^a=G_F^{(\ell)}/[G_F^{(\ell)},G_F^{(\ell)}],\qquad \Pi_F^c=G_F^{(\ell)}/[[G_F^{(\ell)},G_F^{(\ell)}],G_F^{(\ell)}]. \] All commutator subgroups are closed. The kernel $\Delta_F=\ker(\Pi_F^c\to\Pi_F^a)$ is central. Our commutator convention is $[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$. Lifting elements of $\Pi_F^a$ to $\Pi_F^c$ therefore defines a continuous alternating $\Zell$-bilinear map \[ [\ ,\ ]_F:\Pi_F^a\times\Pi_F^a\longrightarrow\Delta_F. \] An isomorphism $\varphi:\Pi_L^a\to\Pi_K^a$ is \emph{bracket-compatible} if it is a continuous $\Zell$-module isomorphism and there is a continuous $\Zell$-module isomorphism $\psi:\Delta_L\to\Delta_K$ such that \[ \psi([\sigma,\tau]_L)=[\varphi\sigma,\varphi\tau]_K \quad(\sigma,\tau\in\Pi_L^a). \] Write $\Isom^c(\Pi_L^a,\Pi_K^a)$ for this set. The units $\Zell^\times$ act by multiplication on $\varphi$; the corresponding map on the central kernel can be multiplied by the square of the same unit. Write $F^i$ for the perfect closure of $F$; in characteristic zero this is $F$ itself. Let $\Isom^i(K,L)$ consist of the field isomorphisms $\alpha:K^i\to L^i$ satisfying $\alpha(k)=l$. If the common characteristic is $p>0$, put \[ \Isom_F^i(K,L)=\Isom^i(K,L)/ \bigl(\alpha\sim\Frob_L^n\circ\alpha,\ n\in\mathbb Z\bigr). \] Frobenius is an automorphism of a perfect field, so negative integers are allowed. In characteristic zero take no quotient. If the characteristics differ, set $\Isom_F^i(K,L)=\varnothing$. Field isomorphisms induce contravariant Galois isomorphisms. Passing to the above quotients gives the canonical map in the following theorem. [statement] Thus the theorem resolves the pro-$\ell$ abelian-by-central Bogomolov--Pop reconstruction conjecture positively. Neither equality of characteristics nor equality of relative transcendence degrees is assumed. Both are forced by a bracket-compatible isomorphism. The input contains no distinguished valuations, inertia groups, or curve quotients. \subsection{Previous work and the proof} Bogomolov introduced the abelian-by-central approach to birational reconstruction \cite{Bogomolov1991}. The local approach detects valuations through commuting elements and their associated multiplicative characters \cite{BT2002,TopazCL2017}. Bogomolov and Tschinkel proved the surface case over algebraic closures of finite fields \cite{BT2008}, followed by higher-dimensional reconstruction \cite{BT2011}; Pop established a corresponding birational reconstruction theorem \cite{PopBI2012}. The precise Isom formulation, including its scalar and Frobenius ambiguities, is recorded in \cite[Introduction, Conjecture~1]{TopazRat2016}. Pop's global reconstruction theorem recovers a function field from its total decomposition graph together with suitable rational quotients \cite{PopGraphs2012,PopBI2012}. His later reconstruction theorem with divisorial inertia applies in relative transcendence degree greater than two \cite[Theorem~1.1]{Pop2018}; its additional inertia datum must itself be recovered to solve the conjecture from the group alone. The local theory of commuting pairs supplies quasi-divisorial valuations, which can be nontrivial on the constants \cite{Pop2018,Topaz2019}. def proEllKernel (ell : ℕ) (G : Type*) [Group G] [TopologicalSpace G] : Subgroup G :=
  ⨅ (H : OpenNormalSubgroup G) (_ : IsPGroup ell (G ⧸ H.toSubgroup)), H.toSubgroup
instance proEllKernel_normal (ell : ℕ) (G : Type*) [Group G] [TopologicalSpace G] :
    (proEllKernel ell G).Normal := by
  unfold proEllKernel
  exact Subgroup.normal_iInf_normal fun H =>
    Subgroup.normal_iInf_normal fun _ => H.isNormal'
abbrev ProEll (ell : ℕ) (F : Type*) [Field F] :=
  Field.absoluteGaloisGroup F ⧸ proEllKernel ell (Field.absoluteGaloisGroup F)
def closedGammaThree (G : Type*) [Group G] [TopologicalSpace G] [IsTopologicalGroup G] :
    Subgroup G :=
  (⁅(commutator G).topologicalClosure, (⊤ : Subgroup G)⁆).topologicalClosure
instance closedGammaThree_normal (G : Type*) [Group G] [TopologicalSpace G]
    [IsTopologicalGroup G] : (closedGammaThree G).Normal := by
  unfold closedGammaThree
  exact Subgroup.is_normal_topologicalClosure _
abbrev PiA (ell : ℕ) (F : Type*) [Field F] := TopologicalAbelianization (ProEll ell F)
abbrev PiC (ell : ℕ) (F : Type*) [Field F] := ProEll ell F ⧸ closedGammaThree (ProEll ell F)
theorem closedGammaThree_le (G : Type*) [Group G] [TopologicalSpace G]
    [IsTopologicalGroup G] : closedGammaThree G ≤ (commutator G).topologicalClosure := by
  exact Subgroup.topologicalClosure_minimal _ (Subgroup.commutator_le_left _ _)
    (Subgroup.isClosed_topologicalClosure _)
def centralProjection (ell : ℕ) (F : Type*) [Field F] : PiC ell F →* PiA ell F :=
  QuotientGroup.map _ _ (MonoidHom.id _) (closedGammaThree_le _)
abbrev Delta (ell : ℕ) (F : Type*) [Field F] := (centralProjection ell F).ker
def sourceCommutator {G : Type*} [Group G] (x y : G) : G := x⁻¹ * y⁻¹ * x * y
def abelianLift (ell : ℕ) (F : Type*) [Field F] (x : PiA ell F) : ProEll ell F :=
  Quotient.out x
theorem abelianLift_spec (ell : ℕ) (F : Type*) [Field F] (x : PiA ell F) :
    QuotientGroup.mk' _ (abelianLift ell F x) = x := Quotient.out_eq x
def bracket (ell : ℕ) (F : Type*) [Field F] (x y : PiA ell F) : Delta ell F :=
  ⟨QuotientGroup.mk' _ (sourceCommutator (abelianLift ell F x) (abelianLift ell F y)), by
    change centralProjection ell F _ = 1
    simp only [centralProjection, sourceCommutator, map_mul, map_inv]
    simp [mul_comm]⟩
def BracketCompatible {ell : ℕ} {K L : Type*} [Field K] [Field L]
    (φ : PiA ell L ≃ₜ* PiA ell K) : Prop :=
  ∃ ψ : Delta ell L ≃ₜ* Delta ell K,
    ∀ σ τ, ψ (bracket ell L σ τ) = bracket ell K (φ σ) (φ τ)
abbrev PerfectFieldClosure (F : Type*) [Field F] := perfectClosure F (AlgebraicClosure F)
def PerfectIsom (k K l L : Type*) [Field k] [Field K] [Field l] [Field L]
    [Algebra k K] [Algebra l L] :=
  { α : PerfectFieldClosure K ≃+* PerfectFieldClosure L //
    α '' Set.range (algebraMap k (PerfectFieldClosure K)) =
      Set.range (algebraMap l (PerfectFieldClosure L)) }
def FrobeniusStep {k K l L : Type*} [Field k] [Field K] [Field l] [Field L]
    [Algebra k K] [Algebra l L] (α β : PerfectIsom k K l L) : Prop :=
  ∃ p : ℕ, p.Prime ∧ (p : L) = 0 ∧
    ∀ x : PerfectFieldClosure K, β.1 x = (α.1 x) ^ p
abbrev IsomF (k K l L : Type*) [Field k] [Field K] [Field l] [Field L]
    [Algebra k K] [Algebra l L] :=
  Quotient (Relation.EqvGen.setoid (FrobeniusStep (k := k) (K := K) (l := l) (L := L)))
abbrev IsomC (ell : ℕ) (K L : Type*) [Field K] [Field L] :=
  {φ : PiA ell L ≃ₜ* PiA ell K // BracketCompatible φ}
def ScalarStep {ell : ℕ} [Fact ell.Prime] {K L : Type*} [Field K] [Field L]
    (φ χ : IsomC ell K L) : Prop :=
  ∃ u : (PadicInt ell)ˣ, ∀ x : PiA ell L,
    Filter.Tendsto (fun n : ℕ => (φ.1 x) ^ PadicInt.appr (u : PadicInt ell) n)
      Filter.atTop (nhds (χ.1 x))
abbrev IsomCUnits (ell : ℕ) [Fact ell.Prime] (K L : Type*) [Field K] [Field L] :=
  Quotient (Relation.EqvGen.setoid (ScalarStep (ell := ell) (K := K) (L := L)))
def absoluteToPiA (ell : ℕ) (F : Type*) [Field F] :
    Field.absoluteGaloisGroup F →* PiA ell F :=
  (QuotientGroup.mk' _).comp (QuotientGroup.mk' _)
def absoluteAction {F : Type*} [Field F] (σ : Field.absoluteGaloisGroup F)
    (x : AlgebraicClosure F) : AlgebraicClosure F := by
  change AlgebraicClosure F ≃ₐ[F] AlgebraicClosure F at σ
  exact σ x
def PullbackGraph {ell : ℕ} {k K l L : Type*}
    [Field k] [Field K] [Field l] [Field L] [Algebra k K] [Algebra l L]
    (α : PerfectIsom k K l L) (φ : IsomC ell K L) : Prop :=
  ∃ β : AlgebraicClosure K ≃+* AlgebraicClosure L,
    (∀ x : PerfectFieldClosure K, β x = ((α.1 x : PerfectFieldClosure L) :
      AlgebraicClosure L)) ∧
    ∀ σ : Field.absoluteGaloisGroup L, ∃ τ : Field.absoluteGaloisGroup K,
      (∀ x : AlgebraicClosure K,
        β (absoluteAction τ x) = absoluteAction σ (β x)) ∧
      φ.1 (absoluteToPiA ell L σ) = absoluteToPiA ell K τ
def PhiGraph {ell : ℕ} [Fact ell.Prime] {k K l L : Type*}
    [Field k] [Field K] [Field l] [Field L] [Algebra k K] [Algebra l L]
    (a : IsomF k K l L) (b : IsomCUnits ell K L) : Prop :=
  ∃ α : PerfectIsom k K l L, ∃ φ : IsomC ell K L,
    Quotient.mk _ α = a ∧ Quotient.mk _ φ = b ∧ PullbackGraph α φ
