FAMILY 093: Dimension-free logarithmic Sobolev inequality for subgaussian log-concave measures
SUBJECT: Convex and metric geometry
OPENAI SUMMARY: Proves a dimension-free logarithmic Sobolev inequality for centered log-concave densities with uniformly subgaussian linear marginals, with constant bounded by a universal multiple of the squared linear subgaussian parameter.

=== MANUSCRIPT: A dimension-free logarithmic Sobolev inequality for subgaussian log-concave measures (September-23-2026) [preprints/A-dimension-free-logarithmic-Sobolev-inequality-for-subgaussian-log-concave-measures-September-23-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove that every centered log-concave probability measure with a Lebesgue density on ℝn and linear subgaussian parameter a satisfies $`\mathop{\mathrm{Ent}}\nolimits _\mu(f^2)\le Ca^2\int|Df|^2\,d\mu`$ for compactly supported smooth f, with one universal constant C. This resolves positively the dimension-free logarithmic Sobolev conjecture for subgaussian log-concave measures.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} A logarithmic Sobolev inequality controls the entropy of a function by its Dirichlet energy. Besides implying a Poincar\'e inequality, it gives Gaussian concentration for Lipschitz functions. For a log-concave measure, a natural question is whether Gaussian tails of all \emph{linear} functions already force this stronger conclusion. We prove that they do, with a constant independent of dimension. For a probability measure \(\nu\) and a nonnegative integrable function \(g\), write \[ \Ent_\nu(g)=\int g\log g\,d\nu -\left(\int g\,d\nu\right)\log\left(\int g\,d\nu\right), \] with \(0\log0=0\). A Lebesgue density \(\rho\) on \(\R^n\) is log-concave if its positive set is convex and \(\log\rho\) is concave there. We call a centered random vector \(X\) \emph{linearly \(a\)-subgaussian} if \begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-subgaussian} \sup_{|\theta|=1}\E\exp\!\left(\frac{\langle X,\theta\rangle^2}{a^2}\right) \le2. \end{equation} This definition concerns only linear functions of \(X\). We write \(D\) for the Euclidean gradient. [statement] No smoothness of the density or positive lower curvature bound is required. In particular, the theorem includes uniform measures on convex bodies. The proof will use smooth positive densities only after a reduction that preserves any hypothetical sequence of counterexamples. The Otto--Villani implication from logarithmic Sobolev to quadratic transport--entropy inequalities \cite[Theorem~1]{OttoVillani2000} gives the following standard consequence, with the same constant. Write $\mathcal P_2(\R^n)$ for the Borel probabilities with finite second moment. For $\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal P_2(\R^n)$, set \[ W_2(\alpha,\beta)^2 =\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\alpha,\beta)}\int|x-y|^2\,d\pi(x,y), \] where $\Pi(\alpha,\beta)$ is the set of their couplings. Relative entropy is $H(\alpha\mid\beta)=\int f\log f\,d\beta$ if $\alpha=f\beta$, with its possible value $+\infty$, and is $+\infty$ if $\alpha\not\ll\beta$. [statement] The proof in Section~\ref{sec:transport-consequence} gives the approximation argument for nonsmooth log-concave densities. \subsection{History and significance} Gross's work on logarithmic Sobolev inequalities connected the Gaussian inequality with hypercontractivity and established a framework in which the constants remain independent of dimension \cite{Gross1975}. The diffusion approach of Bakry and \'Emery \cite{BakryEmery1985} gives the inequality for a density $e^{-V}$ when $D^2V\ge\kappa\Id$ with $\kappa>0$: in the convention used below, its constant is at most $\kappa^{-1}$. Log-concavity alone asserts only $D^2V\ge0$ when the potential is smooth. The question addressed here is whether a global assumption on the tails of linear functions can supply the logarithmic Sobolev bound without positive curvature. A logarithmic Sobolev inequality implies Gaussian concentration for all Lipschitz functions through Herbst's exponential-moment argument; see \cite[Section~2.3]{Ledoux1999}. Under log-concavity, Gaussian concentration for all Lipschitz functions also gives Gaussian isoperimetry by Milman's theorem \cite[Theorem~1.1]{Milman2010}, and hence a logarithmic Sobolev inequality \cite[Lemma~3.3]{Bobkov1999}, with dimension-independent bounds. The question here asks whether control of linear functions suffices. Uniformly subgaussian linear marginals are necessary, up to universal constants, by the same Herbst argument. Bizeul formulated their sufficiency within the log-concave class in 2023 \cite[Conjecture~2]{Bizeul2026}. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} resolves this conjecture for centered log-concave laws with a Lebesgue density, under the precise marginal condition \eqref{eq:intro-subgaussian}. Bobkov's norm-based criterion \cite[Theorem~1.3]{Bobkov1999} bounds the entropy coefficient by a universal multiple of the squared subgaussian norm of $|X|$. [theorem] \label{thm:main} There is an absolute constant \(C<\infty\) with the following property. For every \(n\ge1\), every centered log-concave probability measure \(\mu\) with a Lebesgue density on \(\R^n\), and every \(a>0\) satisfying \eqref{eq:intro-subgaussian} for \(X\sim\mu\), \[ \Ent_\mu(f^2)\le C a^2\int_{\R^n}|Df|^2\,d\mu \qquad\text{for every }f\in C_c^\infty(\R^n). \] The same constant works in every dimension.
[corollary] [Quadratic transport--entropy inequality] \label{cor:intro-transport} Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, every $\nu\in\mathcal P_2(\R^n)$ satisfies \[ W_2(\nu,\mu)^2\le Ca^2 H(\nu\mid\mu), \] with the same absolute constant $C$ as in that theorem.
