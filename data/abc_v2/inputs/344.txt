=== MANUSCRIPT: The metric Blaschke theorem (September-23-2026) [preprints/The-metric-Blaschke-theorem-September-23-2026/build/paper.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove the metric Blaschke conjecture: every connected closed smooth Riemannian manifold of positive dimension whose global injectivity radius equals its diameter is, up to scale, a standard compact rank-one symmetric space.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} On a round sphere, every geodesic minimizes from its starting point to the antipode, at a distance equal to the diameter. The standard real, complex and quaternionic projective spaces and the Cayley plane have the same common minimizing-distance property. The metric Blaschke conjecture asks whether this property characterizes these compact rank-one symmetric spaces. For a connected closed smooth Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$, write \[ \inj(M,g)=\inf_{p\in M}\inj_p(M,g), \qquad \diam(M,g)=\sup_{p,q\in M}d_g(p,q). \] Here $\inj_p(M,g)$ is the supremum of the radii on whose open tangent balls $\exp_p$ is a diffeomorphism onto its image. We call $M$ a \emph{Blaschke manifold} if it has positive dimension and $\inj(M,g)=\diam(M,g)$. The global equality implies that every unit-speed geodesic minimizes precisely up to the common diameter; we give the short deduction in Lemma~\ref{geom:cut-time}. [statement] The classification includes every quaternionic dimension. Its hypothesis is the global equality just defined. Classical Blaschke structure associates to $M$ a compact rank-one symmetric \emph{model} $M_0$ by its cut-locus and integral cohomology type. Throughout the volume comparisons, the standard metric $g_0$ on $M_0$ is scaled to have the same diameter as $(M,g)$. Prime geodesics have length twice the diameter, so fixing the diameter is equivalent to fixing their prime period. \subsection{The problem and its predecessors} Blaschke's original question concerned surfaces with a common reconvergence property for geodesics. Green settled the surface problem \citep{Blaschke1921,Green1963}; the later geometric formulation and its development are described in \citet{Besse1978} and in the corrected account of \citet{McKay2016}. The classical theory connects the minimizing condition to closed geodesics, smooth cut loci and fibrations of tangent spheres by great spheres. The topological restrictions of Bott and Samelson explain the appearance of the compact rank-one symmetric models \citep[Theorem~7.2]{Besse1978}. The sphere and real-projective metric cases were established through the work of Berger, Kazdan, Weinstein and Yang; see \citet[Appendices~D--E]{Besse1978} and \citet[Sections~11--12]{McKay2016}. Berger's sphere argument combines the geometric volume problem with Kazdan's scalar integral inequality \citep{Berger1978AppendixD,Kazdan1978AppendixE}. Berger subsequently proved the sharp isoembolic inequality by a weighted comparison of Jacobi determinants and Jensen's inequality \citep[pp.~263--264]{Berger1980}. These comparisons are central predecessors of the analytic part of the present proof. Under additional hypotheses on families of totally geodesic round spheres of curvature $4$, \citet[Theorem~26, pp.~8--11] {RovenskiiToponogov1996} obtain a sharp projective volume comparison. Their Jacobi determinant splits into an exact curvature-$4$ factor and a complementary factor controlled by Kazdan's inequality. Our fixed-chart Schur-complement argument uses the cut-time intersection pattern without requiring that geometric splitting. For the projective models, it is useful to distinguish three kinds of information: topology, volume at a fixed geodesic period, and metric isometry. Yang proved that a Blaschke manifold of complex projective \emph{homotopy type} has the model volume at the same prime geodesic period \citep[Theorem, p.~380]{Yang1991}. For the projective planes, smooth incidence geometry and the classification of \citet[Corollary~C]{KramerStolz2007} identify the point manifold diffeomorphically. Wilking records the equal-volume theorem for a manifold homeomorphic to a compact rank-one symmetric space whose unit geodesics all have the same least period, attributing it to Reznikov \citep[p.~282]{Wilking2001}. [theorem] [The metric Blaschke theorem]\label{thm:blaschke} Let $(M,g)$ be a connected closed smooth Riemannian manifold of positive dimension. If $\inj(M,g)=\diam(M,g)$, then $(M,g)$ is isometric, up to multiplication of the metric by a positive constant, to a round sphere, a standard real, complex or quaternionic projective space, or the Cayley plane.
[theorem] [Sharp volume comparison]\label{thm:comparison} Every Blaschke manifold and its model at the same diameter satisfy \[ \Vol(M,g)\ge\Vol(M_0,g_0). \] Equality holds if and only if $(M,g)$ is isometric to $(M_0,g_0)$.
[corollary] [Reduction to an upper volume bound] \label{thm:volume-reduction} If a Blaschke manifold has volume at most that of its model at the same diameter, then it is isometric to that model.
