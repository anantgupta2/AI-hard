FAMILY 331: Reflexive midpoint convexity and diamond distortion
SUBJECT: Functional analysis
OPENAI SUMMARY: Constructs a real reflexive Banach space with an asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex norm but no equivalent asymptotically uniformly convex norm, extending Baudier's separation to reflexive spaces. In the same space, depth-k countably branching diamonds require distortion at least $`\sqrt{1+k/12}`$, so midpoint uniform convexity does not force uniformly bounded diamond distortion even under reflexivity.

ABSTRACT: We construct a separable reflexive real Banach space whose given norm is asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex but which admits no asymptotically uniformly convex equivalent norm. Its averaged midpoint modulus is at least $`\sqrt{1+t^2/12}-1`$, and the countably branching diamond of depth k has distortion at least $`\sqrt{1+k/12}`$ in this space. This gives a negative answer to the reflexive diamond converse for asymptotic uniform convexifiability.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Asymptotic convexity describes the convexity that remains after finitely many directions are discarded. The question considered here is whether symmetric midpoint convexity can persist in a reflexive Banach space which admits no asymptotically uniformly convex equivalent norm. All spaces and scalars are real. For an infinite-dimensional Banach space $Y$ with norm $N$, write $\operatorname{cof}(Y)$ for the closed finite-codimensional subspaces of $Y$. For $t>0$, define \begin{align} \overline\delta_N(t) &=\inf_{N(x)=1}\sup_{F\in\operatorname{cof}(Y)} \inf_{y\in F,\,N(y)=1}\bigl(N(x+ty)-1\bigr), \label{eq:auc-modulus}\\ \widehat\delta_N(t) &=\inf_{N(x)=1}\sup_{F\in\operatorname{cof}(Y)} \inf_{y\in F,\,N(y)=1} \left(\frac{N(x+ty)+N(x-ty)}2-1\right). \label{eq:average-modulus} \end{align} The norm is \emph{asymptotically uniformly convex} (AUC) if $\overline\delta_N(t)>0$ for all $t>0$, and \emph{asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex} (AMUC) if $\widehat\delta_N(t)>0$ for all $t>0$. Thus AUC requires a gain in every sufficiently far-out direction; AMUC permits the two opposite directions to be tested together. A norm $N$ is equivalent to a given norm $\|\cdot\|$ if $a\|x\|\le N(x)\le b\|x\|$ for some $0<a\le b<\infty$ and all $x$. A space is \emph{AUC renormable} if it admits an equivalent AUC norm. Dilworth, Kutzarova, Randrianarivony, Revalski, and Zhivkov introduced AMUC and constructed an AMUC norm on $\ell_2$ which is not AUC \cite[Definition~2.2 and Theorem~2.4]{DKRRZ2016}. Their question about equivalence up to renorming \cite[Section~5]{DKRRZ2016} has already been answered negatively by Baudier \cite[Theorem~1 and Corollary~1]{Baudier2026}, using the Kadets--Werner modification of the Bourgain--Rosenthal space \cite[Theorem~2.5]{KW2004}. We give a reflexive example with an explicit midpoint estimate. The historical definition of AMUC uses the maximum of the two signed norms in \eqref{eq:average-modulus}. This gives the same positivity property. The average never exceeds the maximum. Conversely, intersect any chosen finite-codimensional subspace with the kernel of a functional norming $x$. Both signed norms are then at least one, so a gap for their maximum gives half that gap for their average. Our quantitative modulus always uses the average in \eqref{eq:average-modulus}. The example also addresses a metric question. Define the \emph{countably branching diamonds} by taking $D_0$ to be a single edge and replacing every edge at each step by countably many internally disjoint two-edge paths. Each current edge has length one; let $d_k$ be the shortest-path metric on $D_k$. An embedding of $D_k$ into a Banach space has distortion at most $D$ if it is a map $f$ for which some scale $s>0$ satisfies \begin{equation}\label{eq:embedding-definition} s\,d_k(u,v)\le\|f(u)-f(v)\|\le Ds\,d_k(u,v) \qquad(u,v\in D_k). \end{equation} The family embeds uniformly when one finite $D$ works for all $k$. For finite binary diamonds, Lee and Naor obtained quantitative $L_p$ distortion bounds by summing convexity losses \cite[Lemma~2.2]{LeeNaor2004}, and Johnson and Schechtman developed a self-improvement proof \cite[Section~4]{JohnsonSchechtman2009}. Baudier, Causey, Dilworth, Kutzarova, Randrianarivony, Schlumprecht, and Zhang proved that an equivalent AMUC norm prevents uniform embeddings of the countably branching diamonds \cite[Theorem~4.1]{BaudierEtAl2017}. For reflexive spaces with unconditional asymptotic structure, they also proved that failure of AUC renormability implies uniform diamond embeddings \cite[Theorem~B and Corollary~5.3]{BaudierEtAl2017}. Swift extended the embedding construction to countably branching bundle graphs under the same reflexive, unconditional, and non-AUC-renormable hypotheses \cite[Theorem~3.2 and Corollary~3.4]{Swift2018}. def completionEquivOfDense {E : Type uSpace} {F : Type uOther} [NormedAddCommGroup E]
    [NormedSpace ℝ E] [NormedAddCommGroup F] [NormedSpace ℝ F] [CompleteSpace F] (f : E →L[ℝ] F)
    (K : NNReal) (hK : ∀ x, ‖x‖ ≤ K * ‖f x‖) (hdense : DenseRange f) : Completion E ≃L[ℝ] F := by
  let g := f.fromCompletion
  have hbound : ∀ x : Completion E, ‖x‖ ≤ K * ‖g x‖ := by
    intro x
    induction x using Completion.induction_on with
    | hp => exact isClosed_le continuous_norm (continuous_const.mul (g.continuous.norm))
    | ih x => simpa only [g, ContinuousLinearMap.fromCompletion_apply_coe, Completion.norm_coe] using hK x
  have hanti := g.antilipschitz_of_bound hbound
  have hclosed := hanti.isClosed_range g.uniformContinuous
  have hsurj : Function.Surjective g := by
    intro y
    have hh : y ∈ closure (Set.range f) := hdense y
    have hsub : Set.range f ⊆ Set.range g := by
      rintro _ ⟨x, rfl⟩
      exact ⟨(x : Completion E), ContinuousLinearMap.fromCompletion_apply_coe f x⟩
    have hm := closure_mono hsub hh
    rw [hclosed.closure_eq] at hm
    exact hm
  exact ContinuousLinearEquiv.ofBijective g (LinearMap.ker_eq_bot.mpr hanti.injective)
    (LinearMap.range_eq_top.mpr hsurj)

def logarithmicGamma (M β : ℝ) : ℝ :=
  let h := β ^ 2 / (2 * M ^ 2)
  let c := β * Real.sqrt h / 8
  let H := 1 + Real.log (M / c)
  let K := 4 * (2 + Real.sqrt 6) * H / h
  β * h / 8 * Real.exp (-K)
