FAMILY 199: Counterexamples to Auslander–Reiten, Tachikawa and related homological conjectures
SUBJECT: Algebra
OPENAI SUMMARY: Constructs finite-dimensional algebras over a characteristic-two rational-function field that disprove the Auslander–Reiten and Gorenstein-projective conjectures, and Tachikawa's second conjecture. An associated endomorphism algebra also disproves the classical, generalized and strong Nakayama conjectures, the Auslander–Gorenstein conjecture, and the Wakamatsu tilting conjecture. The counterexamples persist under every extension of the base field.

ABSTRACT: We disprove the Auslander–Reiten conjecture for Artin algebras. We construct a finite-dimensional algebra Λ over $`k=\mathbb F_2(q,H_1,H_2)`$ and a finite-dimensional nonprojective left module Z such that $`\mathop{\mathrm{Ext}}\nolimits ^i_\Lambda(Z,Z)=\mathop{\mathrm{Ext}}\nolimits ^i_\Lambda(Z,\Lambda)=0`$ for every i &gt; 0. The module is Gorenstein-projective, so the same example also disproves the Gorenstein-projective conjecture. Both counterexamples persist after every extension of k.
INTRODUCTION (start): The Auslander--Reiten conjecture asks whether a finitely generated module $M$ over an Artin algebra $R$ must be projective when \begin{equation}\label{eq:arc} \Ext_R^i(M,M\oplus R)=0\qquad\text{for every }i>0. \end{equation} Here an Artin algebra is finite over a commutative Artinian central base ring. In particular, every finite-dimensional algebra over a field is an Artin algebra. The conjecture originates in the work of Maurice Auslander and Idun Reiten on the generalized Nakayama conjecture \cite{AuslanderReiten1975}; its module and generator formulations are recalled in \cite[Introduction]{HunekeLeuschke2004}. We give a counterexample. A module is called \emph{Gorenstein-projective} if it is a cokernel in a doubly infinite exact complex of finite projectives that remains exact after applying $\Hom_R(-,R)$. The \emph{Gorenstein-projective conjecture} asks whether a finite Gorenstein-projective module $M$ with $\Ext_R^i(M,M)=0$ for every $i>0$ must be projective~\cite[p.~2]{LuoHuang2008}. The ambient algebra is not assumed to be Gorenstein. Total acyclicity gives $\Ext_R^i(M,R)=0$ for $i>0$, so this conjecture is precisely the restriction of the Auslander--Reiten conjecture to Gorenstein-projective modules. [statement] Thus both conjectures fail for Artin algebras, including finite-dimensional algebras over an algebraically closed field of characteristic two: one can extend scalars to an algebraic closure of $\kk$. The parameters play essential roles in the proof. The powers of $q$ give an infinite resolution with uniform kernel formulas, and $H_1/H_2$ has infinite multiplicative order. The algebraic closure in this assertion contains these transcendental elements. The final algebra is triangular and noncommutative. The companion article \cite[Theorem~1.1 and Corollary~1.2]{OpenAITachikawa2026} constructs a symmetric counterexample over the same field by a different bimodule construction and a transfer requiring control of negative complete self-extensions, and develops the homological consequences of its associated endomorphism algebra. The contribution here is the explicit eight-simple Gorenstein-projective example and its persistence under every field extension. Each paper contains its own complete proof. The commutative restriction is not resolved by either construction; see \cite[Appendix A]{ChristensenHolm2010} for its relationship with the various Auslander and Tachikawa conditions. The generator formulation also fails: $Z\oplus\Lambda$ is a nonprojective generator with no positive self-extensions. Christensen and Holm established a general cohomological criterion: every left-noetherian ring satisfying Auslander's condition satisfies the conjecture \cite[Theorem~A]{ChristensenHolm2010}. That condition requires, for each fixed finitely generated first module, a common bound after which Ext vanishes for every finitely generated second module whose Ext groups vanish eventually. Algebras with radical cube zero form another important positive class. Huneke, \c{S}ega and Vraciu treated commutative Artinian local rings \cite[Theorem~4.1(1)]{HunekeSegaVraciu2004}. [theorem] \label{thm:main} Let $\kk=\mathbb F_2(q,H_1,H_2)$, where $q,H_1,H_2$ are algebraically independent. There exist a finite-dimensional $\kk$-algebra $\Lambda$ and a finite-dimensional nonprojective left $\Lambda$-module $Z$ such that \[ \Ext^i_\Lambda(Z,Z)=0=\Ext^i_\Lambda(Z,\Lambda) \qquad\text{for every integer }i\geq1. \] The module $Z$ is Gorenstein-projective. The semisimple quotient is $\Lambda/\rad\Lambda\simeq\kk^8$, so the algebra is split with eight isomorphism classes of simple modules; also $\rad(\Lambda)^4\ne0$. These conclusions persist after every field extension of $\kk$.

=== MANUSCRIPT: A counterexample to Tachikawa's second conjecture (September-23-2026) [preprints/A-counterexample-to-Tachikawas-second-conjecture-September-23-2026/build/paper.tex]
ABSTRACT: We disprove Tachikawa's second conjecture by constructing a finite-dimensional symmetric algebra over $`k=\mathbb F_2(q,H_1,H_2)`$ with a finite-dimensional nonprojective module whose self-extension groups vanish in every positive degree. The associated endomorphism algebra also gives counterexamples to the classical, generalized, and strong Nakayama conjectures, the Auslander–Gorenstein conjecture, and the Wakamatsu tilting conjecture. These conclusions persist after every extension of k.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Let $A$ be a finite-dimensional algebra over a field $k$. A finite-dimensional $A$-module $M$ is \emph{self-orthogonal} if $\Ext_A^i(M,M)=0$ for every integer $i>0$. Tachikawa's second conjecture asserts that every finite-dimensional self-orthogonal module over a finite-dimensional self-injective algebra is projective. This question belongs to the homological conjectures surrounding quasi-Frobenius algebras and dominant dimension; see Tachikawa's 1973 monograph~\cite[pp.~VI--VII]{Tachikawa1973} and the account of the conjecture in~\cite[p.~7288]{ChenXi2025TAMS}. The Auslander--Reiten conjecture, originating in \cite{AuslanderReiten1975}, asks for finite-dimensional algebras whether the condition $\Ext_A^i(M,M\oplus A)=0$ for every $i>0$ forces $M$ to be projective. For a self-injective algebra, the groups $\Ext_A^i(M,A)$ vanish automatically, so Tachikawa's second conjecture is its self-injective case. Write $D=\Hom_k(-,k)$. An algebra $A$ is \emph{symmetric} if $A\simeq DA$ as $A$-bimodules; in particular, a symmetric algebra is self-injective. We prove the following result in this class. [statement] Since $A$ is self-injective, the same module satisfies $\Ext_A^i(M,M\oplus A)=0$ for every $i>0$. Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:main} also gives a symmetric counterexample to the Auslander--Reiten conjecture. \paragraph{Related results.} Schulz's earlier example of a nonprojective module without positive self-extensions uses an algebra over a skew field, outside the finite-dimensional-over-a-field setting considered here; see \cite{Schulz1994} and the distinction recalled in \cite[Introduction]{DiverisPurin2012}. His quantum-exterior example has nonzero first self-extension but no higher self-extensions \cite[Example~7]{Schulz1995}; the parameter-shifting syzygies in that example are an antecedent for our starting algebra. For symmetric Artin algebras, Diveris and Purin show that a component containing a nonprojective module with eventually vanishing self-extensions has stable part of type $\mathbb Z A_\infty$ \cite[Theorem~A]{DiverisPurin2014}. Over an algebraically closed field, Erdmann classifies, up to Morita equivalence, the indecomposable weakly symmetric algebras with radical cube zero and nonzero radical square that admit such modules. The classification singles out quantum exterior and double Nakayama algebras with parameters that are not roots of unity~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{Erdmann2017}. Chen, Fang and Xi characterize the symmetric case through stratifying ideals and give conditional mirror-reflective constructions from gendo-symmetric counterexamples to the Nakayama conjecture \cite[Theorems~1.1 and~1.2]{ChenFangXi2024}. Chen and Xi relate self-orthogonal Nakayama-stable generators over self-injective algebras to additional categorical and Gorenstein--Morita conditions~\cite[Theorem~1.2 and Corollary~1.4]{ChenXi2025TAMS}. Their further criterion assumes virtual Gorensteinness of the relevant endomorphism algebra~\cite[Theorem~1.3]{ChenXi2025VG}. Zhang and Zhou prove the self-injective case for split algebras with radical cube zero~\cite[Corollary~4.2]{ZhangZhou2026} and subsequently treat Igusa--Todorov algebras~\cite[Theorem~3.2]{ZhangZhouIgusaTodorov2026}. Xia proves the Auslander--Reiten conjecture for quantum complete intersections~\cite[Corollary~4.3]{Xia2026}. In a different direction, B\"ohmler and Marczinzik construct a nonprojective module over a commutative local self-injective algebra whose first two self-extension groups vanish, while the third does not~\cite[Theorem~3.1 and the end of Section~3]{BohmlerMarczinzik2026}. [theorem] \label{thm:main} Let \[ k=\mathbb F_2(q,H_1,H_2), \] where $q,H_1,H_2$ are algebraically independent. There exist a finite-dimensional associative unital symmetric $k$-algebra $A$ and a finite-dimensional unital left $A$-module $M$ such that $M$ is nonprojective and \[ \Ext_A^i(M,M)=0\qquad\text{for every integer }i>0. \] In particular, Tachikawa's second conjecture is false.
[corollary] [Homological conjectures]\label{cor:homological} Let $A,M$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. For every field extension $K/k$, put $A_K=K\otimes_k A$, $M_K=K\otimes_k M$, and \[ \Gamma_K=\End_{A_K}(A_K\oplus M_K)^{\mathrm{op}}. \] Then $\Gamma_K$ is a finite-dimensional $K$-algebra with the following properties, in the category of finite left $\Gamma_K$-modules. \begin{enumerate} \item Every term of the minimal injective resolution of $\Gamma_K$ is projective, but $\idim_{\Gamma_K}\Gamma_K=\infty$. \item There is a nonzero simple module $S$ such that $\Ext_{\Gamma_K}^i(S,\Gamma_K)=0$ for every $i\geq0$. \item There is a projective-injective Wakamatsu tilting module $W$ which is not tilting. \end{enumerate} Consequently the classical, generalized, and strong Nakayama conjectures, the Auslander--Gorenstein conjecture, and the Wakamatsu tilting conjecture are false for finite-dimensional algebras over $K$.
[corollary] [Finitistic dimensions]\label{cor:finitistic} Let $k$ be the field in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, let $K/k$ be any field extension, and let $\Gamma_K$ be the algebra in Corollary~\ref{cor:homological}. For every integer $n\geq1$, there is a finite-dimensional left $\Gamma_K$-module $C_n$ such that \[ \pd_{\Gamma_K}C_n=n. \] Consequently $\Gamma_K$ has infinite little left finitistic dimension and infinite big left finitistic dimension.

def TotallyAcyclicWitness (R : Type u) [Ring R]
    (Z : Type u) [AddCommGroup Z] [Module R Z] : Prop :=
  ∃ P : ChainComplex (ModuleCat.{u} R) ℤ,
    (∀ i : ℤ, Module.Finite R (P.X i) ∧ Module.Projective R (P.X i)) ∧
    (∀ i : ℤ, LinearMap.range (P.d (i+1) i).hom = LinearMap.ker (P.d i (i-1)).hom) ∧
    (∀ (Q : Type u) [AddCommGroup Q] [Module R Q] [Module.Projective R Q]
      (i : ℤ) (f : P.X i →ₗ[R] Q), f.comp (P.d (i+1) i).hom = 0 →
        ∃ g : P.X (i-1) →ₗ[R] Q, f = g.comp (P.d i (i-1)).hom) ∧
    Nonempty (Z ≃ₗ[R] (P.X 0 ⧸ LinearMap.range (P.d 1 0).hom))
def Conclusions (F R Z : Type u) [Field F] [Ring R] [Algebra F R]
    [AddCommGroup Z] [Module R Z] [Module F Z] [IsScalarTower F R Z] : Prop :=
  FiniteDimensional F R ∧
  FiniteDimensional F Z ∧
  ¬ Module.Projective R Z ∧
  TotallyAcyclicWitness R Z ∧
  (∀ i : ℕ, 0 < i →
    Subsingleton (Abelian.Ext (ModuleCat.of R Z) (ModuleCat.of R Z) i)) ∧
  (∀ i : ℕ, 0 < i →
