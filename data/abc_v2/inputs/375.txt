FAMILY 375: De Giorgi's conjecture in dimension eight
SUBJECT: Partial differential equations
OPENAI SUMMARY: Proves De Giorgi's conjecture at its sharp dimension-eight endpoint: every entire C2 solution $`u:\mathbb R^8\to(-1,1)`$ of $`\Delta u=u^3-u`$ that is strictly increasing in one direction depends on only one linear coordinate. A stronger theorem classifies all stable entire solutions $`v:\mathbb R^7\to[-1,1]`$ as constant wells or planar transitions, without an energy-growth assumption.

=== MANUSCRIPT: A positive resolution of De Giorgi's conjecture in dimension eight (September-26-2026) [preprints/De-Giorgis-conjecture-in-dimension-eight-September-26-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We resolve De Giorgi's conjecture positively in dimension eight: every entire C2 solution $`u:\mathbb R^8\to(-1,1)`$ of $`\Delta u=u^3-u`$ with an everywhere positive directional derivative is a planar heteroclinic. We prove that every stable solution $`v:\mathbb R^7\to[-1,1]`$ of this equation is a constant well or a planar heteroclinic, without an energy-growth assumption.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Consider the scalar Allen--Cahn equation \begin{equation}\label{eq:allen-cahn} \Delta u=u^3-u. \end{equation} The function \[ g(s)=\tanh(s/\sqrt2) \] is its increasing one-dimensional transition from $-1$ to $1$. Thus $u(x)=g(e\cdot x-c)$ solves \eqref{eq:allen-cahn} whenever $e$ is a unit vector; its level sets are parallel hyperplanes. De Giorgi's conjecture asks whether every bounded entire solution that is strictly increasing in a fixed direction has this form in ambient dimension at most eight \cite{DeGiorgi1979}. The conjecture connects phase transitions with the Bernstein problem for minimal hypersurfaces. For the quartic potential \[ W(s)=\frac14(1-s^2)^2, \] the rescaled phase-transition energies have surface tension times perimeter as their variational limit \cite{ModicaMortola1977,Modica1987}. The graph condition in the minimal-surface problem is important: nonflat minimizing cones occur in ambient dimension eight, whereas nonaffine entire minimal graphs first occur in ambient dimension nine \cite{Simons1968,BDG1969}. The corresponding distinction between stable and monotone Allen--Cahn solutions will also enter our proof. Theorem~\ref{thm:stable} has a geometric parallel in the stable Bernstein theorem for complete, connected, two-sided stable smooth minimal immersions of boundaryless six-manifolds into $\R^7$: their images are entire affine hyperplanes, with no volume-growth hypothesis \cite[Theorem~1.1]{HongLiWang2026}. The Bernstein theorem is not an input to our argument. Ghoussoub and Gui established the conjecture in dimension two \cite{GhoussoubGui1998}, and Ambrosio and Cabr\'e in dimension three \cite{AmbrosioCabre2000}. Savin proved the result through dimension eight under the additional assumption \[ \lim_{t\to-\infty}u(x',t)=-1,\qquad \lim_{t\to+\infty}u(x',t)=1 \quad\text{for every }x'\in\R^{n-1} \] \cite[Theorem~2.4]{Savin2009}. These limits are pointwise in the transverse variable. Del Pino, Kowalczyk and Wei constructed nonplanar strictly monotone solutions in every ambient dimension at least nine, near large dilations of nonaffine minimal graphs \cite{DPKW2011}. The main result of this paper gives a positive resolution in dimension eight without an assumption on the directional limits. [statement] Neither energy growth nor minimization is assumed. Those properties, and the well limits, will be consequences of the argument. Both rigidity theorems concern the scalar quartic equation \eqref{eq:allen-cahn}; the monotonicity assumption in Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone} is strict positivity of $\partial_8u$. \subsection{The stable equation} For an open set $\Omega\subset\R^n$ write \[ \mathcal E(v;\Omega) =\int_\Omega\left(\frac12|\nabla v|^2+W(v)\right)\dd x. \] A solution $v$ is \emph{stable} if its second variation satisfies \begin{equation}\label{eq:stability-definition} Q_v(\varphi):= \int_{\R^n}\bigl(|\nabla\varphi|^2+(3v^2-1)\varphi^2\bigr)\dd x \ge0 \qquad\text{for every }\varphi\in C_c^1(\R^n). \end{equation} A \emph{global minimizer} minimizes $\mathcal E$ in every ball among $H^1$ competitors with the same boundary trace. Stability requires only the quadratic inequality, so it is a weaker condition than minimization. A strictly positive directional derivative solves the linearized equation and gives stability. Moreover, the limits along that direction are stable solutions in one fewer variable. This observation makes the following classification the analytic core of the paper. [statement] This stability endpoint is sharp: Pacard and Wei constructed bounded stable nonplanar solutions in dimension eight \cite{PacardWei2013}. [theorem] [Monotone rigidity in dimension eight] \label{thm:monotone} Let $u\in C^2(\R^8,(-1,1))$ satisfy \[ \Delta u=u^3-u,\qquad \partial_8u>0\quad\text{in }\R^8. \] Then there are $e\in\mathbb S^7$ with $e_8>0$ and $c\in\R$ such that \[ u(x)=\tanh\!\left(\frac{e\cdot x-c}{\sqrt2}\right) \qquad\text{for every }x\in\R^8. \]
[theorem] [Stable rigidity in dimension seven] \label{thm:stable} Let $v\in C^2(\R^7,[-1,1])$ be a stable solution of $\Delta v=v^3-v$. Then either $v\equiv1$, $v\equiv-1$, or \[ v(x)=\tanh\!\left(\frac{a\cdot x-b}{\sqrt2}\right) \qquad\text{for some }a\in\mathbb S^6,\ b\in\R. \] No energy-growth assumption is required.
[theorem] [Finite-density stable rigidity]\label{thm:finite} Let $4\le n\le7$. Suppose $v\in C^2(\R^n,[-1,1])$ solves $\Delta v=v^3-v$ and satisfies \[ \int_{\R^n}\bigl(|\nabla\varphi|^2+(3v^2-1)\varphi^2\bigr)\dd x \ge0 \qquad\text{for every }\varphi\in C_c^1(\R^n). \] If $M_\infty<\infty$, then $v\equiv1$, $v\equiv-1$, or $v(x)=g(a\cdot x-b)$ for some $a\in\mathbb S^{n-1}$ and $b\in\R$.
