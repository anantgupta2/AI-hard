FAMILY 278: Failure of Kohn–Sham ensemble representation
SUBJECT: Mathematical physics
OPENAI SUMMARY: Constructs a three-electron Coulomb molecule with two equal positive-integer-charge nuclei whose absolute ground-state density has no noninteracting ground-state ensemble representation by a single real spin-independent local potential in $`L^{3/2}(\mathbb R^3)+L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)`$. This disproves Kohn–Sham ensemble representability for that potential class; the required nuclear charge is specified nonnumerically.

=== MANUSCRIPT: A Coulomb ground-state density without Kohn-Sham ensemble representation (September-25-2026) [preprints/A-Coulomb-Ground-State-Density-without-Kohn-Sham-Ensemble-Representation-September-25-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We construct a finite three-electron Coulomb molecule whose spin-summed ground-state density cannot be reproduced by any ground-state ensemble of noninteracting electrons in a single real, spin-independent local potential in $`L^{3/2}(\mathbf R^3)+L^\infty(\mathbf R^3)`$. The molecule has two equal positive integer nuclear charges, specified by an exact finite nonnumerical formula.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} The density-functional variational viewpoint originates in the work of Hohenberg and Kohn~\cite{HK64}. Uniqueness of a density--potential mapping and existence of a representing potential are separate questions; see the review~\cite{Penz23} for their precise distinctions. Kohn--Sham density functional theory seeks to reproduce the density of an interacting electronic ground state by noninteracting electrons in a common local potential~\cite{KS65}. The constrained-search formulations of Levy and Lieb distinguish the existence of states with a prescribed density from the existence of a potential for which those states minimize the energy \cite{Levy79,Lieb83}. Allowing ensembles enlarges the latter class: several degenerate noninteracting ground states may be mixed to obtain a density that is unavailable from one determinant. Ullrich and Kohn make this distinction explicit in their treatment of singlet ground-state ensembles~\cite{UK01}. Thus a failure of a particular orbital ansatz does not settle the ensemble representability question. We consider the usual three-dimensional potential space \[ \mathcal V=L^{3/2}(\R^3;\R)+L^\infty(\R^3;\R). \] For \(v\in\mathcal V\), the noninteracting Hamiltonian \[ H_s(v)=\sum_{i=1}^3\left(-\frac12\Delta_i+v(r_i)\right) \] acts on the antisymmetric space \(\bigwedge^3L^2(\R^3;\C^2)\), with the same scalar potential for both spin components. Its realization is determined by its semibounded closed quadratic form. A ground-state ensemble is a positive trace-class operator \(\Gamma\) of trace one whose range is contained in the eigenspace at \(\inf\sigma(H_s(v))\). We denote its spin-summed one-body density by \(n_\Gamma\); it has integral three. [statement] The two nuclei have positive integer charges and distinct finite positions. The theorem concerns a fixed finite system. The exact specification of \(Z\) uses only spectral data, densities, and an angle minimization for a two-centre auxiliary problem; it does not test representability by other potentials. It is a nonnumerical specification, and no effective numerical bound for the charge is asserted. \Cref{thm:main} shows that a density of a finite interacting Coulomb ground state can fail to have a noninteracting ground-state ensemble representation in \(\mathcal V\). No restriction is placed on the degeneracy of a putative representing ground space or on the complex spinful states in its ensemble. The result makes no assertion about larger classes of distributional potentials, spin-dependent potentials, or nonlocal one-particle operators. The same density also obstructs a local-potential first variation of the ensemble Hartree--exchange--correlation functional. More precisely, Corollary~\ref{cor:hxc-first-variation} excludes a potential in \(\mathcal V\) representing its one-sided variation along every segment in the common finite-energy density domain. \subsection{Relation to earlier work} Explicit prescribed densities can already fail noninteracting \(v\)-representability in \(\mathcal V\). Wagner and collaborators discuss a positive two-electron density with finite kinetic energy whose interior cusp forces its inverse potential outside this class~\cite[Section~IV.G, Eq.~(36)]{WBSBW14}. Their example is specified directly, without a construction as a finite Coulomb ground-state density. Trushin, Erhard, and G\"orling report numerical indications of failures of ground-state noninteracting representability from atomic density inversions~\cite{TEG24}. Open-shell inversion and symmetry restrictions are further investigated in~\cite{ETG25}. The result here is an analytic exclusion for every ground-state ensemble of a specified finite Coulomb system, in the potential class \(\mathcal V\). Existence results for other settings must be distinguished from this question. There is a finite positive integer \(Z\), specified by \Cref{eq:explicit-charge}, for which the Coulomb Hamiltonian \[ H_C=\sum_{i=1}^3 \left(-\frac12\Delta_i -\frac{Z}{|r_i-(D/Z)e_z|} -\frac{Z}{|r_i+(D/Z)e_z|}\right) +\sum_{1\le i<j\le3}\frac1{|r_i-r_j|} \] has a normalizable absolute ground state \(\Psi\) in the sector \(S_z=1/2\), normalized by \(\|\Psi\|_2=1\), but there is no \(v\in\mathcal V\) and no ground-state ensemble \(\Gamma\) of \(H_s(v)\) such that \[ n_\Gamma=n_\Psi\qquad\text{almost everywhere on }\R^3. \]
