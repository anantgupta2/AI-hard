FAMILY 197: A torsion-free group algebra that is not directly finite
SUBJECT: Algebra
OPENAI SUMMARY: Constructs a finitely presented torsion-free nonsofic group whose group algebra over 𝔽2 is not directly finite, disproving Kaplansky's conjecture even without torsion. Companion examples give injective nonsurjective cellular automata on all configurations, refuting Gottschalk's surjunctivity conjecture. Another counterexample is an integral group-ring matrix, invertible over the rational group ring, with Fuglede–Kadison determinant strictly between zero and one, disproving the unrestricted Determinant Conjecture.

ABSTRACT: We construct a finitely presented torsion-free counterexample to Kaplansky's direct finiteness conjecture over the field of two elements. The group admits a finite two-dimensional classifying complex.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} A unital ring $R$ is \emph{directly finite} if $ab=1$ implies $ba=1$ for all $a,b\in R$. For a field $K$ and a group $G$, the group algebra $K[G]$ consists of finite formal sums of elements of $G$ with coefficients in $K$, with multiplication extending the group operation bilinearly. Kaplansky's direct finiteness conjecture asserts that every such group algebra is directly finite. We prove that the implication can fail even when the field is finite and the group is finitely presented and torsion-free. A classifying complex for $G$ is a connected CW complex with fundamental group $G$ and contractible universal cover. [statement] The auxiliary element $c$ certifies failure of the reverse identity: if $ba=1$, then $c=(ba)c=b(ac)=0$. \subsection*{History and related work} Kaplansky's formulation separates the characteristic-zero theorem from the positive-characteristic question~\cite[Part~II, Section~3, Problem~2, pp.~122--123]{Kaplansky1972}. In characteristic zero, group algebras are directly finite for every group. The operator-algebra argument also applies to matrix rings; Montgomery gave a concise $C^*$-algebra proof~\cite{Montgomery1969}. A ring whose matrix rings of every finite size are directly finite is called \emph{stably finite}. In arbitrary characteristic, Ara, O'Meara, and Perera proved stable finiteness for group algebras of free-by-amenable groups over division rings~\cite{AraOMearaPerera2002}. Elek and Szab\'o extended the conclusion to sofic groups~\cite[Corollary~4.7]{ElekSzabo2004}, a class defined by finite approximate permutation actions. Their theorem implies that the group in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is not sofic. A complementary approach studies the finite cancellation patterns forced by a one-sided inverse. Dykema, Heister, and Juschenko organized these patterns into universal groups and established direct finiteness for several small support pairs over $\Ftwo$~\cite{DykemaHeisterJuschenko2015}. Two companion manuscripts are especially relevant. A separate construction gives a scalar counterexample over a finite field of characteristic two for a finitely presented group with odd-order torsion~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{OpenAI2026DF}. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} uses the prime field $\Ftwo$ and a torsion-free group. Its method develops the graph and cone construction of \emph{A Torsion-Free Group Algebra with Zero Divisors}~\cite{OpenAI2026ZD}. That manuscript supplies the parity mechanism for a zero product and the arrangement-exclusion strategy. We adapt those arguments to produce a one-sided inverse and reproduce the estimates and topology needed here. Graphical presentations relate coincidences of labeled paths to geometric properties of quotient groups, as in Gromov's random graphical constructions~\cite{Gromov2003} and Ollivier's small-cancellation theorem~\cite{Ollivier2006}. Bereznyuk's spherical-diagram criterion for graphical asphericity distinguishes coincident graph lifts from genuinely different ones~\cite{Bereznyuk2019}. The same distinction is essential here: pairings of two occurrences of the same graph edge are removed by cone surgeries. We prove the required asphericity and root protection directly in Section~\ref{sec:topology}. \subsection*{The construction and its main estimates} The group is obtained from a bouquet of circles by attaching cones on finite labeled graphs. A cone kills every closed path in its graph, so a vertex in a chosen component determines a group element by reading a path from a fixed root to that vertex. The desired algebra elements are finite sums of these path labels and their inverses. Two graphs are used. A common outgoing label allows a simultaneous step in both coordinates of their vertex products. The group element contributed to the corresponding algebra product is constant along such a step. Kaplansky's direct-finiteness conjecture asks whether this finite support is enough: for every field $K$, group $G$, and $a,b\in K[G]$, must $ab=1$ imply $ba=1$? Here $K[G]$ consists of finite formal sums $\sum_g c_g g$, multiplied using the group law. A unital ring with this property is \emph{directly finite}; it is \emph{stably finite} if all its finite square matrix rings are directly finite. This distinction matters to a counterexample. Producing matrices $AB=I\ne BA$ over a group algebra does not by itself produce scalar elements with the same property. Our construction first produces the matrix pair, then embeds its matrix algebra in a larger group algebra to obtain the following scalar conclusion. The trace rules out a proper one-sided inverse. For an arbitrary characteristic-zero field, all coefficients of a proposed finite matrix pair lie in a finitely generated subfield, which embeds in $\mathbb C$. Thus the complex result gives stable finiteness over every characteristic-zero field. Elek and Szab\'o obtained stable finiteness over arbitrary division rings for all sofic groups from finite approximate models and a rank construction~\cite[Corollary~4.7]{ElekSzabo2004}. INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} A nonsingular integer matrix has determinant of absolute value at least one. The group-ring Determinant Conjecture asks for an analogue when the entries are finite integer combinations of elements of a discrete group. The relevant determinant, introduced by Fuglede and Kadison~\cite{FK1952}, uses the trace of a logarithm rather than a finite-dimensional determinant polynomial. Here are the conventions that fix this question. Let $G$ be a discrete group and let $\Z[G]$ denote its ordinary group ring of finite sums. def identityVector {G : Type} [Group G] {n : ℕ} (i : Fin n) : Hilbert G n :=
  by

def groupTrace {G : Type} [Group G] {n : ℕ}
    (T : Hilbert G n →L[ℂ] Hilbert G n) : ℂ :=
  ∑ i : Fin n, inner ℂ (identityVector i) (T (identityVector i))
def fkDet {G : Type} [Group G] {n : ℕ}
    (T : Hilbert G n →L[ℂ] Hilbert G n) : ℝ :=
  Real.exp ((groupTrace (CFC.log (star T * T))).re / 2)
def IsRegularOperator {G : Type} [Group G] {n : ℕ}
    (A : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) (MonoidAlgebra ℤ G))
    (T : Hilbert G n →L[ℂ] Hilbert G n) : Prop :=
  ∀ (ξ : Hilbert G n) (i : Fin n) (h : G),

      (A i j).coeff.sum fun g z => (z : ℂ) * ξ (j, g⁻¹ * h)
