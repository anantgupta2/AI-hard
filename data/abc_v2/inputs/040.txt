FAMILY 040: Bloch’s conjecture for complex surfaces
SUBJECT: Algebraic and complex geometry
OPENAI SUMMARY: Proves Bloch's conjecture: for every smooth connected projective complex surface S with $`p_g(S)=0`$, the Albanese map $`\mathrm{CH}_0(S)^0\to\mathrm{Alb}(S)(\mathbb C)`$ on integral degree-zero zero-cycles is an isomorphism. This combines the new $`p_g=q=0`$ theorem with the classical theorem of Bloch, Kas, and Lieberman.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Bloch’s conjecture for surfaces with p_g=0 (September-24-2026) [preprints/Blochs-Conjecture-for-Surfaces-with-pg-equals-q-equals-0-September-24-2026/build/paper.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove Bloch's conjecture for smooth connected projective complex surfaces with $`p_g=0`$: the Albanese homomorphism on integral degree-zero zero-cycles is an isomorphism.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} For a smooth connected projective complex surface \(S\), write \[ p_g(S)=h^0(S,\omega_S),\qquad q(S)=h^1(S,\mathcal O_S). \] The Chow group $\CH_0(S)$ is the group of integral zero-cycles modulo rational equivalence, where the relations are generated by divisors of rational functions on curves in $S$. The degree of a complex point is one, and $\CH_0(S)^0$ denotes the kernel of the degree map. The Albanese homomorphism \(\operatorname{alb}_S:\CH_0(S)^0\to\Alb(S)(\mathbb C)\) is surjective. Bloch's conjecture predicts that it is an isomorphism when \(p_g(S)=0\). We prove the conjecture. The main theorem treats the case \(q=0\), when the Albanese variety is a point and the prediction is that any two complex points are rationally equivalent. [statement] Theorem~\ref{thm:bloch} imposes no minimality, fundamental-group, or surface-construction hypothesis. Combining it with the classical non-general-type case gives the full statement. [statement] For a surface of general type, $p_g=0$ forces $q=0$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:bloch} applies. The other cases are the theorem of Bloch--Kas--Lieberman \cite{BKL}. We give the precise reduction in Section~\ref{subsec:integral-proof}. These zero-cycle statements also have consequences for rational Chow motives. For $p_g=q=0$, every rational Chow class on a power of $S$ or a Hilbert scheme of points is determined by its cohomology class. We state the precise consequences and their motivic antecedents after the proof overview. \subsection*{History and antecedents} Mumford showed that a nonzero holomorphic two-form prevents the zero-cycles on a surface from admitting a finite-dimensional parametrization modulo rational equivalence \cite{Mumford}. Bloch's conjecture predicts the converse at the level of the Albanese kernel: if $p_g=0$, degree and the Albanese image should account for all rational-equivalence classes of zero-cycles \cite{BlochLectures}. Bloch, Kas, and Lieberman proved this integral assertion for every surface of Kodaira dimension less than two \cite[pp.~135--136]{BKL}. Thus the remaining general-type problem concerns $p_g=q=0$, where the Albanese variety carries no information and the degree map is predicted to determine a zero-cycle completely. Several successful approaches use additional geometry of the surface. The group-action method of Inose--Mizukami \cite{InoseMizukami} and its extension by Barlow \cite{Barlow} compare zero-cycles with their images on suitable quotient surfaces. Bauer's treatment of Inoue surfaces with $K^2=7$ gives a later application, expressed through relations among the quotient correspondences \cite[Theorem~0.2 and Section~1]{BauerInoue}. Pedrini--Weibel recast involution arguments using the transcendental motive: for a bidouble cover, vanishing of the transcendental motives of the three intermediate quotients forces its own vanishing \cite[Theorem~2.5]{PedriniWeibel}. Their applications include numerical Godeaux and classical Campedelli examples, Keum--Naie, Burniat, and Inoue surfaces, giving examples for each $1\le K^2\le8$. A different route uses families of surfaces and the actions of correspondences on zero-cycles. For a family of regular surfaces whose fibered self-product has a rationally connected smooth projective compactification, Voisin shows that a relative correspondence acting trivially on holomorphic two-forms acts nilpotently on degree-zero zero-cycles. She applies this theorem to Catanese surfaces \cite[Theorems~0.3 and~0.6]{Voisin2014}. Passing to suitable quotients then gives Bloch's conjecture for Barlow surfaces. This extends the earlier result for Barlow surfaces carrying extra symmetries to the general members of that family. The present argument requires no prescribed family, covering construction, or automorphism of the surface. Recent preprints propose proofs with overlapping scope. [theorem] \label{thm:bloch} Let \(S\) be a smooth connected projective surface over \(\mathbb C\) with \(p_g(S)=q(S)=0\). Then \[ \deg:\CH_0(S)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z \] is an isomorphism, where \(\CH_0(S)\) has integral coefficients.
[corollary] [Bloch's conjecture for surfaces with \(p_g=0\)]\label{cor:bloch-pg-zero} Let \(S\) be a smooth connected projective surface over \(\mathbb C\) with \(p_g(S)=0\). Then the Albanese homomorphism \[ \operatorname{alb}_S:\CH_0(S)^0\longrightarrow\Alb(S)(\mathbb C) \] is an isomorphism of abelian groups, where \(\CH_0(S)\) has integral coefficients.
[corollary] [Motivic consequences]\label{cor:tate-motives} Let \(S\) be a smooth connected projective surface over \(\mathbb C\) with \(p_g(S)=0\). Then \(h(S)_{\mathbb Q}\) is finite-dimensional in the sense of Kimura and \(t_2(S)=0\). If, in addition, \(q(S)=0\), then \[ h(S)_{\mathbb Q}\simeq \mathbf 1\oplus\mathbb L^{\oplus b_2(S)}\oplus\mathbb L^2. \] In this case, for every \(m,n\ge0\), the rational Chow motives of the power \(S^m\) and the Hilbert scheme \(S^{[n]}\) of length-\(n\) zero-dimensional subschemes are pure Tate, with \(S^0=S^{[0]}=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C\). For \(Y=S^m\) or \(Y=S^{[n]}\), the rational cycle-class maps satisfy \[ \operatorname{cl}_Y^i:\CH^i(Y)_{\mathbb Q} \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}H^{2i}(Y,\mathbb Q), \qquad H^{2i+1}(Y,\mathbb Q)=0 \quad\text{for every }i\ge0. \]
