FAMILY 330: A uniformly discrete counterexample to bounded approximation in Lipschitz-free spaces
SUBJECT: Functional analysis
OPENAI SUMMARY: Constructs a countable uniformly discrete metric space whose real Lipschitz-free Banach space has the approximation property but not the bounded approximation property, answering Kalton's question negatively. Finite-rank operators approximate the identity on every compact set, but their norms cannot share a finite bound.

=== MANUSCRIPT: A uniformly discrete counterexample to bounded approximation in Lipschitz-free spaces (September-26-2026) [preprints/Failure-of-Bounded-Approximation-in-a-Lipschitz-Free-Space-over-a-Uniformly-Discrete-Metric-Space-September-26-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We construct a countable uniformly discrete metric space whose real Lipschitz-free space has the approximation property but fails the bounded approximation property, answering Kalton's question negatively. The identity can be approximated on every compact set by finite-rank operators, but no uniform bound on their norms is possible.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Uniform discreteness keeps distinct points of a metric space a fixed positive distance apart. For its Lipschitz-free space, this separation guarantees the approximation property: the identity can be approximated on each compact set by finite-rank operators. Kalton asked whether the approximating operators can always be chosen with one bound on their norms \cite[Proposition~4.4 and following remarks, p.~185]{Kalton04}. The question was restated by Godefroy and Ozawa \cite[Question~1]{GodefroyOzawa14} and in Godefroy's survey \cite[Problem~6.2]{Godefroy15}. We construct a countable uniformly discrete space for which no such bound exists. We work over the real scalars. For a pointed metric space $(M,d,o)$, let $\Lip_0(M)$ be the Banach space of real Lipschitz functions vanishing at $o$, with the Lipschitz constant as norm. Its \emph{Lipschitz-free space} is \[ \F(M)=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\delta_x:x\in M\} \subseteq\Lip_0(M)^*,\qquad \delta_x(f)=f(x). \] Thus $\delta_o=0$ and $\norm{\delta_x-\delta_y}=d(x,y)$. The latter identity follows from the Lipschitz inequality and the test function $z\mapsto d(z,y)-d(o,y)$. The construction originates in the molecular spaces of Arens and Eells \cite[\S2]{ArensEells56}; its linearization properties and complemented free subspaces are central to the work of Godefroy and Kalton \cite[\S2]{GodefroyKalton03}. A Banach space $X$ has the \emph{approximation property} (AP) if for every norm-compact set $C\subseteq X$ and every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a bounded finite-rank linear operator $T:X\to X$ with $\sup_{u\in C}\norm{Tu-u}<\varepsilon$. For $\lambda\ge1$, it has $\lambda$-BAP if these operators can always be chosen with $\norm{T}\le\lambda$. The bounded approximation property (BAP) means $\lambda$-BAP for some finite $\lambda$, and the metric approximation property (MAP) means $1$-BAP. In AP the norm may depend on $C$ and $\varepsilon$; in BAP one bound must work for all of them. A metric space is \emph{proper} if its closed bounded balls are compact. [statement] The distinction between discrete and uniformly discrete matters here. Smith gives countable complete discrete spaces whose free spaces have AP and fail BAP, without asserting a common positive lower bound on all nonzero distances \cite[Theorem~1.3 and the surrounding discussion]{Smith26}. \Cref{thm:main} supplies that lower bound. The example is necessarily outside the countable proper case: Dalet proved MAP for free spaces over countable proper metric spaces \cite[Theorem~2.6]{Dalet15}. Our example contains an infinite separated subset in a bounded ball. Uniform discreteness also imposes strong sequential structure: C\'uth and Kalenda prove a quantitative strengthening of the Schur property for these free spaces \cite[Theorem~1.1]{CuthKalenda26}, so weakly convergent sequences converge in norm. Their April 2026 introduction still records the BAP question addressed here. The same example has a nonlinear consequence. Kalton asked whether every separable Banach space is \emph{approximable} \cite[Problem~1, p.~1260]{Kalton12}. For such a space $X$, this means that there is an equi-uniformly continuous sequence of maps $\psi_n:X\to X$, each with relatively compact range, such that $\psi_n(x)\to x$ for every $x\in X$. Equi-uniform continuity means that the choice of $\delta$ for a given $\varepsilon$ in the uniform-continuity condition works for every $\psi_n$. The space $X=\F(M)$ in \Cref{thm:main} is not approximable, although it has AP. \Cref{sec:nonlinear} explains the short implication from Kalton's net theorem that gives this consequence. \subsection{A finite obstruction in each bounded block} The main construction concerns a prescribed operator norm bound $p$. It produces a bounded metric space and a finite set of points, called anchors, at which every finite-rank operator of norm at most $p$ makes a definite error. [theorem] \label{thm:main} There is a countable, unbounded, nonproper pointed metric space $(M,d,o)$ such that $d(x,y)\ge1$ for distinct points and $\F(M)$ has AP but fails $\lambda$-BAP for every finite $\lambda\ge1$.
[theorem] \label{thm:blocks} For each integer $p\ge1$, there are a bounded countably infinite pointed metric space $(M_p,d_p,o)$ and a finite set $A_p\subseteq M_p$ containing $o$ such that \[ d_p(x,y)\ge1\quad(x\ne y),\qquad \operatorname{diam}M_p\le B_p:=300p^2+150p+5, \] and every bounded finite-rank linear operator $T_0:\F(M_p)\to\F(M_p)$ with $\norm{T_0}\le p$ satisfies \[ \max_{a\in A_p}\norm{T_0\delta_a-\delta_a}\ge\frac12. \]
[corollary] \label{cor:renorming} For each integer $p\ge1$, real $\ell_1$ has an equivalent norm $\norm{\cdot}_p$ such that \[ \tfrac12\norm{c}_1\le\norm{c}_p\le B_p\norm{c}_1, \] and $(\ell_1,\norm{\cdot}_p)$ has $2B_p$-BAP but fails $p$-BAP. For $p=1$, this norm fails MAP.

def lipSubmodule (o : M) : Submodule ℝ (M → ℝ) where
  carrier := {f | f o = 0 ∧ ∃ C : ℝ, 0 ≤ C ∧
    ∀ x y, |f x - f y| ≤ C * dist x y}
  zero_mem' := by
    refine ⟨rfl, 0, le_rfl, ?_⟩
    intro x y
    simp
  add_mem' := by
    rintro f g ⟨hfo, C, hC, hf⟩ ⟨hgo, D, hD, hg⟩
    refine ⟨by simpa using congrArg₂ (· + ·) hfo hgo,
      C + D, add_nonneg hC hD, ?_⟩
    intro x y
    change |f x + g x - (f y + g y)| ≤ (C + D) * dist x y
    calc
      _ = |(f x - f y) + (g x - g y)| := by ring_nf
      _ ≤ |f x - f y| + |g x - g y| := abs_add_le _ _
      _ ≤ C * dist x y + D * dist x y := add_le_add (hf x y) (hg x y)
      _ = _ := by ring
  smul_mem' := by
    rintro c f ⟨hfo, C, hC, hf⟩
    refine ⟨by simp [hfo], |c| * C, mul_nonneg (abs_nonneg c) hC, ?_⟩
    intro x y
    change |c * f x - c * f y| ≤ (|c| * C) * dist x y
    calc
      _ = |c| * |f x - f y| := by rw [← mul_sub, abs_mul]
      _ ≤ |c| * (C * dist x y) := mul_le_mul_of_nonneg_left (hf x y) (abs_nonneg c)
      _ = _ := by ring
