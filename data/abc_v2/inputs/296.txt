FAMILY 296: The generator problem for finite factors
SUBJECT: Operator algebras
OPENAI SUMMARY: Proves that every type II1 factor with separable predual is generated by a single operator, equivalently by two self-adjoint operators, resolving the generator problem. More strongly, for every irreducible inclusion $`P\subset M`$ of such factors, the unitaries u with $`M=W^*(P,u)`$ form a dense Gδ subset in the trace 2-norm topology.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Relative generation and the generator problem for finite factors (September-23-2026) [preprints/Relative-generation-and-the-generator-problem-for-finite-factors-September-23-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove that every type II1 factor with separable predual is generated by one operator, equivalently by two self-adjoint operators. Combined with the established direct-integral reduction to this case, this gives an affirmative solution of the generator problem for von Neumann algebras with separable predual.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} For a subset $S$ of a von Neumann algebra $M$, write $W^*(S)$ for the smallest unital von Neumann subalgebra containing $S$. Thus adjoints are included and generation is taken in the weak operator topology. The generator problem asks whether every von Neumann algebra with separable predual has the form $W^*(x)$ for one bounded operator $x$. Equivalently, it asks for two self-adjoint generators: their sum $a+ib$ recovers $a$ and $b$ as its real and imaginary parts. We prove this assertion for type $\mathrm{II}_1$ factors by first establishing a relative generation theorem. For a finite factor with normalized trace $\tau$, put $\norm{x}_2=\tau(x^*x)^{1/2}$. An inclusion $P\subset M$ is \emph{irreducible} when $P'\cap M=\mathbb C1$. Write $\mathcal U(M)$ for the unitary group of $M$. [statement] [statement] Throughout the paper, \emph{separable} for a finite von Neumann algebra means that its predual is separable. This ensures separability of $L^2(M,\tau)$; it does not require operator-norm separability of $M$. \paragraph{Earlier work.} The type~I, hyperfinite, and properly infinite cases of the generator problem were established by Pearcy, Suzuki and Sait\^o, and Wogen, respectively~\cite{Pearcy1962,SuzukiSaito1963,Wogen1969}; see also the historical discussion in~\cite[Section~3]{Sherman2012}. Willig's direct-integral theorem reduces the general question to type~$\mathrm{II}_1$ factors~\cite[Corollary~2]{Willig1974}. Consequently, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} also gives single generation of every von Neumann algebra with separable predual. This last consequence uses the established direct-integral reduction, whereas our proof below concerns finite factors. Much of the progress in the finite case has used additional structure. Behncke established single generation for tensor products of two $\mathrm{II}_1$ factors~\cite{Behncke1972}. Popa treated factors with Cartan subalgebras~\cite{Popa1985Cartan}; Ge and Popa treated factors with property~$\Gamma$ and recovered the tensor-product case~\cite{GePopa1998}; and Ge and Shen obtained arithmetic group examples with a small-support refinement~\cite{GeShen2002}. Shen's generator invariant unified these and other positive classes~\cite{Shen2009}. Dykema, Sinclair, Smith, and White established its exact amplification formula and sharpened its connection with the number of self-adjoint generators~\cite{DSSW2008}. Sherman showed, in particular, that finite generation of every countably generated $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor would suffice for a positive answer~\cite[Theorem~3.8(3)]{Sherman2012}. More recently, Gao, Kunnawalkam Elayavalli, Patchell, and Tan proved single generation when a countable unitary generating family lies in one sequential-commutation orbit in an ultrapower: successive members can be joined there by finite chains with Haar intermediate unitaries and commuting adjacent terms~\cite[Theorem~A]{GaoEtAl2025}. Popa's work on tight decompositions studies stable single generation, which requires every amplification of a factor to be singly generated~\cite{Popa2021Tight}. Requiring generators to vary continuously with a family of factors leads to a different problem. Hayes constructed a $W^*$-bundle---a continuous family of tracial von Neumann algebras---over a compact metrizable base, with separable-predual $\mathrm{II}_1$ fibers, for which no finite family of continuous sections generates every fiber~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{Hayes2026Bundles}. Relative generation by one unitary is already available for finite-index inclusions, by the downward basic construction used in \cite[Lemma~6.2]{DSSW2008}: the associated Jones projection $e$ generates the extension over the subfactor, and $1-2e$ is unitary. Theorem~\ref{thm:relative-generation} applies to every irreducible inclusion and also establishes genericity of the relative generators. [theorem] \label{thm:relative-generation} Let $P\subset M$ be an irreducible inclusion of type $\mathrm{II}_1$ factors, with $M$ of separable predual. The set \[ \{u\in\mathcal U(M):W^*(P,u)=M\} \] is a dense $G_\delta$ subset of the unitary group $\mathcal U(M)$ for the trace $2$-norm topology.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} Every type $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor $M$ with separable predual is generated by two self-adjoint elements. Equivalently, there is an $x\in M$ such that $M=W^*(x)$.
