ABSTRACT: We prove the modularity conjecture for elliptic curves over imaginary quadratic fields: every such curve is modular, with matching local parameters at every place.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Let $K$ be an imaginary quadratic number field and let $E/K$ be an elliptic curve. The automorphy conjecture for $E$ asks for an automorphic representation of $\GL_2(\A_K)$ whose local parameters realize the cohomology of $E$. At a place of good reduction this requires the Hecke polynomial to recover the point count of the reduced curve; at a bad place it requires the full local parameter, including its inertia and monodromy. At infinity the parameter is prescribed by the Hodge types $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$. We prove this conjecture for every $K$ and $E$. We use unitary normalization on the automorphic side and the Tate-normalized local Langlands correspondence on the cohomological side. These conventions are recalled in Section~\ref{sec:conventions}. [statement] Here $r_\iota$ in the CM isobaric case is the sum of the corresponding character realizations with the same Tate normalization. \subsection{Historical context} The arithmetic groups associated with imaginary quadratic fields and their action on hyperbolic three-space originate in Bianchi's work~\cite{Bianchi}. Their presentations and cohomology were developed further by Swan and Harder~\cite{Swan,Harder}. The expected relationship with elliptic curves belongs to the Langlands program: in \cite[Section~7]{LanglandsProblems}, Langlands explicitly discusses the automorphy of local representations attached to elliptic curves over global fields of characteristic zero. Grunewald--Helling--Mennicke, Cremona, and Cremona--Whitley developed substantial computations relating elliptic curves and automorphic forms over imaginary quadratic fields~\cite{GHM,Cremona,CremonaWhitley}. Over $\Q$, Wiles and Taylor--Wiles proved modularity of semistable elliptic curves~\cite{Wiles,TaylorWiles}, and Breuil--Conrad--Diamond--Taylor proved modularity of every elliptic curve~\cite{BCDT}. A central method is to lift automorphy from a residual Galois representation. Wiles's $3$--$5$ switch also shows how an auxiliary elliptic curve, chosen with prescribed torsion and local behavior, can replace a residual representation that does not satisfy the initial lifting hypotheses. For imaginary quadratic fields, the relevant cohomology occurs in more than one degree. The construction of Galois representations and the lifting problem both require additional work. Harris--Soudry--Taylor and Taylor constructed representations attached to Bianchi forms in important cases~\cite{HST,Taylor1994}. For conjugation-invariant central character, Berger--Harcos obtained Frobenius compatibility away from finitely many primes~\cite{BergerHarcos}. Harris--Lan--Taylor--Thorne constructed Galois representations for general regular algebraic cuspidal representations over CM fields~\cite{HLTT}, and Scholze treated torsion cohomology as well~\cite{Scholze}. Calegari--Geraghty developed patching for cohomology in several degrees~\cite{CalegariGeraghty}. The potential automorphy theorem of Allen--Calegari--Caraiani--Gee--Helm--Le Hung--Newton--Scholze--Taylor--Thorne implies potential modularity of every elliptic curve over a CM field~\cite[Theorem~1.0.1]{ACC}. Boxer--Calegari--Gee--Pilloni also proved potential modularity for genus-one curves over quadratic extensions of totally real fields~\cite[Theorem~1.1.4]{BCGP}. Potential modularity permits an extension of the ground field; automorphy over the original field is a further problem. Allen--Khare--Thorne proved residual modularity results in small characteristics and deduced modularity for a positive proportion of elliptic curves over each CM field not containing a primitive fifth root of unity~\cite{AKT}. Caraiani--Newton proved a lifting theorem allowing small coefficient primes and ramified CM fields, together with the local-global compatibility needed for its applications~\cite{CN}. [theorem] \label{thm:main} Let $K$ be an imaginary quadratic number field and $E/K$ an elliptic curve. There is an isobaric automorphic representation $\pi_E$ of $\GL_2(\A_K)$ with trivial central character and weight zero such that, for every prime $l$ and every isomorphism $\iota:\overline\Q_l\xrightarrow{\sim}\C$, \[ r_\iota(\pi_E)\simeq H^1_{\et}(E_{\overline K},\overline\Q_l). \] At every finite place $v\nmid l$ one has \[ \iota\WD\bigl(H^1_{\et}(E_{\overline K},\overline\Q_l) |_{G_{K_v}}\bigr)^{\Fss} \simeq \rec_{K_v}\bigl(\pi_{E,v}\otimes|\det|_v^{-1/2}\bigr). \] The unitary parameter at the complex place is $z\mapsto\diag(z/|z|,\overline z/|z|)$. In particular every local factor agrees, and \[ L(E/K,s)=L(\pi_E,s-1/2). \] If $E$ has no complex multiplication, $\pi_E$ is cuspidal. In the CM case it is given by automorphic induction of a Hecke character, or by an isobaric sum when the CM field is already $K$.
