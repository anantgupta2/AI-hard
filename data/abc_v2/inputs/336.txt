ABSTRACT: For every n ≥ 4, a complete connected smooth Riemannian n-manifold without boundary satisfying $`-4\Delta+\mathop{\mathrm{Scal}}\nolimits \ge1`$ as a quadratic-form inequality admits a continuous map to a simplicial complex of dimension at most $`n-2`$ whose entire fibers have diameter bounded only in terms of n. The bound is measured in the original metric. This extends the uniform Urysohn width theorem from a pointwise scalar-curvature lower bound to a spectral lower bound.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:intro} Positive scalar curvature is expected to force a manifold to lose two dimensions at a controlled geometric scale. A spectral lower bound mixes scalar curvature with the energy of a test function and permits negative scalar curvature on parts of the manifold. We show that the uniform width conclusion survives this weakening. The geometric input is the stabilized width theorem of \cite{OpenAIWidth}, stated precisely in Theorem~\ref{interface:width}; the work here constructs its hypotheses from the spectral inequality. Let $(M^n,g)$ be a connected complete smooth Riemannian manifold without boundary. We use $\Delta_g=\diver_g\nabla_g$ and integrate against the Riemannian volume density. The \emph{normalized spectral scalar-curvature bound} is \begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral-bound} \int_M\bigl(4|\nabla_g\phi|_g^2+\Scal_g\phi^2\bigr)\,\dd\vol_g \ge \int_M\phi^2\,\dd\vol_g \qquad\bigl(\phi\in C_c^\infty(M;\R)\bigr). \end{equation} Thus $-4\Delta_g+\Scal_g\ge1$ in the quadratic-form sense. This is the definition on noncompact manifolds as well; it does not require a lowest eigenvalue to be attained. For a metric space $X$ and an integer $d\ge0$, its \emph{Urysohn $d$-width} is \[ \UW_d(X)=\inf_{f:X\to K}\ \sup_{y\in K}\diam_X f^{-1}(y), \] where $f$ is continuous and $K$ is a simplicial complex of dimension at most $d$ with its usual polyhedral topology. The diameter refers to the entire fiber, including all its components; empty fibers may be omitted. [statement] The constant depends only on the dimension. The theorem applies to compact and noncompact manifolds, without orientability, spin, or bounded-geometry assumptions. If the right side of \eqref{eq:spectral-bound} is multiplied by $\lambda>0$, scaling the metric to $\lambda g$ gives the fiber bound $C_n/\sqrt\lambda$. \subsection{History and relation to earlier work} Gromov's macroscopic dimension program relates positive scalar curvature to the dimension of a space seen at large scales. The quantitative formulation asks whether a fixed positive scalar-curvature normalization forces a dimension-dependent bound for the fibers of a map to an $(n-2)$-dimensional polyhedron. This formulation goes back to \cite[\S\,2.A(c)]{Gromov1986}; see also \cite[\S\,$2\frac12$]{Gromov1996} and \cite{Gromov2023}. Uniform width is stronger than merely asking for a finite fiber bound for each individual metric. The stabilized partition theorem used here is the geometric input from \cite{OpenAIWidth} that gives the uniform conclusion. Several earlier results illustrate the distinction between the geometric and topological versions of the problem. Early three-dimensional distance-level diameter estimates of Gromov--Lawson~\cite{GromovLawson1983} use vanishing rational first homology, as clarified in Gromov's later account~\cite{Gromov2023}. That account also gives a uniform Urysohn $1$-width bound for arbitrary complete three-manifolds with scalar curvature bounded below by a positive constant. Under such a uniform lower bound, Liokumovich--Maximo~\cite{LiokumovichMaximo2023} and Liokumovich--Wang~\cite{LiokumovichWang2026} construct Morse functions with simultaneous area and diameter bounds on connected level components, in the closed and complete noncompact cases, respectively. In dimensions four and five, Chodosh--Li--Liokumovich proved finite Urysohn $1$-width for universal covers of closed positive-scalar-curvature manifolds under the respective assumptions $\pi_2=0$ and $\pi_2=\pi_3=0$ \cite[Theorem~4]{ChodoshLiLiokumovich2023}. That conclusion is qualitative and uses the topology of a compact quotient. The setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} instead allows arbitrary complete manifolds and asks for one constant depending only on the dimension. The coefficient $4$ in \eqref{eq:spectral-bound} is natural in weighted scalar curvature. [theorem] \label{thm:main} For every integer $n\ge4$ there is a finite constant $C_n$ with the following property. Every connected complete smooth Riemannian $n$-manifold $(M,g)$ without boundary satisfying \eqref{eq:spectral-bound} admits a continuous map $f:M\to K$ to a simplicial complex of dimension at most $n-2$ such that \[ \diam_g f^{-1}(y)\le C_n\qquad(y\in K). \] In particular, $\UW_{n-2}(M,g)\le C_n$.

INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} A positive scalar-curvature lower bound need not bound the diameter of a three-manifold: the product of a round two-sphere with a line is a basic example. A more suitable measure of size allows the manifold to extend along a graph while requiring the inverse image of every point of the graph to remain small. For a metric space $(X,d)$, its \emph{Urysohn $1$-width} is \[ \operatorname{UW}_1(X,d) =\inf_{K,f}\ \sup_{y\in K}\diam_d f^{-1}(y), \] where $K$ ranges over simplicial complexes of dimension at most one, with their usual polyhedral topology, and $f:X\to K$ ranges over continuous maps. We give the empty fiber diameter zero. The diameter here is that of the entire fiber, including distances between its different connected components. We use the sign convention $\Delta=\Div\nabla$ for the Laplacian. The following theorem gives a width bound under a spectral, rather than a pointwise, scalar-curvature hypothesis. [statement] The notation $-4\Delta_g+\Scal_g\ge\lambda$ will always mean \eqref{eq:main-spectral}. In particular, a pointwise bound $\Scal_g\ge\lambda$ implies the hypothesis, but is not required. The theorem concerns the given metric $g$; passing to a conformally related metric of positive scalar curvature would not by itself give the required distance estimate. The constant $500$ is convenient and is not optimized. \subsection{Background and scope} Stable minimal surfaces provide a classical link between scalar curvature and the geometry of three-manifolds. Schoen and Yau used the stability inequality and the Gauss equation to obtain curvature and topological restrictions on such surfaces \cite{SchoenYau1979}. Fischer-Colbrie and Schoen developed the associated positive-solution method for Schr\"odinger operators \cite{FischerColbrieSchoen1980}. The stable-surface radius estimate of Schoen and Yau \cite{SchoenYau1983} and the distance-level estimates of Gromov and Lawson \cite[Section~10]{GromovLawson1983} show how local surface stability can control the large-scale geometry of the ambient three-manifold. The latter distance-level argument uses a topological condition on the first homology; it is not a diameter bound for an arbitrary distance sphere in an arbitrary three-manifold. Gromov's torus-stabilized scalar-curvature framework gives the closest precedent for the spectral formulation here. Torus stabilization means considering metrics on $M\times\mathbb T^N$ of the form $g+\sum_{i=1}^N\varphi_i^2\,d\theta_i^2$, with smooth positive functions $\varphi_i$ on $M$, while measuring width in the base metric $g$. In his \emph{Four lectures on scalar curvature}, Gromov gives a three-dimensional graph-width theorem in this framework \cite[Section~3.10.1, Theorem~(G)]{GromovFourLectures2023}. That statement is formulated for orientable complete three-manifolds, with a mean-convex boundary allowed in the torus extension. The relation between torus stabilization and the coefficient $4$ in $-4\Delta+\Scal$ is made explicit in \cite[Sections~1.A--1.B]{GromovStabilized2024}. Thus the spectral viewpoint and the scalar-curvature-to-width principle are established parts of this theory. Our purpose is to give a direct weighted proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, including its nonorientable and noncompact cases, without a global bound on the auxiliary weight or its derivatives. The prescribed-mean-curvature method used below also belongs to this development. Gromov's $\mu$-bubbles minimize area with an added volume term, whose density prescribes the mean curvature. Chodosh and Li developed weighted versions and a slice-and-dice construction that first simplifies the topology and then produces pieces of bounded diameter \cite[Sections~3--6]{ChodoshLi2024}. The two-dimensional differential inequality underlying our closed-surface diameter estimate is equivalent to theirs \cite[Lemma~16]{ChodoshLi2024}. [theorem] \label{thm:main} Let $(M^3,g)$ be a connected complete smooth Riemannian three-manifold without boundary. Suppose that $\lambda>0$ and \begin{equation}\label{eq:main-spectral} \int_M\bigl(4|\nabla_g\phi|_g^2+\Scal_g\phi^2\bigr)\,d\vol_g \ge \lambda\int_M\phi^2\,d\vol_g \qquad\bigl(\phi\in C_c^\infty(M;\R)\bigr). \end{equation} Then there exist a simplicial complex $K$ of dimension at most one and a continuous map $f:M\to K$ such that \[ \diam_g f^{-1}(y)\le \frac{500}{\sqrt\lambda} \qquad(y\in K). \] The complex has its usual polyhedral topology. No orientability, spin, compactness, or bounded-geometry assumption is imposed on $M$.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Positive scalar curvature and uniform codimension-two width (September-23-2026) [preprints/Positive-scalar-curvature-and-uniform-codimension-two-width-September-23-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove the quantitative continuous form of Gromov's scalar-curvature conjecture in every dimension n ≥ 4. Every complete connected smooth boundaryless n-manifold with scalar curvature at least one admits a continuous map to a simplicial complex of dimension at most $`n-2`$ whose entire fibers have diameter bounded only in terms of n. We also obtain the continuous macroscopic-dimension conclusion for universal covers of closed manifolds with positive scalar curvature in every dimension n ≥ 2.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} A lower bound for scalar curvature is a local condition. Gromov's macroscopic dimension conjecture predicts a global consequence: at a controlled scale, two dimensions can be collapsed. We prove the quantitative form of this assertion for arbitrary complete manifolds in dimensions at least four. Throughout, scalar curvature means the ordinary pointwise Riemannian invariant. Fixed-scale macroscopic scalar curvature uses ball-volume comparison at a prescribed positive radius \citep[Definitions~1.2--1.3]{kumarSen2026Width}. For a metric space \(X\) and an integer \(d\ge0\), define its \emph{Urysohn \(d\)-width} by \[ \UW_d(X)=\inf_f\ \sup_{y\in K}\diam_X f^{-1}(y), \] where the infimum runs over continuous maps \(f:X\to K\) to simplicial complexes of dimension at most \(d\). The complexes have their usual polyhedral topology. The diameter is that of the entire fiber, including all its components; empty fibers may be omitted. [statement] The constant is independent of \(M\), its topology, and its geometry. The theorem applies to compact and noncompact manifolds, with no orientability, spin, covering, or bounded geometry assumption. If \(\Scal_g\ge\sigma^2>0\), applying the theorem to \(\sigma^2g\) gives \(\UW_{n-2}(M,g)\le C_n/\sigma\). For a closed connected Riemannian manifold, let \(\operatorname{FillRad}(M,g)\) be Gromov's filling radius \citep{gromov1983Filling}, defined by the Kuratowski embedding and the fundamental class, with coefficients in \(\Z\) if \(M\) is orientable and in \(\Z_2\) otherwise \citep[Definition~3.1]{nabutovskyRotmanSabourau2021}. The next corollary gives the closed-manifold case in dimensions at least four of Gromov's filling-radius Conjecture~\(A^{-}\) \citep[\S3]{gromov2017Dozen}. [statement] \begin{proof} Gromov's comparison, in the form recorded by Nabutovsky, Rotman, and Sabourau \citep[Definition~6.1 and equation~(6.1)]{nabutovskyRotmanSabourau2021}, uses the same continuous-map and entire-fiber width convention as above. A target of dimension at most \(n-2\) is also admissible for \(\UW_{n-1}\). Since \(M\) is closed, the comparison and the scaled form of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} give \[ \operatorname{FillRad}(M,g) \le \tfrac12\UW_{n-1}(M,g) \le \tfrac12\UW_{n-2}(M,g) \le \frac{C_n}{2\sigma}. \] \end{proof} For the closed-cover consequence we also use the \emph{continuous macroscopic dimension}: $\dim_{\mathrm{mc}}X\le d$ means that there is a continuous map to a simplicial complex of dimension at most $d$, with its uniform simplex metric and a finite uniform bound on the diameters of its entire fibers. Each simplex has the same standard Euclidean shape and edge length. The deck group acts isometrically and cocompactly. The scaled form of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives a continuous map from \(X\) to an \((n-2)\)-complex with uniformly bounded entire fibers. [theorem] \label{thm:main} For every integer \(n\ge4\), there is a finite constant \(C_n\) such that every connected complete smooth Riemannian \(n\)-manifold \((M,g)\), without boundary and with \(\Scal_g\ge1\), admits a continuous map \(f:M\to K\) to a simplicial complex of dimension at most \(n-2\) satisfying \[ \diam_g f^{-1}(y)\le C_n\qquad(y\in K). \] In particular, \(\UW_{n-2}(M,g)\le C_n\).
[corollary] [Closed-manifold filling-radius bound] \label{cor:filling-radius} Let \((M,g)\) be a closed connected smooth Riemannian \(n\)-manifold with \(n\ge4\) and \(\Scal_g\ge\sigma^2\) for some \(\sigma>0\). Then \[ \operatorname{FillRad}(M,g)\le \frac{C_n}{2\sigma}, \] where \(C_n\) is the constant in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
[corollary] [Continuous macroscopic dimension of universal covers] \label{cover:main} Let $(M,h)$ be a closed connected smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension $n\ge2$ with positive scalar curvature. Its universal cover, with the lifted original metric $\widetilde h$, satisfies \[ \dim_{\mathrm{mc}}(\widetilde M,\widetilde h)\le n-2. \]
[corollary] [Aspherical obstruction]\label{cor:aspherical} No closed smooth aspherical manifold of dimension \(n\ge4\) admits a Riemannian metric of strictly positive scalar curvature.
