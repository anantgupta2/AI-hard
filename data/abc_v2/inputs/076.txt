FAMILY 076: Real ultraflat Littlewood polynomials and unbounded binary merit factors
SUBJECT: Real and complex analysis
OPENAI SUMMARY: Constructs polynomials with N consecutive coefficients in $`\{-1,1\}`$ whose modulus is $`(1+o(1))\sqrt N`$ uniformly on the entire unit circle, for every sufficiently large integer length N. Thus real Littlewood polynomials are ultraflat, including at the real endpoints. Their binary merit factors tend to infinity, disproving Turyn's bounded-merit-factor conjecture.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Ultraflat real Littlewood polynomials (October-5-2026) [preprints/Ultraflat-real-Littlewood-polynomials-October-5-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: For every $`\varepsilon\in(0,1)`$ and every sufficiently large integer N, there is a polynomial of length N with coefficients in $`\{-1,1\}`$ whose modulus lies between $`(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt N`$ and $`(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt N`$ everywhere on the unit circle. Thus real Littlewood polynomials can be ultraflat through every sufficiently large integer length. The signs may be chosen separately at each length.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} A real Littlewood polynomial of length $N$ is a polynomial \[ P(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\varepsilon_k z^k, \qquad \varepsilon_k\in\{-1,1\}. \] Its squared mean modulus on the unit circle is $N$, by Parseval's identity. Thus $\sqrt N$ is the natural scale, and its maximum modulus is at least $\sqrt N$. A family is \emph{ultraflat} if $|P(z)|/\sqrt N$ tends uniformly to $1$ on the unit circle as the length tends to infinity. We prove that real Littlewood polynomials can be ultraflat, through every sufficiently large integer length. [statement] The signs may depend on $N$. The theorem concerns both extrema of the same polynomial on the entire circle, including the real points $z=1$ and $z=-1$. \subsection{The flatness problem} Erd\H{o}s asked whether real-sign polynomials could stay between two fixed positive multiples of their square-root scale on the entire circle \cite[Problem~26]{Erdos1957}; the problem also appears in Littlewood's work \cite{Littlewood1966}. The Rudin--Shapiro construction gives real-sign polynomials with maximum at most $\sqrt{2N}$ at dyadic lengths \cite{Rudin1959}. Balister, Bollob\'as, Morris, Sahasrabudhe and Tiba proved the two-sided constant-factor statement at every degree at least two \cite[Theorem~1.1]{BalisterEtAl2020}. Their theorem controls the scale of both extrema, while ultraflatness requires both normalized extrema to converge to one. For complex coefficients of modulus one, Kahane constructed ultraflat polynomials \cite{Kahane1980}. Bombieri and Bourgain improved the quantitative bounds and gave effective constructions \cite[Theorems~4 and~7]{BombieriBourgain2009}. The restriction to real signs is substantial. The predecessor \cite[Theorem~1.1]{OpenAIMinimal} obtains an asymptotically minimal maximum through all lengths. Its version-2 refinement \cite[Theorem~1.1]{OpenAILowerEnvelope2026} gives the simultaneous bounds \[ \frac1{16}\sqrt N\le |P(z)|\le(1+\eta)\sqrt N \qquad(|z|=1) \] for every fixed $\eta>0$ and all sufficiently large $N$. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} strengthens that lower bound to its asymptotically optimal value. The present paper develops the additional constructions needed for this strengthening and uses the retained version-2 paper as the source of two shared lemmas. Recent obstructions distinguish the relevant quantifiers. Erd\'elyi proved that the full sequence of partial sums of one fixed unimodular power series cannot be ultraflat \cite[Theorem~2.1]{ErdelyiSections2026}. This does not restrict the independent choice of signs at each length in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. His separate lower bound \cite[Theorem~2.1]{Erdelyi2026}, $\max_{|z|=1}|P(z)|^2\ge N+(N-1)^{1/3}/38$, is compatible with the asymptotic conclusion here.\footnote{Contrary nonflatness claims in \cite{ElAbdalaoui2025,ElAbdalaoui2025Generalization} and specific issues in their arguments are discussed in \cite[Appendix~A]{OpenAIMinimal}. Those claims are not inputs to the present proof.} \subsection{Construction and relation to the earlier version} The proof first constructs a continuous function $B_N$ on the circle whose modulus is close to one. Its Fourier coefficients of indices $0,\ldots,N-1$ are real and at most $(1+o(1))/\sqrt N$ in absolute value, and the sum of the absolute values of all remaining coefficients tends to zero. Parseval's identity then forces the retained coefficients to be almost of sign size in aggregate. A discrepancy estimate rounds them to signs with a small uniform error. The two-sided flatness question appears in Erd\H{o}s's problems \cite[Problem 26]{Erdos1957} and in Littlewood's work \cite{Littlewood1966}. The Rudin--Shapiro construction of real-sign polynomials gives an upper bound $\sqrt{2N}$ at dyadic lengths \cite{Rudin1959}. The real-sign questions and their relation to the Parseval lower bound are discussed in \cite[Problems 4.13 and 4.31]{HaymanLingham2018}. === MANUSCRIPT: Asymptotically minimal maxima of real Littlewood polynomials (September-23-2026) [preprints/Asymptotically-minimal-maxima-of-real-Littlewood-polynomials-September-23-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove that the minimum possible maximum modulus on the unit circle of a polynomial with N consecutive real coefficients in $`\{-1,1\}`$ is $`(1+o(1))\sqrt N`$, as N tends to infinity through all integers. This disproves the real-sign analogue of Erdős's fixed relative-gap conjecture. As a consequence, the largest binary merit factor at length N tends to infinity through all integer lengths, disproving Turyn's conjecture.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Write $\T=\R/\Z$ and $\e(t)=\exp(2\pi i t)$. All tori carry probability Haar measure. A Littlewood polynomial of length $N$ is a polynomial \[ P(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\varepsilon_k z^k, \qquad \varepsilon_k\in\{-1,1\}. \] Its maximum modulus is $\norm{P}_\infty=\max_{t\in\T}|P(\e(t))|$. Define \[ m_N=\min_{\varepsilon_0,\ldots,\varepsilon_{N-1}\in\{-1,1\}} \frac{1}{\sqrt N}\norm{\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\varepsilon_k z^k}_\infty. \] Orthogonality gives $\norm{P}_2^2=N$, so $m_N\ge1$. The real-sign analogue of Erd\H{o}s's fixed-gap question asks whether $m_N$ is eventually bounded below by $1+c$ for some absolute $c>0$. Erd\H{o}s's original question allowed complex unimodular coefficients and was refuted in that class by Kahane; the restriction to real signs is the question addressed here \cite[Problem~22]{Erdos1957}; see also \cite{Kahane1980} and \cite[Problems~4.13 and~4.31]{HaymanLingham2018}. [statement] The theorem concerns every sufficiently large length, rather than a subsequence. It gives no uniform lower bound for $|P(z)|$ and hence does not assert two-sided uniform ultraflatness. The choices in the proof are existential; no useful convergence rate or efficient signing algorithm is asserted. \subsection{Context and comparison} The Shapiro--Rudin construction, obtained earlier by Shapiro and independently by Rudin, gives real-sign polynomials with maximum $O(\sqrt N)$; at dyadic lengths its paired identity gives an upper bound $\sqrt{2N}$ \cite[Theorem~I and its proof]{Rudin1959}. Balister proved the all-length bound $\sqrt{6N-2}-1$ for its initial segments \cite[Theorem 1]{Balister2019}. A different problem asks for upper and lower bounds by fixed positive multiples of $\sqrt N$ throughout the circle. Such two-sided flat Littlewood polynomials were constructed by Balister, Bollob\'as, Morris, Sahasrabudhe and Tiba, whose proof combines the Shapiro--Rudin construction with discrepancy theory \cite{BalisterEtAl2020}. These results control the scale of the maximum, but do not make the upper constant tend to one. The coefficient class matters. Complex coefficients of modulus one allow ultraflat constructions. Littlewood used quadratic phases near this Parseval scale, Kahane obtained ultraflatness, and Bombieri and Bourgain gave quantitative constructions \cite{Littlewood1966,Kahane1980,BombieriBourgain2009}. The smoothed quadratic phases and Poisson summation used in \cite[Sections~2 and~7]{BombieriBourgain2009} are analytic predecessors of the sampling argument below. Their bounded-to-unimodular correction also develops a method of K\"orner \cite[Section~8]{BombieriBourgain2009}. The present proof must instead preserve real coefficients and round them to signs. The distinction between the unimodular and real-sign questions is also explicit in \cite[Problem 4.31]{HaymanLingham2018}. Georgiev, G\'omez-Serrano, Tao and Wagner recently used AlphaEvolve to search for the same real-sign minimum at degrees up to $100$; their computations do not give an asymptotic theorem \cite[Section 6.13]{GeorgievEtAl2025}.
