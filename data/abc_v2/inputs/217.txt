FAMILY 217: The low-temperature Sherrington–Kirkpatrick fluctuation law
SUBJECT: Probability and statistical mechanics
OPENAI SUMMARY: For every fixed inverse temperature β &gt; 1, determines the fluctuation scale and limiting law of the zero-field Gaussian Sherrington–Kirkpatrick log partition function. Its variance is asymptotic to $`c_\beta n^{1/3}`$, with $`c_\beta\gt 0`$, confirming the predicted n1/6 standard-deviation scale. Exact centering and standardization give full-sequence convergence to a uniquely characterized nondegenerate law.

=== MANUSCRIPT: The low-temperature Sherrington–Kirkpatrick free-energy limiting law (September-24-2026) [preprints/The-low-temperature-Sherrington-Kirkpatrick-free-energy-limiting-law-September-24-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: For every fixed inverse temperature β &gt; 1, we prove that the zero-field Gaussian Ising Sherrington–Kirkpatrick free energy, centered by its expectation and divided by its standard deviation, converges in distribution to a nondegenerate law as the system size tends to infinity through all integers. We also prove that its variance divided by n1/3 converges to a finite positive constant.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} At a fixed temperature below the transition, how large are the sample-to-sample fluctuations of the Sherrington--Kirkpatrick free energy, and do they approach a law after centering and rescaling? We prove a limiting law for every fixed inverse temperature $\beta>1$ in the zero-field Gaussian Ising model. The result includes a positive variance prefactor at scale $n^{1/3}$ and a characterization of the law by finite-system moments. Let $(g_{ij})_{1\le i<j\le n}$ be independent standard Gaussian variables. For $\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^n$ and $\beta>1$, put \begin{equation}\label{eq:model} H_n(\sigma)=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i<j}g_{ij}\sigma_i\sigma_j, \qquad Z_n(\beta)=\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^n}e^{\beta H_n(\sigma)}, \qquad F_n(\beta)=\log Z_n(\beta). \end{equation} All expectations concern the Gaussian disorder unless another probability law is explicitly specified. There is no external field. Write \[ s_n(\beta)^2=\Var F_n(\beta), \qquad X_n(\beta)=\frac{F_n(\beta)-\mathbb E F_n(\beta)}{s_n(\beta)}. \] The variance is positive for every $n\ge2$: with all couplings except $g_{12}$ set to zero, the free energy is $n\log2+\log\cosh(\beta g_{12}/\sqrt n)$, a nonconstant continuous function. Full support of the Gaussian law excludes almost-sure constancy. Standard Gaussian moment bounds give finite variance. \subsection{The fluctuation problem and its history} The model \eqref{eq:model} was introduced by Sherrington and Kirkpatrick~\cite{SK1975}. Their symmetric replica calculation gave an unphysical negative entropy at zero temperature. Parisi's hierarchy~\cite{Parisi1979} replaced a single overlap parameter by successive levels, encoded in the infinite-level limit by a function. This development led to a variational formula for the thermodynamic free energy. Gaussian interpolation established the thermodynamic limit \cite{GuerraToninelli2002}, the variational bound of Guerra~\cite{Guerra2003}, and ultimately the matching formula proved by Talagrand~\cite{Talagrand2006}. Panchenko's extension treats general mixed $p$-spin models, including odd interactions~\cite{Panchenko2014}. These results identify the leading order of the mean free energy. They leave the smaller, sample-dependent fluctuations studied here. The one-sixth fluctuation prediction has a separate physics history. Kondor's replica expansion~\cite{Kondor1983} and the finite-size prediction of Crisanti, Paladin, Sommers, and Vulpiani~\cite{CPSV1992} preceded the low-temperature large-deviation calculations of Parisi and Rizzo~\cite{ParisiRizzo2008,ParisiRizzo2009}. The predicted $n^{-5/6}$ scale for the physical free-energy density becomes $n^{1/6}$ for $\log Z_n$ at fixed $\beta$. To pass from rare free energies to the width of typical fluctuations, Parisi and Rizzo assume that the large-deviation tail matches smoothly onto the central part of the distribution~\cite[Section~VI]{ParisiRizzo2009}. They distinguish the two orders of the replica-number and system-size limits. This matching argument is heuristic: it does not itself provide a variance asymptotic or a limiting law. Rigorous fluctuation results depend strongly on the regime. Aizenman, Lebowitz, and Ruelle proved order-one Gaussian fluctuations at high temperature~\cite{AizenmanLebowitzRuelle1987}. Chatterjee obtained superconcentration and its relation to disorder chaos~\cite{Chatterjee2009}, as well as a constant-scale nonconcentration bound~\cite{Chatterjee2019LowerBounds}. Chen and Lam's critical and near-critical bounds concern temperatures approaching the transition~\cite{ChenLam2019Critical}. For every integer $k\ge1$, there are constants $c_k>0$ and $C_k<\infty$ such that \begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-rate} \big|\mathbb E Y_n^k-a_k\big|\le C_k n^{-c_k},\qquad n\ge2. \end{equation} There are also constants $c>0$ and $C<\infty$, depending on $\beta$, for which \begin{equation}\label{eq:exponential-moments} \sup_{n\ge2}\mathbb E e^{c|Y_n|}\le C. \end{equation} The limits satisfy $a_1=0$ and $0<a_2<\infty$.
[proposition] [Moment specification]\label{def:law} Fix $\beta>1$ and let $C=\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}l^{-2}$. For an integer $k\ge1$, draw an integer $J\ge2$ with \[ \mathbb P(J=l)=\frac{l^{-2}}C. \] Conditional on $J$, draw $k+1$ independent copies $F_J^{c,0},\ldots,F_J^{c,k}$ of the completed free energy in \eqref{eq:completed-free-energy}. Set \[ t=\log J, \qquad P_k=\prod_{i=1}^k J^{-1/6}(F_J^{c,0}-F_J^{c,i}). \] Conditional averaging over the independent comparison copies gives $\mathbb E[P_k\mid J=l]=\mathbb E Y_l^k$. Each expectation is taken before the series is summed. The series of these scalar expectations is absolutely convergent. We have $a_2>0$, and the law in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is uniquely determined by \[ \int z^k\,\nu_\beta(dz)=\frac{a_k}{a_2^{k/2}},\qquad k\ge1. \]

The temperature is fixed throughout, with no uniform conclusion as $\beta$ approaches $1$ or infinity. \subsection{History and significance} The Sherrington--Kirkpatrick model \cite{SK1975} separates two questions: the limiting free energy and its fluctuations between disorder samples. Parisi introduced the hierarchy of order parameters underlying the variational prediction for the first \cite{Parisi1979}. Guerra and Toninelli \cite{GuerraToninelli2002} established the thermodynamic limit by Gaussian interpolation, and Guerra's variational bound \cite{Guerra2003} and Talagrand's matching result \cite{Talagrand2006} identified its Parisi value. Panchenko subsequently established the formula for general mixed $p$-spin models, including odd interactions \cite{Panchenko2014}. These results determine the leading extensive term; the fluctuations studied here occur on a smaller scale. The prediction of that scale has a separate history. Kondor's replica expansion near the critical temperature \cite{Kondor1983} is the starting point of the calculation described by Parisi and Rizzo \cite{ParisiRizzo2008,ParisiRizzo2009}. Crisanti, Paladin, Sommers, and Vulpiani \cite{CPSV1992} predicted a scale $n^{-5/6}$ for the physical free energy per spin in the low-temperature, zero-field phase. At fixed $\beta$, this corresponds to $n^{1/6}$ for the log partition function. Parisi and Rizzo developed the associated rare-deviation calculation through the low-temperature phase. Inferring typical fluctuations from that calculation requires an additional matching assumption, discussed in \cite[Section~VI]{ParisiRizzo2009}. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} establishes the predicted one-sixth exponent with arbitrarily small power slack, for every fixed finite $\beta>1$. At fixed high temperature $0<\beta<1$, order-one Gaussian fluctuations go back to Aizenman, Lebowitz, and Ruelle \cite{AizenmanLebowitzRuelle1987}. Chatterjee's work on disorder chaos and superconcentration \cite[Theorem~1.5]{Chatterjee2009} gives $\operatorname{Var}(F_n)=O_\beta(n/\log n)$, and his later lower-bound method proves constant-scale nonconcentration \cite[Theorem~2.4]{Chatterjee2019LowerBounds}. Chen and Lam obtained a critical-temperature variance upper bound of order $(\log n)^2$ \cite{ChenLam2019Critical}. At the critical point, Du and Huang subsequently proved variance $\frac16\log n+O(1)$ and a Gaussian limit \cite[Theorem~1.3]{DuHuang2026Critical}.
