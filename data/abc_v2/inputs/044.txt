FAMILY 044: The equivariant cohomological Hikita conjecture
SUBJECT: Algebraic and complex geometry
OPENAI SUMMARY: Proves the equivariant cohomological Hikita correspondence for every finite quiver, including loops and multiple arrows, with arbitrary dimension and framing vectors and commuting flavor torus. When every semistable point is stable and the gauge action is free, the equivariant cohomology of the Nakajima variety is canonically the coordinate ring of the scheme-theoretic cocharacter-fixed locus of its flavor-deformed Coulomb branch.

=== MANUSCRIPT: The equivariant cohomological Hikita conjecture for arbitrary quivers (September-24-2026) [preprints/The-equivariant-cohomological-Hikita-conjecture-for-arbitrary-quivers-September-24-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove the equivariant cohomological Hikita conjecture for arbitrary finite quivers, including loops and multiple arrows. For any dimension and framing vectors, any commuting flavor torus, and a stability character whose semistable locus is stable and has free gauge action, the equivariant cohomology of the Nakajima variety is canonically isomorphic to the coordinate ring of the scheme-theoretic fixed locus of the stability cocharacter on the flavor-deformed Coulomb branch. This is an isomorphism of graded algebras over the common coefficient ring, retaining nilpotents and including the empty case.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Hikita's conjecture relates two different kinds of algebra: the cohomology of a symplectic resolution and the functions on a fixed subscheme of a corresponding Coulomb branch. The scheme structure is essential. Even when the underlying fixed-point set is small, its nilpotent functions can encode positive-degree cohomology. For quiver gauge theories, the tautological classes on the Higgs side and the equivariant parameters on the Coulomb side give a common coefficient ring. The question is whether the relations among those coefficients agree. We prove this agreement for arbitrary finite quivers, under the precise regularity hypothesis stated below. The proof keeps the coefficient maps and the full fixed schemes throughout. Its two main comparisons have different purposes: a calculation for the full topological torus identifies the cohomology algebra, while a separate argument identifies that torus-fixed scheme with the fixed scheme of the stability cocharacter. \subsection{The statement and its coefficient maps} Let $Q=(Q_0,Q_1)$ be a finite oriented quiver. Loops and multiple arrows are allowed. Fix complex vector spaces $V_i,W_i$ of dimensions $v_i,w_i$, and put \begin{equation}\label{eq:gauge-data} G=\prod_{i\in Q_0}\GL(V_i),\qquad N=\bigoplus_{a:i\to j}\Hom(V_i,V_j) \oplus\bigoplus_{i\in Q_0}\Hom(V_i,W_i). \end{equation} An algebraic torus $F$ acts linearly on $N$ and commutes with $G$; write $\mathfrak f=\Lie F$. The induced action on $T^*N=N\oplus N^*$ is Hamiltonian. For $(s,p)\in N\oplus N^*$, our cotangent moment map is characterized by $\langle\mu(s,p),\xi\rangle=\langle p,\xi s\rangle$. Choose a character $\nu$ of $G$ for which the $\nu$-semistable locus in $\mu^{-1}(0)$ equals the stable locus and $G$ acts freely there. We call such a character \emph{regular}, and set \begin{equation}\label{eq:higgs-definition} D=\mu^{-1}(0)^{\nu\text{-stable}},\qquad X=D/G. \end{equation} Thus $X$ is a smooth Nakajima variety. We allow $D$ and $X$ to be empty. Put $\OO=\C[[t]]$ and $\KK=\C((t))$. The Braverman--Finkelberg--Nakajima space of triples and its flavor-deformed Coulomb algebra are \begin{align} \cR&=\{[g,s]\in G(\KK)\times_{G(\OO)}N(\OO):gs\in N(\OO)\}, \label{eq:triples-definition}\\ A_F&=H_*^{G(\OO)\times F}(\cR;\C),\qquad Y_{\mathfrak f}=\Spec A_F.\label{eq:coulomb-definition} \end{align} Here $(g,s)\sim(gh^{-1},hs)$, and the multiplication is BFN convolution. We use the commutative algebra, without the loop-rotation deformation parameter. Its topological grading is by $\pi_1(G)=\Z^{\{i:v_i>0\}}$, and defines a torus $T_{\mathrm{top}}=\Hom(\pi_1(G),\C^\times)$. The character $\nu$ gives a cocharacter of this torus. Its fixed locus is scheme-theoretic: if $(A_F)_m$ denotes its weight-$m$ subspace, then \begin{equation}\label{eq:nu-quotient} \C[Y_{\mathfrak f}^{\nu}] =A_F/\langle(A_F)_m:m\ne0\rangle. \end{equation} No reduction of this quotient will be taken. Let $T\subset G$ be the diagonal maximal torus, let $\mathcal W$ be its Weyl group, and write \begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficient-ring} R=H^*_{G\times F}(\mathrm{pt};\C) =\C[\Lie T\oplus\mathfrak f]^{\mathcal W},\qquad R_0=H_G^*(\mathrm{pt};\C). \end{equation} The Kirwan map $\phi_H:R\to H_F^*(X;\C)$ and the coefficient map $\phi_C:R\to\C[Y_{\mathfrak f}^{\nu}]$ are the natural maps on the two sides of the Hikita conjecture. On the first side, a gauge Chern class maps to the Chern class of its tautological bundle, and a flavor parameter acts through $H_F^*(\mathrm{pt})$. On the second side, $R$ acts by the coefficient classes of the Coulomb algebra, followed by the fixed-scheme quotient. Each linear gauge or flavor parameter has degree two. [theorem] \label{thm:main} For every datum \eqref{eq:gauge-data}, every commuting flavor torus $F$, and every regular stability character $\nu$, both coefficient maps are surjective and have the same kernel. They therefore induce a canonical isomorphism of graded $R$-algebras \[ H_F^*(X;\C)\ \cong\ \C[Y_{\mathfrak f}^{\nu}]. \] The gradings are ordinary cohomological degree and BFN homological degree, respectively. The assertion includes the empty case, whose ring is zero.
