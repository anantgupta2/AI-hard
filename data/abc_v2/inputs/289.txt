FAMILY 289: Strong Kadison–Kastler stability and its spatial boundaries
SUBJECT: Operator algebras
OPENAI SUMMARY: Proves that sufficiently close unital von Neumann algebras on the same Hilbert space are conjugate by a unitary arbitrarily close to the identity, with a universal tolerance in the operator-norm distance between unit balls. Counterexamples show that near-identity conjugacy fails for one-sided near inclusions, and that arbitrarily close norm-separable C∗-algebras need not be ambiently unitarily conjugate.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Universal strong Kadison–Kastler stability (September-23-2026) [preprints/Universal-strong-Kadison-Kastler-stability-September-23-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove that sufficiently close unital von Neumann algebras are conjugate by a unitary arbitrarily close to the identity. The tolerance depends only on the prescribed distance of that unitary from the identity, uniformly over all algebras, representations, and Hilbert spaces. This resolves the strong Kadison–Kastler conjecture.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} For a von Neumann algebra $M\subseteq\B(H)$, write $M_1$ for its closed operator-norm unit ball. The Kadison--Kastler distance between two such algebras is \begin{equation}\label{eq:kk-definition} d(M,N)=\max\left\{ \sup_{x\in M_1}\inf_{y\in N_1}\norm{x-y},\quad \sup_{y\in N_1}\inf_{x\in M_1}\norm{x-y}\right\}. \end{equation} Kadison and Kastler introduced this metric in 1972 \cite{KadisonKastler1972}. Their perturbation problem asks whether closeness of the unit balls forces spatial isomorphism, implemented by a unitary close to the identity. We prove the uniform form of this assertion. [statement] The tolerance is independent of the algebras, their type, their representations, and the cardinalities of their preduals and Hilbert spaces. Thus \Cref{thm:main} resolves the strong Kadison--Kastler conjecture positively. Its hypothesis concerns the ordinary distance \eqref{eq:kk-definition}. \subsection{Historical context} Early positive results treated type~I algebras: Phillips proved spatial isomorphism, and Christensen obtained a conjugating unitary close to the identity \cite{Phillips1974,Christensen1975}. The work of Christensen, Johnson, and Raeburn--Taylor established the amenable case \cite{Christensen1977,Johnson1977,RaeburnTaylor1977}. Christensen subsequently developed the one-sided theory of near inclusions: an amenable von Neumann algebra sufficiently nearly contained in another can be moved into it by a small unitary \cite{Christensen1980}. His earlier work also treated close von Neumann subalgebras of a common finite von Neumann algebra \cite{Christensen1977II}. The common finite ambient algebra is an essential hypothesis of that result; merely assuming that both represented algebras are finite gives a different problem. In a different common-ambient setting, Ino proved near-identity unitary conjugacy for sufficiently close subalgebras of a countably decomposable ambient algebra when both admit conditional expectations of finite probabilistic index from that algebra \cite[Theorem~3.2]{Ino2016}. The corresponding development for $C^*$-algebras clarifies the role of the conclusion. Christensen, Sinclair, Smith, White, and Winter proved that sufficiently close $C^*$-algebras acting on a separable Hilbert space are spatially isomorphic if one of them is norm separable and nuclear \cite{CSSWW2010,CSSWW2012}. Johnson's examples exclude a uniform bound on the displacement of an implementing unitary that tends to zero with the algebra distance; they do not exclude spatial isomorphism \cite{Johnson1982}. For nonamenable von Neumann algebras, Cameron, Christensen, Sinclair, Smith, White, and Wiggins constructed strongly stable factors of the form $(L^\infty(X)\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb Z))\barotimes R$, where $n\geq3$, the action is free, ergodic, and probability preserving on a standard nonatomic probability space, and $R$ is the hyperfinite type~$\mathrm{II}_1$ factor \cite{CCSSWW2012,CCSSWW2014}. Their proof combines amenable near-inclusion methods, a common standard representation, and bounded cohomology. Their later work transfers property~$\Gamma$ and Cartan structure between sufficiently close type $\II_1$ factors with separable predual, and also studies stability of solidity, strong solidity, and tensor-product structure \cite{CCSSWW2017}. The passage between representations is tied to Kadison's similarity problem: whether a bounded homomorphism from a $C^*$-algebra into $\B(H)$ is conjugate by an invertible operator to a $*$-homomorphism. Christensen's work on property~$\Gamma$ factors provides a foundational special case \cite{Christensen1986,Christensen2001}. Christensen, Sinclair, Smith, and White established perturbation results for the similarity property and operator-algebraic invariants \cite{CSSW2010}; Cameron and collaborators then proved that the similarity property is equivalent to continuity of commutants, with the required uniformity over representations \cite{CCSSWW2013}. === MANUSCRIPT: Near Inclusions of von Neumann Algebras Without Small Spatial Embeddings (October-5-2026) [preprints/Near-Inclusions-of-von-Neumann-Algebras-Without-Small-Spatial-Embeddings-October-5-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We give a negative answer to the unrestricted small-spatial-embedding problem for one-sided near inclusions of von Neumann algebras. On separable complex Hilbert spaces, we construct pairs of unital von Neumann algebras with common identity whose one-sided gaps tend to zero. Spatial embeddings of the source into the target exist, but every implementing unitary stays a fixed positive distance from the identity. The obstruction therefore concerns small implementing unitaries, rather than the existence of spatial embeddings.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Let $M$ and $N$ be von Neumann algebras acting on a complex Hilbert space $H$, both with identity $I_H$. Their one-sided near-inclusion gap is \begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-definition} \gamma(M,N)=\sup_{\substack{x\in M\\\|x\|\leq 1}} \inf_{y\in N}\|x-y\|. \end{equation} The approximating element $y$ is not required to be contractive. A small value of $\gamma(M,N)$ says that every contraction in $M$ is close to some element of $N$; it does not require these choices of approximants to preserve algebraic operations. If a unitary $u$ satisfies $uMu^*\subseteq N$, then $\gamma(M,N)\leq 2\|u-I_H\|$. The converse question asks whether a small near inclusion can always be obtained in this way. The underlying perturbation program began with Kadison and Kastler \cite{KK1972}. They measured the distance between operator algebras by the Hausdorff distance between their unit balls in the operator norm, now called the Kadison--Kastler distance, and proposed that sufficiently close von Neumann algebras should be related by a unitary close to the identity. Christensen, Johnson, and Raeburn--Taylor established the amenable case in the 1970s \cite{Chr1977,Joh1977,RT1977}. Christensen subsequently introduced near inclusions and proved a spatial embedding theorem for an amenable source algebra and an arbitrary target \cite[Definition~2.1 and Theorem~4.3]{Chr1980}. In particular, sufficiently small near inclusions in this setting admit implementing unitaries whose distance from the identity is bounded linearly by the near-inclusion tolerance. We use the following formulation of the unrestricted one-sided question posed by Cameron, Christensen, Sinclair, Smith, White, and Wiggins in the paragraph preceding \cite[Theorem~2]{CCSSWW2012}: \begin{quote} For every $\varepsilon>0$, is there a $\delta>0$ such that, for every complex Hilbert space $H$ and every pair of von Neumann algebras $M,N\subseteq\mathcal B(H)$ with common identity $I_H$, $\gamma(M,N)<\delta$ implies the existence of a unitary $u\in\mathcal B(H)$ satisfying \[ uMu^*\subseteq N \qquad\text{and}\qquad \|u-I_H\|<\varepsilon? \] \end{quote} Here $\delta$ must be independent of the algebras and their representations. We answer this question negatively. [statement] In these examples a unitary embedding of $M_n$ into $N_n$ does exist; the obstruction concerns its distance from the identity. The theorem therefore separates normwise approximation from small spatial implementation even on separable Hilbert spaces. Its hypothesis is one-sided. The construction supplies no reverse near inclusion, and hence does not give a counterexample to the two-sided Kadison--Kastler conjecture. The positive results for nonamenable factors in \cite{CCSSWW2012}, which concern two-sided perturbations under additional structural hypotheses, address a different assertion. The proof transfers a classical finite-dimensional obstruction into the near-inclusion problem. Voiculescu's clock and shift matrices $U_n,V_n\in\mathcal B(\mathbb C^n)$ satisfy $\|U_nV_n-V_nU_n\|\to0$, but cannot be approximated in norm by commuting unitary pairs \cite{Voi1983}. Exel and Loring gave an elementary winding-number proof of this phenomenon \cite{EL1989}; we use its trace-logarithm form \cite[Lemma~3.1]{Exel1993}. We recall the short obstruction argument in Section~\ref{sec:obstruction}. To place these matrices in operator algebras, we use the free group $\F$ on countably many generators. === MANUSCRIPT: Close Separable C*-Algebras Without Spatial Conjugacy (October-5-2026) [preprints/Close-Separable-Cstar-Algebras-Without-Spatial-Conjugacy-October-5-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We disprove the separable C∗-algebraic spatial form of the Kadison–Kastler conjecture. For every ε &gt; 0, we construct unital, norm-separable C∗-algebras on a common separable complex Hilbert space, with the same identity and Kadison–Kastler distance less than ε, that are not conjugate by any unitary. The two algebras have the same von Neumann closure.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:intro} For $C^*$-algebras $A,B\subseteq\B(H)$, write $A_1$ and $B_1$ for their closed unit balls. Their Kadison--Kastler distance is \begin{equation}\label{eq:kk} \KK(A,B)=\max\left\{ \sup_{a\in A_1}\inf_{b\in B_1}\norm{a-b},\, \sup_{b\in B_1}\inf_{a\in A_1}\norm{b-a}\right\}. \end{equation} The spatial form of the perturbation problem asks whether sufficiently close algebras must be related by $uAu^*=B$ for a unitary $u\in\B(H)$. Kadison and Kastler initiated this problem in \cite{KK72}. In its separable $C^*$-algebraic form, one asks for a constant $\delta>0$ valid for all norm-separable $C^*$-algebras on separable Hilbert spaces; no bound on $\norm{u-\id}$ is required. The following theorem resolves negatively this separable $C^*$-algebraic spatial form of the Kadison--Kastler conjecture, even for unital algebras with a common identity. [statement] The scope of this statement is important. Johnson constructed arbitrarily close separable nuclear algebras for which implementing unitaries cannot be chosen close to the identity \cite{Johnson82}; those examples are nevertheless spatially conjugate. Choi and Christensen constructed arbitrarily close nonisomorphic algebras \cite{CC83}, but norm nonseparability is essential to those examples. These distinctions, and the resulting separable spatial formulation, are discussed in \cite{CSSWW10}. In the positive direction, Christensen, Sinclair, Smith, White, and Winter proved spatial conjugacy for sufficiently close separable nuclear $C^*$-algebras on a separable Hilbert space \cite[Theorem~4]{CSSWW10}; see \cite[Theorem~5.4]{CSSWW12} for the full proof. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} imposes no nuclearity assumption. It makes no assertion of abstract nonisomorphism and does not give a counterexample for von Neumann algebras. \paragraph{The construction.} We first isolate a way to turn an obstruction for inclusions into an obstruction to arbitrary spatial conjugacy. Let $D\subseteq\B(L)$ be unital, norm-separable, and have scalar center. For unital subalgebras $P,Q\subseteq D$, consider the algebra \[ \E_D(P)=\{(x_n):x_n\in D,\ x_n\longrightarrow p\in P \text{ in norm}\} \] acting diagonally on $\bigoplus_{n\ge1}L$. Changing $P$ changes only the condition at the limit. The coordinate projections are intrinsic minimal central projections of this algebra. Consequently, a unitary conjugating $\E_D(P)$ onto $\E_D(Q)$ must permute the coordinates and normalize $D$ on every coordinate. Pulling back constant sequences then forces each finite family in $Q$ to be a simultaneous norm limit of families in unitary conjugates of $P$ by normalizers of $D$. Write $D'$ for the commutant of $D$ on $L$, consisting of the bounded operators commuting with every element of $D$. The obstruction we use is the equality \begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-test} \norm{\sum_i p_i z_i}_{\B(L)} =\norm{\sum_i p_i\otimes z_i}_{\B(L\otimes L)}, \qquad p_i\in P,\quad z_i\in D'. \end{equation} This equality is invariant under normalizers of $D$ and passes to simultaneous finite-family norm limits. We construct very close subalgebras $P_0,P_t\subseteq D$ for which it holds for $P_0$ but fails for $P_t$. In fact, $P_0$ and $P_t$ themselves are conjugate by a unitary close to the identity. The obstruction concerns their positions inside $D$, and the sequence construction makes it an obstruction to every spatial conjugacy of the resulting algebras. For the close inclusions we use a real-parameter deformation of the regular representation of the free group on countably many generators. This representation-theoretic ingredient belongs to the Pytlik--Szwarc family \cite{PS86}; its uniform-topology behavior at the regular representation is discussed by Burger, Ozawa, and Thom \cite[Theorem~4.6]{BOT13}. We give a direct tree construction and prove the uniform estimate needed here. The additional mechanism is the combination of the relative tensor-norm test with the convergent-sequence reduction.
