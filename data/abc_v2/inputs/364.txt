FAMILY 364: Kinetic limits and fluctuations over the Boltzmann lifespan
SUBJECT: Partial differential equations
OPENAI SUMMARY: Derives the nonlinear Boltzmann equation from three-dimensional grand-canonical Newtonian gases throughout every regular kinetic interval with uniform Gaussian decay. Stable finite-range radial potentials may have attractive wells and a singular repulsive core; initial pair exclusion and spatially summable Gaussian density and gradient bounds are assumed. A companion gives finite-dimensional hard-sphere Gaussian fluctuations, centered at the exact microscopic expectation and governed by the linear fluctuating Boltzmann equation.

=== MANUSCRIPT: The Boltzmann–Grad limit for stable radial potentials on regular kinetic intervals (September-23-2026) [preprints/The-Boltzmann-Grad-limit-for-stable-radial-potentials-on-regular-kinetic-intervals-September-23-2026/build/paper.tex]
ABSTRACT: We derive the nonlinear Boltzmann equation from a grand-canonical Newtonian gas with initial pair exclusion. We treat stable, finite-range radial potentials that are C2 except for an allowed repulsive singularity at the origin, and C1 initial probability densities with spatially summable Gaussian bounds on the density and its spatial gradient. All fixed-order rescaled factorial marginals converge in L1, uniformly throughout every finite interval on which the classical kinetic solution has a uniform Gaussian bound. The result allows attractive wells and dynamically formed clusters, without restrictions on the differential scattering cross-section.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} The Boltzmann--Grad limit describes a gas whose interaction range tends to zero while the collision frequency remains of order one. In three dimensions the activity is $\mu_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-2}$. The microscopic dynamics is Newtonian, whereas the limiting collision operator is determined by the actual two-body scattering of the pair potential. We prove this limit for stable finite-range radial potentials on every finite interval where the Boltzmann solution has the regularity and Gaussian bound specified below. Write $z=(x,v)\in\R^3\times\R^3$. For a phase-space function $h$ and $\gamma>0$, write \begin{equation}\label{intro:norm} \|h\|_{\mathrm{Bol},\gamma} :=\sum_{k\in\Z^3}\; \sup_{\substack{|x-k|\le1\\v\in\R^3}} e^{\gamma|v|^2}|h(x,v)|. \end{equation} For a vector-valued function we use its Euclidean length inside this definition. The precise potential class, grand-canonical law, Newton equations, and scattering operator are given in Assumption~\ref{pre:potential} and Equations~\eqref{pre:model}, \eqref{pre:dynamics}, and \eqref{pre:collision}. In brief, the potential is radial, vanishes beyond unit distance, and is $C^2$, with either a finite value or a repulsive singularity at the origin. Its only sign condition is thermodynamic stability, in the classical sense of~\cite{Ruelle69}. The initial law is the product activity $f_0$ conditioned by pairwise exclusion at distance $\varepsilon$, with its exact grand-canonical normalization. For each fixed integer $s\ge1$, the scaled factorial density $F_s^\varepsilon(t)$ is characterized by \begin{equation}\label{intro:factorial-test} \int a(Z_s)F_s^\varepsilon(t,Z_s)\,dZ_s =\mu_\varepsilon^{-s}\EE \sum_{\substack{1\le i_1,\ldots,i_s\le N\\ i_1,\ldots,i_s\ \mathrm{distinct}}} a(z_{i_1}(t),\ldots,z_{i_s}(t)) \end{equation} for bounded measurable tests $a$ on $(\R^3\times\R^3)^s$. The sum runs over ordered distinct labels and is zero when $N<s$; $Z_s=(z_1,\ldots,z_s)$ denotes an ordered phase-space configuration. Attractive wells, long scattering delays, and noninjective scattering maps are permitted. The exclusion belongs solely to the initial law. [maintheorem] [Regular-lifespan kinetic limit]\label{thm:main} Let $\Phi$ satisfy Assumption~\ref{pre:potential}, and let $\beta>0$. Let $f_0\ge0$ be a $C^1$ probability density on $\R^3\times\R^3$ such that \begin{equation}\label{intro:data-bound} \|f_0\|_{\mathrm{Bol},2\beta} +\|\nabla_xf_0\|_{\mathrm{Bol},2\beta}<\infty. \end{equation} Fix $0<T<\infty$ for which the equation \begin{equation}\label{intro:boltzmann} (\partial_t+v\cdot\nabla_x)f=Q_\Phi(f,f), \qquad f|_{t=0}=f_0, \end{equation} has a nonnegative classical probability-density solution satisfying \begin{equation}\label{intro:solution-bound} \sup_{0\le t\le T}\sup_{x,v} e^{2\beta|v|^2}f(t,x,v)<\infty. \end{equation} Start the exact Newtonian process from the grand-canonical law \eqref{pre:model}. For its scaled factorial densities $F_s^\varepsilon$, defined in \eqref{pre:factorial}, every fixed integer $s\ge1$ satisfies \begin{equation}\label{intro:chaos} \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\; \sup_{0\le t\le T} \bigl\|F_s^\varepsilon(t)-f(t)^{\otimes s}\bigr\|_ {L^1((\R^3\times\R^3)^s)}=0. \end{equation}

=== MANUSCRIPT: Hard-sphere fluctuations on the regular Boltzmann lifespan (September-23-2026) [preprints/Hard-sphere-fluctuations-on-the-regular-Boltzmann-lifespan-September-23-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove a finite-dimensional central limit theorem away from equilibrium for a deterministic grand-canonical hard-sphere gas in three dimensions. The initial probability density is smooth, with spatially summable Gaussian velocity bounds on the density and its spatial gradient. The limit holds on every finite interval on which the classical Boltzmann solution has uniform Gaussian velocity decay. The empirical measure is centered by its exact microscopic expectation. Its fluctuations converge to the Gaussian solution of the linear fluctuating Boltzmann equation.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} The Boltzmann equation describes the leading empirical distribution of a dilute gas. The next-order question asks whether the small correlations created by deterministic collisions produce Gaussian fluctuations, and which equation governs their covariance. We prove that the finite-dimensional fluctuation law persists throughout an assumed regular kinetic interval, without an equilibrium or small-data hypothesis. Lanford's derivation of the Boltzmann equation established the kinetic limit for short times~\cite{Lanford1975}. The next-order problem has a separate history. Van Beijeren, Lanford, Lebowitz and Spohn derived short-time equilibrium fluctuation covariances governed by the linearized Boltzmann equation~\cite{vanBeijerenLanfordLebowitzSpohn1980}, and Spohn established the nonequilibrium second-moment limit~\cite{Spohn1981}. These covariance results identify the candidate fluctuation dynamics; a Gaussian limit also requires control of higher-order correlations. Pulvirenti and Simonella developed short-time quantitative correlation-error estimates and a cumulant-type reorganization of collision histories \cite{PulvirentiSimonella2017}. Bodineau, Gallagher, Saint-Raymond and Simonella developed the short-time nonequilibrium fluctuation theory \cite{BGSS2020,BGSS2023}, including convergence of the fluctuation process. Their connected-cumulant analysis identifies the Gaussian collision noise and uses exact microscopic centering. Their direct cluster expansion on physical trajectories~\cite{BGSSCluster2022} provides a complementary representation of the short-time statistical dynamics. At equilibrium, they obtained long-time covariance convergence~\cite{BGSSCorrelations2023} and then the full Gaussian fluctuation limit~\cite{BGSS2024}, using the invariant measure. The BGSS fluctuation theorems concern a spatially periodic gas; the present setting is the whole space, with integrable initial data. Our conclusion extends the time interval away from equilibrium but concerns finite-dimensional laws, rather than process convergence. Deng, Hani and Ma extended the hard-sphere kinetic derivation through each prescribed interval on which the regular Boltzmann solution exists with the required bounds~\cite{DHM2025}. They also identify long-time nonequilibrium fluctuations as a further application of their approach~\cite[Section~1.4.3]{DHM2025}. The theorem below establishes the finite-dimensional nonequilibrium fluctuation statement on that regular kinetic lifespan. Its proof retains their layered collision histories and supplies the sharper connected estimates and centering transfer required at the fluctuation scale. [theorem] [Finite-dimensional fluctuation limit]\label{thm:main} Under \Cref{ass:kinetic}, the centered Gaussian generalized field \(\zeta\) with weak equation \begin{equation}\label{eq:fluctuating-equation} \dd\zeta_t(\phi)=\zeta_t(A_t\phi)\,\dd t+\dd W_t(\phi) \end{equation} is well defined by its backward mild form on a scale of tests with small Gaussian growth. Its initial field is independent of \(W\), and \begin{align} \E[\zeta_0(\phi)\zeta_0(\psi)] &=\int_{\R^6}f_0\phi\psi\,\dd z,\label{eq:initial-covariance}\\ \E[W_t(\phi)W_s(\psi)] &=\int_0^{t\wedge s}C_r(\phi,\psi)\,\dd r.\label{eq:noise-covariance} \end{align} The Gaussian noise \(W\) has independent increments.
