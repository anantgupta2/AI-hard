FAMILY 314: Cyclic length and chromatic fixed-point loss
SUBJECT: Topology
OPENAI SUMMARY: Determines the optimal chromatic loss from geometric H-fixed points to geometric G-fixed points for every subgroup H of a finite p-group G. At every nonnegative height, the loss equals the shortest subnormal-chain length from H to G with cyclic quotients. Each quotient counts once regardless of order, and finite spectra witness sharpness.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Cyclic length and chromatic fixed-point loss (September-24-2026) [preprints/Cyclic-Length-and-Chromatic-Fixed-Point-Loss-September-24-2026/build/paper.tex]
ABSTRACT: For a finite p-group G and a subgroup H, we prove that the optimal chromatic fixed-point loss equals the shortest length of a subnormal chain from H to G with cyclic quotients. The equality holds at every prime and every nonnegative height, resolving positively the equality proposed by Kuhn and Lloyd.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:intro} The chromatic Smith problem asks how much information about fixed points at one subgroup is forced by chromatic acyclicity at another. Fix a prime \(p\), and let \(K(i)\) denote Morava \(K\)-theory at \(p\), with \(K(0)=H\Q\). For a finite \(p\)-group \(G\), a subgroup \(H\le G\), and an integer \(n\ge0\), define \(r_n(G,H)\) to be the least nonnegative integer \(r\) such that \begin{equation}\label{eq:def-loss} K(n+r)_*(\Phi^H X)=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad K(n)_*(\Phi^G X)=0 \end{equation} for every finite \(p\)-local genuine \(G\)-spectrum \(X\). Here \(\Phi^H X\) denotes the underlying spectrum of the geometric fixed points, and finite spectra include compact retracts. Let \(\ell(G,H)\) be the least length of a subnormal chain \[ H=G_0\trianglelefteq G_1\trianglelefteq\cdots \trianglelefteq G_s=G \] whose successive quotients are cyclic. We set \(\ell(G,G)=0\). Such chains exist in finite \(p\)-groups. The cyclic chromatic Smith theorem, applied along a chain, gives \(r_n(G,H)\le\ell(G,H)\). Our result is the converse. [statement] Thus the optimal loss is independent of the height. For every integer \(0\le r<\ell(G,H)\), there is a finite genuine \(G\)-spectrum whose geometric \(H\)-fixed points are \(K(n+r)\)-acyclic and whose geometric \(G\)-fixed points have nonzero \(K(n)\)-homology. The theorem includes rational fixed-point detection at \(n=0\). The embedding of \(H\) matters. For example, in \(G=C_{p^2}\times C_p\), take \(H_1=pC_{p^2}\times0\) and \(H_2=0\times C_p\). Both subgroups are isomorphic to \(C_p\), but \(G/H_1\cong C_p\times C_p\) and \(G/H_2\cong C_{p^2}\). Chains above \(H_i\) correspond to chains in these quotients. The first quotient requires two cyclic steps, while the second requires one. Thus \(r_n(G,H_1)=2\) and \(r_n(G,H_2)=1\) at every target height. A cyclic quotient of order \(p^2\) still counts only once. \subsection*{History and significance} The classical starting point is Smith theory: for a finite complex with an action of a finite \(p\)-group, mod-\(p\) acyclicity passes to fixed points. Floyd strengthened this to an inequality between total mod-\(p\) Betti numbers \cite[Theorem~4.4]{Floyd}; the finite \(p\)-group statement follows by iteration from prime-order actions. Chromatic Smith theory asks which implications survive when mod-\(p\) homology is replaced by individual Morava theories. Building on the nonequivariant thick subcategory theorem of Hopkins and Smith \cite{HS}, Balmer and Sanders identified all primes of the category of finite genuine equivariant spectra. They reduced its topology to subgroup--height inclusions and computed the case \(C_p\), crediting Strickland's earlier work \cite[Introduction, Theorems~4.9 and~4.14, and Section~7]{BS}. Barthel, Hausmann, Naumann, Nikolaus, Noel, and Stapleton determined the finite abelian \(p\)-group case: the loss for \(H\le G\) is the minimum number of generators of \(G/H\) \cite[Theorem~2.3]{BHNNNS}. This replaced the original order-based prediction by a rank bound; already \(C_{p^2}\) has loss one rather than two. The sharp elementary abelian examples use the partition-complex work of Arone, Dwyer, Lesh, and Mahowald \cite[Theorems~2.2 and~4.1]{BHNNNS}. Balderrama and Kuhn later gave an elementary proof of the abelian chromatic Smith theorem, including target height zero \cite[Theorem~1.1 and Section~3.2]{BK}. For nonabelian groups, a general lower bound is the rank of the largest elementary abelian quotient of \(G\) in which \(H\) maps to the identity. This can be smaller than the cyclic-chain upper bound. Kuhn and Lloyd proved that chromatic acyclicity implications are equivalent to corresponding inequalities between Morava homology dimensions, allowing representation-theoretic constructions to give stronger lower bounds \cite[Theorem~2.2]{KL}.
