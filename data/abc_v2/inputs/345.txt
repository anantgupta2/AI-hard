FAMILY 345: Infinitely many closed geodesics on Riemannian spheres and closed three-manifolds
SUBJECT: Differential geometry
OPENAI SUMMARY: Proves that every smooth Riemannian metric on Sn, n ≥ 2, has infinitely many prime closed geodesics with pairwise distinct images. The same conclusion holds on every closed manifold admitting a finite smooth spherical cover and on every closed three-manifold, without orientability or nondegeneracy restrictions.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Infinitely many closed geodesic images on every Riemannian sphere (September-24-2026) [preprints/Infinitely-many-closed-geodesic-images-on-every-Riemannian-sphere-September-24-2026/build/paper.tex]
ABSTRACT: We resolve the sphere case of the closed-geodesic infinitude problem: every smooth Riemannian metric on the standard sphere Sn, n ≥ 2, has infinitely many prime closed geodesics with pairwise distinct images. The two-sphere case is classical; the result in higher dimensions includes degenerate metrics. Using finite covers and theorems of Perelman and Rademacher–Taimanov, we also obtain the same conclusion for every smooth Riemannian metric on a nonempty closed smooth three-manifold.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} The closed-geodesic infinitude problem asks whether every closed Riemannian manifold of dimension at least two has infinitely many geometrically distinct closed geodesics. A closed geodesic is \emph{prime} if it traverses its periodic trajectory once. Geometric distinction means distinction of images: changing the initial point, reversing orientation, or taking an iterate produces no new image. A prime geodesic may have self-intersections. We resolve the sphere case of the infinitude problem. [statement] Birkhoff developed a minimax construction for closed geodesics on surfaces of genus zero~\cite[Section~17]{Birkhoff1917}. The existence of a nonconstant closed geodesic on every positive-dimensional closed Riemannian manifold goes back to Lyusternik and Fet~\cite{LyusternikFet1951}. Infinitude requires separating new geodesics from repeated traversals of old ones. Bott's iteration theory~\cite{Bott1956Iteration} and the local Morse theory of Gromoll and Meyer~\cite{GromollMeyer1969} make that distinction quantitative. In particular, for a simply connected closed manifold, unbounded Betti numbers of the free loop space over a field force infinitely many geometrically distinct closed geodesics. Vigu\'e-Poirrier and Sullivan~\cite{ViguePoirrierSullivan1976} showed that rational cohomology requiring at least two algebra generators supplies this topological growth. Spheres have one-generator cohomology and bounded degreewise loop homology, so this criterion does not settle their infinitude problem. The two-sphere case is classical. It follows from Bangert's work together with that of Franks or Hingston~\cite{Bangert1993,Franks1992,Hingston1993}. De Philippis, Marini, Mazzucchelli and Suhr extended this result to reversible Finsler two-spheres~\cite[Theorem 1.5]{DePhilippisMariniMazzucchelliSuhr2022}. Albach's more recent work gives a growth exponent of at least two for the number of geodesic images of bounded length in that reversible Finsler setting~\cite[Theorem 1.1]{Albach2026}. In higher dimensions, Rademacher proved infinitude for strongly bumpy metrics on compact simply connected manifolds and proved that such metrics are generic~\cite[Theorems 1.1--1.3]{Rademacher1994}. Finite multiplicity results also hold without genericity: Long and Duan proved the existence of at least two geometrically distinct closed geodesics on every Riemannian three-sphere~\cite{LongDuan2009}. These results do not establish infinitude for arbitrary degenerate metrics; a limiting argument from generic metrics need not preserve distinct prime images. For manifolds with positive first Betti number, Contreras and Mazzucchelli recently proved infinitude for generic metrics \cite[Corollary~B]{ContrerasMazzucchelli2025}. Our higher-dimensional argument addresses the unrestricted metric problem. Earlier infinitude claims also require care. Asselle and Mazzucchelli~\cite[Introduction]{AsselleMazzucchelli2018} identify the proof in Klingenberg's monograph~\cite{Klingenberg1978} as erroneous. Charles~\cite[Theorem 6.14]{Charles2019} later claimed infinitude on every closed Riemannian manifold. We identify two unsupported steps in that argument: the boundary-rank equality in Equation~(53) requires maximal ranks not established there, and the constant-ball modification in Equation~(54) does not provide the nondegenerate Morse critical points used in the conclusion. [theorem] \label{thm:spheres} For every integer $n\ge2$ and every smooth Riemannian metric on the standard smooth sphere $S^n$, there are infinitely many prime closed geodesics with pairwise different images.
[corollary] \label{cor:finite-cover} Let $N$ be a closed manifold admitting a finite smooth cover diffeomorphic to a standard sphere $S^n$, where $n\ge2$. Every smooth Riemannian metric on $N$ has infinitely many geometrically distinct prime closed geodesics.
[corollary] [Closed three-manifolds]\label{cor:three-manifolds} Let $M$ be a nonempty closed smooth three-manifold, not necessarily orientable. Every smooth Riemannian metric on $M$ has infinitely many prime closed geodesics with pairwise distinct images.
