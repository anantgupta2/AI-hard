FAMILY 307: Failure of rational injectivity for maximal coarse assembly
SUBJECT: Topology
OPENAI SUMMARY: Constructs a uniformly discrete bounded-geometry space whose maximal coarse assembly map is not rationally injective. The example is a coarse disjoint union of finite connected graphs of uniformly bounded degree, with an infinite-order kernel class. A companion gives the analogous failure for reduced coarse assembly, disproving the rational coarse Novikov conjecture.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Failure of rational injectivity for maximal coarse assembly (October-5-2026) [preprints/Failure-of-rational-injectivity-for-maximal-coarse-assembly-October-5-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We construct a uniformly discrete bounded-geometry space whose maximal coarse assembly map has an infinite-order element in its kernel. The space is a coarse disjoint union of finite connected graphs of uniformly bounded degree, so maximal coarse assembly need not be rationally injective even for such graph unions.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Coarse assembly relates the topology visible at bounded scales in a metric space to the indices of operators controlled by its large-scale geometry. Its rational injectivity asks whether a non-torsion topological class can have zero coarse index. We prove that this can occur even when the analytic target is the maximal Roe algebra and the space is a coarse disjoint union of finite graphs of bounded degree. Let $Z$ be a uniformly discrete proper metric space of bounded geometry; thus every ball of a fixed radius contains a uniformly bounded number of points. Write $P_r(Z)$ for the Rips complex whose simplices are the finite subsets of diameter at most $r$, and set \[ \KX_q(Z)=\varinjlim_{r\to\infty}\KK_q(C_0(P_r(Z)),\C). \] All K-theory and K-homology in this article are complex. On the ample module $H_Z=\ell^2(Z)\otimes\ell^2(\N)$, let $\C[Z]$ be the algebra of bounded, locally compact, finite-propagation operators. Local compactness means that $aT$ and $Ta$ are compact for every $a\in C_0(Z)$, and finite propagation means that $T_{xy}=0$ whenever $d(x,y)$ exceeds some fixed bound. In particular, the compact matrix entries of $T$ need not satisfy a uniform rank bound. The universal C*-completion is the \emph{maximal Roe algebra} $C^*_{\max}(Z)$. The canonical maximal coarse assembly map is \[ \mu_Z^{\max}:\KX_q(Z)\longrightarrow K_q(C^*_{\max}(Z)). \] We recall the needed norm estimates and the index construction in Section~\ref{sec:maximal-index}. A \emph{coarse disjoint union} of finite graphs is a metric on their disjoint union which restricts to the graph path metric on each component and for which only finitely many pairs of distinct components are within any fixed distance. We take such metrics to be uniformly discrete. [statement] The theorem strengthens the reduced coarse-assembly result proved in the companion paper~\cite{OpenAICoarseNovikov2026}. If $C^*(X)$ denotes the operator-norm closure of $\C[X]$, the canonical quotient $C^*_{\max}(X)\to C^*(X)$ carries the maximal index to the reduced index. Our class $\alpha$ therefore also lies in the reduced assembly kernel. The stronger conclusion requires a proof in the universal norm: vanishing of a reduced index alone does not imply vanishing of its maximal lift. \subsection{Context and prior work} Roe developed coarse cohomology and coarse index theory as tools for large-scale geometry and index-theoretic questions on noncompact manifolds \cite{Roe93}. Higson and Roe formulated and studied the coarse Baum--Connes conjecture in this framework \cite{HR95}. The connection with Novikov-type questions is that injectivity retains the topological information carried by higher indices. The present theorem concerns the non-equivariant coarse assembly map displayed above. Several fundamental positive results relate assembly to geometric control. Yu proved coarse assembly isomorphism for bounded-geometry spaces of finite asymptotic dimension \cite[Theorem~7.1]{Yu98} and established the coarse Baum--Connes conjecture for bounded-geometry spaces admitting a uniform embedding into Hilbert space \cite{Yu00}. Skandalis, Tu, and Yu developed the coarse-groupoid approach and its relation to these embedding methods \cite{STY02}. These results explain the importance of geometric hypotheses when seeking injectivity or isomorphism theorems. Earlier failures of injectivity also show why bounded geometry and the domain of assembly matter. Yu constructed a coarse-assembly kernel class for a space without bounded geometry \cite[Section~8, Proposition~8.1]{Yu98}. Dranishnikov, Ferry, and Weinberger constructed integral index-kernel examples with locally finite K-homology as domain; their kernel classes disappear under coarsening \cite[Section~6, Remark~6.2]{DFW03}. Here the class belongs to the actual Rips colimit for a bounded-geometry graph union and remains nonzero after rationalization. [theorem] \label{thm:main} There exist finite connected graphs $(X_j)_{j\geq1}$ with a common finite bound on vertex degree, a coarse disjoint union $X=\bigsqcup_{j\geq1}X_j$, and an infinite-order class $\alpha\in\KX_1(X)$ such that \[ \mu_X^{\max}(\alpha)=0. \] Consequently, $\mu_X^{\max}\otimes\Q$ is not injective.

=== MANUSCRIPT: A counterexample to the coarse Novikov conjecture (September-23-2026) [preprints/A-counterexample-to-the-coarse-Novikov-conjecture-September-23-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We disprove the coarse Novikov conjecture: ordinary coarse assembly need not be rationally injective for uniformly discrete spaces of bounded geometry. We construct a coarse disjoint union of finite graphs of uniformly bounded degree and an infinite-order class in its degree-one coarse K-homology whose image under ordinary coarse assembly in the K-theory of the reduced, locally compact Roe algebra vanishes.
INTRODUCTION (start): Coarse index theory relates the large-scale geometry of a space to the K-theory of an algebra of controlled operators. Its assembly map sends geometric K-homology classes to their analytic indices. The injectivity question asks whether that index retains the information in a class after one has passed to large scales. Roe developed this viewpoint and its Novikov-type formulation \cite{Roe93}; Higson and Roe clarified the role of bounded geometry and coarsened K-homology \cite{HR95}. Here we construct a bounded-geometry space for which ordinary reduced coarse assembly loses a class of infinite order. A metric space $X$ is uniformly discrete if distinct points have distance bounded below by a positive constant. It has bounded geometry if, for every $R<\infty$, the cardinalities of its radius-$R$ balls have a common finite bound. For such a space, let $P_r(X)$ be the Rips complex: its simplices are the finite subsets of $X$ of diameter at most $r$. We use locally finite analytic K-homology and its coarsening, \[ K_i^{\lf}(Y)=\KK_i(C_0(Y),\C),\qquad KX_i(X)=\varinjlim_{r\to\infty}K_i^{\lf}(P_r(X)). \] Here and throughout, $i$ is taken modulo two. The reduced, locally compact Roe algebra $C^*(X)$ is the operator-norm closure of the locally compact finite-propagation operators on $\ell^2(X)\otimes\HH_0$, where $\HH_0$ is an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space. Section~\ref{sec:conventions} specifies these conventions and the ordinary coarse assembly map \[ \mu_X:KX_i(X)\longrightarrow K_i(C^*(X)). \] The coarse Baum--Connes conjecture asks whether this map is an isomorphism \cite[Conjecture~6.6]{HR95}. The rational coarse Novikov assertion is that $\mu_X\otimes\id_{\Q}$ is injective. Passing through all Rips scales is essential: a cycle at one scale can bound at a larger scale and therefore represent zero in $KX_*$. An obstruction to injectivity must survive every such coarsening while its analytic index vanishes. Yu proved coarse assembly isomorphism for bounded-geometry spaces of finite asymptotic dimension \cite[Theorem~7.1]{Yu98}, and subsequently for those admitting a coarse embedding into Hilbert space \cite{Yu00}. Skandalis, Tu and Yu placed the latter result in a groupoid framework \cite{STY02}. Further positive injectivity results include coarse embeddings into uniformly convex Banach spaces \cite[Theorem~1.1]{KY06} and fibred coarse embeddings into Hilbert space \cite[Theorem~34]{FS14}. These theorems impose geometric hypotheses beyond bounded geometry. Our result shows that bounded geometry alone does not suffice. We prove the following integral statement. [statement] \subsection*{Predecessors and the scope of the obstruction} Yu already constructed a non-bounded-geometry example with a nontrivial coarse K-homology class of zero index, using spheres of increasing dimension and size \cite[Section~8, Proposition~8.1]{Yu98}. Dranishnikov, Ferry and Weinberger constructed integral noninjectivity examples for a map from locally finite K-homology to Roe-algebra K-theory, also allowing spaces without bounded geometry \cite[Introduction, pp.~920--921]{DFW}. Their Remark~6.2 distinguishes those examples from the Rips-coarsened formulation: the relevant classes die under coarsening. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} retains bounded geometry and detects its class in the actual direct limit. Higson, Lafforgue and Skandalis constructed injectivity and surjectivity obstructions for several groupoid assembly maps \cite{HLS}. Their Hausdorff groupoid example in Section~5 combines a degree-one Bott class with a Kazhdan projection to produce a kernel class. This is a close methodological antecedent of the winding and constant-vector averaging used here. Their coarse-space construction in Section~6 instead establishes non-surjectivity for specific congruence expander unions \cite[Section~6, Proposition~11]{HLS}. [theorem] \label{thm:main} There is a uniformly discrete bounded-geometry metric space $X$, which is a coarse disjoint union of finite connected graphs of uniformly bounded degree, and an infinite-order element $\alpha\in KX_1(X)$ such that \[ \mu_X(\alpha)=0\quad\text{in }K_1(C^*(X)). \] Consequently, ordinary coarse assembly for $X$ fails to be injective both integrally and after tensoring its domain and target with $\Q$.
