FAMILY 251: Amenability, unitarizability, and strong Ulam stability
SUBJECT: Group theory
OPENAI SUMMARY: Resolves Dixmier's problem for all discrete groups: amenability is equivalent to every uniformly bounded Hilbert-space representation being similar to a unitary representation. For countable discrete groups, amenability is also equivalent to strong Ulam stability: sufficiently accurate unitary approximate representations are uniformly close in operator norm to genuine representations on the same, possibly infinite-dimensional, Hilbert space.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Unitarizability implies amenability for discrete groups (September-23-2026) [preprints/Unitarizability-Implies-Amenability-for-Countable-Groups-September-23-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove that a discrete group is amenable if and only if every uniformly bounded representation on a complex Hilbert space is similar to a unitary representation. This resolves Dixmier's unitarizability problem affirmatively for discrete groups.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} A representation \(\pi:G\to\mathrm{GL}(H)\) of a discrete group on a complex Hilbert space is \emph{uniformly bounded} if \[ |\pi|:=\sup_{g\in G}\norm{\pi(g)}<\infty. \] Here \(\mathrm{GL}(H)\) consists of the bounded invertible operators. The representation is \emph{unitarizable} if some bounded invertible \(S\) makes every \(S\pi(g)S^{-1}\) unitary. Equivalently, the Hilbert space admits an equivalent inner product invariant under the representation. A group is unitarizable when every uniformly bounded representation on every complex Hilbert space is unitarizable. The similarity may depend on the representation; its condition number \(\norm{S}\norm{S^{-1}}\) is not assumed to have a common bound. A discrete group \(G\) is \emph{amenable} if there is a positive linear functional \(m:\ell^\infty(G)\to\C\) with \(m(1)=1\) and \(m(L_gf)=m(f)\), where \((L_gf)(x)=f(g^{-1}x)\). The one-operator case goes back to Sz.-Nagy: an invertible operator whose integer powers are uniformly bounded is similar to a unitary operator \cite[Theorem~I]{SzNagy1947}. His generalized-limit construction of an invariant inner product preceded the invariant-mean argument of Day and Dixmier \cite{Day1950,Dixmier1950}. Thus amenability implies unitarizability. Dixmier explicitly asked whether this property characterizes groups admitting an invariant mean \cite[\S5, p.~221]{Dixmier1950}. The question asks whether changing the Hilbert norm separately for every uniformly bounded representation already forces the existence of such a mean. [statement] We work in ZFC. All Hilbert spaces below are complex, and \(\N=\{1,2,\ldots\}\). The main point is the absence of a similarity for one representation, without any a priori control on possible similarities. For an uncountable group the induced witness may be nonseparable, as is allowed by the definition of unitarizability used here. \subsection*{Prior work and the remaining obstruction} Early counterexamples showed that uniform boundedness alone does not force unitarizability. Ehrenpreis and Mautner constructed examples for the locally compact group \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)\) \cite{EhrenpreisMautner1955}; explicit families for free groups include those of Pytlik and Szwarc \cite{PytlikSzwarc1986}. Nonunitarizability passes from a subgroup to the ambient group by induction. The free-group examples therefore cover groups containing nonabelian free subgroups, but leave nonamenable groups without such subgroups to be understood. Quantitative unitarizability gives one route to amenability. Pisier proved that a discrete group \(G\) is amenable exactly when there are constants \(C\) and \(\alpha<3\), depending only on \(G\), such that every uniformly bounded representation admits a unitarizer with condition number at most \(C|\pi|^\alpha\) \cite[Theorem~0.1]{Pisier1998}. The estimate is uniform over all Hilbert spaces and representations of the fixed group. Unitarizability itself implies a polynomial bound of this form for some finite exponent \cite[Theorem~4.1]{Pisier2004}; the additional content of the amenability criterion is the threshold \(\alpha<3\). Random forests gave a route beyond free subgroups. Epstein and Monod used invariant random forests to relate unitarizability to \(\ell^2\)-Betti numbers and cost. Their estimates imply nonunitarizability for residually finite groups with positive first \(\ell^2\)-Betti number, and for finitely generated residually finite groups of cost greater than one \cite[Theorems~1.1--1.3]{EpsteinMonod2009}. Combining their criterion with group constructions, Osin obtained finitely generated torsion and torsion-free nonunitarizable groups containing no nonabelian free subgroup \cite[Theorems~1.2--1.3]{Osin2009}. A different approach uses measurable actions. [theorem] \label{thm:main} A discrete group is amenable if and only if it is unitarizable. For every discrete nonamenable group \(G\) and every \(\varepsilon>0\), there are a complex Hilbert space \(\mathcal K\) and a representation \(\pi:G\to\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal K)\) with \(|\pi|\le1+\varepsilon\) that is not similar to a unitary representation. If \(G\) is countable, \(\mathcal K\) may be chosen separable.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Strong Ulam Stability Characterizes Amenability (October-5-2026) [preprints/Strong-Ulam-Stability-Characterizes-Amenability-October-5-2026/build/strong-ulam-stability.tex]
ABSTRACT: A countable discrete group is amenable if and only if it is strongly Ulam stable: every sufficiently accurate unitary almost representation, on any complex Hilbert space, is uniformly close in operator norm to a genuine representation on the same space. We prove the converse to Kazhdan's amenable stability theorem, answering the question of Burger, Ozawa, and Thom. The inclusion of infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces is essential.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} Let $G$ be a countable discrete group and let $H$ be a complex Hilbert space. For a map $\mu:G\to\U(H)$ with $\mu(e)=I$, put \[ \defect(\mu)=\sup_{g,h\in G}\norm{\mu(gh)-\mu(g)\mu(h)}. \] The group is \emph{strongly Ulam stable} if, for every $\varepsilon>0$, there is $\delta>0$ such that every such map with $\defect(\mu)\le\delta$ is within $\varepsilon$, uniformly on $G$, of a unitary representation on the same space $H$. All norms are operator norms. The number $\delta$ must work simultaneously for all Hilbert spaces, including infinite-dimensional ones. A group is amenable if $\ell^\infty(G)$ admits a positive linear functional $m$ with $m(1)=1$ and $m(L_gf)=m(f)$, where $(L_gf)(x)=f(g^{-1}x)$. Our main result is the following characterization. [statement] Kazhdan proved the implication from amenability to strong Ulam stability \cite{Kazhdan}; see also \cite[Theorems~3.1 and~3.2]{BurgerOzawaThom}. We prove the converse. More precisely, for every nonamenable countable group and every $\delta>0$, we construct a separable Hilbert space $H$ and a normalized map $\mu:G\to\U(H)$ with $\defect(\mu)<\delta$ such that \begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-goal} \sup_{g\in G}\norm{\mu(g)-\rho(g)}>\frac1{10} \quad\text{for every unitary representation }\rho:G\to\U(H). \end{equation} \subsection*{History and context} The uniform stability problem asks whether an approximate homomorphism can be corrected without losing uniform control over the group. Kazhdan's 1982 theorem supplies this correction for amenable groups; his work also gives unstable almost representations of fundamental groups of closed orientable surfaces of genus at least two \cite{Kazhdan,BurgerOzawaThom}. Burger, Ozawa, and Thom proved that every group containing a nonabelian free subgroup fails strong Ulam stability \cite[Theorem~1.2]{BurgerOzawaThom}. They explicitly asked whether strong Ulam stability characterizes amenability \cite[p.~111]{BurgerOzawaThom}. Alpeev subsequently proved instability for restricted wreath products $A\wr G$ when $A$ is a nontrivial countable abelian group and $G$ is countable and nonamenable \cite[Theorem~B]{Alpeev}. His use of measured equivalence relations reaches examples without nonabelian free subgroups. The inclusion of infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces is essential. The term \emph{Ulam stability}, without ``strong'', commonly denotes the uniform statement restricted to finite-dimensional unitary groups \cite[Definition~2.1]{BurgerOzawaThom}. Nonamenable groups can satisfy that property. Glebsky, Lubotzky, Monod, and Rangarajan developed asymptotic cohomology and proved finite-dimensional stability for broad classes of higher-rank lattices \cite{GlebskyLubotzkyMonodRangarajan}. Fournier-Facio and Rangarajan applied this theory to wreath products over infinite amenable groups and to Thompson groups \cite[Theorems~1.2 and~1.3]{FournierFacioRangarajan}. These results concern a different stability property. The failure of the corresponding wreath-product statement for strong stability is explained in \cite[Remark~6.1]{FournierFacioRangarajan}. \subsection*{The construction} The proof starts with a nontrivial minimal strongly proximal action of $G$ on a compact space, supplied by Furstenberg's boundary theory \cite{Furstenberg,KalantarKennedy}. From this action we obtain finite choices of steps in $G$, each marked $\E$ or $\F$ at every possible departure point. The marks have two opposing counting properties: all but a small proportion of steps from a fixed point are $\E$, whereas all but a small proportion of steps ending at a fixed point are $\F$. They also satisfy a compatibility condition along paths: an $\E$ step can never be followed by an $\F$ step. We use these paths to construct a Hilbert space $H$ with finitely many levels and a copy at each group element. Fix a set $\cC$ of at most three translated markings.
