=== MANUSCRIPT: A hyperbolic group with no geometric CAT(0) action (September-25-2026) [preprints/A-hyperbolic-group-with-no-geometric-CAT0-action-September-25-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We construct a hyperbolic group with a finite classifying space that admits no geometric action on a proper complete CAT(0) space. Consequently, a finite aspherical simplicial complex with a linear combinatorial disk-filling inequality need not have a finite locally CAT(0) homotopy model, and hence need not have a finite locally CAT(−1) model. Here metric models carry geodesic length metrics inducing the given complex topology.
INTRODUCTION (start): \label{sec:introduction} A linear isoperimetric inequality is a large-scale expression of negative curvature: for finitely presented groups, it characterizes word hyperbolicity. Local comparison geometry is more restrictive. The geometric realization question asks whether every hyperbolic group admits a geometric action on a proper $\CAT(0)$ space, or on a proper $\CAT(-1)$ space. We construct a group for which neither action exists. Throughout the paper, a \emph{geometric action} is a proper, cocompact action by isometries, and metric models of finite complexes carry geodesic length metrics inducing the given complex topology. [statement] The proof is an existence argument and does not give an effective size for $K$. The filling constant may depend on the selected finite presentation. \subsection{Context} The realization problem goes back to the development of hyperbolic groups. In his account of that development, Gromov recalls introducing linear isoperimetry as a group-theoretic form of negative curvature and then spending years seeking geometric realizations by negatively curved spaces~\cite[p.~396]{RaussenSkau2010}. The equivalence between linear filling and word hyperbolicity became part of the foundational theory~\cite{Gromov1987}; see also \cite[Theorem~III.$\Gamma$.2.6 and Remark~III.$\Gamma$.2.7(2)]{BH1999}. The $\CAT(-1)$ realization question appears as Problem~H11 in Baumslag--Myasnikov--Shpilrain's 1999 problem list~\cite{BaumslagMyasnikovShpilrain1999}. Both the $\CAT(-1)$ and $\CAT(0)$ questions appear as open problems in Stark's 2025 survey~\cite[Open Problem~2.15]{Stark2025} and in the March 2026 preprint of Duchesne--Simon~\cite[Question~W]{DuchesneSimon2026}. There are several successful ways to obtain such geometric actions. Finitely generated free groups act on trees and closed hyperbolic manifold groups act on hyperbolic space. More generally, a finite complex carrying a locally $\CAT(-1)$ or locally $\CAT(0)$ geodesic length metric inducing its topology has a universal cover with the corresponding global curvature bound; its deck action is geometric~\cite[Theorem~II.4.1]{BH1999}. Cubulation provides another route to $\CAT(0)$ models. For example, Ollivier--Wise prove that random groups in the density model at density less than $1/6$ act freely and cocompactly on $\CAT(0)$ cube complexes with probability tending to one as relator length grows, with the number of generators fixed~\cite[Theorem~10.4]{OllivierWise2011}. Thus random presentations can produce geometric $\CAT(0)$ actions as well as hyperbolicity; the relations used here must enforce an additional obstruction. Dimension restrictions already yield obstructions. Brady--Crisp~\cite[Theorem~2]{BradyCrisp2007} construct infinitely many torsion-free hyperbolic groups of geometric dimension two which admit no proper isometric action on a proper two-dimensional $\CAT(0)$ space. Their Theorem~1 gives a hyperbolic group of $\CAT(0)$ dimension two and $\CAT(-1)$ dimension three. These conclusions rely on constraints on translation lengths in two-dimensional targets. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} excludes geometric $\CAT(0)$ actions in every dimension. Its finite-model consequence also allows the entire simplicial complex to change within its homotopy type: any compatible locally $\CAT(0)$ geodesic length metric on such a model would recover a geometric action by passing to the universal cover. Random-presentation proofs of hyperbolicity combine counting for bounded diagrams with a local-to-global argument. Our treatment of repeated relator occurrences is related to the multiplicity and constraint methods in Ollivier~\cite[Section~2.2]{Ollivier2004} and Antoniuk--Friedgut--\L uczak~\cite[Section~3]{AntoniukFriedgutLuczak2017}. [theorem] \label{thm:main} There exist a finite connected two-dimensional aspherical simplicial complex $K$ and a constant $C<\infty$ with the following properties. \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}] \item Every combinatorial edge loop of length $\ell$ in the universal cover of $K$ bounds a disk diagram with at most $C\ell$ triangular faces. \item The group $\pi_1K$ admits no geometric action on a proper complete $\CAT(0)$ space. \end{enumerate} In particular, $\pi_1K$ is word-hyperbolic, and no finite simplicial complex homotopy equivalent to $K$ admits a locally $\CAT(0)$ geodesic length metric inducing its given topology. Consequently, no such model admits a compatible locally $\CAT(-1)$ geodesic length metric.

def IsSegment {X : Type*} [MetricSpace X] (γ : ℝ → X) (x y : X) : Prop :=
  γ 0 = x ∧ γ 1 = y ∧
  ∀ s ∈ Set.Icc (0 : ℝ) 1, ∀ t ∈ Set.Icc (0 : ℝ) 1,
    dist (γ s) (γ t) = |s - t| * dist x y

  ∀ x y : X, ∃ γ, IsSegment γ x y
def CATZero (X : Type*) [MetricSpace X] : Prop :=
  ∀ x y : X, ∃ γ, IsSegment γ x y ∧
    ∀ z : X, ∀ t ∈ Set.Icc (0 : ℝ) 1,
      dist z (γ t) ^ 2 ≤ (1 - t) * dist z x ^ 2 + t * dist z y ^ 2 -
        t * (1 - t) * dist x y ^ 2
def CATNegOne (X : Type*) [MetricSpace X] : Prop :=
  ∀ x y : X, ∃ γ, IsSegment γ x y ∧

        (2 * Real.cosh (dist x y / 2))
