## Main statement
**Thm cor:lie** (01-introduction.tex:9-14) states: for n ≥ 1, a locally compact, second-countable, Hausdorff group acting faithfully and jointly continuously on a connected, Hausdorff, second-countable n-manifold without boundary is a Lie group. These are the standard hypotheses of the conjecture.

**Thm main** (01:30-35) is the p-adic exclusion: every continuous Z_p-action on such a manifold has nonzero kernel.
- **This is exactly the known p-adic reformulation** (Lee 1997; Pardon 2013, intro).
- The headline follows from it by the classical reduction, which rests on Gleason–Montgomery–Zippin–Yamabe and Newman. The reduction is *cited, not re-proved* (06:518-526).

**Corollary** (01:42-55): Pardon's Conjecture 1.4 holds. Almost periodic homeomorphisms have an iterate that lies on an R-flow.

**Known cases:**
- n ≤ 2: classical.
- n = 3: Pardon 2013.
- Lipschitz (Repovš–Ščepin), Hölder (Maleshich), quasiconformal (Martin) and symplectic C⁰-Hamiltonian (Shelukhin) cases.

The paper uses none of these known cases. It treats all n uniformly.

## Proof architecture
1. **Chart reduction.** Newman's theorem gives an open subgroup G ≅ Z_p acting faithfully and orientation-preservingly on a connected invariant chart set O ⊂ ℝⁿ.
2. **Stabilization and test map.** Stabilize to W = O × ℝ^{d−n} with **d odd ≥ 3**. Use the collapse c: S^d → W⁺ and the orbit map q: W⁺ → Y.
   - **Averaged test.** Average a ball pinch over a small open subgroup with Haar measure and normalize. This gives a: Y → S^d with deg(aqc) = 1.
   - This is the *only* place where small subgroups are used. A finite group cannot give a degree-one test.
3. **Test categories T(X).** These are generated by Rf_*ℝ_B for *arbitrary continuous* f from compact polyhedra. Stalks may be infinite-dimensional. The paper proves:
   - Verdier duality restricts to T (via proper pushforward);
   - Woolf-style homotopy invariance;
   - Balmer–Walter localization;
   - quotients are germs near the complement;
   - open covers give dense inclusions;
   - dense doubling: kernel and cokernel are killed by 2.
4. **Fixed lattice.**
   - A Mayer–Vietoris induction compares T with the semialgebraic subcategory P up to 2^{e_j}, with e_j = 3(9^j−1)/4.
   - P has integral generic signatures (Hardt).
   - Together these give im E_d(S^d) ⊂ L_d⁻¹ℤ ū_d with L_d = 2^{e_d}.
   - Because d is odd, E_d(X) → W_d(T(X)/T(U)) is an *integral* isomorphism for contractible open U.
5. **Character classes.**
   - The orbit sheaf q_*ℂ splits by characters Λ = μ_{p^∞}, and the projectors are self-adjoint away from b = t(y_∞).
   - For a finite partition, z_i(t) is the integral lift of [A,α] + [A, α(2e_i − 1)].
   - The classes sum to 2[A,α], are covariant, and are homotopy invariant (via the close-pair space C_d).
6. **Concentration.**
   - Faithfulness gives a region V with p^k labelled sheets and a sheet multiplier β.
   - Rational orbit injectivity plus the K(ℚ,d) telescope give h ≃ D_s a with h constant off V.
   - Multiplication by β cycles the transversal partition D_i = ζ^i D_0, so the z_i(h) are all equal.
7. **Contradiction.**
   - s·z̄_i(a) = z̄_i(h) for every i, so the z̄_i(a) are all equal.
   - They sum to 4ū_d, so each equals (4/p^k)ū_d.
   - This lies outside L_d⁻¹ℤ once p^k > 4L_d. L_d is fixed before k is chosen.

## Eureka candidates
1. **I42: integral character classes z_i(t). Provisional N3, type F1 with an F5 flavour.**
   - *For:* the invariant is integral, defined for each character part of a profinite action with infinite-dimensional stalks, and homotopy invariant even though the decomposition fails at ∞. It needs two tricks:
     - "Twice the summand" without splitting idempotents.
     - Odd d, so that the localization lift is integral.

     Real coefficients split the characters, and Witt groups restore the integrality that ℂ-homology loses. Everything runs through this item.
   - *Against:* isotypic G-signatures for finite groups are classical (Atiyah–Singer; equivariant and hermitian Witt groups). A judge may grade it N2 as "the profinite, localized version".
2. **I49: equal parts in a fixed lattice (L_d fixed before k). Provisional N3, type F5.** Its shape is the classical divisibility contradiction (Smith theory and transfers), so most of its novelty lives in I42 and I26.
3. **I37: moving a multiple of the test into any open set of a wild orbit space. N2.** The pieces are standard: rational transfer, and the odd-sphere telescope already used in Dranishnikov–Ferry–Weinberger 2003, which the paper cites.
4. **I32: averaged degree-one test. N2, type F3.** It is classical compact-group averaging, cf. Repovš–Ščepin.
5. **I23 / I07: bounded comparison and the T categories. N2.** They adapt Woolf 2008 and Ranicki–Weiss 2010, both named by the paper.

## Correctness audit
**Verdict: no gap found, but unverified. Treat as an extraordinary unreviewed claim.**

I checked every proof line by line. The points where I verified the details:
- **Chart reduction.** The clopen argument is correct, Newman applies, and 2H handles orientation when p = 2.
- **Averaged test.** Uniform continuity on G × W⁺ makes it work. It correctly fails for finite groups.
- **Finite sheets and β-equivariance.** Correct.
- **Move-test.** Correct. An elementary cross-check also passes: the m clopen sheet preimages force m | s, which is consistent with z̄_0(h) = 4s/m being integral.
- **Cutoff and germ theorem.** Correct.
- **Contractible-quotient isomorphism.** My initial worry was that for d ≡ 1 (mod 4) the boundary term E_{d−1}(U) = ℤ might block the lift. It does not: split injectivity of E_{d−1}(U) → E_{d−1}(X) kills the boundary.
- **Dense doubling.** Correct; it is Hornbostel–Schlichting cofinality.
- **Exponent recursion.** Correct.
- **Real orthogonality.** The adjoint computation is correct.
- **Sum isometry.** S = ΣE_jP_j satisfies S² = 1 and S†Q₀S = Q.
- **β intertwining.** T M_β = M_{ζ⁻¹} M_β T holds.
- **Close-pair homotopy.** Correct.
- **Final arithmetic and quantifier order.** Correct.

"Proves too much" tests, all passed:
- **Finite cyclic actions.** There is no degree-one test, and the parts stay integral.
- **Circle actions.** There are no small subgroups.
- **Raymond–Williams Z_p actions on compacta.** The machine needs an oriented open subset of S^d as the source, so it does not apply.
- **Profinite groups such as (ℤ/p)^ℕ.** The machine would exclude them too, which is consistent with Newman.
- **Where the proof bites.** For finite groups each character part has an integral local signature. For Z_p the D_i parts have infinite rank, and the invariant assigns them the Haar fraction 4/p^k, which the lattice forbids. This is the exact mechanism of the contradiction.

**Where gaps could hide:**
1. **The Witt theory of the non-constructible categories T.** This covers the Balmer–Walter hypotheses for T and its Verdier quotients (including the TR4⁺ axiom, asserted to pass to quotients), the germ description, and dense doubling in quotients. **This is the crux.** An error here voids the fixed lattice L_d.
2. **Homotopy invariance under non-proper homotopies** of open test spaces. It rests on compact-support pushforward, is used everywhere, and looks right.
3. **The sign of the induced H⁰ pairing** for both odd classes of d (Appendix A). Any odd d can be chosen, so this is not fatal.
4. **The cited reduction and Serre locators.** They are standard; I did not check the exact locators.

The short length (about 25 pages, with no dimension theory of orbit spaces) is itself a reason for caution. There is **no machine check**: this audit is one careful reading.
