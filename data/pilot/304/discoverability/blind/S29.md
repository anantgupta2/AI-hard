# Proposal: a sheet-divisibility lemma for the Witt degree

## The invariant: the Witt degree of a test on Y

Fix the chart ball B ⊂ W from tool 4. For x ∈ E_d(Y), coming from a map f: Y -> S^d, define

  δ(x) := deg_W( (f∘q)|_B ) ∈ E_d(S^d), read as an element of L_d^{-1} Z u_d (tool 2).

Because f∘q is G-invariant, δ(x) equals its own Haar average ∫_G deg(f∘q∘g|_B) dg. So δ is a well-defined homomorphism on the subgroup of E_d(Y) generated by such tests, and it is additive under multiples. Tool 4 gives δ(a) = 1.

## Lemma (sheet divisibility)

Let V ⊂ Y be the open set from tool 5 at level k, with m = p^k sheets, and suppose h: Y -> S^d is constant (equal to the basepoint) on Y \ V. Then

  δ(h) ∈ p^k · L_d^{-1} Z.

**Proof sketch.**
1. h∘q is supported in q^{-1}V. The map q factors as q^{-1}V -> V_k := q^{-1}V/p^kG -> V, and by tool 5 the second map is a trivial m-sheeted cover V_k = S_1 ⊔ … ⊔ S_m. The quotient G/p^kG = Z/p^k permutes the sheets simply transitively.
2. Pull back along the proper maps involved (E_d is covariant for proper maps and homotopy invariant, tool 1). The class of h∘q then splits as a sum of m contributions c_i, one for each sheet: c_i is the degree carried by q^{-1}(S_i) ∩ B, after G-averaging. The multiplier β satisfies β(gw) = ζ^g β(w), so a generator g ∈ G carries the contribution of sheet i to that of sheet i+1, and h∘q is g-invariant. Hence after Haar averaging all the c_i are equal.
3. Each c_i is the pushforward of a class from E_d(S^d), namely h restricted to one sheet, which is a homeomorphic copy of V. By tool 2, c_i ∈ L_d^{-1} Z. So δ(h) = m·c_1 ∈ p^k L_d^{-1} Z. ∎

## Companion lemma (bounded moving)

In tool 6 the multiple N with N·a ≃ h can be chosen with N | L_d, independent of k and V.

**Sketch.** Let U ⊂ S^d be the complement of a closed small ball D around a regular value. U is contractible, so tool 3 gives E_d(S^d) ≅ W_d(T(S^d)/T(U)) over Z. To move a off Y \ V, collapse the part of the test lying outside V into U. That is, replace a by c∘a, where c: S^d -> S^d is a degree-one map that crushes the complement of a small ball into the basepoint, followed by a homotopy along the contractible U. The obstruction to doing this is a class that becomes zero after tensoring with Q (tool 2: E_d(S^d) ⊗ Q is rank one and spanned by u_d). Its order is bounded by the index of E_d(S^d) in the lattice L_d^{-1} Z u_d, which divides L_d. So N = L_d always works.

This is where the proof has to be checked carefully. The torsion of E_d(S^d) and of the Verdier quotient is controlled by tool 2 alone, so it is uniform in V and k.

## Contradiction with faithfulness

Suppose the Z_p action is faithful. Then tools 4 and 5 apply at every level k: by Newman/Yang, the sheets exist for every k precisely because p^kG still acts nontrivially. Fix k with p^k > L_d^2, and take the V given by tool 5 at this level. The two lemmas give

  N = N·δ(a) = δ(N a) = δ(h) ∈ p^k L_d^{-1} Z,

so p^k divides N·L_d, which divides L_d^2. This contradicts p^k > L_d^2 (for p = 2 as well, since L_d is fixed before k is chosen). So no faithful action exists. By the GMZY reduction, this gives Hilbert–Smith.

## Why this is the right pivot

Degree-type arguments over Z fail here because Y has cohomological dimension n+2 (Yang / Bredon–Raymond–Williams), so ordinary degrees on Y are meaningless. The Witt-group degree of the test categories T(Y) still makes sense on such a quotient, because the categories are generated by maps from compact polyhedra. The only arithmetic input is that its denominators are uniformly bounded: the lattice L_d^{-1} Z with L_d a power of 2. The dynamics then forces divisibility by arbitrarily large p^k, which arithmetic forbids.

In SAT terms, the averaged test map is a single "extension variable" (a degree-one class). Divisibility by p^k at every level would be an unbounded family of learned clauses, and the fixed lattice refutes all of them at once.
