## C1 in detail
The ramp is u = Σ_{k<ℓ}(v_{≤2^{k+ℓ}} − v_{≤2^k}). It gives multiplicities 1,2,…,ℓ,…,2,1 on the dyadic magnitude bins.

**Why it matters.**
- A single window (1,2^w] has energy ≈ w against error^p ≈ H^{p−2}. That forces log K ≈ (log n)^{p−2}, which is subpolynomial only for 2<p<3.
- The tent has energy ≈ ℓ^{p+1} against error^p ≈ H^{p−2}ℓ. That gives (log n)^{1−2/p} for every p>2.

**Test results.**
- **Test 2 (non-analytic): passes.** The ramp is nowhere in the goal or in L.
- **Test 3 (load-bearing): passes.** In the Lean, `ramp_sum_bounded_law` dominates 28% of the DAG, and the ramp branch is 48% of the mathematical core.
- **Test 1 (no near-equivalent): fails at the level of mechanism.**
  - Swap partial sums S_k for truncations and the identity becomes Σ_{k<ℓ}(S_{k+ℓ}−S_k) = ℓ(V_{ℓ,2ℓ} − σ_ℓ). Here V is the de la Vallée Poussin mean and σ the Fejér mean, so the ramp is the classical trapezoid/triangle summability window.
  - The reason it helps is the familiar "spread a cutoff over many scales" effect from capacity theory (the logarithmic cutoff). Errors at different scales add incoherently, while the signal adds coherently.
  - The application itself is uncited and unprecedented in my searches: a tent of magnitude truncations of a Lévy-measure vector field, corrected by a bounded projection.
  - Under the strict rule, importing a known device is transfer plus adaptation, so the grade is N2.
- **Pivot distance:** about 1 (estimated). The paper motivates the ramp in two lines.

It is the item to re-open. If it is promoted, its F type is F3. It already has Lean declarations (`dyadicRamp`, DyadicRamps.lean:18).
