## 1. Main statement
**Paper, Thm 1.1 (`paper.tex:75-102`).** Fix 1<p<∞ with p≠2. Set γ(p)=2−p for p<2 and γ(p)=1−2/p for p>2. Then for every D>1 and every n≥2:

  log n / log(1+2D) ≤ d_p(n,D) ≤ exp(C_{p,D} (log n)^{γ(p)}).

For exact embeddings, ⌊(n−1)/4⌋² ≤ d_p(n,1) ≤ C(n,2) for n≥9. Consequently log d_p(n,D)/log n → 0 when D>1, and → 2 when D=1.

- **Definition of d_p(n,D).** It is the least d such that every n-point subset of *any* L_p space has images in ℓ_p^d with distortion ≤ D. The maps are nonlinear and a scale s>0 is allowed.
- **Paraphrase.** For p≠2 in (1,∞), every n-point subset of L_p embeds into ℓ_p^{n^{o(1)}} with any fixed distortion D>1. Exact embeddings need Θ(n²) coordinates. This answers the nonlinear sublinear-dimension question (Johnson–Lindenstrauss Problem 3; Naor's survey) affirmatively. The previous best bounds were O(n) for p<2 (Schechtman) and n^{p/2} log n for p>2.
- **Comparator.** `ComparatorChallenges/SubpolynomialLp.lean` targets `OAI.SubpolynomialLp.source_main`, with solution module `OAI.Analysis.LpDimension.Main`. Permitted axioms: propext, Quot.sound, Classical.choice.
  - `GoodDimension` quantifies over every measure space (Ω : Type u), every μ and every injective `x : Fin n → Lp ℝ (ofReal p) μ`.
  - `dimension` is the sInf of the d satisfying it.
  - The bounds use ℕ-division for ⌊(n−1)/4⌋ and `n.choose 2`, and the limits are stated for D ≥ 1.
- **Match check.** The match is exact, and the definitions are identical to `Model.lean`. The comparator also asserts that the infimum is attained, which follows from Ball's bound.
- **Formalized vs claimed.** All of Thm 1.1 is formalized. Two qualifications:
  - The localization lemma is formalized only for graphs in which every pair of vertices is adjacent (the complete graph). That is all the proof uses.
  - The Lean route differs from the paper (§7).

## 4. Proof architecture
1. **Discretize.** Ball and Carathéodory reduce the input to x_i ∈ ℓ_p^m. The normalized gradients are V = im(diag(1/δ)·incidence). (I02, I03)
2. **Weighted moment criterion** (I04). It suffices, for each positive test probability λ on pairs, to give a random F ∈ V with:
   - ‖F‖_∞ ≤ K;
   - Σ λ_e |E|F_e|^p − b| ≤ ηb;
   - b and K uniform in λ.

   Minimax then yields one distribution, and Hoeffding sampling gives d ≈ K^{2p}/(ηb)² · log n.
3. **Projection with controlled row sums** (I05–I07).
   - GGNS gives ‖|Q|‖₂→₂ ≤ 2 log n for the transfer-current matrix, so Σ σ_e R_e² ≤ (2 log n)².
   - A Brouwer reweighting with σ ≥ λ/2 makes *every* row sum of the ℓ2(σ)-projection P onto V at most H = 4 log(2n).
4. **Homogeneous increments** (I08–I10). The Lévy intensity p r^{-p-1} dr times g_e(t) gives every edge the same magnitude law. Signed Poisson sampling stays inside V.
5. **p<2** (I11–I12). Take a p-stable-like Y ∈ V, clip it at T, and project back: F = P[Y]_T.
   - The additive error is O(H^{2−p}).
   - The capped common moment is b = E min(|Y|,T/H)^p ≈ log(T/H).
   - Choosing T = exp(A H^{2−p}) gives log K = O((log n)^{2−p}).
6. **p>2** (I13–I16).
   - The ramp u = Σ_{k<ℓ}(v_{≤2^{k+ℓ}} − v_{≤2^k}) has p-energy ≈ ℓ^{p+1}.
   - Its projection error is O(H^{p−2}(ℓ+1)), by dyadic L∞/L2 interpolation.
   - Choose ℓ ≈ H^{1−2/p}.
   - Poisson-sample Z = Pu, using a Rosenthal-type bound with leading constant 1+ε (App. B).
   - Discard the sample on a rare bad event. This gives log K = O((log n)^{1−2/p}).
7. **Lower bounds and limits.** Volumetric packing gives the lower bound (I18). The antipodal row/column grid together with the Lamperti defect gives d_p(n,1) ≥ ⌊(n−1)/4⌋² (I19, I20). The limits follow (I23).

## 6. Eureka candidates (quick literature check only)
1. **I13: ramp of overlapping truncations (p>2). N3 (weak), F3.**
   - *For:* a tiny, non-analytic definition. A single truncation band gives energy ≈ ℓ and no gain. The triangular multiplicity profile gives ℓ^{p+1}, through superadditivity of t^p, while the projection error stays linear in ℓ. That gap is exactly what yields γ = 1−2/p. In the Lean, `ramp_sum_bounded_law` dominates 28% of the DAG, and the ramp branch is 48% of the mathematical core.
   - *Against:* the shape resembles de la Vallée Poussin / Fejér averaging of truncation windows and smooth Littlewood–Paley cutoffs. The paper motivates it in two lines (low pivot distance). It is a one-off device.
   - *Lit check:* no identical construction found (snippet level).
2. **I12: critical-moment amplification (p<2). N2, borderline.**
   - *For:* it uses the logarithmic divergence of the p-th moment of a p-stable law. The additive projection error stays fixed while b ≈ log T grows. The same "let b grow to absorb an additive error" principle (`paper.tex:287-293`) drives both ranges.
   - *Against:* stable variables with truncation and Maurey sampling are the standard L_p (p<2) toolkit (Schechtman 1987/2011, Indyk 2006).
3. **I05+I06/I07: an ℓ∞-bounded, ℓ2(σ)-contractive reweighted projection onto the gradient space, used to undo coordinatewise clipping. N2 plus a cross-field bridge** (electrical flows → metric embeddings).
   - The bridge is measured separately and is not a jump.
   - Nearest neighbours: Goranci–Henzinger–Räcke–Sachdeva–Sricharan 2023 (reweighted electrical routing), SRS18 and GGNS. GGNS is itself model-originated.
4. **I04: weighted moment criterion. N1.** It is minimax plus Maurey, with Eskenazis 2024 named as the antecedent.
5. **I20: grid obstruction. N2.** It is a variant of Ball 1990, extended to p>2 through Lamperti's criterion.

## 8. Correctness audit
**Verdict.** This is a major claim, and trust is high. Every step I checked by hand holds:
- the separation direction in Lemma 2.1;
- the Q = DPD^{-1} row-sum identity and the Brouwer self-map;
- the p<2 balance at a = T/H;
- the p>2 energy ℓ^{p+1}, the A_k tail split, and H^γ/ℓ;
- the dimension arithmetic;
- the grid incidence count.

In addition, the Lean formalizes the full theorem with a clean trust surface.

**Concerns:**
1. **Provenance (GGNS).** The bound is essential and model-originated, but it is re-proved in both the paper and the Lean.
2. **Lean localization covers the complete graph only.** The general Lemma 3.1 is unformalized, which does not affect the theorem.
3. **Paper details asserted rather than proved.**
   - The paper asserts, but does not prove, continuity of σ ↦ P(σ) for Brouwer.
   - It is brief on convergence and measurability of the infinite small-jump Poisson sum.

   The Lean avoids both.
4. **Constants.** They are non-uniform in p and D, as the paper states.
5. **No optimality claims.** The theorem is consistent with Brinkman–Charikar (γ→1 as p→1), with Naor–Ren's p>2 lower bound, and with linear lower bounds (the maps are nonlinear).
