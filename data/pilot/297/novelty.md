## family_D: **D3 (provisional), confidence 0.40**, resting on C1
- **Why C1 survives Test 1.**
  - The precedents join states by a crossed product with a global KOS automorphism. AW04 Cor 3 needs a separable base and controls two states. FH17 Lemma 2.3 controls countable families.
  - The all-states version through a global automorphism is open: CF22 Q8.2.
  - The bridge replaces the automorphism by a partial isomorphism between two copies of C(ℕ∪∞) generated by peaking projections with matched traces. Its data are countable, so it works over nonseparable bases. It gives unique extension for **every** old pure state with exactly one join.
  - No searched source (AW04, CF22, FH17, AWW10 in full; web searches on HNN, amalgamated or partial crossed product with unique pure-state extension) contains this.
- **Why confidence is low.**
  - CF22 §8, which is **in L**, explicitly proposes "an amalgamation C of B ⋊ Z and A … a 'partial crossed product' of sorts" preserving unique extension (Q8.1).
  - CF22 Prop 8.3 shows that a bridge with (U) for all pure states suffices under CH.
  - Ueda's HNN construction (in L) and AWW10's free-product unique-extension lemma supply the ingredients.
  - The direction was therefore posed; the eureka content is the choice of HNN data (peaking projection algebras, matched traces) and the endpoint analysis that makes it work for every state.
- **Falls to D2 if:**
  - Test 4 (arm L-) beats the threshold; the arm without CF22 §8 measures how much the posed question helps;
  - an expert identifies an earlier HNN or partial-crossed-product unique-extension result;
  - the unread Tanaka Brief Report or MathlibAnnex sources, or any other pre-24-Sep model output, contain an HNN bridge.
- **Transfer exclusion.** HNN extensions (from group theory, via Ueda), KOS and the set-theoretic bookkeeping are N0 transfer. The candidate is their use for selective unique-extension control, which has no found precedent.
