FAMILY 206: Finite lattice representation and undecidability
SUBJECT: Algebra
SUMMARY: Some finite lattices are not congruence lattices of any finite algebra, answering the finite lattice representation problem negatively. Moreover, no algorithm decides whether a finite lattice has such a representation, or whether it is a full subgroup interval of a finite group.
LEAN: yes

--- MANUSCRIPT: Finite congruence lattices: characterization and undecidability (September-24-2026)
ABSTRACT: We give an explicit colored-graph characterization of the finite nonempty lattices that occur as full congruence lattices of finite algebras, and prove that deciding this representation property is undecidable. In particular, the finite lattice representation problem has a negative answer. We also prove that recognition of full subgroup intervals in finite groups is undecidable.
[theorem] \label{m:main} The following statements hold. \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}] \item A finite nonempty lattice is representable if and only if it satisfies the finite colored-graph criterion in \Cref{f:criterion}. For each candidate graph size the criterion is decidable by a finite test. \item There is no deterministic Turing machine that, given the order table of a finite nonempty lattice, always halts and decides \(\mathcal R(L)\). \item There is a nonrepresentable finite lattice \(L_0\) of minimum cardinality, specified by the finite formulas in \Cref{sec:minimum}. Every finite lattice of smaller cardinality is representable, and \(\lvert L_0\rvert>7\). \item Given the order table of a finite nonempty lattice \(L\), it is undecidable whether \(L\cong[H,G]\) for some finite group \(G\) and subgroup \(H\le G\), where \([H,G]\) is the full subgroup interval. \end{enumerate}
[theorem] [Graph criterion]\label{f:criterion} The lattice $L$ is the full congruence lattice of a finite algebra if and only if there is an $L$-colored complete graph $(A,d)$ satisfying all three conditions below. \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}] \item\label{f:graph-triangle} If $p\ne q$, then $d(p,q)\ne0$, and \[ d(p,q)\le d(p,u)\vee d(u,q)\qquad(p,q,u\in A). \] \item\label{f:graph-reflection} Whenever $a\not\le b$ in $L$, there are vertices $p,q$ with $d(p,q)\le a$ and $d(p,q)\not\le b$. \item\label{f:graph-connectivity} For every nonempty edge set $S$, vertices $p,q$ satisfying $d(p,q)\le a_S$ lie in the same connected component of $\Gamma_S$. \end{enumerate} For each fixed $|A|$, these conditions involve only lattice inequalities, finite vertex labelings, and paths.
[proposition] \label{f:prop-l7-interval} With their usual subfield inclusion, the groups \[ H=\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbb F_2) \le G=\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbb F_{64}) \] have subgroup interval $[H,G]\cong L_7$.

--- MANUSCRIPT: A negative solution to the finite lattice representation problem (September-24-2026)
ABSTRACT: We give a negative solution to the finite lattice representation problem. We prove that there is a finite nonempty lattice that is not the full congruence lattice of any finite nonempty algebra of any finite signature.
[theorem] \label{main:congruences} There exists a finite nonempty lattice $L$ such that \[ L\not\cong\Con(A) \] for every finite nonempty algebra $A$ of every finite signature.
[theorem] \label{main:group} There exists a finite lattice that is not isomorphic to $[D,G]$ for any subgroup $D$ of any finite group $G$.
[proposition] [Equal-type transport] \label{frm:equal-transport} Let $D<X<Y$ be labelled vertices. Then $F_Y\le F_X$, and $T_X$ is isomorphic to a section of $T_Y$. Put \[ E_X=(X\cap N_Y)/F_Y\le S_Y. \] The following conditions are equivalent: \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}] \item $T_X\cong T_Y$; \item $E_X$ is subdirect in $S_Y$. \end{enumerate} When they hold, the quotient map induces an isomorphism \begin{equation} \label{frm:equal-socle-identification} E_X\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }S_X, \qquad N_X=F_X(X\cap N_Y), \qquad F_X\cap N_Y=F_Y. \end{equation} Moreover $m_X<m_Y$, and the strip supports give a canonical $X$-equivariant surjection \begin{equation} \label{frm:equal-support-map} \sigma_{YX}:I_Y\longrightarrow I_X. \end{equation} For three comparable labelled vertices of the same simple type these maps compose: $\sigma_{ZX}=\sigma_{YX}\sigma_{ZY}$.
