FAMILY 110: Optimal-order randomized k-server on arbitrary metrics
SUBJECT: Theoretical computer science
SUMMARY: Establishes a randomized competitive ratio $`O(\log^2(k+1))`$ for k-server on every metric space, matching the worst-case lower-bound order. One policy serves every finite oblivious request sequence, including on infinite unbounded metrics. On finite rational metrics, a uniform implementation has polynomial preprocessing and per-request bit cost in the input length and $`\log(t+1)`$ at request t, with a finite instance-dependent additive movement constant.
LEAN: yes

--- MANUSCRIPT: Squared-logarithmic randomized k-server on arbitrary metrics (September-24-2026)
ABSTRACT: We prove that randomized k-server has competitive ratio $`O((\log(k+1))^2)`$ on every metric space against oblivious request sequences, matching the known worst-case lower bound. For each metric and initial configuration, one policy works for all finite request sequences, including on infinite and unbounded spaces. When the initial server positions are distinct, no additive term is needed.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There is an absolute constant \(C<\infty\) such that, for every integer \(k\ge2\), every metric space \((X,d)\) with at least \(k+1\) points, and every \(s\in X^k\), there exist a randomized online policy \(A\) and a finite \(B\ge0\) satisfying \begin{equation}\label{eq:main} \E\,\cost_{A,s}(\sigma) \le C\bigl(\log(k+1)\bigr)^2\OPT_{X,d,k,s}(\sigma)+B \end{equation} for every finite request sequence \(\sigma\). The constant \(C\) is independent of every property of the metric. The number \(B\) may depend on \((X,d),k,s\), but not on the sequence or its length. The same policy serves all finite sequences and horizons. If the initial positions are distinct, one may take \(B=0\).
[proposition] [Finite distributional estimate]\label{prop:finite-law} There is an absolute \(a<\infty\) with the following property. For every finite metric \(Y\) of positive diameter, every \(k\ge2\), and every start \(s\in Y^k\), there is a finite \(D_0=D_0(Y,k,s)\) such that, for every finite horizon \(H\) and every probability law on \(Y^H\), there is a causal strongly lazy policy satisfying \begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-law} \E\,\cost\le a\ell^2\E\,\OPT+D_0 . \end{equation} The policy may depend on the law and horizon. The number \(D_0\) does not.
[theorem] [Dominated tree allocation]\label{thm:allocation} There are absolute constants $C_0,C_1$ and $\tau_0$ with the following property. For $\tau\ge\tau_0$, the observed histories determine amounts $q_v(t)$ satisfying \begin{equation}\label{eq:allocation-constraints} q_{\mathrm{root}}=k,\qquad 0\le q_v\le C_0m_v,\qquad \sum_{i\prec v}q_i\le q_v,\qquad q_{x_t}\ge1. \end{equation} Moreover, with $\ell=1+\log(k+1)$, \begin{equation}\label{eq:allocation-bound} V:=\E\sum_{t\ge1}\sum_{v\ne\mathrm{root}} r_{|v|-1}|q_v(t)-q_v(t-1)| \le C_1\ell P+D_{\mathcal T}. \end{equation} Here sums stop at the chosen finite horizon. The finite constant $D_{\mathcal T}$ depends only on the fixed tree, radii, and $k$; it can be chosen independently of the input law, horizon, and number of histories. No initial allocation is prescribed.
