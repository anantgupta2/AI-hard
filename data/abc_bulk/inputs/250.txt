FAMILY 250: Boone–Higman embeddings with higher finiteness
SUBJECT: Group theory
SUMMARY: A finitely generated group has decidable word problem exactly when it embeds in a finitely presented simple group, proving the Boone–Higman conjecture. The target can have type F∞: a classifying space with finitely many cells in each dimension. A single group of type F∞ can also contain every finitely presented group.
LEAN: yes

--- MANUSCRIPT: Finite algebraic envelopes and the Boone–Higman conjecture (September-23-2026)
ABSTRACT: We prove the Boone–Higman conjecture. A finitely generated group has decidable word problem if and only if it embeds in a finitely presented simple group.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} For every finitely generated group \(G\), the following are equivalent: \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}] \item \(G\) has decidable word problem. \item \(G\) embeds in a finitely presented simple group. \end{enumerate}
[theorem] \label{thm:envelope} Every finitely generated group with decidable word problem embeds in a finitely presented group \(\Gamma\) admitting a faithful two-transitive action on a countably infinite set. Every point stabilizer of this action is finitely generated.
[theorem] \label{thm:algebra-package} For every finitely generated group \(L\) with decidable word problem, there are the following data over \(k=\F_2\): \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}] \item A nonzero finitely presented unital algebra \(B\), a simple subalgebra \(A\subseteq B\) with identity \(e\), and an embedding \(L\hookrightarrow A^\times\). \item An injective nonunital algebra homomorphism \(\phi:B\to B\) with \(\phi(B)\subseteq A\) and \({p=\phi(1_B)\ne1_B}\). \item Elements \(s_0,s_1,t_0,t_1\in pBp\) commuting with \(\phi(B)\) and satisfying \[ t_i s_j=\delta_{ij}p,\qquad s_0t_0+s_1t_1=p \quad(i,j\in\{0,1\}). \] \item An element \(b_*\in B\) with the following property. Put \[ R=B\otimes_k B^{\op},\qquad \psi=\phi\otimes\phi^{\op}, \qquad (a\otimes b^{\op})\cdot m=amb. \] Then \[ r\ne0\ \Longrightarrow\ \psi(r)\cdot b_*\ne0 \qquad(r\in R). \] \end{enumerate}

--- MANUSCRIPT: Simple F∞ overgroups of groups with decidable word problem (September-23-2026)
ABSTRACT: Every finitely generated group with decidable word problem embeds in a simple group of type F∞. This resolves the higher-finiteness strengthening of the Boone–Higman conjecture.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} Every finitely generated group \(G\) with decidable word problem admits an injective homomorphism into a nontrivial simple group \(H\) of type \(\Finfty\).
[theorem] \label{thm:torus} Let $f:U\to U$ be an injective endomorphism. Assume the following. \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}] \item There are a finitely presented group $P$ and homomorphisms $a:U\to P$, $b:P\to U$ with $f=ba$. \item For each $\ell\in\{2,3\}$ there are homomorphisms $h_E:U\to U$, indexed by $E\in\mathcal P_\ell$, such that $h_I=f$. Whenever $E,P',E+P'\in\mathcal P_\ell$ and $EP'=P'E=0$, the subgroups $h_E(U)$ and $h_{P'}(U)$ commute elementwise, and \[ h_{E+P'}(u)=h_E(u)h_{P'}(u)\qquad(u\in U). \] \end{enumerate} Then $T(U,f)$ is of type $\Finfty$.
[proposition] \label{prop:uniform-fillings} Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:torus}, for every finite bound $A$ and every $n\ge0$ there are an integer $k\ge0$ and a finite bound $B$ such that $f^k$ sends every reduced integral $n$-cycle of $C_*^A(U)$ to a boundary in $C_*^B(U)$.

--- MANUSCRIPT: A universal group of type F∞ (September-23-2026)
ABSTRACT: We construct a single group of type F∞ containing every finitely presented group. Its finitely generated subgroups, up to isomorphism, are exactly the finitely generated recursively presented groups. This answers the F∞ form of the higher-dimensional Higman embedding question.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There exists a group $H$ of type $\Finfty$ such that every finitely presented group admits an injective homomorphism into $H$.
[proposition] \label{prop:finite-envelope} For every finitely presented group $U$ there are a finitely presented group $\widehat W$ and homomorphisms \[ k_{\ell,E}:U\longrightarrow\widehat W \qquad(\ell\in\{2,3\},\ E\in\mathcal P_\ell) \] such that $k_{2,I}=k_{3,I}$ is injective. For each $\ell$, whenever $E,E',E+E'\in\mathcal P_\ell$ and $EE'=E'E=0$, the subgroups $k_{\ell,E}(U)$ and $k_{\ell,E'}(U)$ commute elementwise, and \[ k_{\ell,E+E'}(u)=k_{\ell,E}(u)k_{\ell,E'}(u) \qquad(u\in U). \]
[theorem] [Ascending-torus theorem] \label{thm:torus} Let $U$ be a group and $f:U\to U$ an injective endomorphism. Suppose that the following conditions hold. \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}] \item There are a finitely presented group $P$ and homomorphisms $a:U\to P$ and $b:P\to U$ such that $f=ba$. \item For each $\ell\in\{2,3\}$ there are homomorphisms $h_{\ell,E}:U\to U$, indexed by the finite set $E\in\mathcal P_\ell$ defined above, with $h_{\ell,I}=f$. Whenever $E,E',E+E'\in\mathcal P_\ell$ and $EE'=E'E=0$, the subgroups $h_{\ell,E}(U)$ and $h_{\ell,E'}(U)$ commute elementwise, and \[ h_{\ell,E+E'}(u) =h_{\ell,E}(u)h_{\ell,E'}(u) \qquad(u\in U). \] \end{enumerate} Then $U$ embeds naturally in $T(U,f)$, and $T(U,f)$ is of type $\Finfty$.
