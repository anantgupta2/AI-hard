--- MANUSCRIPT: Sharp One-Third Stability of Brenier Maps (September-25-2026)
ABSTRACT: For the uniform probability measure ρ on a compact convex body in ℝd, d ≥ 2, quadratic optimal transport maps are one-third Hölder continuous in $`L^2(\rho)`$ with respect to the target's 2-Wasserstein distance. The constant is uniform over all targets supported in a fixed compact set, and the exponent is optimal. A three-atom family on a fixed cube disproves Letrouit's conjectured uniform square-root estimate.
[theorem] \label{main:stability} For every \(K,Y\) as above there is a finite constant \(C(K,Y)\) such that \[ \|T_\mu-T_\nu\|_{L^2(\rho)} \le C(K,Y)\,W_2(\mu,\nu)^{1/3} \qquad\bigl(\mu,\nu\in\Prob(Y)\bigr). \] The targets may be atomic, singular, or absolutely continuous. The exponent \(1/3\) cannot be replaced by any larger exponent in general, even for \(K=Y=[-1,1]^d\) and targets with exactly three atoms.
[proposition] [Optimality of the exponent]\label{sharpness:exponent} For every fixed $d\ge2$, let $K=Y=[-1,1]^d$ and $\rho=2^{-d}1_K\,dx$. For every $\alpha>1/3$, \[ \sup_{\substack{\mu,\nu\in\Prob(Y)\\ \mu\ne\nu}} \frac{\|T_\mu-T_\nu\|_{L^2(\rho)}}{W_2(\mu,\nu)^\alpha} =+\infty. \] The supremum remains infinite when both targets are required to have exactly three atoms.
[proposition] [Cell differentials and connectivity] \label{cells:calculus} On the open set of configurations with distinct sites and $M_i>0$ for every $i$, the maps $M_i$ and $B_i$ are jointly $C^1$ in the intercepts and sites. For an intercept direction $r=(r_i)$ and a site direction $v=(v_i)$, \begin{align} D M_i[r,v] &=\sum_{j\ne i}\bigl[g_{ij}(r_i-r_j) +b_{ij}\cdot(v_i-v_j)\bigr], \label{cells:mass-differential}\\ D B_i[r,v] &=\sum_{j\ne i}b_{ij}(r_i-r_j) +\frac1{|K|}\sum_{j\ne i}\frac1{|y_i-y_j|} \int_{F_{ij}}x\bigl(x\cdot(v_i-v_j)\bigr)\,dS(x). \label{cells:moment-differential} \end{align} The graph on $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ with edges $g_{ij}>0$ is connected. Consequently, the symmetric operator \begin{equation} \label{cells:laplacian} (Gq)_i=D M_i[q,0]=\sum_{j\ne i}g_{ij}(q_i-q_j) \end{equation} has kernel consisting of the constant vectors and range $\{z\in\R^N:\sum_i z_i=0\}$.
