FAMILY 179: The circulant Hadamard and Barker-sequence conjectures
SUBJECT: Combinatorics
SUMMARY: Proves that real circulant Hadamard matrices exist exactly in orders 1 and 4, resolving the circulant Hadamard conjecture. Together with classical Barker-sequence results, this shows that binary sequences whose nontrivial aperiodic autocorrelations have magnitude at most 1 exist at lengths n &gt; 1 exactly when $`n\in\{2,3,4,5,7,11,13\}`$.

--- MANUSCRIPT: The circulant Hadamard conjecture (September-23-2026)
ABSTRACT: We prove the circulant Hadamard conjecture: a real circulant Hadamard matrix has order 1 or 4. As a consequence, Barker sequences of length greater than one exist exactly at lengths 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 11, 13, proving the Barker-sequence conjecture.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} For a positive integer \(n\), a real circulant Hadamard matrix of order \(n\) exists if and only if \(n\in\{1,4\}\).
[proposition] \label{prop:order} If a real circulant Hadamard matrix has order \(n>1\), then \(n=4u^2\) for an odd positive integer \(u\).
[proposition] \label{prop:alternating} Suppose \(x\in\Z[i][P]\) satisfies \(xx^*=u^2\). Then the alternating product \(\Delta(x)\) defined in \eqref{eq:delta} belongs to \(B\) and is a root of unity. For each \(p\in I\), its order is a power of \(p\). In particular, its order is odd; if \(u\) has at least two distinct prime divisors, then \(\Delta(x)=1\).
