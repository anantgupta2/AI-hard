--- MANUSCRIPT: Nonuniqueness with local conservation for the hard-sphere Boltzmann equation (October-5-2026)
ABSTRACT: We prove nonuniqueness for the three-dimensional periodic hard-sphere Boltzmann equation among global entropy solutions satisfying exact local conservation of mass, momentum, and kinetic energy. We construct one nonnegative initial density with bounded velocity support and finite mass, energy, and absolute entropy that gives rise to two distinct such solutions. Both are strongly continuous in L1, and their collision gain and loss terms are integrable with every polynomial velocity weight on every bounded time interval.

Both solutions belong to \(C([0,\infty);L^1(\Omega))\). Moreover, for every finite \(T\) and every \(k\ge0\), they satisfy \begin{equation}\label{eq:strong-collision-integrability} \int_0^T\!\!\int_\Omega \langle v\rangle^k\bigl[Q^+(F,F)+Q^-(F,F)\bigr] \dd x\dd v\dd t<\infty, \qquad \langle v\rangle=(1+|v|^2)^{1/2}. \end{equation}

--- MANUSCRIPT: Nonuniqueness for the periodic hard-sphere Boltzmann equation (September-23-2026)
ABSTRACT: We prove nonuniqueness for the periodic hard-sphere Boltzmann equation by constructing two distinct global renormalized solutions with the same nonnegative initial density on $`\mathbb T^3\times\mathbb R^3`$. This density has bounded velocity support and finite mass, energy, and absolute entropy. Both solutions conserve local mass and total momentum and satisfy the global energy and entropy-dissipation inequalities. On a common initial interval, they are strongly continuous in L1, and their collision gains and losses are integrable.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There is a nonnegative density \(F_0\) on \(\T^3\times\R^3\), with bounded velocity support and \[ \int_{\T^3\times\R^3} F_0(1+|v|^2+|\log F_0|)\dd x\dd v<\infty, \] for which the hard-sphere Boltzmann equation has two distinct global admissible solutions, in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:admissible}, with initial datum \(F_0\). On some common initial interval both solutions are strongly continuous in \(L^1\), and their collision gains and losses are integrable in space-time-velocity.
