FAMILY 331: Reflexive midpoint convexity and diamond distortion
SUBJECT: Functional analysis
SUMMARY: Constructs a real reflexive Banach space with an asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex norm but no equivalent asymptotically uniformly convex norm, extending Baudier's separation to reflexive spaces. In the same space, depth-k countably branching diamonds require distortion at least $`\sqrt{1+k/12}`$, so midpoint uniform convexity does not force uniformly bounded diamond distortion even under reflexivity.
LEAN: yes

--- MANUSCRIPT: Asymptotic midpoint uniform convexity and unbounded diamond distortion in a reflexive tree space (September-27-2026)
ABSTRACT: We construct a separable reflexive real Banach space whose given norm is asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex but which admits no asymptotically uniformly convex equivalent norm. Its averaged midpoint modulus is at least $`\sqrt{1+t^2/12}-1`$, and the countably branching diamond of depth k has distortion at least $`\sqrt{1+k/12}`$ in this space. This gives a negative answer to the reflexive diamond converse for asymptotic uniform convexifiability.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} The space $X=J^*$ defined by \eqref{eq:segment-norm} is infinite dimensional, separable, and reflexive, and has no equivalent AUC norm. Its given norm satisfies \[ \widehat\delta_X(t)\ge\sqrt{1+t^2/12}-1\qquad(t>0). \] For every integer $k\ge0$, every embedding of $D_k$ into $X$ has distortion at least $\sqrt{1+k/12}$.

ABSTRACT: We derive quantitative distortion bounds for countably branching diamond graphs from midpoint estimates and direct tree energies. --- MANUSCRIPT: Exact asymptotic moduli in a Daugavet subspace of L1 (September-27-2026)
ABSTRACT: For an infinite-dimensional real subspace of L1 whose unit ball is totally bounded in measure and whose norm has the Daugavet property, we compute the averaged midpoint and one-sided asymptotic moduli at every unit center. They are $`\max\{t/2,t-1\}`$ and $`\max\{0,t-2\}`$, respectively. The same weak-neighborhood geometry excludes every equivalent asymptotically uniformly convex norm. A quantitative realization of the Kadets–Werner construction supplies a space with both hypotheses.
[theorem] \label{thm:exact} Let $E$ be an infinite-dimensional closed real subspace of $L^1(\Omega,\mathbb P)$, where $\mathbb P$ is a probability measure. Suppose its closed unit ball is totally bounded for convergence in measure and its inherited norm has the Daugavet property. Then every $x\in S_E$ and $t>0$ satisfy \[ H_E(x,t)=\max\{t/2,t-1\},\qquad D_E(x,t)=\max\{0,t-2\}. \] Consequently these are also the formulas for $\widehat\delta_E(t)$ and $\overline\delta_E(t)$. The space $E$ admits no equivalent AUC norm.
[theorem] [Kadets--Werner construction]\label{kw:existence} There is an infinite-dimensional closed real subspace $E\subseteq L^1$ such that $B(E)$ is totally bounded for $d$ and has the following slice property: for every $w\in S(E)$, every nonempty slice \[ H=\{y\in B(E):\ell(y)>\alpha\},\qquad \ell\in E^*,\quad\alpha\in\R, \] and every $\rho>0$, there is $y\in H\cap S(E)$ such that $\norm{w+y}\ge2-\rho$. In particular, $E$ has the Daugavet property.

--- MANUSCRIPT: Midpoint convexity from bounded tree potentials and path costs (September-27-2026)
ABSTRACT: Four real Banach spaces defined by bounded tree potentials satisfy the averaged asymptotic midpoint bound $`\widehat\delta(t)\ge\sqrt{1+t^2/4}-1`$ for $`0\lt t\lt 1`$, while none admits an asymptotically uniformly convex (AUC) renorming. On finite-height trees, the globally constrained norm equals the least additive cost of a Hilbert vector and root paths. The quadratic path and segment-start outer Hilbert sums are reflexive and asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex, and admit no equivalent AUC norm.
[theorem] \label{thm:four} Each of the four completed spaces $X_K$ is a separable infinite-dimensional real Banach space. For $K\in\{K_{\rm s},K_{\rm p},K_{\rm a},K_{\rm g}\}$ and $0<t<1$, \begin{equation}\label{eq:common-bound} \widehat\delta_{X_K}(t)\ge\sqrt{1+t^2/4}-1. \end{equation} None of these spaces admits an equivalent AUC norm.
[theorem] \label{thm:free-root-modulus} The real Banach space $X_{\mathrm e}$ defined by \eqref{fr:tests} and \eqref{fr:norm} satisfies \begin{equation} \widehat\delta_{\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm e}}(t) \ge\frac{\sqrt{1+t^2/576}-1}{4}\qquad(0<t<1). \label{fr:modulus} \end{equation} It admits no equivalent AUC norm. More precisely, if $\alpha\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm e}\le N\le\beta\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm e}$, where $0<\alpha\le\beta<\infty$, then $\overline\delta_N(\alpha/(4\beta))=0$.

--- MANUSCRIPT: Midpoint convexity from two recursive potentials (September-27-2026)
ABSTRACT: We study tree norms computed by two least nonnegative fields whose difference is the vector. For Euclidean child aggregation, two root-sum spaces have an averaged asymptotic midpoint modulus of at least $`t^3/128`$ for $`0\lt t\lt 1`$, including a reflexive joining-root space. A reflexive construction with a fixed zero root also satisfies a homogeneous cubic estimate. These spaces admit no equivalent asymptotically uniformly convex norm. We also obtain sixth-power and cubic estimates when the aggregation exponent depends on the height of a finite component.
[theorem] \label{rec:main}\label{rec:joined-root-stability} For $N=N_\Sigma$ on $X_\Sigma$, or $N=N_J$ on $X_J$, one has \begin{equation}\label{rec:sum-modulus} \widehat\delta_N(t)\ge t^3/128\qquad(0<t<1). \end{equation} For $X_0$, a finite initial set $H$, a vector $x$ supported in $H$, and $z\in F_H\setminus\{0\}$, one has \begin{equation}\label{rec:equal-estimate} \frac{N_0(x+z)+N_0(x-z)}2\ge N_0(x) +\frac{N_0(z)^3}{8(2N_0(x)+N_0(z))^2}. \end{equation} Each of $X_\Sigma,X_0,X_J$ admits no equivalent AUC norm.
[proposition] \label{rec:completion} These formulas define infinite-dimensional Banach spaces after completion. Coordinate evaluations and the fields extend continuously. If $H$ is a finite set containing the root and all predecessors of its nodes, then coordinate restriction $\Pi_H$ is a contractive projection. Its kernel $F_H$ has finite codimension, and finitely supported elements of $F_H$ are dense in $F_H$. Both $X_0$ and $X_J$ are reflexive.
