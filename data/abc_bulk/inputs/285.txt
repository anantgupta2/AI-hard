FAMILY 285: Counterexamples to Baum–Connes and Kadison–Kaplansky
SUBJECT: Operator algebras
SUMMARY: Disproves the coefficient-free reduced Baum–Connes conjecture through a failure of rational injectivity and a separate failure of surjectivity of assembly. A finitely generated torsion-free example witnesses the injectivity failure. Separately, a torsion-free group has a nontrivial projection in its reduced group C∗-algebra, disproving the Kadison–Kaplansky conjecture.
LEAN: no

--- MANUSCRIPT: A torsion-free counterexample to reduced Baum–Connes injectivity (September-23-2026)
ABSTRACT: We disprove rational injectivity of the coefficient-free reduced Baum–Connes assembly map for torsion-free groups. Specifically, we construct a finitely generated torsion-free discrete group whose degree-zero assembly map has a kernel class of infinite order.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There exist a finitely generated torsion-free discrete group \(\Ginj\), an injective homomorphism \(i:\Z^2\to \Ginj\), and a class \(c\in H^2(B\Ginj;\Z)\) such that \[ \ip{(Bi)^*c}{[T^2]}=1,\qquad i_*(\beta)=0\quad\text{in }K_0(\Cr \Ginj), \] where \(\beta\) is a rank-zero Bott generator of \(K_0(\Cr\Z^2)\). In particular, \(h=(Bi)_*[D_{T^2}]\) has infinite order in \(K_0(B\Ginj)\), while its reduced analytic assembly image is zero. Here \([D_{T^2}]\) is the spin Dirac \(K\)-homology class of the oriented torus.
[proposition] [The deterministic labeled tower]\label{prop:tower-labels} For every fixed finite type set and every sufficiently large $s$ as above, the construction has the following properties. \begin{enumerate} \item The auxiliary state sizes are \eqref{eq:state-sizes}. In a fixed extra slice an incidence has degrees $d,D$. Without slicing, its smaller side acquires the extra factor $2^b$ and its larger side does not. The total degrees are at most $T2^b d$ on A and $T2^bD$ on B. \item The labeling is folded: no two outgoing oriented edges at a vertex have the same label. Its signed alphabet satisfies \begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp-alphabet} |\mathcal S|\le 2TM2^b d2^r, \qquad \frac{\sqrt{|\mathcal S|}}q \le 2^{-.000025s+O(\log s)+O_{T,b}(1)}\longrightarrow0. \end{equation} \item At every height the fraction of removed vertices on each side is at most $\varepsilon_{\rm gate}+\varepsilon_{\rm sep}$. 
[proposition] [Sequential selection]\label{prop:sequential-law} There is a probability law on all the permutation tables such that every auxiliary incidence has centered singular norm at most the fixed number $\rho<1$ in \eqref{eq:uniform-incidence-gap}. At each individual source-block update, conditional on all earlier updates, any joint test of $k$ specified current tables has its probability increased by at most $e^{\varepsilon_s k}$ relative to independent uniform current tables. The bound is uniform in the earlier history and the height, with $\varepsilon_s\to0$.

--- MANUSCRIPT: A torsion-free counterexample to the Kadison–Kaplansky projection conjecture (September-23-2026)
ABSTRACT: We construct a finitely generated torsion-free discrete group whose reduced group C∗-algebra contains a projection other than zero and the identity. This disproves the Kadison–Kaplansky projection conjecture.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There exist a finitely generated torsion-free discrete group \(\Gproj\) and a projection \(e\in C_r^*(\Gproj)\) such that \[ 0<\tau_{\Gproj}(e)<\frac12. \] In particular, \(e\) is neither zero nor the identity.
[proposition] [Scalar compression after a free product] \label{scalar:compression} Let \(\Lambda\) be a discrete group and \(p\in M_N(C_r^*(\Lambda))\) a projection with \[ 0<a:=\tau_{\Lambda,N}(p)<\frac12. \] Put \(K=\Lambda*\mathbb F_N\), where \(t_1,\ldots,t_N\) freely generate \(\mathbb F_N\), and regard \(p\) as a matrix over \(C_r^*(K)\) by Lemma~\ref{scalar:subgroup}. Set \[ U=(\lambda_K(t_1),\ldots,\lambda_K(t_N)),\qquad A=pU^*Up. \] Then \(A\) is invertible in the unital corner \(pM_N(C_r^*(K))p\), and \begin{equation} e=(Up)A^{-1}(Up)^* \label{scalar:projection-formula} \end{equation} is a projection in the scalar algebra \(C_r^*(K)\) with \(\tau_K(e)=a\). If \(\Lambda\) is finitely generated and torsion-free, then so is \(K\).
[theorem] [Matrix construction] \label{setup:matrix-target} There exist a finitely generated torsion-free discrete group \(\Gbase\), a finite integer \(N\ge1\), and a self-adjoint element \(T\in M_N(\mathbb C[\Gbase])\), in its regular representation, with a nonempty separated spectral subset whose spectral projection \(p_0\) satisfies \[ p_0\in M_N(C_r^*(\Gbase)),\qquad 0<\tau_{\Gbase,N}(p_0)<\frac12. \]

--- MANUSCRIPT: An irrational-trace counterexample to reduced Baum–Connes (September-23-2026)
ABSTRACT: We construct a finitely generated discrete group whose reduced group C∗-algebra contains a projection of irrational canonical trace. Its K0-class lies outside the image of the coefficient-free reduced Baum–Connes assembly map. This disproves the coefficient-free reduced Baum–Connes conjecture for countable discrete groups.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There exist a finitely generated discrete group \(\Gsur\) and a projection \(b_0\in C_r^*(\Gsur)\) with irrational canonical trace. Moreover, \[ [b_0]\notin\mu_0^{\Gsur}\bigl(K_0^{\Gsur}(\underline E\Gsur)\bigr). \] In particular, the coefficient-free reduced Baum--Connes conjecture for countable discrete groups is false.
[theorem] [L\"uck]\label{thm:trace-image} For every countable discrete group \(G\), \[ \tau_{G*}\!\left(\mu_0^G(K_0^G(\underline EG))\right)\subseteq\Q. \]
[proposition] \label{prop:cone-geometry} There is an integer \(D_0\), independent of all the above data, such that every vertex on a geodesic triangle in \(\widehat X\) is within \(D_0\) of a vertex on another side. Moreover: \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}] \item\label{item:avoidance} If an edge path of length \(L\) joins \(x,a\in P\) while avoiding \(c_P\), then \begin{equation}\label{eq:avoidance} d_P(x,a)\le12(L+1)\lambda\sigma(P). \end{equation} \item\label{item:simple-cayley} If \(\lambda<1/12\), the graph \(X\) is simple: no nonfreely-trivial closed label word in \(X\) has length at most two. \end{enumerate}
