FAMILY 240: Shelah's eventual categoricity and the prescribed-threshold obstruction
SUBJECT: Mathematical logic
SUMMARY: Proves Shelah's eventual categoricity conjecture in ZFC: for each bound on the Löwenheim–Skolem number, a uniform threshold makes categoricity of an abstract elementary class in one cardinal above that threshold imply categoricity throughout the same tail. Categoricity means uniqueness up to isomorphism at a given cardinality. Under the continuum hypothesis, a proposed specific Hanf threshold need not suffice.
LEAN: yes

--- MANUSCRIPT: A CH obstruction to a prescribed categoricity threshold (September-24-2026)
ABSTRACT: Assuming the continuum hypothesis, we construct an abstract elementary class with Löwenheim–Skolem number ℵ0 that is categorical in every sufficiently large cardinal but has at least two nonisomorphic models of cardinality $`\beth_{\omega_2}`$. Thus categoricity does not transfer down to the proposed bound $`\beth_{(2^{\aleph_0})^+}`$, which equals $`\beth_{\omega_2}`$ under CH. Consequently, if ZFC is consistent, the prescribed-threshold form of Shelah's categoricity conjecture is not provable in ZFC.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} Assume the continuum hypothesis. There is an abstract elementary class \(K\) in a countable finitary relational language such that \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}] \item \(\LS(K)=\aleph_0\); \item \(K\) has at least two nonisomorphic models of cardinality \(\beth_{\omega_2}=H(K)\); \item putting \(\Lambda=\beth_{(2^{\aleph_1})^+}\), the class \(K\) is categorical in every cardinal \(\mu\ge\Lambda\). \end{enumerate} In particular, \(\lambda=\Lambda^+>H(K)\) is a categoricity cardinal from which downward transfer to \(H(K)\) fails.
[theorem] \label{aec:class} The pair \(K=(\mathcal C,\le_K)\) of Definition~\ref{aec:objects} is an abstract elementary class in a fixed countable finitary relational language, with \[ \LS(K)=\aleph_0. \] It has the union and common-upper-bound properties for every nonempty set-indexed directed family of strong substructures.
[theorem] \label{end:two-models} The class \(K\) has two nonisomorphic models of cardinality \(\beth_\eta\). One has an order sort of cardinality \(\aleph_1\), and the other has a countable order sort.

--- MANUSCRIPT: Eventual categoricity for abstract elementary classes (September-24-2026)
ABSTRACT: We prove Shelah's eventual categoricity conjecture for abstract elementary classes in ZFC. For each infinite bound on the Löwenheim–Skolem number there is a uniform threshold such that categoricity in any one cardinal at or above that threshold implies categoricity in every cardinal at or above the same threshold.
[theorem] [Uniform eventual categoricity]\label{main:uniform} For every infinite cardinal $\lambda$ there is a cardinal $\mu(\lambda)\ge\lambda$ such that the following holds for every AEC $K$ with $\LS(K)\le\lambda$: \[ \bigl(\exists\kappa\ge\mu(\lambda)\quad K\text{ is categorical in }\kappa\bigr) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \bigl(\forall\kappa'\ge\mu(\lambda)\quad K\text{ is categorical in }\kappa'\bigr). \] The same threshold is used in the premise and the conclusion.
[theorem] [The qualitative form]\label{main:qualitative} An AEC categorical in unboundedly many cardinals is categorical on a tail of the cardinals.
[proposition] [The uniform reduction] \label{found:uniform-reduction} The uniform assertion in Theorem~\ref{main:uniform} is equivalent to the following qualitative assertion: \begin{quote} Every AEC categorical in unboundedly many cardinals is categorical in all cardinals above some bound depending on that AEC. \end{quote}
