FAMILY 155: A counterexample to periodic tiling in dimension three
SUBJECT: Combinatorics
SUMMARY: Constructs a finite translational tile in ℤ3 that tiles space but admits no fully periodic tiling, disproving the periodic tiling conjecture in the smallest possible lattice dimension. Its unit-cube thickening gives the same counterexample in ℝ3, even with arbitrary real translation vectors.
LEAN: yes

--- MANUSCRIPT: A translational tile with no fully periodic tiling in dimension three (September-23-2026)
ABSTRACT: We construct a finite translational tile in ℤ3 that admits tilings but no fully periodic tiling. Its unit-cube thickening has the same property in ℝ3, even when arbitrary real translations are allowed. This gives a negative resolution of the periodic tiling conjecture in dimension three.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There is a finite nonempty set $T\subset\Z^3$ such that: \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}] \item $T$ tiles $\Z^3$ by translations, but no translation set $A$ with $A\oplus T=\Z^3$ is invariant under a subgroup of finite index in $\Z^3$; \item the set $\Omega=T+[0,1]^3$ tiles $\R^3$ by translations up to null sets, but no such tiling has a translation set invariant under a lattice of full rank in $\R^3$. \end{enumerate}
[proposition] [The vertical-period obstruction] \label{word:obstruction} There is no array $W\colon I\times\Z\to\Sigma$ satisfying the line rule, having every column nonconstant, and having a positive vertical period.
[proposition] \label{act:nonperiodic} No common solution of this finite system is invariant under a finite-index subgroup of $G$.
