FAMILY 198: A counterexample to finitistic-dimension finiteness
SUBJECT: Algebra
SUMMARY: Constructs a finite-dimensional complex algebra whose finite-dimensional modules have unbounded finite projective dimensions. This disproves the little finitistic-dimension conjecture.
LEAN: yes

--- MANUSCRIPT: An algebra of infinite little finitistic dimension (September-23-2026)
ABSTRACT: We construct a finite-dimensional complex algebra whose finite-dimensional modules have unbounded finite projective dimensions. This disproves the little finitistic-dimension conjecture.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There is a finite-dimensional unital complex algebra \(A\) such that, for every integer \(m\ge1\), there is a finite-dimensional left \(A\)-module \(N_m\) with \[ 2m-2\le\pd_A N_m<\infty. \] In particular, \(\findim A=\infty\).
[proposition] \label{prop:selection} There exist a finitely presented unital complex algebra $R$, a central idempotent $e\in R$, and an automorphism $\alpha$ of $R$ such that, for every integer $m\geq 1$, a finite-dimensional left $R$-module $Y_m$ satisfies \begin{equation} H^{\circ j}(Y_m)\neq 0\quad(0\leq j<m), \qquad H^{\circ m}(Y_m)=0. \label{eq:extinction} \end{equation} The algebra, idempotent, and automorphism are independent of $m$.
[proposition] [Fixed lifted diagram]\label{prop:lifted-diagram} For the fixed data $R,e,\alpha$ and the algebra $B$ above, there are bounded complexes $D_0,D_1,D_2$ of finitely generated projective right $B$-modules, chain maps \[ f_a:D_i\longrightarrow D_j\qquad(a:i\to j), \] and right-$B$-linear maps $h_\rho:D_0\to D_2$ of degree $-1$ for $\rho\in\mathcal R$, such that \begin{equation}\label{eq:relation-homotopies} d_{D_2}h_\rho+h_\rho d_{D_0} =\sum_{b,a}\rho_{ba}f_bf_a. \end{equation} For every finite-dimensional left $R$-module $Y$, the diagram whose objects and arrows in $\D^b(k)$ are \begin{equation}\label{eq:evaluated-diagram} D_i\otimes_B M(Y),\qquad f_a\otimes_B\id_{M(Y)} \end{equation} is isomorphic to the diagram having $eY\oplus(eY)[3]$ at all three vertices, identities on $s,s'$, and the action of $\alpha(x_h)$ on both summands for $a_h,a'_h$.
