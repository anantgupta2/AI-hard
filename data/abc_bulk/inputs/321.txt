FAMILY 321: A counterexample to Wall's finite D(2) problem
SUBJECT: Topology
SUMMARY: Constructs a finite connected three-dimensional CW complex whose universal cover has no integral homology above degree two and whose third cohomology vanishes for every local coefficient module, but which has no finite two-dimensional homotopy model. This disproves Wall's finite D(2) conjecture; the example has infinite fundamental group.
LEAN: no

--- MANUSCRIPT: A Counterexample to Wall's D(2) Problem (October-6-2026)
ABSTRACT: We give a negative answer to Wall's finite $`D(2)`$ problem. We construct a finite connected three-dimensional CW complex satisfying the $`D(2)`$ finiteness condition but not homotopy equivalent to any finite CW complex of dimension at most two. The example has infinite fundamental group.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There is a finite connected three-dimensional CW complex $X$ such that \[ H_i(\widetilde X;\Z)=0\quad(i>2),\qquad H^3(X;M)=0\quad\text{for every }\Z[\pi_1(X)]\text{-module }M, \] but $X$ is not homotopy equivalent to any finite CW complex of dimension at most two.
[theorem] [Tower obstruction] \label{thm:tower} Let $Y$ be a finite connected CW complex of dimension at most two, and let $L$ be a rank-one complex local system on $Y$ with $H_2(Y;L)=0$. There is no map $f:F\to Y$ from a finite connected simplicial complex satisfying $b_1(F;\Q)=0$ for which $f^*L$ has nontrivial monodromy.
[proposition] [Finite plus construction]\label{prop:plus} Let $B$ be a finite connected CW complex of dimension at most two, with $\pi_1(B)=P$. Suppose that the normal closure $K$ of elements $t_1,\ldots,t_r\in P$ is perfect, and put $G=P/K$. There is a finite complex $X\supseteq B$, obtained by attaching $r$ cells of dimension two and $r$ cells of dimension three, such that: \begin{enumerate} \item the inclusion induces the quotient $P\to G$ on fundamental groups; \item $H_i(\widetilde X;\Z)=0$ for every $i>2$, and $H^3(X;M)=0$ for every left $\Z G$-module $M$; \item for every right $\Z G$-module $N$, the inclusion induces $H_*(B;N)\cong H_*(X;N)$, with the coefficients on $B$ pulled back along $P\to G$. \end{enumerate}
