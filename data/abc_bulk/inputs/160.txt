FAMILY 160: Superexponential van der Waerden numbers
SUBJECT: Combinatorics
SUMMARY: Resolves Erdős's superexponential-growth question for van der Waerden numbers. If $`W_r(k)`$ is the least interval length forcing a monochromatic k-term progression in every r-coloring, then $`W_r(k)\gt k^{ck\lfloor\log_2 r\rfloor}`$ for an absolute c &gt; 0, all r ≥ 2 and sufficiently large k, uniformly in r. In particular, $`W_r(k)^{1/k}\to\infty`$ for each fixed r.
LEAN: yes

--- MANUSCRIPT: Quantitative Superexponential Bounds for van der Waerden Numbers (September-23-2026)
ABSTRACT: We prove that there are absolute constants c &gt; 0 and K0 such that $`W_r(k)\gt k^{ck\lfloor\log_2 r\rfloor}`$ for every $`k\ge K_0`$ and r ≥ 2. Consequently $`W_r(k)^{1/k}\to\infty`$ for each fixed r ≥ 2, giving a quantitative positive resolution of Erdős's superexponential-growth question, including the two-color case.
[theorem] \label{main:intro} There is an absolute integer $K_0$ such that, for every integer $k\ge K_0$ and every integer $r\ge2$, \[ W_r(k)>k^{c k\lfloor\log_2r\rfloor}, \qquad c=10^{-5}. \]
[proposition] [Balanced outer coloring]\label{outer:coloring} For all sufficiently large $k$, there is a map $c_*:\cL\to\{0,1\}$ with the following properties. \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}] \item In every cyclic progression having at least $\delta k$ light positions, each bit occurs on at least one quarter of its light positions when labels are colored by $c_*$. \item In every eligible recorded pattern of \Cref{count:eligible}, each bit occurs on at least one quarter of its regular positions. \end{enumerate} The threshold on $k$ is absolute.
[theorem] [Outer-color dichotomy]\label{dichotomy:main} For every sufficiently large $k$, the coloring $c_0$ from \Cref{outer:pullback} has the following property. Every cyclic progression $n_j=n_0+jd$, $0\le j<k$, with $d\ne0$ in $G$, satisfies at least one of these alternatives: \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}] \item each bit occurs among $c_0(n_0),\ldots,c_0(n_{k-1})$ at least $\gamma k$ times; \item there are $A,B\in\R^D$ such that $\tilde y(n_j)=A+jB$ for all $0\le j<k$. \end{enumerate} The threshold on $k$ is absolute.
