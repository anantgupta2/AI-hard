FAMILY 074: Kakeya in three and four dimensions
SUBJECT: Real and complex analysis
SUMMARY: Resolves the Kakeya maximal conjecture in three dimensions and the Hausdorff-dimension conjecture in four. In three dimensions, the radius-δ tube maximal operator maps $`L^3(\mathbb R^3)`$ to $`L^3(S^2)`$ with norm $`O_\varepsilon(\delta^{-\varepsilon})`$ for every ε &gt; 0. In four dimensions, every set containing a unit segment in every direction has Hausdorff dimension four.
LEAN: no

--- MANUSCRIPT: The Kakeya maximal conjecture in three dimensions (September-23-2026)
ABSTRACT: We prove the Kakeya maximal conjecture in three dimensions. For every ε &gt; 0, the maximal average over unit tubes of radius δ maps $`L^3(\mathbb R^3)`$ to $`L^3(S^2)`$ with norm at most $`C_\varepsilon\delta^{-\varepsilon}`$.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} For every \(\varepsilon>0\) there exists a finite constant \(C_\varepsilon\) such that, for every \(0<\delta<1\) and every \(f\in L^3(\R^3)\), \[ \|K_\delta f\|_{L^3(S^2)} \le C_\varepsilon\delta^{-\varepsilon} \|f\|_{L^3(\R^3)}. \]
[theorem] [Kakeya set estimate]\label{thm:set-input} For every $\varepsilon>0$ there are $\eta_{\mathrm{in}}>0$ and $\delta_0>0$ such that, if $0<\delta<\delta_0$, \[ \Delta_{\max}(\mathbb T)\le\delta^{-\eta_{\mathrm{in}}}, \qquad \lambda(\mathbb T,Y)\ge\delta^{\eta_{\mathrm{in}}}, \] then \begin{equation}\label{eq:set-input} \left|\bigcup_{T\in\mathbb T}Y(T)\right| \ge\delta^\varepsilon\sum_{T\in\mathbb T}|T|. \end{equation}

--- MANUSCRIPT: Every four-dimensional Kakeya set has full Hausdorff dimension (September-24-2026)
ABSTRACT: We prove the four-dimensional Hausdorff-dimension Kakeya conjecture: every subset of ℝ4 containing a unit line segment in every direction has Hausdorff dimension four. No compactness or regularity assumption is imposed on the set or its witnessing line family.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} Let \(K\subset\R^4\). Suppose that for every \(e\in S^3\) there is a point \(a_e\in\R^4\) such that \[ \{a_e+te:0\leq t\leq1\}\subset K. \] Then \(\dim_H K=4\).
[proposition] [Initial horizon bound]\label{a01:horizon-bound} Every homogeneous exact problem satisfies \(H(E)\le L\).
[theorem] [Reduction to a bad weighted problem] \label{thm:model-reduction} If a set \(K\subset\R^4\), with no measurability or compactness assumption, contains a unit segment in every direction and satisfies \(\dim_{\mathrm H}K<4\), then there are \(0<d<1\), \(S,\eta>0\), and a tempered homogeneous exact problem \(E\) of version \(2\) in \(C(d)\) such that \(H(E)>0\).
