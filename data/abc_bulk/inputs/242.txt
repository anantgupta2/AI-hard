FAMILY 242: Single-fold Diophantine representations and undecidability under an at-most-one-solution promise
SUBJECT: Mathematical logic
SUMMARY: Every recursively enumerable set of tuples of natural numbers has a Diophantine representation with exactly one auxiliary solution for each member and none for nonmembers. This proves the single-fold conjecture and hence the finite-fold conjecture. Diophantine solvability over the nonnegative integers remains undecidable even with an at-most-one-solution promise.
LEAN: yes

--- MANUSCRIPT: Single-fold Diophantine representations (September-24-2026)
ABSTRACT: Every recursively enumerable set of natural-number tuples has a polynomial Diophantine representation with exactly one complete auxiliary tuple for each member. This proves the single-fold conjecture and, consequently, the finite-fold conjecture.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} For every integer $n\ge1$ and every recursively enumerable set $S\subseteq\NN^n$, there are an integer $m\ge1$ and a polynomial $P\in\ZZ[A_1,\ldots,A_n,W_1,\ldots,W_m]$ such that, for every $a\in\NN^n$, \[ \#\{w\in\NN^m:P(a,w)=0\}= \begin{cases}1,&a\in S,\\0,&a\notin S.\end{cases} \]
[proposition] \label{prop:rank-one} The group \(E(\QQ)\) has rank one and torsion subgroup \[ \mathcal T=\{O,(0,0),(5,0),(-5,0)\}. \] Consequently there exists \(P\in E(\QQ)\) such that every rational point has a unique expression \(nP+t\), with \(n\in\ZZ\) and \(t\in\mathcal T\).
[proposition] \label{prop:growth} There are a total function \(g:\NN\to\NN\), a real constant \(\delta>0\), and an integer \(u_0\ge4\) such that the graph of \(g\) has a single-fold Diophantine representation, including uniqueness of its output and every auxiliary coordinate for each input, and \begin{equation}\label{eq:growth-bounds} \delta(\log u)^2<\log g(u)<u\log u \qquad(u\ge u_0). \end{equation}
