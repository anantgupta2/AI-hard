FAMILY 004: Hilbert’s tenth problem over ℚ
SUBJECT: Number theory
SUMMARY: Proves that no algorithm decides whether an integer-coefficient polynomial in an arbitrary number of variables has a rational zero, resolving Hilbert's tenth problem over ℚ negatively.
LEAN: no

--- MANUSCRIPT: Hilbert’s tenth problem over the rational numbers (October-6-2026)
ABSTRACT: We give a negative answer to Hilbert's tenth problem over the rational numbers: no algorithm decides whether a polynomial with integer coefficients has a rational zero. The number of variables is part of the input.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There is no algorithm which, given a polynomial $f\in\Z[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$, with $n$ part of the input, decides whether $f$ has a zero in $\Q^n$.
[proposition] \label{ell:rank} The group $E(F)$ has rank one. Consequently there exist a positive integer $m$ and a nontorsion point $P\in E(F)$ such that \[ mE(F)=\Z P. \] We fix such $m$ and $P$ once and for all.
[proposition] \label{ell:formal} There is a fixed finite set of rational primes $S_E$ with the following properties. Let $p\notin S_E$, let $v$ be any place of $F$ above $p$, normalized by $v(p)=1$, and let $n,n'\in\Z$, with $n'\ne0$. Assume $nP,n'P$ reduce to $O$ at $v$. Then \begin{equation}\label{ell:ratio} v\!\left(\frac{z(nP)}{z(n'P)}\right)=v_p(n/n'). \end{equation} For every integer $K\ge1$, if in addition $p>K+1$ and $v_p(n/n')\ge0$, then $\ell_K(z(n'P))\ne0$ and \begin{equation}\label{ell:truncation} v\!\left(\frac{\ell_K(z(nP))}{\ell_K(z(n'P))}-\frac n{n'}\right) \ge K. \end{equation} Both assertions also hold at the same place $v$ after replacing $E,P,\ell_K$ by $E^\sigma,P^\sigma,\ell_K^\sigma$ and imposing the corresponding reduction-to-$O$ hypotheses on the conjugate points. Here and below the valuation of zero is $+\infty$, so $n=0$ is included.

--- MANUSCRIPT: A pointwise 2-converse for elliptic curves with rational two-torsion (October-7-2026)
ABSTRACT: We prove a pointwise 2-converse for elliptic curves over $`\mathbf Q`$ with nonzero rational two-torsion: if the $`2^\infty`$-Selmer corank is zero or one, then the analytic rank and Mordell–Weil rank equal that corank, and the Shafarevich–Tate group is finite. The result allows arbitrary reduction at 2.
[theorem] [The rational-two-torsion $2$-converse]\label{thm:main} Let $E/\Q$ be an elliptic curve with $E(\Q)[2]\ne0$ and $s_2(E)\in\{0,1\}$. Then \[ \ord_{s=1}L(E,s)=\rank_{\Z}E(\Q)=s_2(E), \qquad \Sha(E/\Q)\text{ is finite}. \]
[theorem] [Cyclotomic lower bound]\label{cy:lower-bound} There is a constant $C_E$ such that, for every allowed $h$ with $L(E^{(h)},1)\ne0$, \[ v_2(\mathcal L(h))\ \geq\ 2w(h)+s(h)-C_E. \] The constant is independent of the number and sizes of the primes dividing $h$.
[theorem] [Transfer to the missing vertex]\label{cy:missing-vertex} Let $h_0$ be an allowed discriminant with $s_2(E^{(h_0)})=0$. Suppose that for arbitrarily large $b$ there are a finite set $Q$ of distinct primes $q\nmid 2Nh_0$ and linear forms $\lambda_q:\mathbb F _2^b\longrightarrow\mathbb F _2$ giving \[ h_x=h_0\prod_{q\in Q}(q^*)^{\lambda_q(x)},\qquad x\in\mathbb F _2^b, \] all in the fixed local and sign classes. Suppose that a number $B$, independent of the family, satisfies \[ L(E^{(h_x)},1)\ne0,\qquad D(h_x)\leq B\qquad(x\ne0). \] Then $L(E^{(h_0)},1)\ne0$ and $D(h_0)\leq B+C_E$. Here $C_E$ is independent of $h_0$, $Q$, and $b$; the dimension needed in the proof is allowed to depend on the fixed precision being tested.
