=== MANUSCRIPT: The crossing number of complete bipartite graphs [preprints/The-crossing-number-of-complete-bipartite-graphs-September-23-2026/build/paper.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove the Zarankiewicz crossing-number conjecture, resolving Turán's brickyard problem. For all positive integers m, n, the ordinary crossing number of the complete bipartite graph $`K_{m,n}`$ is
[theorem] \label{thm:main} For all positive integers $m,n$, \[ \crn(K_{m,n})=d_m d_n. \]
[corollary] [Rank sum for differences] \label{cor:rank-sum} Let $X_1,\ldots,X_m,Y_1,\ldots,Y_m$ be real $d\times d$ matrices. If $X_i-Y_i$ is invertible for every $i$, then \begin{equation} \label{eq:rank-sum} \sum_{\substack{1\leq i,j\leq m\\i\ne j}} \rank(X_i-Y_j)\geq2d_m d, \qquad d_m=\left\lfloor\frac m2\right\rfloor \left\lfloor\frac{m-1}{2}\right\rfloor. \end{equation} The sum is over ordered pairs of distinct indices.

- Tur\'an described the problem's origin in rail connections between kilns and storage yards at a brick factory near Budapest in 1944~\cite[pp.~8--9]{Turan1977}. Zarankiewicz supplied the balanced-axis construction, but the published argument for its optimality contained a gap \cite{Zarankiewicz1955,Turan1977,ErdosGuy1973}. Kleitman established the exact formula when one vertex class has at most six vertices~\cite{Kleitman1970}. His permutation argument already used the local cyclic orders of incident edges to bound crossings between two stars \cite[Section~8, equation~(17)]{Kleitman1970}. - Tutte developed an algebraic approach to crossing numbers~\cite{Tutte1970}. Our proof combines signed intersections of edge interiors with a contribution determined by the incident-edge order at a common endpoint. The resulting cycle pairing separates the topological information from the algebraic estimates.
- The proof separates the geometry of a drawing from two linear-algebraic statements. In Section~\ref{sec:subspaces}, we prove that subspaces $U_i,V_i$ of an $N$-dimensional real vector space satisfying $U_i\cap V_i=0$ obey \begin{equation} \label{eq:intro-subspaces} \sum_{i,j=1}^m\dim(U_i\cap V_j) \le \left\lfloor\frac{m^2}{4}\right\rfloor N. \end{equation} The argument uses interpolation with subspace constraints at finitely many nodes and counts the zeros of polynomial minors. Related determinant multiplicity arguments control sums of intersection dimensions in the theory of subspace designs~\cite{GuruswamiKopparty2016}. Here the degree filtration is adapted to arbitrary subspaces $U_i$; diagonal disjointness bounds the rank at the interpolation nodes. Applied to graphs of linear maps, the resulting inequality gives a lower bound on the sum of the ranks of differences $X_i-Y_j$ of square matrices, provided every diagonal difference $X_i-Y_i$ is invertible. Both formulations are independent of graph drawings.
