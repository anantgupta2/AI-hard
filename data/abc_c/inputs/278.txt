=== MANUSCRIPT: A Coulomb ground-state density without Kohn-Sham ensemble representation [preprints/A-Coulomb-Ground-State-Density-without-Kohn-Sham-Ensemble-Representation-September-25-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We construct a finite three-electron Coulomb molecule whose spin-summed ground-state density cannot be reproduced by any ground-state ensemble of noninteracting electrons in a single real, spin-independent local potential in $`L^{3/2}(\mathbf R^3)+L^\infty(\mathbf R^3)`$. The molecule has two equal positive integer nuclear charges, specified by an exact finite nonnumerical formula.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} Set \(D=10^{200}\) and \(e_z=(0,0,1)\). There is a finite positive integer \(Z\), specified by \Cref{eq:explicit-charge}, for which the Coulomb Hamiltonian \[ H_C=\sum_{i=1}^3 \left(-\frac12\Delta_i -\frac{Z}{|r_i-(D/Z)e_z|} -\frac{Z}{|r_i+(D/Z)e_z|}\right) +\sum_{1\le i<j\le3}\frac1{|r_i-r_j|} \] has a normalizable absolute ground state \(\Psi\) in the sector \(S_z=1/2\), normalized by \(\|\Psi\|_2=1\), but there is no \(v\in\mathcal V\) and no ground-state ensemble \(\Gamma\) of \(H_s(v)\) such that \[ n_\Gamma=n_\Psi\qquad\text{almost everywhere on }\R^3. \]
[proposition] [Dilation to integer nuclear charges] \label{prop:scaling} Suppose that the density of a ground state of \(H_{3,1/Z}\), for a positive integer \(Z\), has no noninteracting ground-state ensemble representation with a real spin-independent potential in \(L^{3/2}(\R^3)+L^\infty(\R^3)\). The physical Coulomb system with three electrons and charges \(Z,Z\) at \[ (0,0,D/Z),\qquad (0,0,-D/Z) \] then has a ground-state density with the same obstruction.
[proposition] [Two-centre spectrum] \label{prop:two-centre-spectrum} For \(D\ge100\), the two lowest eigenvalues of \(h_D\) are simple. They have normalized real, axisymmetric eigenfunctions \(b,e\), where \(b>0\) is even and \(e\) is odd and strictly positive on \(z>0\). Write \[ \epsilon_b=-\frac{\gamma^2}{2},\qquad \epsilon_e=-\frac{k_0^2}{2},\qquad d_*=\epsilon_e-\epsilon_b . \] Then \begin{equation} 0<d_*<\frac{100}{D},\qquad |\gamma-1|+|k_0-1|<\frac{200}{D}. \label{eq:two-centre-numerics} \end{equation} The spectrum on \(\{b,e\}^{\perp}\) lies more than \(0.2\) above \(\epsilon_e\). In particular, the spectral gap above \(b\) in the even subspace is greater than \(0.2\).
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- \begin{abstract} We construct a finite three-electron Coulomb molecule whose spin-summed ground-state density cannot be reproduced by any ground-state ensemble of noninteracting electrons in a single real, spin-independent local potential in \(L^{3/2}(\R^3)+L^\infty(\R^3)\). The molecule has two equal positive integer nuclear charges, specified by an exact finite nonnumerical formula. \end{abstract}
- The asymptotic mechanism behind our construction was investigated by Gori-Giorgi, G\'al, and Baerends~\cite{GGB}. When the leading removal amplitude has a nodal plane, coupling to other ionic channels can give the density the same exponential decay rate on and off that plane, with an additional polynomial suppression on the plane. Their analysis identifies the role of the angular part of the Laplacian and the resulting difficulties for a regular Kohn--Sham potential. Gori-Giorgi and Baerends subsequently studied the effective equation for the square root of the density and exact harmonically confined examples~\cite{GGB18}. That effective equation is distinct from the common local potential determined below by two orbitals and an ensemble kinetic certificate. Here we prove the necessary estimates for a finite Coulomb system, and supply two further ingredients: a certificate valid against all ground-state ensembles and an obstruction to the entire sum space \(\mathcal V\).
- \subsection{Organization and conventions}
- The leading removal amplitude has a simple zero on the median plane, but a complementary ionic channel supplies a nonzero density tail there with the same exponential rate. This is the nodal-plane mechanism discussed in \cite[Section~V.A]{GGB}. We prove the estimates, including their uniformity as the interaction tends to zero, without assuming an asymptotic expansion of finitely many Dyson orbitals.
