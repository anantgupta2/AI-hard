FAMILY 197: A torsion-free group algebra that is not directly finite
SUBJECT: Algebra
OPENAI SUMMARY: Constructs a finitely presented torsion-free nonsofic group whose group algebra over 𝔽2 is not directly finite, disproving Kaplansky's conjecture even without torsion. Companion examples give injective nonsurjective cellular automata on all configurations, refuting Gottschalk's surjunctivity conjecture. Another counterexample is an integral group-ring matrix, invertible over the rational group ring, with Fuglede–Kadison determinant strictly between zero and one, disproving the unrestricted Determinant Conjecture.
HEADLINE (v2, semi-formal): ∃ finitely presented torsion-free G ∃ a,b,c ∈ F_2[G]: ab = 1 ∧ ac = 0 ∧ c ≠ 0 (hence ba ≠ 1)

- \maketitle \begin{abstract} We construct a finitely presented torsion-free counterexample to Kaplansky's direct finiteness conjecture over the field of two elements. The group admits a finite two-dimensional classifying complex. \end{abstract}
- A unital ring $R$ is \emph{directly finite} if $ab=1$ implies $ba=1$ for all $a,b\in R$. For a field $K$ and a group $G$, the group algebra $K[G]$ consists of finite formal sums of elements of $G$ with coefficients in $K$, with multiplication extending the group operation bilinearly. Kaplansky's direct finiteness conjecture asserts that every such group algebra is directly finite. We prove that the implication can fail even when the field is finite and the group is finitely presented and torsion-free. A classifying complex for $G$ is a connected CW complex with fundamental group $G$ and contractible universal cover.
- In arbitrary characteristic, Ara, O'Meara, and Perera proved stable finiteness for group algebras of free-by-amenable groups over division rings~\cite{AraOMearaPerera2002}. Elek and Szab\'o extended the conclusion to sofic groups~\cite[Corollary~4.7]{ElekSzabo2004}, a class defined by finite approximate permutation actions. Their theorem implies that the group in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is not sofic. A complementary approach studies the finite cancellation patterns forced by a one-sided inverse. Dykema, Heister, and Juschenko organized these patterns into universal groups and established direct finiteness for several small support pairs over $\Ftwo$~\cite{DykemaHeisterJuschenko2015}.
- Two companion manuscripts are especially relevant. A separate construction gives a scalar counterexample over a finite field of characteristic two for a finitely presented group with odd-order torsion~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{OpenAI2026DF}. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} uses the prime field $\Ftwo$ and a torsion-free group. Its method develops the graph and cone construction of \emph{A Torsion-Free Group Algebra with Zero Divisors}~\cite{OpenAI2026ZD}. That manuscript supplies the parity mechanism for a zero product and the arrangement-exclusion strategy. We adapt those arguments to produce a one-sided inverse and reproduce the estimates and topology needed here.
- Graphical presentations relate coincidences of labeled paths to geometric properties of quotient groups, as in Gromov's random graphical constructions~\cite{Gromov2003} and Ollivier's small-cancellation theorem~\cite{Ollivier2006}. Bereznyuk's spherical-diagram criterion for graphical asphericity distinguishes coincident graph lifts from genuinely different ones~\cite{Bereznyuk2019}. The same distinction is essential here: pairings of two occurrences of the same graph edge are removed by cone surgeries. We prove the required asphericity and root protection directly in Section~\ref{sec:topology}.
