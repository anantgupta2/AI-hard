=== MANUSCRIPT: Weak pure infiniteness and O-infinity absorption [preprints/Weak-pure-infiniteness-and-O-infinity-absorption-September-25-2026/build/manuscript.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove that every complex C∗-algebra in which each positive element is properly infinite is strongly purely infinite. This answers the ordinary-to-strong part of Kirchberg–Rørdam's comparison question. For exact algebras, we also prove that proper infiniteness of one fixed finite amplification of every positive element implies proper infiniteness of each positive element. Consequently, every separable nuclear algebra with this fixed-amplification property absorbs $`\mathcal O_\infty`$, without assumptions of unitality or simplicity.
[theorem] \label{thm:individual-to-strong} Let \(C\) be a complex \(C^*\)-algebra satisfying \(\propP\). For all \(a,b\in C_+\), \(c\in C\), and \(\varepsilon>0\), there are \(s,t\in C\) such that \begin{equation}\label{eq:main-diagonalization} \|s^*as-a\|<\varepsilon,\qquad \|t^*bt-b\|<\varepsilon,\qquad \|s^*ct\|<\varepsilon. \end{equation} Consequently \(C\) is strongly purely infinite.
[theorem] \label{thm:exact-infiniteness} Let \(C\) be an exact complex \(C^*\)-algebra. Suppose that there is one integer \(n\ge1\) such that \(h^{\oplus n}\) is properly infinite for every \(h\in C_+\). Then every positive element of \(C\) is properly infinite. Consequently \(C\) is strongly purely infinite.
[corollary] \label{cor:absorption} Let \(A\) be a separable nuclear complex \(C^*\)-algebra. If there is one integer \(n\ge1\) such that \(h^{\oplus n}\) is properly infinite for every \(h\in A_+\), then \[ A\cong A\otimes_{\min}\Oinf. \]
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- \begin{abstract} We prove that every complex \(C^*\)-algebra in which each positive element is properly infinite is strongly purely infinite. This answers the ordinary-to-strong part of Kirchberg--R{\o}rdam's comparison question. For exact algebras, we also prove that proper infiniteness of one fixed finite amplification of every positive element implies proper infiniteness of each positive element. Consequently, every separable nuclear algebra with this fixed-amplification property absorbs \(\Oinf\), without assumptions of unitality or simplicity. \end{abstract}
- Pure infiniteness expresses the ability of positive elements of a \(C^*\)-algebra to contain several copies of themselves in Cuntz comparison. For a nonsimple algebra, an individual comparison and the simultaneous comparison of two elements are different demands: the latter must also control a mixed entry. We prove that proper infiniteness of every positive element already provides this compatibility. We then give two ways to remove a fixed finite amplification, one for exact algebras and an independent construction for nuclear algebras. In the separable nuclear case the result yields absorption of the Cuntz algebra \(\Oinf\).
- The cross term \(c\) is arbitrary and need not be a product of the diagonal inputs. Taking \((a,b,c)=(x^2,y^2,xy)\) gives the stated consequence. Arbitrary-entry diagonalization is a known equivalent form of strong pure infiniteness \cite[Remarks~3.2(i)--(ii)]{KS}; our proof obtains it directly from \(\propP\). It also gives coefficient bounds for positive contraction inputs that are independent of the requested mixed-entry accuracy; see Proposition~\ref{prop:bounded-diagonalization}.
- \subsection{Proof strategy and the independent routes}
- The construction has two parts. A graph of orthogonal copies first turns possible failure into finitely many explicit equalities. Once those equalities have been excluded, ideal cutoffs convert the resulting nonvanishing into one global support estimate. We prove both parts here while leaving the finite exclusion problem to the later arguments. Throughout this section put \[ s=\frac14,\qquad \alpha_0=\frac1{32}. \] When the integer \(k\ge1\) has been chosen, fix \(\gamma_g,\sigma_g>0\) with \begin{equation}\label{eq:graph-constants} \gamma_g^2+k\sigma_g^2<1. \end{equation} For example, \(\gamma_g=1/2\) and \(\sigma_g=1/(2\sqrt{k+1})\) satisfy this inequality. Both the number of coordinates and these coefficients are fixed before the measurements below are computed.
