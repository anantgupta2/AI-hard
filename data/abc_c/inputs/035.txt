ABSTRACT: We prove the numerical-dimension-one case of log abundance for threefolds over algebraically closed fields of characteristic p &gt; 3. If $`(X,B)`$ is a projective log canonical threefold pair with effective rational boundary, and $`K_X+B`$ is ℚ-Cartier, nef, and of numerical dimension one, then $`K_X+B`$ is semiample. Neither terminality nor ℚ-factoriality of X is required.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p>3$. Let $(X,B)$ be a projective log canonical threefold pair over $k$, where $X$ is normal, $B$ is an effective $\Q$-divisor, and $K_X+B$ is $\Q$-Cartier. If $K_X+B$ is nef and has numerical dimension one, then $K_X+B$ is semiample.

- The abundance conjecture predicts that a nef canonical divisor on a projective variety with mild singularities is semiample: some positive Cartier multiple is generated by its global sections. Such sections turn numerical information about the canonical divisor into a morphism that records the canonical geometry of the variety. For a nef divisor $L$ on a threefold, numerical dimension one means $L\cdot H^2>0$ and $L^2\cdot H=0$ for an ample Cartier divisor $H$. We prove this case of abundance for terminal threefolds in characteristic greater than three.
- Section~\ref{sec:laurent} turns the calculation into a formal-neighborhood criterion, Theorem~\ref{thm:laurent-vanishing}: a bundle satisfying an Euler identity, bounded polar parts, and a concentration condition for leading jets has vanishing first Laurent cohomology. A bound on the source cuts of incoming differentials ensures that the constructed primitives actually converge with bounded poles. We prove this criterion directly for finite covers and completed lattices. The criterion applies to one-forms and exact forms.
- The adjunction calculation for \(\Sigma\) uses an intersection inequality on excellent local surfaces, whose residue fields need not be perfect. We prove that inequality first and also derive regularity of \(X\) in codimension two. For a resolution unchanged over \(X_{\mathrm{reg}}\setminus\Supp N_0\), this regularity ensures that exceptional divisors with centers outside \(\Supp N_0\) are disjoint from its total pullback.
- We claim \(R^1h_*\cO_T(M)=0\). For every nonzero effective integral exceptional cycle \(Z\), the divisor \(Z-A_{\mathrm{fr}}\) has a positive coefficient. Lemma~\ref{lem:exceptional-intersections} gives a component \(E_j\) with positive coefficient in \(Z\) such that \((Z-A_{\mathrm{fr}})\cdot E_j<0\). Using \eqref{eq:surface-relative-vanishing-input}, \[ (M-Z+E_j)\cdot E_j>(K_T+E_j)\cdot E_j =\deg_l\omega_{E_j/l}. \] Curve duality gives \(H^1(E_j,\cO_{E_j}(M-Z+E_j))=0\): the dual space consists of sections of a line bundle of negative degree. The exact sequence \[ 0\longrightarrow\cO_{E_j}(M-Z+E_j) \longrightarrow\cO_Z(M) \longrightarrow\cO_{Z-E_j}(M)\longrightarrow0 \] then proves \(H^1(Z,\cO_Z(M))=0\) by induction on the sum of the coefficients of \(Z\). Exceptional cycles give a cofinal system of neighborhoods of the exceptional fiber. Formal functions, followed by faithful completion for the coherent higher direct image, proves the claim.
