ABSTRACT: For every integer d ≥ 3, we construct arbitrarily large finite d-dimensional simplicial complexes with uniformly bounded vertex degrees and uniform 𝔽2 coboundary expansion in every degree below d. Together with the known graph and two-dimensional cases, this establishes the existence of bounded-degree 𝔽2 coboundary expanders in every positive dimension.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} For every integer $d\ge3$, there exist constants $D<\infty$, $\eps>0$, and finite connected pure $d$-dimensional simplicial complexes $X_m$, with $|X_m(0)|\longrightarrow\infty$, such that every vertex belongs to at most $D$ faces of dimension $d$ and, for every $f\in C^i(X_m;\F)$, \begin{equation}\label{eq:goal} \norm{\delta_i f}_{X_m,i+1} \ge \eps\,\dist_{X_m,i}(f,B^i(X_m)) \qquad(0\le i<d). \end{equation} The constants are independent of $m$, $i$, and $f$.
[proposition] \label{prop:L-acyclic} The complex $L$ satisfies \eqref{eq:L-acyclic}.
[corollary] \label{cor:generation} The simple generators $x_i$, $i\in S$, generate $U$.
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- \maketitle \begin{abstract} For every integer $d\ge3$, we construct arbitrarily large finite $d$-dimensional simplicial complexes with uniformly bounded vertex degrees and uniform $\mathbb F_2$ coboundary expansion in every degree below $d$. Together with the known graph and two-dimensional cases, this establishes the existence of bounded-degree $\mathbb F_2$ coboundary expanders in every positive dimension. \end{abstract} \section{Introduction} \label{sec:introduction}
- In an expander graph, the edge boundary of a set of vertices controls its distance from the empty set or the whole vertex set. Coboundary expansion extends this condition to sets of faces in a simplicial complex. Over $\F$, the coboundary of a set of $i$-faces consists of the $(i+1)$-faces containing an odd number of them. We construct complexes of increasing size for which this boundary controls distance from the space of coboundaries, uniformly in the size and with a bounded number of top-dimensional faces at each vertex.
- The distinction between cocycles and coboundaries is essential. If $f\in Z^i(Y)\setminus B^i(Y)$, its coboundary vanishes while its distance from $B^i(Y)$ is positive. Thus \eqref{eq:goal} requires the vanishing of $H^i(Y;\F)=Z^i(Y)/B^i(Y)$, with reduced cohomology understood in degree zero. A uniform inequality against $Z^i(Y)$ alone allows nonzero cohomology. Our proof supplies both that quantitative inequality and the required vanishing for the same complexes.
- \paragraph{Proof strategy.} Fix $d$ and put $n=2d$ and $N=2d+1$. Over a sufficiently large fixed odd prime field $k$, let $U\le\SL_N(k[t])$ be the inverse image of the upper unitriangular group under evaluation at zero. We construct an infinite $n$-dimensional coset complex $L$ for $U$ and its finite congruence quotients $T_m$. The desired $X_m$ is the $d$-skeleton of $T_m$. The local groups are fixed finite groups, which gives uniform degree bounds; the congruence quotients grow with $m$.
- \paragraph{Organization.} Section~\ref{sec:construction} defines the congruence complexes. Section~\ref{sec:geometry} proves the local group identities and the acyclicity of $L$. Section~\ref{sec:homology} proves the cohomology vanishing of the finite quotients. Section~\ref{sec:expansion} checks the KMS hypotheses, compares skeleton weights, and proves \cref{thm:main}.
