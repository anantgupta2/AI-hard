FAMILY 321: A counterexample to Wall's finite D(2) problem
SUBJECT: Topology
OPENAI SUMMARY: Constructs a finite connected three-dimensional CW complex whose universal cover has no integral homology above degree two and whose third cohomology vanishes for every local coefficient module, but which has no finite two-dimensional homotopy model. This disproves Wall's finite D(2) conjecture; the example has infinite fundamental group.
HEADLINE (v2, semi-formal): ∃ finite connected 3-dim CW complex X: X satisfies D(2) ∧ ¬∃ finite CW complex Y of dim ≤2 with X ≃ Y

=== MANUSCRIPT: A Counterexample to Wall's D(2) Problem [preprints/A-Counterexample-to-Walls-D2-Problem-October-6-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We give a negative answer to Wall's finite $`D(2)`$ problem. We construct a finite connected three-dimensional CW complex satisfying the $`D(2)`$ finiteness condition but not homotopy equivalent to any finite CW complex of dimension at most two. The example has infinite fundamental group.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There is a finite connected three-dimensional CW complex $X$ such that \[ H_i(\widetilde X;\Z)=0\quad(i>2),\qquad H^3(X;M)=0\quad\text{for every }\Z[\pi_1(X)]\text{-module }M, \] but $X$ is not homotopy equivalent to any finite CW complex of dimension at most two.
[theorem] [Tower obstruction] \label{thm:tower} Let $Y$ be a finite connected CW complex of dimension at most two, and let $L$ be a rank-one complex local system on $Y$ with $H_2(Y;L)=0$. There is no map $f:F\to Y$ from a finite connected simplicial complex satisfying $b_1(F;\Q)=0$ for which $f^*L$ has nontrivial monodromy.
[corollary] [Characters and torsion] \label{cor:torsion} Let $Y$ be a finite connected CW complex of dimension at most two. If a character $\rho:\pi_1(Y)\to\C^\times$ satisfies $H_2(Y;\C_\rho)=0$, then $\rho(g)=1$ for every finite-order element $g\in\pi_1(Y)$.

- \subsection{Background and approach}
- Our obstruction uses a character. For a connected space $Y$, a homomorphism $\rho:\pi_1(Y)\to\C^\times$ determines a one-dimensional complex local system $L_\rho$. We prove that, when $Y$ is a finite two-dimensional complex, \begin{equation}\label{eq:obstruction-overview} H_2(Y;L_\rho)=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \rho(g)=1\ \text{for every finite-order }g\in\pi_1(Y). \end{equation} The proof gives the stronger statement that every map $F\to Y$ from a finite connected simplicial complex with $b_1(F;\Q)=0$ pulls $L_\rho$ back to a system with trivial monodromy. The method is a tower of infinite cyclic covers and image subcomplexes, following the cyclic-tower method of Howie~\cite{Howie81Pairs}; see also the formulation of Louder--Wilton~\cite[Section~3]{LouderWilton17} and its account of the earlier work of Papakyriakopoulos and Stallings. The additional input is that vanishing of rank-one twisted second homology persists under these cyclic covers, by a Laurent-polynomial rank argument.
- Section~\ref{sec:plus} presents the finite plus construction with its explicit cellular splitting. Section~\ref{sec:construction} then gives a five-generator, four-relator presentation and a perfect normal subgroup generated normally by two powers. The quotient has an element of order two detected by a complex character. An exact four-row boundary calculation shows that the presentation complex has zero second homology with that character. The plus construction preserves this vanishing and gives the required finite $D(2)$ complex. Equation~\eqref{eq:obstruction-overview} excludes its two-dimensional realization.
