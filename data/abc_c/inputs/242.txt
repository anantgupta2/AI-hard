=== MANUSCRIPT: Single-fold Diophantine representations [preprints/Single-fold-Diophantine-representations-September-24-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: Every recursively enumerable set of natural-number tuples has a polynomial Diophantine representation with exactly one complete auxiliary tuple for each member. This proves the single-fold conjecture and, consequently, the finite-fold conjecture.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} For every integer $n\ge1$ and every recursively enumerable set $S\subseteq\NN^n$, there are an integer $m\ge1$ and a polynomial $P\in\ZZ[A_1,\ldots,A_n,W_1,\ldots,W_m]$ such that, for every $a\in\NN^n$, \[ \#\{w\in\NN^m:P(a,w)=0\}= \begin{cases}1,&a\in S,\\0,&a\notin S.\end{cases} \]
[corollary] [Undecidability with an at-most-one-solution promise] \label{cor:unique-h10} There exist integers $m,D\ge1$ for which no algorithm has the following property: on every input polynomial $F\in\ZZ[W_1,\ldots,W_m]$ of total degree at most $D$ satisfying the promise \[ \#\{w\in\NN^m:F(w)=0\}\le1, \] the algorithm halts and correctly decides whether this set is nonempty.
[proposition] \label{prop:rank-one} The group \(E(\QQ)\) has rank one and torsion subgroup \[ \mathcal T=\{O,(0,0),(5,0),(-5,0)\}. \] Consequently there exists \(P\in E(\QQ)\) such that every rational point has a unique expression \(nP+t\), with \(n\in\ZZ\) and \(t\in\mathcal T\).
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- Our arithmetic construction develops the classical strategy of recovering powers from Pell solutions and controlling their indices through growth bounds. Cantone, Cuzziol, and Omodeo give a recent account, including a paired-Pell representation and the problem of replacing its exponential cutoff \cite[Section~6, Lemma~6.1 and Theorem~5; Section~7]{cco2024}. We supply a cutoff using rational points on a rank-one elliptic curve. Poonen uses rank-one curves and denominator growth to obtain Diophantine definitions between rings of integers \cite[Theorem~1 and Section~2.3, Lemma~8]{poonen2002}. Here the N\'eron--Tate height compares the full logarithmic x-coordinate height with a quadratic function of an integer index, with uniformly bounded additive error \cite[Chapter~VIII, Proposition~9.1 and Theorem~9.3]{silverman}. The bounded additive error makes the exact index selection below possible.
- \subsection{Proof overview} The problem is to retain uniqueness when a computation is expressed by polynomial equations. We first obtain unique arithmetic witnesses, then a unique encoding of the accepting run. Section~\ref{sec:conventions} explains how composition and disjoint cases preserve the complete witness, including variables introduced inside subsidiary calls.
