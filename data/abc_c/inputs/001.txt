=== MANUSCRIPT: Milne's rationality conjecture for abelian varieties [preprints/Milnes-rationality-conjecture-for-abelian-varieties-October-7-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove Milne's rationality conjecture for abelian varieties, including residue characteristic 2. After good reduction, the pairing of a rational Hodge class with any complementary product of divisor classes on the reduction is the same rational number in every prime-to-p realization and in crystalline cohomology. Using the Hodge theorem for CM abelian varieties, we also show that every such specialized Hodge class is represented by a single rational algebraic cycle in all these realizations.

[corollary] [CM transport of a Hodge class] \label{cor:cm-transport} Let \(A/\Qbar\) be an abelian variety of positive dimension with good reduction at \(w\), and let \(\gamma\in H^{2r}(A(\C),\Q(r))\) be a rational Hodge class, where \(0\leq r\leq\dim A\). There are a positive integer \(n\), a CM abelian variety \(B/\Qbar\) with good reduction at \(w\), a rational Hodge class \(\kappa\) of codimension \(r\) on \(B\), and a quasi-isogeny \(a:A_0^n\to B_0\) such that \[ \gamma_0=(aj)^*\kappa_0, \] where \(j:A_0\to A_0^n\) is the first-factor inclusion. This is an equality of the usual absolute-Hodge specializations in every prime-to-\(p\) realization and in crystalline cohomology extended to \(\Cw\).
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- \maketitle \vspace{-1em} \begin{abstract} We prove Milne's rationality conjecture for abelian varieties, including residue characteristic \(2\). After good reduction, the pairing of a rational Hodge class with any complementary product of divisor classes on the reduction is the same rational number in every prime-to-\(p\) realization and in crystalline cohomology. Using the Hodge theorem for CM abelian varieties, we also show that every such specialized Hodge class is represented by a single rational algebraic cycle in all these realizations. \end{abstract} \section{Introduction}
- Suppose an abelian variety has good reduction at a prime. A rational algebraic cycle on the variety specializes to a cycle on the reduction. Its intersection with any complementary product of divisors has a rational degree, and every cycle-class realization computes that same number. Milne's rationality conjecture asks for this conclusion when the original class is only known to be a rational Hodge class. We prove it for every residue characteristic.
- Milne proved that the rationality assertion for all CM abelian varieties is equivalent to the existence of such a good theory on varieties over \(\F\) whose connected components are products of abelian varieties and projective spaces \cite[Theorem~4.5]{Milne2009}. Our proof also expresses each specialized Hodge class as the pullback, along a rational homomorphism of special fibers, of a specialized Hodge class from a CM abelian variety. This extends the theory's specialization property beyond the CM case. In Corollary~\ref{cor:all-hodge-specialization}, every rational Hodge class on a good-reduction abelian variety specializes to a unique class in one fixed good theory. Its crystalline component gives the stated specialization after extension to \(\Cw\).
- A distinct additional input gives algebraicity on the special fiber. Combining the same transport with the Hodge theorem for CM abelian varieties \cite[Theorem~1.1]{OpenAICMHodge2026}, Corollary~\ref{cor:algebraic-specialization} represents each specialized Hodge class by one rational algebraic cycle in all the stated realizations. Its pairing with any complementary algebraic cycle is therefore a rational intersection number. This consequence uses the CM Hodge theorem; the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is independent of it.
- The determinant classes used in the proof belong to the theory of Weil classes developed by Weil, Deligne and Andr\'e; see \cite[Section~4 and Endnotes~M.10--M.12]{Deligne1982}. Andr\'e's refinement of the CM reduction expresses Hodge classes by pullbacks from varieties of split Weil type \cite{Andre1992}; a detailed account is \cite[Theorem~1]{Milne2020}. We prove directly the balanced form of that reduction needed here in Lemma~\ref{lem:cm-reduction}. Balanced signatures assert equal Hodge multiplicities at conjugate embeddings; split Weil type also requires the associated Hermitian form to have a half-dimensional isotropic subspace. Andr\'e's later theory of motivated cycles establishes a broader unconditional motivic setting for abelian Hodge classes \cite[Theorem~0.6.2]{Andre1996}, without identifying them with algebraic cycles.
