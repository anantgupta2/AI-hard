=== MANUSCRIPT: Hilbert’s tenth problem over the rational numbers [preprints/Hilberts-tenth-problem-over-the-rational-numbers-October-6-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We give a negative answer to Hilbert's tenth problem over the rational numbers: no algorithm decides whether a polynomial with integer coefficients has a rational zero. The number of variables is part of the input.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There is no algorithm which, given a polynomial $f\in\Z[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$, with $n$ part of the input, decides whether $f$ has a zero in $\Q^n$.
[proposition] \label{ell:rank} The group $E(F)$ has rank one. Consequently there exist a positive integer $m$ and a nontorsion point $P\in E(F)$ such that \[ mE(F)=\Z P. \] We fix such $m$ and $P$ once and for all.

PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- One approach studies the subrings \(\mathbb Z[S^{-1}]\subseteq\mathbb Q\), whose denominators use only primes in \(S\). Poonen constructed disjoint recursive sets of primes \(T_1,T_2\), each of natural density zero, such that positive-integer arithmetic has a Diophantine model in \(\mathbb Z[S^{-1}]\) whenever \(S\supseteq T_1\) and \(S\cap T_2=\varnothing\). Hilbert's tenth problem is undecidable for every such ring. Taking \(S\) to be the complement of \(T_2\) gives a recursive set of density one and a proper subring of \(\mathbb Q\) \cite[Theorem~1.3]{PoonenSubrings}. The construction uses selected multiples of a point on a rank-one elliptic curve whose real coordinates approximate integers.
- The elliptic approach also illuminates nearby rational decision problems. In a rank-one group, a multiple \(nP\) of a nontorsion point can represent the integer \(n\), and local coordinates or heights can reveal arithmetic information about that index. Cornelissen and Zahidi used elliptic divisibility sequences and existential valuation predicates in this direction. Under a conjectural odd primitive-divisor property for one such sequence, they obtained a Diophantine model of \((\mathbb Z,+,\mid)\) and proved undecidability of a positive \(\Pi_2\) fragment with one universal quantifier \cite[Conjecture~3.16 and Theorems~3.17 and~5.3]{CornelissenZahidi2007}. The assertion with one universal quantifier uses the correction by Cornelissen and Shlapentokh \cite[Remark~2.3 in the author version]{CornelissenShlapentokh2009}.
- Garcia-Fritz, Pasten, and Vidaux instead adjoined predicates comparing the logarithmic heights of rational tuples to the rational ring language. In this expanded language they gave a positive existential interpretation of \(\mathbb N\), and hence proved undecidability; comparisons between tuples of length at most three suffice \cite[Theorems~1.1--1.2]{GarciaFritzPastenVidaux2025}. Their construction uses elliptic canonical heights to encode consecutive squares. The present paper concerns ordinary rational polynomial solvability, and its height estimates enter the proof of that ordinary statement.
- \subsection{Proof strategy}
- Constant polynomials can be decided directly. For each nonconstant integral polynomial \(f\), we construct an effectively generated sequence of finite tests. Each test can be answered by finitely many rational-solvability queries, and the sequence satisfies \begin{equation}\label{intro:finite-test-interface} f\text{ has an integer zero} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{every test succeeds}. \end{equation} An integer zero supplies witnesses for all the tests. If a rational-solvability algorithm existed, we could run two searches in parallel: enumeration of integer zeros, and enumeration of tests until one fails. The arithmetic work is to prove that a failed test exists whenever \(f\) has no integer zero.
- We construct the finite rational tests announced in the introduction and prove that success of every test forces an ordinary integer zero. The construction has an ordinary integer model. Conversely, compactness will embed a model of all the tests in an elementary extension of \(\Q\), where arithmetic restrictions on its ring values will force an integer solution. The later sections prove the arithmetic inputs used here.

=== MANUSCRIPT: A pointwise 2-converse for elliptic curves with rational two-torsion [preprints/A-pointwise-2-converse-for-elliptic-curves-with-rational-two-torsion-October-7-2026/build/paper.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove a pointwise 2-converse for elliptic curves over $`\mathbf Q`$ with nonzero rational two-torsion: if the $`2^\infty`$-Selmer corank is zero or one, then the analytic rank and Mordell–Weil rank equal that corank, and the Shafarevich–Tate group is finite. The result allows arbitrary reduction at 2.
[theorem] [The rational-two-torsion $2$-converse]\label{thm:main} Let $E/\Q$ be an elliptic curve with $E(\Q)[2]\ne0$ and $s_2(E)\in\{0,1\}$. Then \[ \ord_{s=1}L(E,s)=\rank_{\Z}E(\Q)=s_2(E), \qquad \Sha(E/\Q)\text{ is finite}. \]
[theorem] [Cyclotomic lower bound]\label{cy:lower-bound} There is a constant $C_E$ such that, for every allowed $h$ with $L(E^{(h)},1)\ne0$, \[ v_2(\mathcal L(h))\ \geq\ 2w(h)+s(h)-C_E. \] The constant is independent of the number and sizes of the primes dividing $h$.
[theorem] [Transfer to the missing vertex]\label{cy:missing-vertex} Let $h_0$ be an allowed discriminant with $s_2(E^{(h_0)})=0$. Suppose that for arbitrarily large $b$ there are a finite set $Q$ of distinct primes $q\nmid 2Nh_0$ and linear forms $\lambda_q:\mathbb F _2^b\longrightarrow\mathbb F _2$ giving \[ h_x=h_0\prod_{q\in Q}(q^*)^{\lambda_q(x)},\qquad x\in\mathbb F _2^b, \] all in the fixed local and sign classes. Suppose that a number $B$, independent of the family, satisfies \[ L(E^{(h_x)},1)\ne0,\qquad D(h_x)\leq B\qquad(x\ne0). \] Then $L(E^{(h_0)},1)\ne0$ and $D(h_0)\leq B+C_E$. Here $C_E$ is independent of $h_0$, $Q$, and $b$; the dimension needed in the proof is allowed to depend on the fixed precision being tested.

- Here is the common interpolation problem. Fix the curve that is to be tested, and index a finite family of quadratic twists by $x\in\mathbb F_2^b$, with $x=0$ denoting that curve. Each auxiliary prime divides the twist parameter according to a linear form in $x$. We construct these families so that every nonzero address has the required analytic order and a uniform normalized valuation bound. An integral interpolation argument then recovers the value or derivative at the missing address $0$. The number and sizes of the auxiliary primes may grow with $b$; the valuation bound may not.

This companion does not vary with $h$ or with the cube dimension. The quadratic corank identity gives $s_2(E/K)=1$. We construct families with every new prime split in $K$ for which, at every nonzero address, $E^{(h)}$ has a simple zero and $E^{(hk)}$ has nonzero central value. The two coefficient tests together bound the normalized index of the ring-class Heegner sum over $K$. Ring-class interpolation then recovers the derivative at $h=1$. Thus $h_*$ defines the detector, $k'(h)$ proves its lower bound, and the fixed $k$ permits the final transfer.
