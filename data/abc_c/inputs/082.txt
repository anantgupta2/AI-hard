=== MANUSCRIPT: Annular variation of the triangular Hilbert transform at the symmetric point [preprints/Annular-variation-of-the-triangular-Hilbert-transform-at-the-symmetric-point-October-5-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove the annular r-variation estimate for the triangular Hilbert transform from complex $`L^3\times L^3`$ to L3/2 for every r &gt; 2. The partitions may depend on the output point and range over all positive scales. The estimate yields the two-endpoint maximal bound and joint almost-everywhere and norm principal values, and resolves the symmetric scalar triangular Hilbert transform problem.
[theorem] [Full annular variation]\label{thm:variation} For every real $r>2$ there is a finite constant $C_r$ such that \[ \|V_r(F,G)\|_{L^{3/2}(\R^2)} \le C_r\|F\|_{L^3(\R^2)}\|G\|_{L^3(\R^2)} \] for all complex $F,G\in L^3(\R^2)$.
[theorem] [Disjoint-annulus count estimate]\label{thm:count} There is an absolute constant $C$ such that, for every integer $n\ge1$ and every complex $F,G\in L^3(\R^2)$, \begin{equation}\label{eq:count-bound} \|\mathcal S_n(F,G)\|_{3/2} \le C\sqrt n\log(2+n)\|F\|_3\|G\|_3. \end{equation}
[proposition] [Smooth annuli with repeated choices] \label{prop:smooth-switches} Let $G_v\in C_c^\infty(\R^2)$ be complex. At each output pair $(a,b)$, make a step choice of at most $n\ge1$ disjoint radius intervals $(\varepsilon_j,R_j)$ and coefficients $|\beta_j|\le1$. For \[ \mathcal K_{a,b}(t)=\sum_j\beta_j I_{\varepsilon_j,R_j}(t) \] one has \[ |\Lambda_{\mathcal K}(G_0,G_1,G_2)| \le C\sqrt n\prod_{v=0}^2\|G_v\|_3. \] The constant is independent of the step partition, the menu of choices, and all endpoints.

- The strategy of controlling an entangled form by the change of a nonnegative energy has precedents in Kova\v{c}'s Bellman-function framework \cite[Section~2]{Kovac2011} and his telescoping identity for the twisted paraproduct \cite[Lemma~2]{Kovac2012}. These provide a methodological precedent, rather than a bound for the present triangular form. In particular, Kova\v{c} explicitly identifies the obstruction to applying that framework to non-bipartite graphs, including the triangle, in \cite[Section~6.2]{Kovac2011}. Here the energy is a trace of a matrix power, and the averaging is over the four children selected by the XOR constraint.
- The proof reduces the form to finite matrices by taking inputs constant on sufficiently small dyadic squares. On a fixed admissible triple, each input becomes a matrix between the atom spaces of its two variables. At each variable, we join its two incident matrices into a single row operator \(R\) and assign it the energy \(\tr((R^*R)^{3/2})\). Let \(e(\mathbf I)\) be the sum of these three energies, and let \(\operatorname{ch}(\mathbf I)\) consist of the four admissible triples obtained by halving each interval. The key estimate is \begin{equation}\label{eq:overview-gap} \abs{L_{\mathbf I}(F_0,F_1,F_2)} \le C_0\,2^{2k} \left(\frac14\sum_{\mathbf I'\in\operatorname{ch}(\mathbf I)} e(\mathbf I')-e(\mathbf I)\right), \qquad \mathbf I\in\cA_k, \end{equation} with an absolute constant \(C_0\). At each scale, the weighted total energy \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in\cA_k}2^{2k}e(\mathbf I)\) is bounded by an absolute constant times the sum of the cubed input norms.
- To prove \eqref{eq:overview-gap}, a matrix trace estimate bounds the triangular product by three Hessian quadratic forms of \(P\mapsto\tr(P^{3/2})\). Conditional on one child half, each neighboring half remains equally likely to be left or right. The two neighbors are correlated, but \(RR^*\) is a sum of the two edge Gram matrices; it contains no product between their multipliers and has the same nonzero spectrum as \(R^*R\). Convexity therefore converts the Hessian bound into an increase of the child energy. Writing the local integrals in normalized interval measures leaves the weight \(2^{2k}\). Each child has one parent and weight \(2^{2(k-1)}\), one quarter of the parent's weight, so these increases telescope.

ABSTRACT: For arbitrary complex inputs in $`L^3(\mathbb R^2)`$, we prove the pointwise maximal $`L^3\times L^3\to L^{3/2}`$ estimate for the triangular Hilbert transform, with the supremum over both hard truncation endpoints. The estimate yields joint almost-everywhere and L3/2 convergence as the lower endpoint tends to zero and the upper endpoint tends to infinity. This also proves the conjectured scalar estimate at the symmetric point.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There is an absolute constant $C$ such that, for every pair of complex functions $F,G\in L^3(\R^2)$, \[ \|B_*(F,G)\|_{L^{3/2}(\R^2)} \le C\|F\|_{L^3(\R^2)}\|G\|_{L^3(\R^2)}. \] The supremum is over both finite hard truncation endpoints.
[proposition] [Dissipation with output-dependent endpoints] \label{prop:masked-dissipation} For every $h>0$, $N\ge0$, complex arrays $A_v$ on $\{-N,\ldots,N\}^2$, and endpoint choices as above, let $\alpha$ be any permutation of $(1,11/10,11/10)$ and use variances $\sigma_v=\alpha_vs$. The corresponding masked costs satisfy \begin{equation}\label{eq:masked-dissipation} \int_{s_-}^{s_+}\int_{\Pi_0} \sum_{v=0}^2 V_v(p,s)\,\dd\pi\,\dd s \le C\mathcal S(A). \end{equation} The constant is independent of the arrays, the mesh, the endpoints, and the number of distinct endpoint values.
[proposition] [Selected smooth truncations]\label{prop:selected-kernel} Let $h>0$, let $N\ge0$ be an integer, set $u_i=ih$, and let $A_v(i,j)$, $v=0,1,2$, be complex arrays indexed by $-N\le i,j\le N$. Assign an arbitrary endpoint pair $h\le\varepsilon_{ij}<\rho_{ij}<\infty$ to each $(i,j)$. Then \begin{equation}\label{eq:selected-kernel} \left|h^3\sum_{i,j,l}A_0(i,j)A_1(j,l)A_2(l,i) I_{\varepsilon_{ij},\rho_{ij}}(u_i+u_j+u_l)\right| \le C\prod_{v=0}^2\left(\sum_{i,j}h^2|A_v(i,j)|^3\right)^{1/3}. \end{equation} The constant is independent of the arrays, mesh and selected endpoints.
