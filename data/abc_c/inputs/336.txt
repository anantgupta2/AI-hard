[proposition] [Quadratic reserve]\label{pre:reserve} For every integer $n\ge4$ there is a constant $C(n)<\infty$ with the following property. If $h$ is a complete smooth metric on a connected boundaryless $n$-manifold $M$ and $w\in C^\infty(M)$, there are a smooth complete metric $h^+\ge h$ and a smooth real-valued function $t^+$ such that \begin{equation}\label{pre:reserve-bound} \Dcal(h^+,t^+)\ge \Dcal(h,w)+(t^+)^2-C(n) \quad\hbox{on }M. \end{equation} One may take $C(n)=100n+203$.
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- The first new ingredient supplies that reserve without estimating $w$. For an arbitrary complete metric $h$ and smooth $w$, we construct a smooth function $u$, a global graph metric $h^+=h+\dd u^2$, and a new signed drift $t^+$ such that \[ \Dcal(h^+,t^+)\ge\Dcal(h,w)+(t^+)^2-C(n). \] Here $C(n)$ is independent of the geometry and of $w$. The graph equation has pressure proportional to the discrepancy $u-w$; subtracting the graph height in the drift update turns that discrepancy into the required square. Local barriers and an angle estimate give a global solution even when $w$ and the geometry are unbounded at infinity. The precise statement and proof are in Proposition~\ref{pre:reserve}.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Positive scalar curvature and uniform codimension-two width [preprints/Positive-scalar-curvature-and-uniform-codimension-two-width-September-23-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove the quantitative continuous form of Gromov's scalar-curvature conjecture in every dimension n ≥ 4. Every complete connected smooth boundaryless n-manifold with scalar curvature at least one admits a continuous map to a simplicial complex of dimension at most $`n-2`$ whose entire fibers have diameter bounded only in terms of n. We also obtain the continuous macroscopic-dimension conclusion for universal covers of closed manifolds with positive scalar curvature in every dimension n ≥ 2.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} For every integer \(n\ge4\), there is a finite constant \(C_n\) such that every connected complete smooth Riemannian \(n\)-manifold \((M,g)\), without boundary and with \(\Scal_g\ge1\), admits a continuous map \(f:M\to K\) to a simplicial complex of dimension at most \(n-2\) satisfying \[ \diam_g f^{-1}(y)\le C_n\qquad(y\in K). \] In particular, \(\UW_{n-2}(M,g)\le C_n\).
[corollary] [Closed-manifold filling-radius bound] \label{cor:filling-radius} Let \((M,g)\) be a closed connected smooth Riemannian \(n\)-manifold with \(n\ge4\) and \(\Scal_g\ge\sigma^2\) for some \(\sigma>0\). Then \[ \operatorname{FillRad}(M,g)\le \frac{C_n}{2\sigma}, \] where \(C_n\) is the constant in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
[corollary] [Continuous macroscopic dimension of universal covers] \label{cover:main} Let $(M,h)$ be a closed connected smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension $n\ge2$ with positive scalar curvature. Its universal cover, with the lifted original metric $\widetilde h$, satisfies \[ \dim_{\mathrm{mc}}(\widetilde M,\widetilde h)\le n-2. \]
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- \maketitle \begin{abstract} We prove the quantitative continuous form of Gromov's scalar-curvature conjecture in every dimension $n\ge4$. Every complete connected smooth boundaryless $n$-manifold with scalar curvature at least one admits a continuous map to a simplicial complex of dimension at most $n-2$ whose entire fibers have diameter bounded only in terms of $n$. We also obtain the continuous macroscopic-dimension conclusion for universal covers of closed manifolds with positive scalar curvature in every dimension $n\ge2$. \end{abstract} \begingroup\small\tableofcontents\endgroup \clearpage \section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
- A lower bound for scalar curvature is a local condition. Gromov's macroscopic dimension conjecture predicts a global consequence: at a controlled scale, two dimensions can be collapsed. We prove the quantitative form of this assertion for arbitrary complete manifolds in dimensions at least four.
- The geometric method uses the minimal-hypersurface approach of Schoen and Yau, in which stability and the Gauss equation pass scalar-curvature information to hypersurfaces \citep[Section~1]{schoenYau1979Structure}, and the torical symmetrization mechanism described by Gromov \citep[Section~2]{gromov2018Metric}. Gromov's \(\mu\)-bubble method uses prescribed mean curvature to localize the hypersurfaces entering scalar-curvature descent \citep[\S\(5\frac56\)]{gromov1996}. The weighted variational formulation and its relation to warped-circle geometry and stability are developed by Chodosh and Li \citep[Section~3 and Remark~11]{chodoshLi}.
- Fix an original length \(D\), depending only on \(n\), and a separated net \((x_i)\) in \((M,g)\). The inner balls of radius \(D\) cover \(M\), and the closed outer balls of radius \(2D\) are locally finite. Each center has its own label. We construct a continuous collapse map $q:M\to M$ with $d_g(q(x),x)<1$, together with compatible walls separating the inverse images of each inner ball and the exterior of its outer ball. The map $q$ records original positions while cylinders are inserted along the cuts. The final walls have no common intersection of \(n-1\) distinct labels. Continuous sign coordinates $t_i:M\to[0,1]$ are chosen to equal $1$ on the $q$-preimage of the inner ball and $0$ on the $q$-preimage of the exterior of its outer ball. Their transition regions lie in wall neighborhoods chosen simultaneously to preserve the intersection bound. They give the desired map: at most \(n-2\) coordinates are fractional at a point, and some coordinate is one. Equality of all coordinates confines both collapse images to the same original outer ball, giving a bound for the entire fiber.
