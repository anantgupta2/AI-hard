=== MANUSCRIPT: The Subcritical Hénon–Lane–Emden Conjecture [preprints/The-Subcritical-Henon-Lane-Emden-Conjecture-September-24-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove the subcritical Hénon–Lane–Emden conjecture: for every dimension n ≥ 2, positive powers p, q, and real weights A, B, the system has no strictly positive entire solution when $`(n+A)/(p+1)+(n+B)/(q+1)\gt n-2`$. Solutions need only be continuous at the origin and classical elsewhere, without a condition at infinity. For n ≥ 3 and $`A,B\gt -2`$, combining this result with the radial existence theorem of Bidaut-Véron and Giacomini proves Phan's Conjecture C in full: a positive radial entire solution exists whenever the strict inequality fails.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} Let \(n\ge2\) be an integer, let \(p,q>0\), and let \(A,B\in\R\). Suppose that \begin{equation}\label{eq:subcritical} \frac{n+A}{p+1}+\frac{n+B}{q+1}>n-2. \end{equation} There are no functions \[ u,v\in C^2(\R^n\setminus\{0\})\cap C(\R^n), \qquad u(x)>0,\quad v(x)>0\quad(x\in\R^n), \] satisfying Equation~\eqref{eq:system} at every \(x\ne0\).
[corollary] [Unweighted Lane--Emden conjecture] \label{cor:lane-emden} For every integer \(n\ge3\) and \(p,q>0\) satisfying \[ \frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}>\frac{n-2}{n}, \] there is no strictly positive \(C^2(\R^n)\) solution of \[ -\Delta u=v^p,\qquad -\Delta v=u^q\qquad\text{in }\R^n. \]
[proposition] [Universal centered estimates]\label{prop:uniform} Fix $n\ge3$, $p,q>0$, $A,B>-2$, and $pq>1$. There is a universal constant $C$ such that every solution satisfies, for all $R>0$, \begin{equation}\label{eq:source-bounds} \int_{B_R}f\le CR^{n-2-\beta},\qquad \int_{B_R}g\le CR^{n-2-\alpha}. \end{equation} The constants in \eqref{eq:potential-constants} vanish, so that \begin{equation}\label{eq:potentials} u=K*g,\qquad v=K*f\quad\hbox{on }\R^n\setminus\{0\}. \end{equation} Moreover, \begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform} \begin{split} &\int_{B_4}(f+g+u+v)\le C,\\ &\int_{|x-y|\ge\ell}K(x-y)(f(y)+g(y))\,\dd y \le C\ell^{2-n} \qquad (x\in B_2,\ 0<\ell\le1). \end{split} \end{equation}
