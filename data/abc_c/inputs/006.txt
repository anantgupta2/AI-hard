=== MANUSCRIPT: Goldfeld's analytic density conjecture and the 2-converse for elliptic curves [preprints/Goldfelds-analytic-density-conjecture-and-the-2-converse-for-elliptic-curves-October-7-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove Goldfeld's analytic density conjecture: for every elliptic curve E over ℚ, the quadratic twists of E with analytic rank zero and one each have density 1/2 among signed squarefree twist parameters ordered by absolute value. We also prove the low-corank 2-converse: if the $`2^\infty`$-Selmer corank of E is zero or one, then it equals the analytic and Mordell–Weil ranks, and the Tate–Shafarevich group is finite.
[theorem] [The $2$-converse]\label{thm:main-converse} For every elliptic curve $E/\Q$ with $c_2(E)\in\{0,1\}$, \[ a(E)=r(E)=c_2(E),\qquad \Sha(E/\Q)\text{ is finite}. \]
[theorem] [Goldfeld's analytic density conjecture]\label{thm:main-density} For every elliptic curve $E/\Q$ and each $j\in\{0,1\}$, \[ \lim_{X\to\infty} \frac{\#\{d\in\mathcal D(X):a(E^{(d)})=j\}}{\#\mathcal D(X)} =\frac12. \] Consequently the twists of analytic rank at least two have density zero. For a density-one set of signed squarefree parameters $d$, the analytic and Mordell--Weil ranks of $E^{(d)}$ agree and its whole Tate--Shafarevich group is finite.
[corollary] [A finite Selmer criterion]\label{cor:finite-descent} If $d_2(E)\in\{0,1\}$, then \[ a(E)=r(E)=c_2(E)=d_2(E),\qquad \Sha(E/\Q)\text{ is finite},\qquad \Sha(E/\Q)[2^\infty]=0. \]

- The rank and finiteness assertions are distinct from the Birch--Swinnerton-Dyer leading-coefficient formula. The pointwise two-primary formula of \citet[Theorem~1.1]{OpenAITwoPrimaryBSD2026}, combined with the Selmer-corank densities in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-density}, gives the exact two-primary formula on a density-one set of signed squarefree twists. Their common analytic and Mordell--Weil ranks are zero and one with density $1/2$ each. This consequence is \citet[Corollary~16.1]{OpenAITwoPrimaryBSD2026}; its leading-coefficient assertion concerns the valuation at $2$.
- \subsection{Proof strategy}
- Fix the curve under consideration. We construct finite binary families of twists \[ h_x=\prod_{q\in\mathcal Q}(q^*)^{\lambda_q(x)},\qquad q^*=(-1/q)q,\qquad x\in\F_2^b, \] where $\mathcal Q$ is a finite set of distinct good odd primes and the $\lambda_q$ are linear forms. The parameter at $x=0$ is $1$. The families have the same local twisting classes at every fixed bad place. Their nonzero vertices have analytic order zero or one, according to the corank of the fixed curve. A scalar determinant coordinate attached to the relevant class and Selmer complex has a nonzero central specialization there, with uniformly bounded $2$-adic valuation. Bounded integral interpolation then recovers nonvanishing at the missing vertex $0$.

=== MANUSCRIPT: The mean analytic rank of quadratic twists of elliptic curves [preprints/The-mean-analytic-rank-of-quadratic-twists-of-elliptic-curves-October-6-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: For every elliptic curve over ℚ, we prove that the average analytic rank of its quadratic twists tends to 1/2 when signed squarefree twist parameters are ordered by absolute value. This resolves Goldfeld's mean analytic-rank conjecture in this counting convention.
[theorem] \label{thm:mean} For every elliptic curve \(E/\Q\), \[ \lim_{Y\to\infty}\frac{1}{\#\mathcal D(Y)} \sum_{d\in\mathcal D(Y)}a(E^{(d)})=\frac12. \]
[theorem] \label{thm:tail} For every elliptic curve \(E/\Q\), there are constants \(C_E>0\) and an integer \(R_E\ge1\) such that, for every integer \(R\ge R_E\), \[ \limsup_{Y\to\infty}\frac1Y \sum_{\substack{d\in\mathcal D(Y)\\a(E^{(d)})>R}}a(E^{(d)}) \le \frac{C_E}{R}. \]
[corollary] [Fixed algebraic-rank moments]\label{cor:algebraic-moments} Fix an elliptic curve \(E/\Q\). For every fixed real \(t\), \[ \lim_{Y\to\infty}\frac1{\#\mathcal D(Y)} \sum_{d\in\mathcal D(Y)}\exp\!\bigl(t\,r(E^{(d)})\bigr) =\frac{1+e^t}{2}. \] For every fixed positive integer \(m\), \[ \lim_{Y\to\infty}\frac1{\#\mathcal D(Y)} \sum_{d\in\mathcal D(Y)}r(E^{(d)})^m=\frac12. \]

- Koymans and Smith prove exponential moment bounds for Mordell--Weil rank in polynomial quadratic-twist families \cite[Theorem~1.4]{KoymansSmith2026}. The specialization in the proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:algebraic-moments} supplies the needed algebraic tail control. Such bounds do not supply an analytic-rank identity or higher analytic-rank moments.
- Earlier work on a fixed elliptic curve already connected derivative moments to nonvanishing and analytic-rank averages. Perelli and Pomyka\l a proved first-derivative nonvanishing results and bounds for the sum of analytic ranks \cite{PerelliPomykala1997}; Pomyka\l a extended this nonvanishing approach to higher derivatives of fixed order in a congruence-restricted twisting family \cite{Pomykala1997}. In the family of weight-two newforms of varying prime level, Kowalski, Michel, and VanderKam combined high completed derivatives, mollification, and consecutive derivative orders to control both functional-equation signs. Their passage from these nonvanishing counts to rank-weighted tails also uses a separate bound for the second moment of analytic rank \cite[Sections~2, 4, and~8]{KowalskiMichelVanderKam2000}. In the present quadratic-twist argument the derivative order grows with height. This uniformity, together with a pointwise rank bound, controls the rank mass beyond the available counting range without requiring a bounded second analytic-rank moment.
- Two features make the resulting estimates uniform for \(k\) as large as a fixed power of \(\log X\). First, a Poisson argument and inflation give unmollified second moments with only powers of logarithms (Propositions~\ref{analytic:moment} and~\ref{analytic:polynomial-moment}). The inflation mechanism is related to Li's work on quadratic twists \cite[Lemmas~2.7 and~3.1, Proposition~3.2]{Li2022}; we prove the estimate needed here for the entire fixed finite family of curves, including its local factors at bad primes. The mean-square estimates use the whole probability space, without discarding exceptional outcomes. Proposition~\ref{analytic:comparison} transfers these bounds to integer averages.
