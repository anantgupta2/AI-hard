[proposition] \label{geo:compact-type} Suppose that $k$ contains $\mu_m$. At $R=O$, the special fiber of the model in Lemma~\ref{geo:semistable-models} is a rational curve joined at distinct points to $m$ copies of $E$, all defined over $k$. Rotations cyclically permute the elliptic components. The Jacobian family of $C_{m,R}$ therefore extends to an abelian scheme on a Zariski neighborhood of $O$ in the parameter curve, with fiber $E^m$ at $O$.
[proposition] \label{mon:integral-module} The cycles $a_i,b_i$ form an integral symplectic basis of $H_1(C_{m,R_s},\Z)$. Consequently, for every smooth fiber of the family, \begin{equation}\label{mon:free-lattices} H_1(C_m,\Z)\simeq\Z[\Gamma_m]^2, \qquad H^1(C_m,\Z)\simeq\Z[\Gamma_m]^2. \end{equation} In particular, after comparison, $H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(C_m,\Z_\ell)$ is free of rank two over $\Z_\ell[\Gamma_m]$ for every prime $\ell$, including $\ell\mid m$.
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- \maketitle \begin{abstract} We prove the modularity conjecture for elliptic curves over imaginary quadratic fields: every such curve is modular, with matching local parameters at every place. \end{abstract} \section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
- We prove this conjecture for every $K$ and $E$. We use unitary normalization on the automorphic side and the Tate-normalized local Langlands correspondence on the cohomological side. These conventions are recalled in Section~\ref{sec:conventions}.
- The difficulty is to reach a residual representation to which the known modularity theorem applies while retaining control at the primes where automorphy will be lifted. We construct the intermediate representations as character pieces of a single family of Jacobians.
- We construct a family of cyclic covers of quadratic twists of a fixed elliptic curve. Two boundary fibers explain the features needed later. At the first, the Jacobian becomes a product of copies of the original elliptic curve; at the second, the cover acquires nodes whose vanishing cycles will give explicit monodromy operators. All degenerations in this section take place over a parameter curve in characteristic zero.
