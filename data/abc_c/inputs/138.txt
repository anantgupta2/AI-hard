=== MANUSCRIPT: Subset Sum in Time $`O(2^{0.49n})`$ [preprints/Subset-Sum-in-Time-2-power-0-49n-October-4-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We give a uniform randomized classical algorithm for Subset Sum with bounded error and worst-case running time $`O(2^{0.49n})`$ on polynomial-bit inputs in a word-RAM model, where n is the number of input integers. The time bound holds on every random execution.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There is a uniform randomized classical algorithm $\mathcal A$ for Subset Sum with the following properties in the word-RAM model \eqref{eq:word-length}. For every input with $n\ge2$, the algorithm halts on every execution and outputs the correct answer with probability at least $2/3$. For every fixed positive integer $c$, there are constants $C_c>0$ and $N_c\ge2$ such that every input with $n\ge N_c$ and $b\le n^c$ is processed, on every outcome of the random choices, in at most \[ C_c\,2^{0.49n} \] word operations. The program is not given $c$ and does not depend on $c$.
[proposition] [Capped preliminary search] \label{prop:preliminary} Given fixed transformed weights and targets from \eqref{eq:transformed}, there is a randomized procedure that either returns the correct decision for the original instance or returns \textup{CONTINUE}. On every execution its running time is at most \[ 2^{0.489985n}\operatorname{poly}(n+b+2). \] If $V$ is uniform among the $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$-element subsets and $\mathbb E d(V)>0.01004n$, then the probability of \textup{CONTINUE} is $O(1/n)$, uniformly in the fixed weights.
[proposition] [Filtering moments]\label{prop:filtering-moments} The functions in \eqref{eq:filter-scores} satisfy \begin{equation}\label{eq:continuous-filtering-moments} \begin{split} \int_{\mathbb T}M(x)^2 2^{8.5(\Delta-u(x))}\,dx&<2^{0.5168},\\ \int_{\mathbb T}M(x)^2 2^{8.3(a v(x)-u(x))}\,dx&<2^{0.5168}. \end{split} \end{equation}
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- \subsection{Related approaches}
- Two other directions explain both the progress on Subset Sum and the difficulty of a worst-case improvement in $n$. Pseudopolynomial algorithms measure their complexity in the numerical target $t$. Bellman's dynamic-programming approach~\cite{Bellman1957} yields $O(nt)$ time. Koiliaris and Xu~\cite{KoiliarisXu2017} improved this bound deterministically, and Bringmann~\cite{Bringmann2017} obtained a randomized $\widetilde O(n+t)$ algorithm. Chan~\cite{Chan2026} subsequently obtained a deterministic near-linear pseudopolynomial algorithm. These bounds are particularly effective for small targets; polynomial \emph{bit length}, however, permits much larger targets. Here and in historical comparisons, $\widetilde O$ suppresses polylogarithmic factors.
- \subsection{Proof overview}
- For a prime $P$, write $e_P(x)=\exp(2\pi\mathrm i x/P)$. With independent uniform residues $\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_n$ modulo $P$, assign a subset $I$ the phase \[ \chi(I)=e_P\left(\sum_{i\in I}\phi_i\right). \] For each transformed target $t'$, a subset is an \emph{alias} if its sum is $t'$ modulo $P$ but not over the integers. The identity \begin{equation}\label{eq:overview-subtraction} \sum_{\sum_{i\in I}c_i=t'}\chi(I) =\sum_{\sum_{i\in I}c_i\equiv t'\pmod P}\chi(I) -\sum_{\substack{I\text{ an alias}\\\text{for }t'}}\chi(I) \end{equation} is the organizing principle. On a negative instance the left side is zero for every transformed target; an isolated positive target leaves a single phase of modulus one. The two sums on the right can be much larger, so their difference must be recovered to small \emph{absolute} error. A relative approximation to the modular sum would not suffice. We choose a random prime modulus larger than the desired running time, so neither scanning all residues nor evaluating every Fourier frequency is affordable. We estimate the modular sum and subtract the phases of a complete list of aliases.
- \paragraph{A checksum without a full Fourier transform.} Writing $V_{t'}$ for the modular sum in \eqref{eq:overview-subtraction}, Fourier inversion gives \[ V_{t'}=\frac1P\sum_{k=0}^{P-1}J_{t'}(k), \qquad J_{t'}(k)=e_P(-kt')\prod_{i=1}^n \bigl(1+e_P(\phi_i+kc_i)\bigr). \] For each fixed $k$, independent uniform phases give $\mathbb E_\phi|J_{t'}(k)|^2=2^n$. Thus the elementary second-moment bound for uniform frequency sampling does not give the required running time. For a chosen digit base $L$, writing $k=hL+l$ expresses the phase vector $(\phi_i+kc_i)_i$ as the difference between $(\phi_i+hLc_i)_i$ and $(-lc_i)_i$, all modulo $P$. These high- and low-digit vectors form two lists. The filters must keep the stored lists short, cover every frequency, and control the second moment of the resulting estimate. A table specifies random coordinate shifts, with some coordinates ignored; a sum of logarithmic density scores tests each vector against the table. A \emph{match} consists of a table and a digit pair whose two vectors both pass. This two-sided organization is related to asymmetric locality-sensitive filtering~\cite{Christiani2017}.

=== MANUSCRIPT: A Low-Space Algorithm for Worst-Case Subset Sum [preprints/A-Low-Space-Algorithm-for-Worst-Case-Subset-Sum-September-26-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We give a uniform classical randomized decision algorithm for worst-case Subset Sum. Under every fixed polynomial bound on input-integer bit length, it uses $`\mathop{\mathrm{poly}}\nolimits (n)2^{n/2}`$ time and ordinary $`O(2^{n/5})`$ writable words of $`O(n+b)`$ bits, where b is the largest input bit length. Both resource bounds hold on every execution. The error is one-sided: the algorithm always rejects unsolvable instances and accepts each solvable instance with probability at least 2/3.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There is a single uniform classical randomized algorithm in this model that never answers YES incorrectly and answers each YES instance correctly with probability at least $2/3$. For every fixed positive integer $c$, there are a constant $A_c>0$ and positive integers $k_c,N_c$ such that, whenever $n\ge N_c$ and $b\le n^c$, every outcome of the algorithm's randomness uses at most \[ A_c n^{k_c}2^{n/2}\quad\text{operations} \qquad\text{and}\qquad A_c(n+b+2)^{15}2^{0.199n}\quad\text{writable words}. \] The algorithm does not depend on $c$.
[corollary] \label{cor:target} For every fixed $\rho>0.199$, the same algorithm has ordinary $O_{c,\rho}(2^{\rho n})$ word space under each fixed polynomial-bit convention, with the time and correctness guarantees of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. In particular, it has $O_c(2^{n/5})$ word space.

PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- \subsection{Two trials and the proof strategy}
- Both trials use the same three facts about an arbitrary fixed input. The number of distinct sums changes little when one index is replaced; prescribed-cardinality choices have accurate entropy estimates; and a random prime rarely identifies two fixed unequal integer weights. We prove these facts first. We then give the small-state streams that turn stored lists into much larger generated lists.
- This trial uses the classical stored-list and generated-stream viewpoint of Schroeppel and Shamir~\cite{SS81}. Its modular regeneration and overflow rule also belong to the dissection approach: Dinur, Dunkelman, Keller, and Shamir~\cite{DDKS12} developed the earlier framework under randomness assumptions, and Austrin, Kaski, Koivisto, and M\"a\"att\"a~\cite{AKKM13} adapted dissection to worst-case Subset Sum. The trial and its bounds here are proved directly, including the dependence on the input bit length.
