=== MANUSCRIPT: Bloch’s conjecture for surfaces with p_g=0 [preprints/Blochs-Conjecture-for-Surfaces-with-pg-equals-q-equals-0-September-24-2026/build/paper.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove Bloch's conjecture for smooth connected projective complex surfaces with $`p_g=0`$: the Albanese homomorphism on integral degree-zero zero-cycles is an isomorphism.
[theorem] \label{thm:bloch} Let \(S\) be a smooth connected projective surface over \(\mathbb C\) with \(p_g(S)=q(S)=0\). Then \[ \deg:\CH_0(S)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z \] is an isomorphism, where \(\CH_0(S)\) has integral coefficients.
[corollary] [Bloch's conjecture for surfaces with \(p_g=0\)]\label{cor:bloch-pg-zero} Let \(S\) be a smooth connected projective surface over \(\mathbb C\) with \(p_g(S)=0\). Then the Albanese homomorphism \[ \operatorname{alb}_S:\CH_0(S)^0\longrightarrow\Alb(S)(\mathbb C) \] is an isomorphism of abelian groups, where \(\CH_0(S)\) has integral coefficients.
[corollary] [Motivic consequences]\label{cor:tate-motives} Let \(S\) be a smooth connected projective surface over \(\mathbb C\) with \(p_g(S)=0\). Then \(h(S)_{\mathbb Q}\) is finite-dimensional in the sense of Kimura and \(t_2(S)=0\). If, in addition, \(q(S)=0\), then \[ h(S)_{\mathbb Q}\simeq \mathbf 1\oplus\mathbb L^{\oplus b_2(S)}\oplus\mathbb L^2. \] In this case, for every \(m,n\ge0\), the rational Chow motives of the power \(S^m\) and the Hilbert scheme \(S^{[n]}\) of length-\(n\) zero-dimensional subschemes are pure Tate, with \(S^0=S^{[0]}=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C\). For \(Y=S^m\) or \(Y=S^{[n]}\), the rational cycle-class maps satisfy \[ \operatorname{cl}_Y^i:\CH^i(Y)_{\mathbb Q} \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}H^{2i}(Y,\mathbb Q), \qquad H^{2i+1}(Y,\mathbb Q)=0 \quad\text{for every }i\ge0. \]
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- \maketitle \begin{abstract} We prove Bloch's conjecture for smooth connected projective complex surfaces with \(p_g=0\): the Albanese homomorphism on integral degree-zero zero-cycles is an isomorphism. \end{abstract} \section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
- For a smooth connected projective complex surface \(S\), write \[ p_g(S)=h^0(S,\omega_S),\qquad q(S)=h^1(S,\mathcal O_S). \] The Chow group $\CH_0(S)$ is the group of integral zero-cycles modulo rational equivalence, where the relations are generated by divisors of rational functions on curves in $S$. The degree of a complex point is one, and $\CH_0(S)^0$ denotes the kernel of the degree map. The Albanese homomorphism \(\operatorname{alb}_S:\CH_0(S)^0\to\Alb(S)(\mathbb C)\) is surjective. Bloch's conjecture predicts that it is an isomorphism when \(p_g(S)=0\). We prove the conjecture. The main theorem treats the case \(q=0\), when the Albanese variety is a point and the prediction is that any two complex points are rationally equivalent.
- These zero-cycle statements also have consequences for rational Chow motives. For $p_g=q=0$, every rational Chow class on a power of $S$ or a Hilbert scheme of points is determined by its cohomology class. We state the precise consequences and their motivic antecedents after the proof overview.
- Several successful approaches use additional geometry of the surface. The group-action method of Inose--Mizukami \cite{InoseMizukami} and its extension by Barlow \cite{Barlow} compare zero-cycles with their images on suitable quotient surfaces. Bauer's treatment of Inoue surfaces with $K^2=7$ gives a later application, expressed through relations among the quotient correspondences \cite[Theorem~0.2 and Section~1]{BauerInoue}. Pedrini--Weibel recast involution arguments using the transcendental motive: for a bidouble cover, vanishing of the transcendental motives of the three intermediate quotients forces its own vanishing \cite[Theorem~2.5]{PedriniWeibel}. Their applications include numerical Godeaux and classical Campedelli examples, Keum--Naie, Burniat, and Inoue surfaces, giving examples for each $1\le K^2\le8$.
- For line bundles $L_1,L_2,D$, consider rank-zero quotients of $L_1\oplus L_2$ whose determinant has Picard class $D$. Opposite torus weights on the summands give a localization formula. We construct its obstruction theory and the compatible fixed theory using a derived determinant fiber normalized at a point of $X$ (Sections~\ref{sec:derived} and~\ref{sec:fixed}). A fixed quotient splits into a quotient of each line bundle, each described by an effective divisor and a zero-dimensional subscheme. The divisor line bundles $T,H$ satisfy $T+H=D$; on an irregular surface this determinant condition also relates their Picard parameters.
- Our goal is an equivariant perfect obstruction theory on the determinant-fixed Quot scheme, together with its actual restriction to the fixed locus. Surface Quot obstruction theories and localization relations for a trivial target bundle were developed by Marian, Oprea, and Pandharipande \cite[Section~1]{MOP}; subsequent intersection-theoretic and $K$-theoretic developments appear in \cite{OP,AJLOP}. Here the based determinant fiber must also work on the irregular auxiliary surface. We construct that fiber as a derived moduli problem so that its obstruction morphism, and not only its virtual tangent class, is specified.
