OPENAI SUMMARY: Proves that Thompson's group F, the group of dyadic piecewise linear homeomorphisms of the interval, is nonamenable, resolving its longstanding amenability problem.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Thompson's group F is nonamenable [preprints/Thompsons-group-F-is-nonamenable-September-23-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove that Thompson's group F is nonamenable. This confirms Geoghegan's conjecture and resolves the amenability problem for F.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} Thompson's group \(F\) is not amenable.
[proposition] \label{avg:boundary} For every nonempty finite set $A\subset F$, \begin{equation}\label{avg:boundary-bound} \max_{h\in S}\frac{|hA\mathbin{\triangle}A|}{|A|} \geq \frac{\delta^2/L^2-4/D}{4(1-1/D)}>0. \end{equation}
[corollary] \label{cor:nonunitarizable} For every \(\varepsilon>0\), there are a separable complex Hilbert space \(H\) and a representation \(\pi:F\to\mathrm{GL}(H)\) such that \(\sup_{g\in F}\norm{\pi(g)}\le1+\varepsilon\), but no bounded invertible operator \(S\) on \(H\) makes every \(S\pi(g)S^{-1}\) unitary.
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- \maketitle \begin{abstract} We prove that Thompson's group \(F\) is nonamenable. This confirms Geoghegan's conjecture and resolves the amenability problem for \(F\). \end{abstract}
- Thompson introduced \(F\) in 1965; an early published construction appears in McKenzie and Thompson \cite{McKenzieThompson1973}, with the identification explained by Cannon, Floyd, and Parry \cite[pp.~215--216]{CannonFloydParry1996}. The latter authors record Geoghegan's 1979 conjecture that \(F\) is nonamenable \cite[p.~227]{CannonFloydParry1996}. We prove this conjecture.
- There have also been claims of a complete resolution in both directions. Akhmedov's 2021 version claims nonamenability through a height-function criterion \cite{Akhmedov2021}. Shavgulidze claimed amenability \cite{Shavgulidze2009}; Moore identified errors in that approach \cite{Moore2011Critique}. Moore separately withdrew an amenability claim after Akhmedov identified an error in Lemma~4.13 of that manuscript \cite{Moore2012Withdrawn}. That withdrawal concerns a different work from his published Ramsey characterization cited above.
- \paragraph{Proof strategy.} The proof turns approximate translation invariance of finite averages of scalar functions on \(F\) into an approximate fixed point of a Lipschitz map on a Hilbert ball. The following infinite-dimensional phenomenon supplies a map for which that conclusion is impossible.
- We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:main} using the map supplied by Lemma~\ref{lem:displacement}. The argument uses a single finite average over the group. We first record the dyadic facts that make its correlations comparable.
- Define a color $p(T)\in B$ for every basic partition $T$ by induction on its number of cells: \begin{equation}\label{color:recursion} p(T)= \begin{cases} \displaystyle f\left(\frac1D\sum_{j=1}^D p(T_{I_j})\right), &\text{if $T$ respects every $I_j$},\\[6pt] 0, &\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \end{equation} This induction is well founded. In the first case each $T_{I_j}$ has strictly fewer cells than $T$, since $I_j$ is internal and $T$ has cells outside it. The average of the previously defined colors belongs to the convex ball $B$, so the argument of $f$ is always in its domain, and $p(T)\in B$.
