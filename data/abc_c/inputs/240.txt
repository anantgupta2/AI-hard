SUBJECT: Mathematical logic
OPENAI SUMMARY: Proves Shelah's eventual categoricity conjecture in ZFC: for each bound on the Löwenheim–Skolem number, a uniform threshold makes categoricity of an abstract elementary class in one cardinal above that threshold imply categoricity throughout the same tail. ABSTRACT: Assuming the continuum hypothesis, we construct an abstract elementary class with Löwenheim–Skolem number ℵ0 that is categorical in every sufficiently large cardinal but has at least two nonisomorphic models of cardinality $`\beth_{\omega_2}`$. Thus categoricity does not transfer down to the proposed bound $`\beth_{(2^{\aleph_0})^+}`$, which equals $`\beth_{\omega_2}`$ under CH. [theorem] \label{thm:main} Assume the continuum hypothesis. There is an abstract elementary class \(K\) in a countable finitary relational language such that \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}] \item \(\LS(K)=\aleph_0\); \item \(K\) has at least two nonisomorphic models of cardinality \(\beth_{\omega_2}=H(K)\); \item putting \(\Lambda=\beth_{(2^{\aleph_1})^+}\), the class \(K\) is categorical in every cardinal \(\mu\ge\Lambda\). \end{enumerate} In particular, \(\lambda=\Lambda^+>H(K)\) is a categoricity cardinal from which downward transfer to \(H(K)\) fails.
[theorem] \label{aec:class} The pair \(K=(\mathcal C,\le_K)\) of Definition~\ref{aec:objects} is an abstract elementary class in a fixed countable finitary relational language, with \[ \LS(K)=\aleph_0. \] It has the union and common-upper-bound properties for every nonempty set-indexed directed family of strong substructures.
[theorem] \label{end:two-models} The class \(K\) has two nonisomorphic models of cardinality \(\beth_\eta\). One has an order sort of cardinality \(\aleph_1\), and the other has a countable order sort.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Eventual categoricity for abstract elementary classes [preprints/Eventual-Categoricity-for-Abstract-Elementary-Classes-September-24-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove Shelah's eventual categoricity conjecture for abstract elementary classes in ZFC. For each infinite bound on the Löwenheim–Skolem number there is a uniform threshold such that categoricity in any one cardinal at or above that threshold implies categoricity in every cardinal at or above the same threshold.
[theorem] [Uniform eventual categoricity]\label{main:uniform} For every infinite cardinal $\lambda$ there is a cardinal $\mu(\lambda)\ge\lambda$ such that the following holds for every AEC $K$ with $\LS(K)\le\lambda$: \[ \bigl(\exists\kappa\ge\mu(\lambda)\quad K\text{ is categorical in }\kappa\bigr) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \bigl(\forall\kappa'\ge\mu(\lambda)\quad K\text{ is categorical in }\kappa'\bigr). \] The same threshold is used in the premise and the conclusion.
[theorem] [The qualitative form]\label{main:qualitative} An AEC categorical in unboundedly many cardinals is categorical on a tail of the cardinals.
[proposition] [The uniform reduction] \label{found:uniform-reduction} The uniform assertion in Theorem~\ref{main:uniform} is equivalent to the following qualitative assertion: \begin{quote} Every AEC categorical in unboundedly many cardinals is categorical in all cardinals above some bound depending on that AEC. \end{quote}

- Categoricity asks whether cardinality determines a model up to isomorphism. For abstract elementary classes, the eventual categoricity conjecture predicts that uniqueness in one sufficiently large cardinal forces uniqueness in every cardinal above a uniform threshold. We prove this prediction in ZFC, with the threshold depending only on a bound for the L\"owenheim--Skolem number.
- Morley's categoricity theorem gives the countable first-order prototype: a countable theory categorical in one uncountable cardinal is categorical in every uncountable cardinal \cite{Morley1965}. Its structural approach connects uniqueness of models with stability and dimension. The geometry of strongly minimal sets, developed by Baldwin and Lachlan \cite[Sections~1--2]{BaldwinLachlan1971}, provides a central antecedent for classification by bases. In an AEC, elementary compactness and the first-order description of types are not part of the axioms. The corresponding amalgamation, independence, and geometry therefore require separate constructions.
- A version-sensitive issue matters for the order argument. Boney and Vasey \cite[Fact~1.4 and the discussion of Claim~1.5, pp.~3--4]{BoneyVasey2015} identify a gap in the unrestricted syntactic-characterization claim of Shelah's Chapter~IV and withdraw an earlier attempted repair. Their valid variants with exponentiation or cofinality assumptions do not cover all the starting cardinals of Theorem~\ref{main:uniform}. We prove the required reversal of strong maps directly in Theorem~\ref{found:reversal}.

- \begin{proof} The union is a well-defined $\tau$-structure: finitely many arguments are contained in one family member, and any two choices of such a member agree after passing to a common strong upper member. Here finitarity is used for functions and relations alike. We prove the union and common-upper-bound assertions together by induction on the cardinality of the family. A finite directed family has a member containing every other member, so its union is that member. A countable directed family has a cofinal increasing sequence: enumerate the family and successively choose a member above the previous choice and the next enumerated member. The chain axioms apply to this sequence, including their common-upper-bound clause.
