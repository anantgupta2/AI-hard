ABSTRACT: We prove that the real Banach space ℓ1 has metric Markov cotype two, answering a question of Mendel and Naor. As a consequence, every Lipschitz map from an arbitrary subset of a real Hilbert space into ℓ1 extends to the whole Hilbert space with a universal multiplicative loss in its Lipschitz constant, resolving Ball's extension problem for this target.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} The real space $\ell_1$ satisfies \[ N_2(\ell_1)\leq12\sqrt{21}. \]
[corollary] \label{cor:martingale} Let $(M_m)_{m\geq0}$ be an $H$-valued martingale with respect to a filtration $(\calF_m)$, taking values in $Q$ and converging almost surely to $M_\infty$. If $e$ is an $\calF_0$-measurable binary vector, then \begin{align} \E\sum_{m=0}^\infty \|\Phi(M_{m+1})-\Phi(M_m)\|_{1,w}^2 &\leq108\bigl(\E\|M_\infty-e\|_H^4 -\E\|M_0-e\|_H^4\bigr)\notag\\ &\leq108\E\|M_\infty-e\|_H^4. \label{eq:martingale-energy} \end{align}
[corollary] \label{cor:complex} For complex scalars, $N_2(\ell_1(\mathbb C))\leq12\sqrt{42}$.
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- \begin{abstract} We prove that the real Banach space $\ell_1$ has metric Markov cotype two, answering a question of Mendel and Naor. As a consequence, every Lipschitz map from an arbitrary subset of a real Hilbert space into $\ell_1$ extends to the whole Hilbert space with a universal multiplicative loss in its Lipschitz constant, resolving Ball's extension problem for this target. \end{abstract}
- One important consequence concerns extension of Lipschitz maps. For a map $f$ between metric spaces, $\Lip(f)$ denotes the least $L\geq0$ such that $d(f(x),f(y))\leq Ld(x,y)$ for all $x,y$ in its domain. Ball's extension problem asks whether a Lipschitz map from an arbitrary subset of Hilbert space into $\ell_1$ admits an extension with a universal multiplicative loss in its Lipschitz constant. Mendel and Naor explain that an affirmative answer to their metric Markov cotype question gives such an extension \cite[Corollary~1.13 and the discussion after Question~1.15]{MendelNaor13}. The extension problem is also discussed in \cite[Section~1]{NaorYoung22}. We record the consequence precisely in Section~\ref{sec:consequences}; the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} itself is elementary and independent of extension theorems.
- We prove the cut representation in Section~\ref{sec:cuts} and the martingale estimate in Section~\ref{sec:flat}. The comparison in Section~\ref{sec:comparison}, valid in any metric space, converts the terminal geometric endpoint cost to the Ces\`aro average in \eqref{eq:cotype}. These ingredients are assembled in Section~\ref{sec:proof}. The resulting points have a simple interpretation: each $y_i$ is the expected coordinatewise median of three independent geometric-walk endpoints started at $i$. This interpretation is established after the main proof.
- The stopped martingale will produce a geometric average of endpoint distances. The next estimate converts it to the finite average in Definition~\ref{def:cotype}. This comparison is part of the resolvent approach in \cite[Section~7, especially Lemma~7.1]{MendelNaor13}. We give an elementary version with an explicit constant; only stationarity, not reversibility, is needed.
