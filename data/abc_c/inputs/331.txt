=== MANUSCRIPT: Asymptotic midpoint uniform convexity and unbounded diamond distortion in a reflexive tree space [preprints/Asymptotic-midpoint-uniform-convexity-and-unbounded-diamond-distortion-in-a-reflexive-tree-space-September-27-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We construct a separable reflexive real Banach space whose given norm is asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex but which admits no asymptotically uniformly convex equivalent norm. Its averaged midpoint modulus is at least $`\sqrt{1+t^2/12}-1`$, and the countably branching diamond of depth k has distortion at least $`\sqrt{1+k/12}`$ in this space. This gives a negative answer to the reflexive diamond converse for asymptotic uniform convexifiability.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} The space $X=J^*$ defined by \eqref{eq:segment-norm} is infinite dimensional, separable, and reflexive, and has no equivalent AUC norm. Its given norm satisfies \[ \widehat\delta_X(t)\ge\sqrt{1+t^2/12}-1\qquad(t>0). \] For every integer $k\ge0$, every embedding of $D_k$ into $X$ has distortion at least $\sqrt{1+k/12}$.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Midpoint lenses in segment spaces [preprints/Midpoint-lenses-in-segment-spaces-September-27-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: For a real segment-forest dual with unbounded finite component heights and the real infinite-height coordinate predual, we bound the tail of an arbitrary displacement in a symmetric lens by $`2\sqrt{R^2-\|x\|^2}`$, where R is the lens radius and x is its finitely supported center. Both given norms are asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex, although neither space admits an equivalent asymptotically uniformly convex norm. The finite-height forest dual is reflexive.
[theorem] \label{thm:main-tail}\label{mol:prop:joint-budget} Let $Y$ be either the finite-height forest dual $X=J^*$ or the infinite-height coordinate predual $B_\infty$. For every finite ancestral $H$, every $x,y\in Y$ with $x=P_Hx$, and every $R\ge0$ with $\|x+y\|,\|x-y\|\le R$, \begin{equation}\label{eq:main-squared-tail} \|Q_Hy\|\le2\sqrt{R^2-\|x\|^2}. \end{equation}
[corollary] [The completed atomic ball]\label{cor:closed-atomic-ball} Let $\mathcal A$ be the set of all represented atoms in $X$. Then \[ B_X=\overline{\operatorname{conv}\mathcal A}^{\,\|\cdot\|}. \] Every segment functional $d_S$ has norm one.
[corollary] \label{mol:cor:all-radius-averaged} In either of the segment-atomic completions considered above, \begin{equation}\label{mol:eq:all-radius-averaged} \widehat\delta(t)\ge \frac{\sqrt{1+t^2/4}-1}{2}\qquad(t>0). \end{equation}
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- For a normed space $Y$, a vector $x\in Y$, and $R>0$, consider the symmetric lens of displacements \[ \mathcal L(x,R)=\{y\in Y:\|x+y\|\le R,\ \|x-y\|\le R\}. \] When $\|x\|$ is close to $R$, convexity restricts this set. In an infinite-dimensional space one can ask for smallness after projecting away finitely many coordinates. We prove such an estimate for a particular tree norm, with a bound determined by the squared-radius deficit $R^2-\|x\|^2$ of the center.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Distortion of countably branching diamonds from midpoint and tree energies [preprints/Diamond-distortion-from-midpoint-and-tree-energies-September-27-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We derive quantitative distortion bounds for countably branching diamond graphs from midpoint estimates and direct tree energies. In the dual of a finite-height segment forest, every distortion-D embedding of the depth-k diamond satisfies $`D^2\ge1+k/4`$. The same bound holds in the infinite-height coordinate predual. We also obtain power-type distortion bounds for path-cost and recursive tree norms.
[theorem] \label{met:intro-main}\label{met:joint-distortion} For every integer $k\ge0$, every distortion-$D$ embedding of $\mathcal D_k$ into either $X$ or $B_\infty$ satisfies \[ D^2\ge1+k/4. \]
[proposition] \label{dir:deterministic-descent} Every embedding of $\mathcal D_k$ into $X$ with distortion at most $D$ satisfies $k\le256D^2$.
[proposition] \label{dir:bin-descent} Every noncontracting $D$-Lipschitz embedding of $\mathcal D_k$ into $X$ satisfies $D>\sqrt{k}/144$ for $k\ge1$.
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- \subsection{Direct proofs and the organization of the paper} The forest also admits direct graph arguments that construct their own energy budgets. These proofs use the segment norm itself, without invoking the companion's midpoint inequality. The common first step is to select one replacement path at each retained edge. Infinite branching permits coordinate choices at this step; subsequent descent uses only the two half-edges of each selected path.
