FAMILY 015: Torus-packet equidistribution in prime, quartic, and sextic degrees
SUBJECT: Number theory
OPENAI SUMMARY: Proves Haar equidistribution without escape of mass for complete volume-weighted torus packets from totally real fields: arbitrary lattices and prescribed local types in fixed prime degree at least five, arbitrary-order Picard packets in primitive quartic fields, and maximal-order ideal-class packets in primitive sextic fields. Here primitive means having no proper intermediate field; the relevant order or field discriminant tends to infinity.
HEADLINE (v2, semi-formal): ∀ prime n ≥ 5, ∀ sequences (K_i,M_i,σ_i) of totally real degree-n fields with full lattices and D(M_i) → ∞: μ_{K_i,M_i,σ_i} → m_n weakly (Haar, no escape of mass)

=== MANUSCRIPT: Equidistribution of Prime-Degree Torus Packets with Arbitrary Local Type [preprints/Equidistribution-of-Prime-Degree-Torus-Packets-with-Arbitrary-Local-Type-September-24-2026/build/paper.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove the packet form of the higher-dimensional Duke equidistribution problem for totally real fields of any fixed prime degree at least five, allowing arbitrary local homothety types of full lattices. As the multiplier-order discriminant tends to infinity, the volume-weighted packet measures converge to Haar probability measure, with no escape of mass.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} For every fixed prime $n\ge5$ and every sequence $(K_i,M_i,\sigma_i)$ as above with \mbox{$D(M_i)\to\infty$}, \[ \mu_{K_i,M_i,\sigma_i}\longrightarrow m_n. \] The convergence is weak convergence of probability measures. In particular, it holds against every continuous compactly supported function, and no mass escapes into the cusp.
[proposition] \label{prop:unfolding} Writing $\mu_*=\mu_{K,M,\sigma}$, one has \begin{equation}\label{eq:unfolding} \int_{X_n}E_f\dd\mu_* =\frac1{\sqrt D\,\kappaK} \sum_{\mathfrak a\subset R,\ \mathfrak a\ne0} P(\mathfrak a)\cV_f(\N\mathfrak a/Q). \end{equation} The identity holds for nonnegative extended integrals.
[proposition] \label{prop:weighted-count} Fix $A_0\ge1$ and $0<\eta\le1$. For $Q$ sufficiently large in terms of $n,A_0,\eta$, \begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-count} \sum_{x-y<\N\mathfrak a\le x}P(\mathfrak a) \ll_n \frac{\kappaK y}{q} \qquad (Q^{3/4}\le x\le A_0Q,\quad \eta x\le y\le x). \end{equation} The implied constant is independent of $A_0$ and $\eta$.

- \maketitle \begin{abstract} We prove the packet form of the higher-dimensional Duke equidistribution problem for totally real fields of any fixed prime degree at least five, allowing arbitrary local homothety types of full lattices. As the multiplier-order discriminant tends to infinity, the volume-weighted packet measures converge to Haar probability measure, with no escape of mass. \end{abstract}
- Compact diagonal orbits in the space of unimodular lattices encode ideal classes and units of totally real number fields. The higher-dimensional Duke equidistribution problem asks whether arithmetically complete families of these orbits become uniformly distributed as their discriminants grow. The grouping matters: one local homothety type specifies a complete packet, whereas a multiplier order alone can combine several packets. We prove the prime-degree packet statement for every such local type.
- Linnik's ergodic approach connected arithmetic distribution problems with dynamics \cite{Linnik1968}. Duke proved equidistribution of the length-weighted closed geodesics associated with fundamental real-quadratic discriminants on the modular surface \cite[Theorem~1]{Duke1988}. In higher rank, Einsiedler, Lindenstrauss, Michel, and Venkatesh developed the relationship between arithmetic separation and entropy for periodic torus orbits \cite{ELMV2009}. Their cubic equidistribution theorem combines subconvexity, local analysis, and measure classification \cite[Theorem~1.4]{ELMV2011}. Their packet construction already distinguishes local homothety from the coarser condition of having the same multiplier order \cite[Section~5.5]{ELMV2011}.
- The proof follows the counting-to-rigidity strategy of Einsiedler--Lindenstrauss--Michel--Venkatesh \cite[Section~2.7.1]{ELMV2011}: packet averages of lattice-vector counts control the cusp and small ordinary balls, and these bounds feed into positive-entropy rigidity. Their cubic argument obtains asymptotics for these averages using global and local subconvexity \cite[Sections~2.7.2--2.7.3]{ELMV2011}. For the same rigidity step, we obtain the needed upper bounds by retaining the Dedekind-zeta residue in the unfolded ideal count. Stark's exceptional-zero descent \cite{Stark1974}, together with Shiu's theorem in Pollack's uniform formulation \cite{Shiu1980,Pollack2020}, supplies the required bounds without a subconvexity hypothesis. Odd degree excludes the quadratic subfields that occur in Stark's exceptional-zero alternative.
- For an arbitrary prescribed local type, we keep maximal-order ideal counting but insert local containment probabilities: at each prime, the weight records the probability that a vector belongs to a randomly chosen unit translate of the prescribed module. The essential estimate is a small positive moment of the local norm, uniform over full local modules whose maximal-order span is the full ring. Its proof uses a unit in each component projection, not independence of the components or invertibility of the module. The unit-mass argument in \cite[Lemma~9.7]{ELMV2011} is an antecedent; here we prove the exact shell normalization and the positive moment for arbitrary local modules with this full-span condition.

- Ideal classes in a totally real number field give compact orbits of a diagonal group on a space of lattices. A natural arithmetic question is whether the finite collections of orbits obtained in this way become uniformly distributed as their discriminants grow. We prove this for the complete packets attached to invertible ideals of every order in a primitive totally real quartic field. The order may have arbitrarily large index in the ring of integers.
