=== MANUSCRIPT: Product-projection localization and the QAC0 parity lower bound [preprints/Product-projection-localization-and-the-QAC0-parity-lower-bound-September-24-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove that constant-depth quantum circuits with arbitrary one-qubit and unbounded-arity Toffoli gates cannot compute parity with any fixed positive worst-case advantage using polynomially many qubits. Ancillas start in zero, only one output qubit is measured, and all final garbage is unrestricted. This resolves Moore's parity conjecture in the measured-output model.
[theorem] [Parity lower bound] \label{thm:parity} Fix a nonnegative integer \(d\), a real number \(c\ge1\), and \(0<\varepsilon\le1/2\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), no circuit of depth at most \(d\) on \(n\) input qubits and at most \(n^c\) total qubits, using arbitrary one-qubit unitaries and unbounded-arity Toffoli gates, computes \(\parity(x)\) with probability at least \(1/2+\varepsilon\) for every \(x\in\{0,1\}^n\). Gates in each layer have disjoint supports, ancillas start in \(\ket0\), and one output qubit is measured; the other qubits may be discarded.
[theorem] [Localization of conjugated product projections] \label{thm:localization} Fix a nonnegative integer \(d\) and real numbers \(0\le s<t\). For all sufficiently large \(N\), every \(U\in\cU_d(N)\) and every pair of counts \(M,D\) on \(\mathcal H_N\) satisfy \begin{equation} \label{eq:localization} \norm{[M\ge N^t]\,U[D=0]U^\dagger[M=0]} \le \exp(-N^s). \end{equation} The sufficient lower bound on \(N\) depends only on \(d,s,t\), not on the circuit, the counted subsets, or their reference vectors.
[corollary] [State-preparation obstructions] \label{cor:state-preparation} Fix a nonnegative integer $d$, $c\ge1$, $a>0$, and $0<\delta<1$. For all sufficiently large $n$, every $n$-qubit reduced state $\rho$ prepared from zero inputs by an allowed depth-at-most-$d$ circuit on at most $n^c$ total qubits satisfies \[ \mathcal F_n(\rho)<n^{-a}. \] Moreover, for every integer $k$ with $n^\delta\le k\le n/2$, \[ \operatorname{TD}(\rho,\proj{D_k^n})>\frac1{80k}. \] The remaining qubits may be discarded in arbitrary final states.

- Unbounded gate arity is the first obstacle to a lower bound. A single Toffoli gate can involve every input, so the number of wires in an output's backward light cone gives no useful restriction. Our argument instead bounds transitions from a fixed product condition to states with many mismatches from that condition. We keep the amplitude lost when imposing a projection, without normalizing the resulting state.
- Here the order of the operators is decisive. Put $P=[M=0]$, $Q=U[D=0]U^\dagger$, and $H=[M\ge N^t]$. Squaring the transition norm gives $\norm{HQP}^2=\norm{HQPQH}$. Replacing only the first $Q$ by $p(UDU^\dagger)$ leaves an error acting on $PQ$. After conjugation by $U^\dagger$, the high-count part of that error is another localization problem, with the two counts exchanged and a larger mismatch threshold. We prove the universal statement at that larger threshold first. A finite chain of thresholds ends above the maximum possible count $N$, where localization is automatic; working backward proves the original statement (\Cref{sec:bootstrap}). The same-depth inverse-circuit invocation is therefore part of a finite induction.
- The polynomial ingredients have a substantial history. The combination of Chebyshev approximation with exact roots at exceptional integer points follows an established approximation-and-interpolation pattern for symmetric predicates~\cite[Section 3.1]{Sherstov2009}. Polynomial localization and concentration for quantum states appear in work of Kuwahara, Arad, Amico, and Vedral~\cite{KuwaharaAradAmicoVedral2017} and Anshu and Metger~\cite{AnshuMetger2023}. The latter also track the total norm of local terms. For $\QAC$, the spectral-norm approximation approach of Anshu, Dong, Ou, and Yao~\cite{ADOY2025} is a particularly relevant predecessor. We prove the required polynomial and coefficient estimates here. The finite inverse-circuit induction supplies the additional scale improvement: localization holds for every fixed exponent pair $0\le s<t$, uniformly in both counts and the entire uncounted register.
- \paragraph{Organization.} \Cref{sec:localization} states the localization theorem and fixes the circuit model. \Cref{sec:polynomial} proves the polynomial approximation. \Cref{sec:depth} reduces small-support projections to the preceding depth, and \Cref{sec:bootstrap} completes the finite scale induction. \Cref{sec:parity} derives \Cref{thm:parity}, and \Cref{sec:consequences} proves the consequences just described.
- The parity proof will use localization for a single output projection. We prove the stronger uniform statement for every product projection, because its induction exchanges the projected and counted registers.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Regular trajectories, pruning and quantum parity [preprints/Regular-trajectories-pruning-and-quantum-parity-September-24-2026/build/paper.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove that constant-depth quantum circuits with arbitrary one-qubit gates and unbounded-arity Toffoli gates cannot compute parity with any fixed positive worst-case advantage using polynomially many total qubits. Ancillary qubits are initialized to $`|0\rangle`$, one output qubit is measured, and all other final registers may be discarded without restriction. This resolves Moore's parity conjecture in the measured-output model.
[theorem] \label{thm:parity} Fix positive integers $d,k,C$ and a real $0<\varepsilon\le1/2$. There is an integer $n_0=n_0(d,k,C,\varepsilon)$ such that, for every $n\ge n_0$, every allowed circuit of depth at most $d$ on at most $C(n+1)^k$ total qubits has an input $x\in\{0,1\}^{n}$ on which its answer equals $\Parity_n(x)$ with probability strictly less than $1/2+\varepsilon$.
[proposition] [Low-degree propagation] \label{prop:propagation} Let $V$ be a circuit with a bounded number of analytic layers, and suppose the trajectory from $\zeta$, with $\norm{\zeta}\le 1$, is regular for $V$. Here $k,K$ denote degree exponents: fix $0<k<K<1$. For every operator $E$ with \[ \deg E\le m^k, \qquad \norm{E}\le \exp(m^k), \] there is, eventually, an operator $E'$ such that \begin{equation} \label{eq:propagation} \begin{gathered} \norm{VE\zeta-E'V\zeta}\le \exp(-m^k),\\ \deg E'\le m^K, \qquad \norm{E'}\le \exp(m^K). \end{gathered} \end{equation} The starting vector need not be a product vector.
[proposition] [Projection insertion]\label{prop:projection-insertion} Let $W=U_t\cdots U_1$ have a bounded number of layers, and suppose the trajectory \[ \psi_j=U_j\cdots U_1\psi_0,\qquad 0\le j\le t, \] is regular, with $\norm{\psi_0}\le1$. For any product test $B$ at the output, set \[ \chi_t=P_B\psi_t,\qquad \chi_j=U_{j+1}^{\dagger}\cdots U_t^{\dagger}\chi_t \quad(0\le j<t). \] Then the trajectory $(\chi_j)_{j=0}^t$ is regular for $W$.

- \maketitle \begin{abstract} We prove that constant-depth quantum circuits with arbitrary one-qubit gates and unbounded-arity Toffoli gates cannot compute parity with any fixed positive worst-case advantage using polynomially many total qubits. - We prove Moore's parity conjecture in the measured-output model and therefore also exclude coherent fanout. The lower bound holds for bounded-error computation with a single measured output, even when the computation changes the input and leaves arbitrary garbage in the other registers.
- The first tool, Proposition~\ref{prop:propagation}, transports operators of low degree along a regular trajectory, with an arbitrarily small fixed loss in the degree exponent. Its conclusion is an approximation on the trajectory vector. The second tool, Proposition~\ref{prop:projection-insertion}, shows that regularity survives insertion of an arbitrary product projection followed by reverse evolution. The key step is Lemma~\ref{lem:tail-transport}, which compares two regular trajectories. One polynomial detects a tail at the starting end; another approximates the product projection at the finishing end. They are applied on different sides of an inner product, so their large global norms are paid for by the appropriate tail estimates.
