FAMILY 172: Classification of finite Euclidean Ramsey configurations
SUBJECT: Combinatorics
OPENAI SUMMARY: Classifies finite point configurations that occur monochromatically, at their original scale, in every finite coloring of sufficiently high-dimensional Euclidean space. The characterization is an algebraic condition over the coordinate field. It also disproves the Leader–Russell–Walters conjecture that every such configuration is a subset of a finite transitive set.
HEADLINE (v2, semi-formal): ∀ finite A⊂ℝ^d (s≥2 points, affine span ℝ^d): A is Ramsey ⇔ ∃P∈Mat_{d+1}(F⊗_ℚF) satisfying the tensor evaluation and spatial conditions

=== MANUSCRIPT: A classification of finite Euclidean Ramsey configurations [preprints/A-classification-of-finite-Euclidean-Ramsey-configurations-September-23-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We classify finite Euclidean Ramsey configurations by a necessary and sufficient tensor condition over their coordinate fields. The Ramsey property here concerns monochromatic congruent copies at the original scale under arbitrary finite colorings. The criterion shows that every nonempty subtransitive set and every nonempty set of at most five points on a circle is Ramsey. In particular, some Ramsey cyclic quadrilaterals are not subtransitive, disproving the necessity direction of the Leader–Russell–Walters conjectured characterization.
[theorem] [Classification]\label{thm:classification} Let $A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_s\}\subset\R^d$ be a finite set of at least two points with affine span $\R^d$, and define $p_i,F,B,m_F$ as above. Then $A$ is Ramsey if and only if there is a matrix $P\in\operatorname{Mat}_{d+1}(B)$ such that \begin{align} (p_i\otimes1)^T P(1\otimes p_i)&=0 &&(1\le i\le s),\label{eq:field-evaluations}\\ m_F(P_{\alpha\beta})&=\delta_{\alpha\beta} &&(1\le\alpha,\beta\le d).\label{eq:field-spatial} \end{align} Here the entries of $p_i\otimes1$ and $1\otimes p_i$ are formed componentwise. There are no separate conditions on the multiplied constant row or constant column of $P$.
[proposition] \label{alg:equivalence} The matrix condition in Theorem~\ref{thm:classification} is equivalent to~\eqref{alg:tensor}.
[proposition] \label{alg:necessity} If~\eqref{alg:tensor} has no solution, then there is an integer $r_0\geq2$ such that every $\R^D$ admits an $r_0$-coloring with no monochromatic congruent copy of $A$. In particular, every Euclidean Ramsey set satisfies the matrix condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:classification}.
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- Second, we associate a free-group generator to each real sequence with finite support. A \emph{path} is an ordered product of $s$-tuples: each factor either records an ordered scaled copy of $A$ or has the same group element in every position. Its \emph{endpoint} is the coordinatewise product, and its \emph{weight} is the sum of the squared copy scales. We prove that equal endpoints with arbitrarily small weight ratios force the Ramsey property. The obstacle, familiar from the group and word methods of Leader, Russell and Walters~\cite[Sections~2--3]{lrw-transitive}, is the initially unknown number $t$ of variable positions in a Hales--Jewett line. After fixing the Hales--Jewett word length $n$, we prepare one endpoint at weights $1,1/2,\ldots,1/n$ before coloring the words. An ultrafilter construction then realizes the products by finite strings, keeping common factors identical. The $t$ variable positions give squared-distance multiplier $t(1/t)=1$. Compactness supplies a finite ambient dimension for each number of colors.
- Sections~\ref{sec:tensors}--\ref{pairs:section} establish the algebraic formulation, necessity, and the exact pair construction. Section~\ref{paths:section} states and proves the group-endpoint criterion that suffices for Ramsey forcing. Section~\ref{sec:groups} constructs the required endpoints and completes the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:classification}. Section~\ref{cons:section} derives the geometric examples. All arguments take place in ZFC.
- We next prove the necessary direction of the classification. Failure of~\eqref{alg:tensor} gives an algebraic functional, and its values provide a fixed finite coloring that works in every ambient dimension. The invariant-and-coloring strategy goes back to Erd\H{o}s et al. \cite[Theorem~13 and Lemma~15]{egm-rss}; here rational tensor separation supplies the invariant, and the modular coloring is proved directly. Their real-variable coloring lemma extends Rado's work on partition regularity \cite{rado-note}.
