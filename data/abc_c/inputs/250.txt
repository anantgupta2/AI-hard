=== MANUSCRIPT: Simple F∞ overgroups of groups with decidable word problem [preprints/Simple-F-infinity-overgroups-of-groups-with-decidable-word-problem-September-23-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: Every finitely generated group with decidable word problem embeds in a simple group of type F∞. This resolves the higher-finiteness strengthening of the Boone–Higman conjecture.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} Every finitely generated group \(G\) with decidable word problem admits an injective homomorphism into a nontrivial simple group \(H\) of type \(\Finfty\).
[theorem] \label{thm:torus} Let $f:U\to U$ be an injective endomorphism. Assume the following. \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}] \item There are a finitely presented group $P$ and homomorphisms $a:U\to P$, $b:P\to U$ with $f=ba$. \item For each $\ell\in\{2,3\}$ there are homomorphisms $h_E:U\to U$, indexed by $E\in\mathcal P_\ell$, such that $h_I=f$. Whenever $E,P',E+P'\in\mathcal P_\ell$ and $EP'=P'E=0$, the subgroups $h_E(U)$ and $h_{P'}(U)$ commute elementwise, and \[ h_{E+P'}(u)=h_E(u)h_{P'}(u)\qquad(u\in U). \] \end{enumerate} Then $T(U,f)$ is of type $\Finfty$.
[proposition] \label{prop:uniform-fillings} Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:torus}, for every finite bound $A$ and every $n\ge0$ there are an integer $k\ge0$ and a finite bound $B$ such that $f^k$ sends every reduced integral $n$-cycle of $C_*^A(U)$ to a boundary in $C_*^B(U)$.
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- Our final step uses the twisted Brin--Thompson construction of \citet{BelkZaremsky2022}. Its input is a faithful action of a group \(\Lambda\) on a countably infinite set \(S\). The action must be \emph{oligomorphic}, meaning that it has finitely many orbits on finite subsets of each fixed cardinality. If \(\Lambda\) and all its finite-set stabilizers have type \(\Finfty\), their theorem gives a simple group \(SV_\Lambda\) of type \(\Finfty\) containing \(\Lambda\). Consequently the problem is to embed \(G\) in a group equipped with such an action, including the finiteness properties of its stabilizers. We construct a \emph{highly transitive} action: any two ordered tuples of distinct points of the same finite length lie in the same orbit. This supplies the orbit condition. We prove type \(\Finfty\) for the pointwise stabilizers; the corresponding setwise stabilizers are finite extensions and inherit the property.
- The action begins with a finitely presented ring \(B\), the elementary matrix group \(Q=\E_7(B)\), and its action on the column set \(X=B^7\). Here \(\E_r(B)\) is generated by the off-diagonal elementary matrices. We construct compatible injections \[ \delta:Q\longrightarrow Q,\qquad \sigma:X\longrightarrow X. \] At each new stage, matrices can permute any finite collection of the compressed columns \(\sigma(X)\). The direct limits of these two systems, with the stage shift adjoined to the group, give the action \(\Lambda\curvearrowright S\). This makes high transitivity a local matrix calculation. The same construction embeds \(G\) into \(Q\).

=== MANUSCRIPT: A universal group of type F∞ [preprints/A-universal-group-of-type-F-infinity-September-23-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We construct a single group of type F∞ containing every finitely presented group. Its finitely generated subgroups, up to isomorphism, are exactly the finitely generated recursively presented groups. This answers the F∞ form of the higher-dimensional Higman embedding question.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There exists a group $H$ of type $\Finfty$ such that every finitely presented group admits an injective homomorphism into $H$.
[corollary] \label{cor:recursive} A finitely generated group embeds in $H$ if and only if it is recursively presented.
[proposition] \label{prop:finite-envelope} For every finitely presented group $U$ there are a finitely presented group $\widehat W$ and homomorphisms \[ k_{\ell,E}:U\longrightarrow\widehat W \qquad(\ell\in\{2,3\},\ E\in\mathcal P_\ell) \] such that $k_{2,I}=k_{3,I}$ is injective. For each $\ell$, whenever $E,E',E+E'\in\mathcal P_\ell$ and $EE'=E'E=0$, the subgroups $k_{\ell,E}(U)$ and $k_{\ell,E'}(U)$ commute elementwise, and \[ k_{\ell,E+E'}(u)=k_{\ell,E}(u)k_{\ell,E'}(u) \qquad(u\in U). \]
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- \maketitle \begin{abstract} We construct a single group of type $\Finfty$ containing every finitely presented group. Its finitely generated subgroups, up to isomorphism, are exactly the finitely generated recursively presented groups. This answers the $\Finfty$ form of the higher-dimensional Higman embedding question. \end{abstract}
- The proof of their conditional theorem uses a universal finitely presented group, an embedding through an intermediate overgroup and back, and an ascending HNN extension. They relate this strategy to earlier work of Baumslag--Dyer--Miller and Fournier-Facio--L\"oh--Moraschini~\cite[Section~2]{FFZ2026}.
- The finite diagrams supply these fillings by induction. For each $\ell\in\{2,3\}$, the diagram is indexed by idempotents over $\F_\ell$. Orthogonal sums correspond to pointwise products of homomorphisms with elementwise commuting images. Lemma~\ref{lem:idempotents} says that an additive assignment has its value at the identity annihilated by $\ell$. In degrees $n\ge2$, the chain diagonal includes mixed terms from lower degrees, so the corresponding cycle classes are not automatically additive. Using lower-degree fillings, the induction constructs, below degree $n$, a controlled chain homotopy from the map induced by an iterate of $f$ to the constant map. Lemma~\ref{lem:controlled-additivity} uses it to remove the mixed terms. For each input cycle, both diagrams then give additive assignments into the homology of the same bounded chain complex. Their common value at the identity idempotent is represented by the image of the input cycle under an iterate of $f$. It is annihilated by both $2$ and $3$, and hence vanishes.
