- The six-vertex model assigns an arrow to each edge of the square lattice, subject to the rule that two arrows enter and two arrows leave each vertex. Its height function is a discrete random surface. We prove the Gaussian free field limit of this surface throughout the critical range \(a=b=1\), \(0<c\le2\), for the plane state specified below.
- At \(c=1\), Lieb computed the exact square-ice entropy~\cite{Lieb1967}. The Gaussian description of critical six-vertex heights and the prediction of its coupling belong to the Coulomb-gas approach. In the F-model regime \(1<c<2\), Di Francesco, Saleur and Zuber~\cite[Section~2.3]{DFSZ1987} give a Gaussian-action prediction which, after converting their height steps of \(\pi/2\) to unit steps, agrees with \eqref{eq:multiplier}. This physics prediction is distinct from convergence in the measure classes and distribution topologies of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
- \subsection{Proof overview}\label{sec:overview}
- The finite formulas of Section~\ref{sec:finite} reduce a row word to sums over finitely many selected Bethe roots. To pass to a plane kernel we must control those sums even when several selected roots approach the sparse ends of the root configuration. We first construct the real roots, then prove the inverse and determinant estimates that justify this passage. All constants in this Section may depend on the fixed parameter $0<\lambda<\pi$. They need not remain bounded as $\lambda\downarrow0$ or $\lambda\uparrow\pi$.
