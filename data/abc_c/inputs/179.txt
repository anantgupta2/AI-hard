FAMILY 179: The circulant Hadamard and Barker-sequence conjectures
SUBJECT: Combinatorics
OPENAI SUMMARY: Proves that real circulant Hadamard matrices exist exactly in orders 1 and 4, resolving the circulant Hadamard conjecture. Together with classical Barker-sequence results, this shows that binary sequences whose nontrivial aperiodic autocorrelations have magnitude at most 1 exist at lengths n &gt; 1 exactly when $`n\in\{2,3,4,5,7,11,13\}`$.

=== MANUSCRIPT: The circulant Hadamard conjecture [preprints/The-circulant-Hadamard-conjecture-September-23-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove the circulant Hadamard conjecture: a real circulant Hadamard matrix has order 1 or 4. As a consequence, Barker sequences of length greater than one exist exactly at lengths 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 11, 13, proving the Barker-sequence conjecture.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} For a positive integer \(n\), a real circulant Hadamard matrix of order \(n\) exists if and only if \(n\in\{1,4\}\).
[corollary] [Barker-sequence lengths]\label{cor:barker} For an integer \(n>1\), a Barker sequence of length \(n\) exists if and only if \[ n\in\{2,3,4,5,7,11,13\}. \]
[proposition] \label{prop:order} If a real circulant Hadamard matrix has order \(n>1\), then \(n=4u^2\) for an odd positive integer \(u\).

- \maketitle \begin{abstract} We prove the circulant Hadamard conjecture: a real circulant Hadamard matrix has order $1$ or $4$. As a consequence, Barker sequences of length greater than one exist exactly at lengths $2,3,4,5,7,11,13$, proving the Barker-sequence conjecture. \end{abstract} \section{Introduction}
- A real Hadamard matrix of order \(n\) is a matrix \(H\) with entries in \(\{-1,1\}\) such that \(HH^{\mathsf T}=nI_n\). It is \emph{circulant} if there are signs \(h_0,\ldots,h_{n-1}\) for which \[ H_{ij}=h_{j-i\bmod n}\qquad(0\leq i,j<n). \] Thus every row is a cyclic shift of the first. For such a sign matrix, define the periodic autocorrelations by \[ P_h(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}h_jh_{j+t\bmod n} \qquad(0\leq t<n). \] Here \(P_h(0)=n\), and the Hadamard condition is equivalent to \(P_h(t)=0\) for every \(1\leq t<n\). The circulant Hadamard conjecture, traditionally attributed to Ryser \cite{Ryser1963,LeungSchmidt2012}, asserts that the only possible orders are \(1\) and \(4\). We prove this conjecture.
