=== MANUSCRIPT: Brennan's conjecture and sharp inverse-square integral means [preprints/Brennans-conjecture-and-sharp-inverse-square-integral-means-September-24-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove Brennan's conjecture: if a simply connected plane domain admits a conformal bijection φ onto the unit disk, then $`|\varphi'|^s`$ is area-integrable for every $`4/3\lt s\lt 4`$. We also prove the sharp inverse-square integral-means exponent $`B_{\mathcal S}(-2)=1`$ for the normalized schlicht class $`\mathcal S`$.
[theorem] [Brennan's conjecture and inverse-square means] \label{thm:main} For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $C_\varepsilon<\infty$ such that \begin{equation}\label{eq:main-uniform} M_{-2}[f'](r)\le C_\varepsilon(1-r)^{-1-\varepsilon} \qquad\left(f\in\SSS,\ \frac12\le r<1\right). \end{equation} Moreover, $B_{\SSS}(-2)=1$. Let $W\subset\CC$ be any simply connected domain whose boundary in the Riemann sphere contains at least two points. Every conformal bijection $\varphi:W\to\DD$ satisfies \[ \int_W|\varphi'(z)|^s\dd A(z)<\infty \qquad\left(\frac43<s<4\right). \] Equivalently, every univalent map $f:\DD\to\CC$ satisfies \[ \int_{\DD}|f'(w)|^t\dd A(w)<\infty \qquad\left(-2<t<\frac23\right). \]
[corollary] [Sharpness of the open range]\label{cor:sharp-endpoints} The Koebe function $f(z)=z/(1-z)^2$ belongs to $\SSS$ and satisfies \[ \int_{\DD}|f'|^{-2}\dd A=\infty, \qquad \int_{\DD}|f'|^{2/3}\dd A=\infty. \] Its inverse therefore fails area integrability at both $s=4$ and $s=4/3$.
[proposition] [Uniform disk comparison] \label{prop:disk-transfer} If $\rho\le2$, then for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $C_\varepsilon<\infty$ such that \begin{equation} M_{-2}[f'](r)\le C_\varepsilon(1-r)^{-1-\varepsilon} \quad\bigl(f\in\SSS,\ 1/2\le r<1\bigr). \label{prelim:uniform-disk} \end{equation} The constant depends only on $\varepsilon$.

- To state its radial form, let \[ \SSS=\{f:\DD\to\CC\text{ univalent}:f(0)=0,\ f'(0)=1\} \] be the normalized schlicht class. For $f\in\SSS$, $t\in\mathbb R$, and $0<r<1$, define \[ M_t[f'](r)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} |f'(re^{i\theta})|^t\dd\theta, \] and put \begin{align*} \beta_f(t)&=\limsup_{r\uparrow1} \frac{\log M_t[f'](r)}{\log(1/(1-r))},\\ B_{\SSS}(t)&=\sup_{f\in\SSS}\beta_f(t). \end{align*} Equivalently, $\beta_f(t)$ is the infimum of the real $b$ for which $M_t[f'](r)=O((1-r)^{-b})$ as $r\uparrow1$; the implicit constant may depend on $f$. In particular, the supremum defining $B_{\SSS}$ is taken after forming each map's limiting exponent. We prove the following uniform estimate directly, and then derive the area assertion.
- Subsequent progress connected the area question to finer estimates for derivative integral means. Pommerenke developed this approach in \cite{Pommerenke1985IntegralMeans,Pommerenke1985IntegralMeansII}. Carleson and Makarov related negative integral means to the distribution of large harmonic measure and proved the linear spectral tail $B_{\SSS}(t)=|t|-1$ for all sufficiently negative $t$ \cite[\S1.4]{CarlesonMakarov1994}. The proposed threshold $t=-2$ is the one associated with Brennan's conjecture \cite{HedenmalmShimorin2005}. The statement for sufficiently negative parameters alone does not reach that endpoint.
- \subsection{The obstacle and the proof strategy}
- The classical distortion theorem bounds $|f'(z)|^{-1}$ by a constant times $(1-|z|)^{-1}$ for $f\in\SSS$. Squaring this estimate gives radial growth with exponent $2$, while the required exponent is $1$. The missing information concerns how often very small derivatives can occur around a circle. Our proof encodes hypothetical excess growth in a probability law on conformal maps. That law imposes an algebraic sign on an expected Jacobian; a geometric pairing of critical points imposes the opposite sign.
- The critical points of $V_\xi^F$ solve \[ G_F(z)=\xi,\qquad G_F(x+iy)=F(x+iy)-\frac{iy}{k}F'(x+iy). \] Section~\ref{sec:critical-pairing} shows that the sign of the normalized real Jacobian of $G_F$ distinguishes saddles from maxima when $\xi$ is a regular value. For these targets, an index count on compact superlevel boundaries gives at least as many maxima as saddles above each positive level. Ordering the two lists by their values of $V_\xi^F$ therefore pairs each saddle with a distinct maximum of at least the same value. We prove that this assignment is Borel and respects every affine change of source coordinates. These properties are essential when the pointwise pairing is integrated against $P$.

=== MANUSCRIPT: A strict inverse-first-power bound for univalent functions [preprints/A-strict-inverse-first-power-bound-for-univalent-functions-September-24-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove a uniform upper bound for inverse-first-power integral means of normalized univalent disk maps with exponent strictly below 1/4. Consequently, the bounded universal integral-means spectrum satisfies $`B_b(-1)\lt 1/4`$, disproving Kraetzer's conjectured spectrum at p = −1.
[theorem] [Uniform inverse-first-power bound]\label{thm:main} There exist constants \(0<\varepsilon<1/4\) and \(C<\infty\) such that \begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform} M_{-1}[f'](r)\le C(1-r)^{-1/4+\varepsilon} \qquad\text{for every }f\in\SSS\text{ and every }1/2\le r<1. \end{equation} Consequently \(B_b(-1)<1/4\), and Kraetzer's conjectured identity \eqref{eq:kraetzer} is false.
[proposition] [From dyadic samples to disk means]\label{prop:disk} There is a universal constant \(C_0\) such that, for \(f\in\SSS\), \[ M_{-1}[f'](r)\le C_0\|L^n\| \quad\text{if}\quad 1/2\le r<1,\quad v=2^{-n}\le1-r<2v. \] Consequently \(B_b(-1)\le\beta\).
[proposition] [Exact-parameter affine law]\label{prop:law} Assume \(\beta=\log_2\rho\ge1/4\), where \(\rho\) is defined in \eqref{eq:rho}. There is a Borel probability \(P\) on \(\KK\) such that, writing \(\E\) for expectation under \(P\), \begin{equation}\label{eq:law} \E\bigl[|Q_F(x+iy)|\,\Phi(T_{x+iy}F)\bigr] =y^{-\beta}\E\Phi(F) \end{equation} for every \(x+iy\in\HH\) and every nonnegative Borel test \(\Phi:\KK\to[0,\infty]\).
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- \maketitle \begin{abstract} We prove a uniform upper bound for inverse-first-power integral means of normalized univalent disk maps with exponent strictly below \(1/4\). Consequently, the bounded universal integral-means spectrum satisfies \(B_b(-1)<1/4\), disproving Kraetzer's conjectured spectrum at \(p=-1\). \end{abstract}
- Kraetzer's experiments with conformal maps onto bounded domains led to the prediction \begin{equation}\label{eq:kraetzer} B_b(p)= \begin{cases} p^2/4,&|p|\le2,\\ |p|-1,&|p|\ge2. \end{cases} \end{equation} The quadratic part is stated in his 1996 paper and explicitly restated in his thesis \cite{Kraetzer,KraetzerThesis}; the full quadratic--linear formulation also appears in Beliaev's discussion of numerical spectra \cite{Beliaev}. We prove a strict upper bound at the negative interior exponent \(p=-1\).
- The distinction between bounded and unrestricted disk maps matters. Carleson and Jones connected coefficient problems with conformal dimension \cite{CJ}. Makarov's comparison theorem identifies the unrestricted spectrum with the maximum of the bounded spectrum and the Koebe contribution \(3p-1\) \cite[Theorem 5.4]{Makarov}. In particular, the bounded spectrum in \eqref{eq:kraetzer} should not be replaced by the unrestricted disk spectrum at positive exponents. Hedenmalm and Shimorin developed a weighted Bergman-space approach to bounds for these spectra, keeping this distinction explicit \cite{HedenmalmShimorin}. Their upper bound at \(p=-1\) for the unrestricted normalized disk class is \(0.403\) \cite[Table 1]{HedenmalmShimorin}. Sola subsequently obtained the bound \(0.388\) by related Bergman-space methods \cite{Sola}; see also the author's account in \cite[p.~6]{SolaThesis}. These estimates remain above the conjectured value \(1/4\); Theorem~\ref{thm:main} establishes a uniform exponent strictly below it.
- Random conformal snowflakes provide another approach. Beliaev and Smirnov constructed random conformal maps whose averaged spectrum approaches the universal spectrum at any prescribed exponent, and related lower bounds to positive integral operators \cite[Sections 3--4]{BeliaevSmirnov}. Beliaev further investigated these operators numerically, distinguishing the numerical estimates from rigorous error control \cite{Beliaev}. The common feature with the present argument is the use of a law on conformal maps and a positive operator. Here the operator samples inverse derivatives through affine changes of base point, and its growth controls an upper bound uniformly over the entire class \(\SSS\).
- The affine-law and saddle-to-maximum construction is also used for inverse-square means in the companion article \emph{Brennan's conjecture and sharp inverse-square integral means} \cite{BrennanMethod}. The two arguments share the compact normalization of half-plane maps, dyadic sampling, and a comparison of critical points over a common target. We prove every inverse-first-power estimate and every transport assertion needed here; the companion's inverse-square endpoint theorem is not an input.
