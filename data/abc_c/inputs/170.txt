FAMILY 170: Sharp logarithmic exponents for off-diagonal Ramsey numbers
SUBJECT: Combinatorics
OPENAI SUMMARY: For every fixed integer s ≥ 5, proves $`r(s,t)=t^{s-1}/(\log t)^{s-2+o(1)}`$ as $`t\to\infty`$, determining the logarithmic exponent and matching the classical upper bound at that scale. Here $`r(s,t)`$ is the least number of vertices forcing an s-clique or a t-vertex independent set.
HEADLINE (v2, semi-formal): ∀ fixed s≥5 ∃C_s ∀ε>0 ∀^∞t: t^{s−1}/(log t)^{s−2+ε} ≤ r(s,t) ≤ C_s t^{s−1}/(log t)^{s−2}; i.e. r(s,t)=t^{s−1}/(log t)^{s−2+o(1)}

PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- \subsection{Proof overview}
- Fix a small \(\eta>0\) and a large prime \(q\). We construct a \(K_5\)-free graph on roughly \(q^4\log q\) positions whose independent sets have fewer than \(q(\log q)^{1+\eta}\) positions. A position receives a random incident pair \((a,b)\), where \(a\) is a point of \(\PG(4,q)\) and \(b\) is a hyperplane containing it. For positions \(i<j\), place an edge when \(a_i\) lies on \(b_j\) but \(a_j\) does not lie on \(b_i\). A five-clique would give five linearly independent points in one hyperplane.
- We first prove the upper bound, independently of the construction used for the lower bound. The estimate is classical \cite{AKS1980}. Our proof uses the uniform-independent-set method of Alon~\cite{Alon1996}; see also Shearer~\cite{Shearer1983} for the triangle-free independence bound and Davies, Jenssen, Perkins, and Roberts~\cite{DJPR2018} for sharp occupancy estimates.
- \begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:upper}] We prove the estimates for sufficiently large $t$; increasing the final constant covers the finitely many smaller values. First, if $G$ is triangle-free and $\alpha(G)<t$, every neighborhood is independent, so its maximum degree is at most $t-1$. Lemma~\ref{lem:occupancy}, with $D=t-1$, gives $|V(G)|<8t^2/\log t$. Consequently \begin{equation}\label{eq:ramsey-three-upper} r(3,t)\le C_3\frac{t^2}{\log t} \end{equation} for an absolute constant $C_3$.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Sharp logarithmic exponents for fixed off-diagonal Ramsey numbers [preprints/Sharp-Logarithmic-Exponents-for-Fixed-Off-Diagonal-Ramsey-Numbers-September-24-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: For every fixed integer s ≥ 6, we determine the sharp logarithmic exponent of the off-diagonal Ramsey number:
[theorem] \label{intro:main} For every fixed integer $s\ge6$ there is a constant $C_s>0$ such that, for every $\varepsilon>0$ and every sufficiently large integer $t$, \[ \frac{t^{s-1}}{(\log t)^{s-2+\varepsilon}} \le r(s,t) \le C_s\frac{t^{s-1}}{(\log t)^{s-2}}. \] In particular, \[ \lim_{t\to\infty} \frac{(s-1)\log t-\log r(s,t)}{\log\log t}=s-2. \]
[theorem] \label{intro:prime-construction} Fix an integer $d\ge5$ and a real number $0<\eta<1/10$. For every sufficiently large prime $q$, with $\sigma=\log q$, there is a graph $G$ such that \[ |V(G)|=\lfloor q^d\sigma\rfloor,\qquad K_{d+1}\not\subseteq G,\qquad \alpha(G)<\lfloor q\sigma^{1+\eta}\rfloor. \]
[proposition] [Repeated projection]\label{geom:projection} For $j\geq4$, assume Lemma~\ref{geom:description} in projective dimension $j-1$, for every fixed value of its hypothesis constant. Then the lemma holds in dimension $j$.
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- The geometric ingredients also have an earlier history. Projective orthogonality and its spectral estimates appear in Alon and Krivelevich \cite{AlonKrivelevich1997}. Ordered incidence constructions of Codenotti, Pudl\'ak, and Resta \cite{CodenottiPudlakResta2000}, and Kostochka, Pudl\'ak, and R\"odl \cite{KostochkaPudlakRodl2010}, are predecessors of the flag approach. We use the ordered incident-flag graph of Brada\v{c}'s higher-dimensional construction \cite[Section~2.5]{Bradac2026} and adapt its marking argument \cite[Claim~2.13]{Bradac2026}. The distinction between a step that substantially shrinks a candidate set and a step with few possible choices also appears in the independent-set counting argument of Alon and R\"odl \cite[proof of Theorem~2.1]{AlonRodl2005}. Here it must be combined with an entropy estimate for a sequence selected from the random stream: selection need not preserve the independence or uniformity of its flags.
- The companion paper \emph{The sharp logarithmic exponent of $r(5,t)$} \cite[Lemma~3.4 and Theorem~4.1]{RamseyFive} develops the selected-law entropy and low-dimensional sparse-pair framework for $s=5$. The present paper supplies all the required arguments locally. To pass to arbitrary fixed dimension, we prove a repeated-projection reduction for sparse pairs and a two-row high-rank description using a rich-subspace bound that follows from Nie and Wang's finite-degree closure inequality \cite{NieWang2015}. The repeated projections and the high-rank construction require their own estimates; neither is inferred from the five-clique theorem. The low-dimensional polynomial argument adapts methods of Dvir, Guth--Katz, and Elekes--Kaplan--Sharir \cite{Dvir2009,GuthKatz2010,ElekesKaplanSharir2011}, with the positive-characteristic obstructions analyzed by Ellenberg and Hablicsek explicitly controlled \cite{EllenbergHablicsek2016}.
- Here is the construction and the organization of its proof. A point of $\PG(d,q)$ is a one-dimensional vector subspace of $\F_q^{d+1}$. A dual point is a one-dimensional subspace of the dual vector space; it specifies a projective hyperplane. Write $a\perp b$ when the covector $b$ vanishes on the point $a$. An incident flag is a pair $(a,b)$ with $a\perp b$.
- The proof is organized as dimension induction. We first prove validation without assuming this lemma, then establish the induction step and the geometric preparation in dimensions two and three. The final sampling step is Proposition~\ref{learn:short-row}; the induction is closed explicitly at the end of Section~\ref{learn:section}.
