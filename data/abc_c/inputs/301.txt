=== MANUSCRIPT: The trace cone classifies Razak–Jacelon stabilizations [preprints/The-trace-cone-classifies-Razak-Jacelon-stabilizations-September-25-2026/build/paper.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove that the canonical topological cone of all extended lower-semicontinuous tracial weights determines a separable nuclear C∗-algebra after tensoring with the Razak–Jacelon algebra and the compact operators, answering Robert's trace-cone classification question positively. The isomorphism realizes the prescribed cone map, with arbitrary ideal structure and without a density assumption on the finite domains of the weights.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} Let \(A\) and \(B\) be separable nuclear complex \(C^*\)-algebras. If \(T(A)\) and \(T(B)\) are isomorphic as topological cones, then \[ A\otimes\W\otimes\K\ \cong\ B\otimes\W\otimes\K, \] where \(\K=\K(\ell^2(\N))\) and all tensor products are spatial.

PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- \maketitle \begin{abstract} We prove that the canonical topological cone of all extended lower-semicontinuous tracial weights determines a separable nuclear \(C^*\)-algebra after tensoring with the Razak--Jacelon algebra and the compact operators, answering Robert's trace-cone classification question positively. The isomorphism realizes the prescribed cone map, with arbitrary ideal structure and without a density assumption on the finite domains of the weights. \end{abstract}
- The Razak--Jacelon algebra \(\W\) is a simple, monotracial, stably projectionless nuclear \(C^*\)-algebra \cite{Jacelon}. Tensoring by \(\W\) removes the \(K\)-theoretic part of the classification problem and makes the order on the Cuntz semigroup visible through traces. The question is whether the resulting trace data determine the algebra even when its ideal structure is unrestricted. We prove that they do.
- Razak's classification of simple inductive limits of stably projectionless building blocks made the cone of traces, together with its scale, an effective classification invariant \cite[Theorems~1.1 and~2.1]{Razak}. Jacelon's construction singled out the simple monotracial algebra $\W$ in this setting \cite{Jacelon}. The existence, uniqueness, and approximate-intertwining organization of those classification arguments also provides the broad method used here, with new local estimates needed for arbitrary ideals.
- The strategy of lifting tracial data and then correcting a map through an extension has a methodological precedent in Schafhauser's proof of the Tikuisis--White--Winter theorem \cite{Schafhauser}. His setting uses a faithful amenable trace and the universal coefficient theorem (UCT); the present local error ideals and support estimates address a different invariant, with no UCT assumption. The use of quotient cone approximations and carriers in prescribed ideals also has a precedent in Bosa--Gabe--Sims--White \cite[Section~3]{BGSW}. Here the small UHF fractions must satisfy both upper trace bounds and lower comparisons into fixed positive cuts. The suspension-and-corner passage follows the method in \cite[Remark~6.4]{GabeO2} and \cite[proof of Theorem~14.1]{Gabe}; its trace normalization is proved below for all extended weights.
- The main difficulty is to preserve both the finite and the infinite parts of \eqref{intro:model}. Fix a regularized limit $\rho$, put $\sigma=\rho|_Q$, and write $Q(V)=\Fin(\sigma)$. On $C_0(Y)\otimes P(V)$, controlled trace moments will identify the pullback of $\rho$. This alone does not prevent the pullback from becoming finite on a larger ideal. A separate lower ideal comparison rules that out. Once the finite ideal is exactly $C_0(Y)\otimes P(V)$, both sides of \eqref{intro:model} are infinite on every positive element outside it. This two-part recovery is the reason for the following constructions.
