- \subsection{Proof strategy}
- The construction realizing this scheme is as follows. Use the additive circle $X=\R/\Z$, with Haar probability $\mu$, and write $\sigma x=x+\theta$. Write $\{t\}\in[0,1)$ for fractional part. We construct divisible integers $1=q_0\mid q_1\mid q_2\mid\cdots$ and positive costs $c_i$. The measurable model is the weight \[ g_*(x)=\sum_{i\ge1}c_i\lfloor\{q_ix\}+\{q_i\theta\}\rfloor, \qquad f_*(x)=e^{-g_*(x)}. \] The series is finite almost everywhere although its integral diverges. Large divisible frequencies allow its trial phases to be realized at the fixed angle $\theta$, while finitely many sampling tests approximate independent Haar coordinates. The circle characters themselves are dependent. A mixed-radix expansion supplies independent digits, from which we build the ordered couplings with controlled marginal densities. The prescribed-marginal viewpoint belongs to the classical coupling theory of Strassen and Edwards \citep{Strassen1965,Edwards1978}; here we also need approximation by positive orbit pairs.
- All identities involving measurable fields hold on appropriate conull sets. When integer iterates are used, we intersect the countably many translates of these sets. The constants implicit in $O(\cdot)$ below are absolute unless a fixed construction stage is specified. Every new ingredient needed for the proof is established in the paper.

- \subsection{Proof overview and elementary inputs} The proof first makes the possible fiber subspaces completely explicit. Section~\ref{sec:criterion} classifies invariant subspaces of a weighted translation whose logarithmic weights have opposite quadratic growth at the two ends. Section~\ref{sec:fibers} constructs weights over $\Z_2$ satisfying that criterion in every fiber. Their decay also gives the uniform power estimate, which proves norm-quasinilpotence of the operator on $H_0$ rather than merely of its individual fibers.
- The convex-envelope and support-separation argument below follows the method of Domar's weighted-translation work \cite[Section~3]{Domar1981}. We prove the stated quadratic specialization in full, including the normalized estimates for both convolution tails. The signed exponent for a negative index is essential: the corresponding summand is $w_{-n}^{-1/n}$ for $n>0$. \begin{proof} Multiplying the weight by a constant, assume $w_0=1$. Put $P_n=\log w_{-n}$ and $Q_n=-\log w_n$ for $n\geq0$. These sequences are convex, vanish at zero, and grow quadratically. For either sequence $G=P,Q$, convexity gives $G_n-G_{n-1}\geq G_n/n$. Put $\beta_j=w_{j+1}/w_j$. For $n\geq1$, \[ \beta_{-n}=e^{-(P_n-P_{n-1})}\leq w_{-n}^{-1/n},\qquad \beta_{n-1}=e^{-(Q_n-Q_{n-1})}\leq w_n^{1/n}, \] The signed-root sum therefore gives \begin{equation}\label{eq:p3-ratio-sum} \beta_j\leq1, \qquad \sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\beta_j<\infty. \end{equation} In particular, $R$ is bounded. If $|c_j|w_j\leq1$ and $|b_j|/w_j\leq1$, then for each integer $r\geq1$, \[ \sum_j|b_jc_{j-r}| \leq\sum_j\frac{w_j}{w_{j-r}} \leq\sum_j\beta_{j-r}<\infty. \]
- We prove the following support assertion: if nonzero $b,c$ satisfy these bounds and \begin{equation}\label{eq:p3-correlations} \sum_jb_jc_{j-r}=0\qquad(r\geq1), \end{equation} then $\operatorname{supp}b$ has a finite maximum, $\operatorname{supp}c$ has a finite minimum, and \begin{equation}\label{eq:p3-support-separation} \max\operatorname{supp}b\leq\min\operatorname{supp}c. \end{equation} Independent scaling of $b,c$ gives the same assertion when the two weighted sequences are merely bounded. We will apply this assertion to a vector in an invariant subspace and to a continuous annihilator of that subspace. After proving support separation, the zero-shift pairing will identify the exact coordinate tail.
