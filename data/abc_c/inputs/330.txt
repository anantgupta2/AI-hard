=== MANUSCRIPT: A uniformly discrete counterexample to bounded approximation in Lipschitz-free spaces [preprints/Failure-of-Bounded-Approximation-in-a-Lipschitz-Free-Space-over-a-Uniformly-Discrete-Metric-Space-September-26-2026/build/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We construct a countable uniformly discrete metric space whose real Lipschitz-free space has the approximation property but fails the bounded approximation property, answering Kalton's question negatively. The identity can be approximated on every compact set by finite-rank operators, but no uniform bound on their norms is possible.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There is a countable, unbounded, nonproper pointed metric space $(M,d,o)$ such that $d(x,y)\ge1$ for distinct points and $\F(M)$ has AP but fails $\lambda$-BAP for every finite $\lambda\ge1$.
[theorem] \label{thm:blocks} For each integer $p\ge1$, there are a bounded countably infinite pointed metric space $(M_p,d_p,o)$ and a finite set $A_p\subseteq M_p$ containing $o$ such that \[ d_p(x,y)\ge1\quad(x\ne y),\qquad \operatorname{diam}M_p\le B_p:=300p^2+150p+5, \] and every bounded finite-rank linear operator $T_0:\F(M_p)\to\F(M_p)$ with $\norm{T_0}\le p$ satisfies \[ \max_{a\in A_p}\norm{T_0\delta_a-\delta_a}\ge\frac12. \]
[corollary] \label{cor:renorming} For each integer $p\ge1$, real $\ell_1$ has an equivalent norm $\norm{\cdot}_p$ such that \[ \tfrac12\norm{c}_1\le\norm{c}_p\le B_p\norm{c}_1, \] and $(\ell_1,\norm{\cdot}_p)$ has $2B_p$-BAP but fails $p$-BAP. For $p=1$, this norm fails MAP.

- Uniform discreteness keeps distinct points of a metric space a fixed positive distance apart. For its Lipschitz-free space, this separation guarantees the approximation property: the identity can be approximated on each compact set by finite-rank operators. Kalton asked whether the approximating operators can always be chosen with one bound on their norms \cite[Proposition~4.4 and following remarks, p.~185]{Kalton04}. The question was restated by Godefroy and Ozawa \cite[Question~1]{GodefroyOzawa14} and in Godefroy's survey \cite[Problem~6.2]{Godefroy15}. We construct a countable uniformly discrete space for which no such bound exists.
- Each individual block nevertheless has BAP. The coefficient identification for bounded uniformly discrete spaces appears in \cite[Theorem~4.14]{AACD20}, with the bounds used here in \cite[Lemma~3.4]{CuthKalenda26}. Our proof in \Cref{prop:coefficients} identifies $\F(M_p)$ with $\ell_1(M_p\setminus\{o\})$, and coordinate truncations give $2B_p$-BAP alongside the obstruction at $p$. Transporting the free-space norm to real $\ell_1$ gives the following quantitative consequence. It also answers a separate question: does every equivalent norm on real $\ell_1$ have MAP? Kalton observed that a positive answer would imply BAP for every uniformly discrete free space \cite[p.~1260]{Kalton12}. The case $p=1$ below gives a direct negative answer.
- The source graphs have a fixed large degree. For every fixed radius, the frequencies of their colored neighborhoods approach the corresponding probabilities in a regular tree of that degree with independent uniform vertex colors. A vertex is labeled by its capped distances to the color classes. These labels vary by at most one across an edge, and each label has only a bounded number of coordinates below the cap. Vertices with the same label attach to the same anchor by a long edge. There are only finitely many possible labels, hence finitely many anchors. Joining the anchors makes the whole block bounded, while sufficiently small metric balls remain inside a single source graph and contain a uniformly bounded number of points.
