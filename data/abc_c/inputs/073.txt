ABSTRACT: We resolve the Falconer distance conjecture in every dimension. For every integer d ≥ 2, a compact subset of ℝd with Hausdorff dimension greater than $`d/2`$ determines a set of Euclidean distances of positive Lebesgue measure.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} Let \(d\ge2\) be an integer and let \(E\subset\R^d\) be compact. If \(\dim_H E>d/2\), then \[ \cL^1(\Delta(E))>0. \]

[theorem] [Planar discretized Furstenberg estimate] \label{ang:furstenberg} Let $0<b<1$ and $b<f<2$. There is $\epsilon_0>0$ such that the following holds for sufficiently small $\delta$. Suppose that $P$ is a nonempty bounded $(\delta,f,\delta^{-\epsilon_0})$-set and that each $x\in P$ has a nonempty family $\mathcal T_x$ of incident $\delta$-tubes whose line parameters form a $(\delta,b,\delta^{-\epsilon_0})$-set. Then \[ \left|\bigcup_{x\in P}\mathcal T_x\right|_\delta \ge\delta^{-2b-\epsilon_0}. \] The exponent may be decreased to absorb fixed bounded charts and fixed width multiples.
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- For \(E\subset\R^d\), its Euclidean distance set is \[ \Delta(E)=\{|x-y|:x,y\in E\}. \] The Falconer distance conjecture asks whether a compact set of Hausdorff dimension greater than half the ambient dimension must determine a set of distances of positive length. We prove this assertion.
- The problem originates in Falconer's work~\cite{Falconer1985}, which established the sufficient threshold \((d+1)/2\). His lattice-type examples at dimension \(d/2\) explain the strict inequality in the positive-measure formulation. Mattila's spherical-average criterion~\cite{Mattila1987} made Fourier \(L^2\) estimates a central approach to the problem. Bourgain connected improvements in dimensions two and three to Fourier restriction phenomena on spheres~\cite{Bourgain1994}. Among the subsequent milestones were Wolff's planar threshold \(4/3\)~\cite{Wolff1999} and Erdo\u{g}an's threshold \(d/2+1/3\) for \(d\ge3\)~\cite[Theorem~2]{Erdogan2005}; these positive-measure conclusions require strict dimension inequalities. For the parallel finite point-set problem in \(\R^d\), \(d\ge3\), see the distinct-distances theorem in~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{OpenAIDistinctDistances2026}.
- Our angular preparation is related to the multiscale incidence approach of Orponen and Shmerkin~\cite[Section~1.5]{OS2023}, the radial thin-tube bootstrap of Orponen, Shmerkin and Wang~\cite[Section~1.3]{OSW2024}, and the higher-dimensional radial and plate-concentration methods of Ren~\cite{Ren2023} and Du, Ou, Ren and Zhang~\cite{DORZ2024}. The retained incidence input is the theorem of Orponen and Shmerkin \cite[Theorem~1.3 and Remark~1.4]{OS2023}, in the tube formulation of \cite[Definitions~2.5--2.6 and Theorem~2.7]{OSW2024}. The auxiliary radial estimate is proved here by Orponen's finite-energy method~\cite[Section~3 and the proof of Theorem~1.13]{Orponen2019}. It is applied only in a dimension where its energy hypotheses hold.
- We first record two finite-tree facts. Neither requires lower density estimates for the original measure. Dyadic cubes are taken half open, so that they form genuine measurable partitions; a fixed translation and rescaling places each measure under consideration in one unit cube. Related decompositions into dyadically regular measures occur in \cite[Corollary~3.5]{KS2019}; we prove the version needed here.
