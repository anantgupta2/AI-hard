FAMILY 314: Cyclic length and chromatic fixed-point loss
SUBJECT: Topology
OPENAI SUMMARY: Determines the optimal chromatic loss from geometric H-fixed points to geometric G-fixed points for every subgroup H of a finite p-group G. At every nonnegative height, the loss equals the shortest subnormal-chain length from H to G with cyclic quotients. Each quotient counts once regardless of order, and finite spectra witness sharpness.

PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- \maketitle \begin{abstract} For a finite \(p\)-group \(G\) and a subgroup \(H\), we prove that the optimal chromatic fixed-point loss equals the shortest length of a subnormal chain from \(H\) to \(G\) with cyclic quotients. The equality holds at every prime and every nonnegative height, resolving positively the equality proposed by Kuhn and Lloyd. \end{abstract} \section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
- For a finite \(p\)-group \(P\) and a spectrum \(D\), write \[ U_P(D)=\Phi^P\Bor_P(D), \] where \(\Bor_P(D)\) is the genuine cofree spectrum associated to the trivial \(P\)-action on \(D\). Let \(T(l)\) be a telescope at height \(l\). The first main step is the rational estimate \begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-rational} \bigl(U_P(L_{T(l)}S)\bigr)_{\Q}\ne0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \ell(P,1)\le l. \end{equation} To prove it at positive \(l\), we rationalize morphisms in the tensor category of dualizable \(T(l)\)-local spectra and split Burnside idempotents. A maximizing construction produces a pro-\(p\) group \(\Gamma\) mapping onto \(P\), with every finite section (a finite continuous quotient of a closed subgroup) generated by at most \(l\) elements. Cochains admit evaluation factors indexed by continuous homomorphisms out of \(\Gamma\). Height zero is handled separately by rational averaging.
- Finally, finite chromatic fracture converts \eqref{eq:intro-height} into vanishing estimates for the right adjoints to subgroup geometric fixed points. The support argument adapts the strategy of Balmer--Sanders \cite[proof of Theorem~9.1, \textup{(B)}$\Rightarrow$\textup{(A)}]{BS}. At a least height where the proposed bound fails, the estimates isolate the offending subgroup component from the other components. In the finite-height tensor-triangular spectrum, this forces the support of a compact object to split into two nonempty disjoint closed parts, although that support is the closure of a point and hence irreducible. The contradiction supplies the lower bound for every embedded subgroup.
