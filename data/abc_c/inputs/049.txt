=== MANUSCRIPT: A stable coordinate that is not a coordinate in four variables [preprints/A-stable-coordinate-that-is-not-a-coordinate-in-four-variables-October-5-2026/build/source/main.tex]
ABSTRACT: We construct an explicit degree-five polynomial over $`\mathbf C`$ that is not a coordinate in four variables but becomes one after adjoining a single variable. This gives a counterexample to the stable coordinate conjecture in four variables. Every fiber is isomorphic to affine three-space, yet its embedding in affine four-space is not rectifiable. Thus the example also disproves the Abhyankar–Sathaye embedding conjecture in ambient dimension four.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} In $R=\C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]$, put \begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-counterexample} Q=x_2^2-x_4^2+x_1x_3, \qquad f=x_1-2Q\bigl(Q(x_2+x_4)+x_1x_4\bigr). \end{equation} There is a $\C$-algebra automorphism of $R[w]$ sending $f$ to $x_1$, but there is no such automorphism of $R$.
[corollary] \label{cor:fibers} For every $\lambda\in\C$, the hypersurface $f^{-1}(\lambda)\subset\A^4$ is isomorphic to $\A^3$ and its embedding in $\A^4$ is not rectifiable.
[proposition] \label{prop:polynomial} There are polynomial coordinates $p',s',F',J$ on $A$ such that \begin{equation}\label{con:candidate} x=(s')^2-J^2+p'F',\qquad p=p'-2x\bigl(x(s'+J)+p'J\bigr). \end{equation} Thus, under the identification $(p',s',F',J)=(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)$, the element $p$ is the polynomial $f$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

An element $f\in k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ is a \emph{coordinate} if there are elements $f_2,\ldots,f_n$ such that \[ k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]=k[f,f_2,\ldots,f_n] \] as a polynomial ring. The stable coordinate conjecture asks whether an element that becomes a coordinate after adjoining finitely many variables was already a coordinate.
