[theorem] \label{thm:main} For every integer \(N\ge2\), \[ \dim\Ker H_N=1. \] The kernel consists of \(\Spin(9)\)-invariant states and is even in the fermion grading just specified.
[proposition] [Square of a charge] \label{prop:square} On the gauge-invariant core, \begin{equation} \label{eq:square} \begin{aligned} \cH(h,m)&=\frac12p^2+U_{h,m} +\frac{\ii}{2}\theta^t\left(\frac32mT-hK_j\gamma^j\right)\theta, \qquad K_j=\ad(x_j),\\ 2U_{h,m}&=\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}} \abs{h[x_a,x_b]-2mx_c}^2 +h^2\sum_{\substack{i<j\\j>3}}\abs{[x_i,x_j]}^2 +m^2\sum_{d=4}^9\abs{x_d}^2,\\ D_v(h,m)^2&=\cH(h,m)+mJ(R(v)), \qquad R(v)_{ij}=s_jv^t\gamma^i\gamma^jTv. \end{aligned} \end{equation} The cyclic sum uses \((a,b,c)=(1,2,3),(2,3,1),(3,1,2)\). The matrix \(R(v)\) is skew, preserves \(\R^3\oplus\R^6\), and has zero spin-basis average. The same formulas hold for an orthogonal direct sum of compact color algebras and abelian color spaces.
[proposition] [Singlet property and parity] \label{prop:singlet-parity} Every gauge-invariant \(L^2\) vector in the undeformed kernel is \(\Spin(9)\)-invariant and even. In particular it is \(G\)-invariant. This assertion does not require finite-dimensionality of the kernel or any moment of its vectors.
PROOF STRATEGY (paper's own words):
- \maketitle \begin{abstract} For every finite \(N\ge2\), we prove that undeformed \(\SU(N)\) BFSS matrix quantum mechanics has exactly one normalizable zero-energy state after removing the center of mass. This establishes the threshold-bound-state conjecture. \end{abstract}
- Supersymmetric matrix quantum mechanics appears in the regularization of supermembranes~\cite[Section~3]{DeWitHoppeNicolai1988} and in the matrix-theory conjecture of Banks, Fischler, Shenker, and Susskind~\cite{BFSS1997}. The model studied here describes nine Hermitian matrices and their fermionic partners. Its commuting configurations form noncompact flat directions. The D0-brane bound-state prediction of Witten~\cite[Section~4.2]{Witten1996BoundStates}, subsequently central to matrix theory, is that after removing the free center of mass every finite matrix size has exactly one normalizable state of zero energy. We prove this threshold-bound-state conjecture for the \(\SU(N)\) model on its full configuration space. In the matrix-theory interpretation, this relative bound state supplies the internal state of a single supergraviton carrying \(N\) units of longitudinal momentum~\cite[Section~5]{BFSS1997}. The continuous spectrum starts at zero~\cite{DeWitLuscherNicolai1989}, so the proposed eigenstate lies at its threshold rather than below a gapped continuum.
- The supersymmetric index counts even and odd zero modes with opposite signs~\cite{Witten1982Index}. In the present noncompact problem, contributions from infinity are essential. Yi~\cite{Yi1997} computed the $\SU(2)$ bulk term and proposed a free-particle argument for its boundary correction; Sethi--Stern~\cite{SethiStern1998} obtained the two-particle index by including its boundary term. Moore--Nekrasov--Shatashvili~\cite{MNS2000} computed the matrix integrals entering the principal contribution. Green--Gutperle~\cite[Section~5]{GreenGutperle1998} argued that corrections from free identical clusters account for the divisor sum, assuming the free-cluster boundary prescription extends to general $N$. Konechny~\cite[Sections~3 and~5]{Konechny1999} derived a free leading asymptotic Hamiltonian and used it to conclude that the full $\SU(N)$ index equals one. His expansion assumes that the eigenvalue differences of one diagonalized matrix grow at the same order. Our proof will control also the degenerating and nested cluster regions through explicit norm estimates.

=== MANUSCRIPT: Positive eigenvalues of the relative SU(2) BFSS Hamiltonian [preprints/Positive-eigenvalues-of-the-relative-SU-2-BFSS-Hamiltonian-October-5-2026/build/positive-eigenvalues-relative-su2-bfss.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove that the relative $`\mathop{\mathrm{SU}}\nolimits (2)`$ BFSS Hamiltonian has infinitely many positive eigenvalues tending to infinity, with square-integrable eigenvectors. The operator is defined by closing the gauge-invariant supercharge form. This refutes, at N = 2, the exclusion of normalizable positive-energy states stated in the original BFSS paper.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There are an orthonormal sequence $\Psi_j\in\Dom(H)$ and real numbers $E_j>0$ tending to infinity such that \[ H\Psi_j=E_j\Psi_j. \] In particular, $\sigma_{\mathrm p}(H)\cap(0,\infty)$ is infinite.
[proposition] [Confinement in a rotation sector] \label{prop:confinement} Let $C$ be as in \eqref{eq:fermion-bound}, and choose an integer $m\geq0$ such that $c_m=(m+9)/3-C>0$. If $\lambda_2>m+M$, every $\Psi\in\Dom(q)\cap\Hphys_\lambda$ belongs to $H^1(\R^{27};\F)$, satisfies $|x|^{1/2}\Psi\in L^2(\R^{27};\F)$, and obeys \begin{equation} q(\Psi)\ \geq\ \tfrac14\norm{\nabla\Psi}_2^2 +c_m\int_{\R^{27}}|x|\norm{\Psi(x)}^2\,dx. \label{eq:confinement} \end{equation} The operator $H_\lambda$ has compact resolvent and $\ker H_\lambda=\{0\}$.

- The Banks--Fischler--Shenker--Susskind (BFSS) model is supersymmetric matrix quantum mechanics obtained by reducing ten-dimensional supersymmetric Yang--Mills theory to one time dimension. Its quartic potential vanishes on commuting matrices, leaving unbounded directions of classical escape. For the relative $\SU(2)$ Hamiltonian defined below, we prove an unbounded sequence of positive eigenvalues with square-integrable eigenvectors. The proof selects $\Spin(9)$ sectors in which symmetry excludes low transverse oscillator levels, so the remaining oscillator energy confines those sectors. We first specify the operator and its physical Hilbert space.
