=== MANUSCRIPT: The free uniform spanning forest is a factor of IID [preprints/The-free-uniform-spanning-forest-is-a-factor-of-IID-September-25-2026/build/paper.tex]
ABSTRACT: We prove that the free uniform spanning forest is a factor of IID on every infinite connected locally finite simple unweighted graph. One Borel rule works for all such graphs and uses no root, answering affirmatively the general factor question for unimodular random graphs. We also show that every translation-invariant strongly Rayleigh process indexed by a countable group is a factor of IID, including invariant determinantal processes with Hermitian positive-contraction kernels. This group-action conclusion requires no amenability.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There is one Borel, root-independent, equivariant rule \[ \Phi(G,U,e)\in\{0,1\} \] such that, for every infinite connected locally finite simple undirected unweighted graph $G$ and independent $\operatorname{Uniform}[0,1]$ vertex labels $U=(U_v)_{v\in V(G)}$, the entire edge set \[ \{e\in E(G):\Phi(G,U,e)=1\} \] has law $\FUSF_G$.
[corollary] \label{cor:unimodular} The free uniform spanning forest is a graph factor of IID under every unimodular law supported on the stated class of graphs. The same rule works for every such law.
[theorem] [Strongly Rayleigh processes on countable groups] \label{thm:sr-group} Let $\Gamma$ be a countable group and let $\nu$ be a strongly Rayleigh probability law on $\{0,1\}^{\Gamma}$ invariant under every $\tau_g$. There is a Borel map \[ \Phi_\nu:[0,1]^{\Gamma}\longrightarrow\{0,1\}^{\Gamma} \] such that $\Phi_\nu(\tau_g u)=\tau_g\Phi_\nu(u)$ for every $g\in\Gamma$ and every $u\in[0,1]^{\Gamma}$, and such that $\Phi_\nu(U)$ has law $\nu$ when the coordinates of $U$ are independent $\operatorname{Uniform}[0,1]$ variables.

- For general graph laws, Tim\'ar proves FIID representability of the free forest on recurrent unimodular random graphs and, more generally, on invariantly amenable unimodular random graphs \cite[arXiv version, Theorem~1 and Corollary~5]{Timar2025}. Under invariant amenability he obtains the stronger conclusion of a finite-valued finitary factor~\cite[arXiv version, Theorem~2(1)]{Timar2025}. His construction uses compatible monotone finite-volume limits along a hyperfinite exhaustion. The introduction to that December 2025 version states the unrestricted FUSF factor question as open. We prove the unrestricted graphwise statement by a different construction: the same Borel rule works for every graph in our class, independently of any random-graph law. The challenge is to obtain a single equivariant sample from the infinite-volume law while controlling all edges jointly.
- This construction adapts an established observation mechanism. Stochastic localization was introduced by Eldan~\cite{Eldan2013}; a finite-dimensional form with fixed noise covariance appears in~\cite[Section~2, Proposition~7]{Eldan2020}. El Alaoui and Montanari~\cite[Section~3, Theorem~2]{ElAlaouiMontanari2022} develop its Gaussian-channel observation and innovation formulation. A particularly close antecedent is Nam, Sly and Zhang~\cite[Theorem~1 and Section~2.2]{NamSlyZhang2022}: for free Ising measures on regular trees in their parameter range, they obtain FIID codings through Brownian-driven systems with posterior-mean drift and recover spins at large times. Here we prove the countable-coordinate posterior and joint innovation identities, then use the uniform response bound to obtain an inverse defined at every continuous input.
- The analytic input is a response estimate whose constant is independent of the number of edges and of their weights. We first prove it on finite graphs, then use the free spanning-tree limit to construct a drift on every field of real edge marks. The finite determinant and transfer-current identities are classical; see Burton and Pemantle~\cite{BurtonPemantle1993} and the weighted formulation in \cite[Section~4]{BLPS2001}. We prove the needed identities and their free-limit consequence below.
