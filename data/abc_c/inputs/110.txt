FAMILY 110: Optimal-order randomized k-server on arbitrary metrics
SUBJECT: Theoretical computer science
OPENAI SUMMARY: Establishes a randomized competitive ratio $`O(\log^2(k+1))`$ for k-server on every metric space, matching the worst-case lower-bound order. One policy serves every finite oblivious request sequence, including on infinite unbounded metrics. On finite rational metrics, a uniform implementation has polynomial preprocessing and per-request bit cost in the input length and $`\log(t+1)`$ at request t, with a finite instance-dependent additive movement constant.
HEADLINE (v2, semi-formal): ∃ C, ∀ k ≥ 2, ∀ metric space (X,d) with ≥ k+1 points, ∀ s ∈ X^k, ∃ randomized online policy A, ∃ B < ∞, ∀ finite request sequence σ: E cost_{A,s}(σ) ≤ C log²(k+1) OPT(σ) + B

ABSTRACT: We prove that randomized k-server has competitive ratio $`O((\log(k+1))^2)`$ on every metric space against oblivious request sequences, matching the known worst-case lower bound. For each metric and initial configuration, one policy works for all finite request sequences, including on infinite and unbounded spaces. When the initial server positions are distinct, no additive term is needed.
[theorem] \label{thm:main} There is an absolute constant \(C<\infty\) such that, for every integer \(k\ge2\), every metric space \((X,d)\) with at least \(k+1\) points, and every \(s\in X^k\), there exist a randomized online policy \(A\) and a finite \(B\ge0\) satisfying \begin{equation}\label{eq:main} \E\,\cost_{A,s}(\sigma) \le C\bigl(\log(k+1)\bigr)^2\OPT_{X,d,k,s}(\sigma)+B \end{equation} for every finite request sequence \(\sigma\). The constant \(C\) is independent of every property of the metric. The number \(B\) may depend on \((X,d),k,s\), but not on the sequence or its length. The same policy serves all finite sequences and horizons. If the initial positions are distinct, one may take \(B=0\).

- The main upper-bound approach passes through hierarchically separated trees. Bartal introduced probabilistic approximation by these metrics \cite{Bartal96,Bartal98}, and Fakcharoenphol, Rao, and Talwar obtained expected distortion \(O(\log n)\) for \(n\)-point metrics \cite{FRT04}. Recursive allocation methods were developed by Coté, Meyerson, and Poplawski \cite{CMP08} and by Bansal, Buchbinder, and Naor \cite{BBN10}. Bansal, Buchbinder, Mądry, and Naor then gave the first polylogarithmic bound for arbitrary finite metrics, \(O(\log^2 k\,\log^3 n\,\log\log n)\) \cite{BBMN15}. Bubeck, Cohen, Lee, Lee, and Mądry achieved \(O(\log^2 k)\) on HSTs using multiscale entropic regularization \cite{BCLLM18}. The static embedding reduction yields \(O(\log^2 k\log n)\) on general finite metrics. Its dependence on \(n\) does not give a bound in \(k\) alone. Coester and Cosson recently made the HST and finite-metric bounds polynomial-time, using a total number of random bits independent of the request length \cite[Theorem~1 and Corollary~2]{CoesterCosson2026}. Their general finite-metric ratio retains the factor $\log n$.
- Changing the embedding as requests arrive is also an established direction. The dynamic construction of \cite[Section~4]{BCLLM18} gives \(O(\log^3 k\log(1+\Delta))\) on finite metrics, where \(\Delta\) is the ratio of the largest distance to the smallest positive distance. Lee's evolving, fusible HST framework used heavy-ball nets to merge clusters near concentrated mass and introduced a fission potential intended to pay for later splits \cite[Sections~3.1.2--3.2 and~5.3]{Lee18}. Its general competitive-ratio claim was withdrawn after a gap in Section~5.3.2 \cite{LeeErratum}. Heavy-ball protection and accounting for changing partitions are methodological antecedents of our construction. Here the partition schedule is driven by the posterior measure of a hidden comparator; it produces a fixed tree of label strings. We prove the required edit and retained-anchor travel estimates in Sections~\ref{sec:partitions} and~\ref{sec:travel}, without invoking a theorem from the withdrawn argument.
- For exposition we prove the reusable allocation theorem first, then construct its partition input. The four components are as follows.
- \subsection{Organization}
- At a vertex $v$, the local task is to distribute its amount $q_v$ among the children without violating their serving lower bounds. We construct child allowances $d_i$, then remove at least $(\sum_i d_i-q_v)_+$ from them; additional removals leave mass parked at $v$. The removal choices must remain stable under posterior fluctuations. Section~\ref{sec:proportions} constructs these choices, and Section~\ref{sec:allocation-finish} proves that the resulting allocations meet their lower bounds and move slowly.

[theorem] [Existence from distinct initial positions, {\cite[Theorem~1.1]{SquaredLog2026}}]\label{thm:existence} There is an absolute constant $C_0<\infty$ such that, for every integer $k\ge2$, every finite metric space with at least $k+1$ points, and every distinct initial labeled tuple $u$, there exists a randomized online policy $A$ satisfying \begin{equation}\label{eq:external-existence} \E\bigl[\operatorname{cost}_{A,u}(w)\bigr] \le C_0\bigl(\log(k+1)\bigr)^2\operatorname{OPT}_u(w) \end{equation} for every fixed finite request word $w$ chosen independently of its random choices. [proposition] \label{prop:finite-table} For every positive integer $H$, a terminating deterministic procedure on rational input constructs a rational number $a$ and a finite table defining a strongly lazy policy $\mathcal T_H$ from $u$ such that \begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-table-bound} 0\le a\le C_0\bigl(\log(k+1)\bigr)^2,\qquad \E\bigl[\operatorname{cost}_{\mathcal T_H,u}(w)\bigr] \le a\operatorname{OPT}_u(w)+D \quad (|w|\le H). \end{equation} Every table decision uses exactly $b$ independent unbiased bits, where $b$ is the least nonnegative integer with $2^b\ge kH^2$. - Finite-policy synthesis and bounded-sequence reductions already have a substantial history. M\"omke \cite[Sections~3--4]{Moemke2013} gives a competitive-ratio approximation scheme on fixed finite metrics, reducing the analysis to bounded request sequences and expressing randomized strategies by a finite linear program. We use the same relationship between causal probabilities and linear constraints, spelling out exact elimination and the conversion to finite blocks of unbiased random bits. Mikkelsen~\cite[Section~5.2, proof of Lemma~9]{Mikkelsen2016}, using Lemma~8 there, develops related reset constructions for finite lazy task systems: candidate policies are simulated together, universally free requests are discarded, and a cost cap triggers a fallback within a phase. Our universal filter and fallback follow this general pattern, with explicit bounds for the retained word and the finite state used by the implementation. The proof retains the full construction so that its halting, sampling, and storage properties can all be checked in the same bit model.
- Finite-horizon linear descriptions of randomized $k$-server strategies appear in M\"omke~\cite[Section~4]{Moemke2013}. We use configuration marginals and transition flows, with a single marginal shared by all continuations of each request prefix. The proposition also records the finite-bit sampling guarantee needed later.
