Which surviving items are most plausibly eureka steps

The items that survived the novelty pass at N3, ranked into four priority tiers for the next experiments: the discoverability test and the speedup test. Tiers weigh the novelty confidence, how far the step sits from known material or from the model's own learned lessons, whether anyone found it independently, and how strong the nearest precedent is. This is a judgement for prioritising, not a result: none is a confirmed eureka step.

The tests run in Lean, so only items formalized in the openai/math Lean library are ranked. Each item shows where it lives in the Lean, whether the comparator target depends on it, and the related problems to try it on, with how easily each can be stated in Lean. Items with no Lean counterpart are listed at the bottom as excluded.

F1 extension definitions and where they might reach

Every item graded as an F1 extension definition, a new defined object like Cook's pigeonhole variables. For each: the definition, its abstract mechanism, the precedent check, newer open problems it might help with, known theorems to use as speedup-test targets, and the exact Lean source.

What this atlas is testing

The hypothesis: a model can resolve anything reachable by recombining existing definitions and theorems (AI-easy), but struggles where a proof needs a eureka step, a non-analytic cut. The anchor example is the pigeonhole principle: plain resolution proofs are exponential, but Cook and Reckhow's few extension variables make them polynomial. A eureka step is hard to construct and buys a large drop in proof budget. Importing a known concept from another field is transfer, not a eureka step.

Eureka step
A new definition, lemma or construction that occurs nowhere in the goal or the existing library, is hard to find, and makes other theorems much cheaper to prove. Also called a non-analytic cut.
Transfer
Using a concept or theorem that already exists in another field. Recorded under fields and graded N0.
New bridge
A first-ever connection between one field's tools and a problem type, built from existing parts.
Base library L
Mathlib at the pinned commit plus the family's cited bibliography. Analyticity is judged against L; existence (N0) against all of mathematics.
Provisional vs final
Paper, Lean, family and trace analysts give provisional grades. Only the separate novelty pass gives final grades; in family 287 it kept one of six provisional N3 candidates.
Builds on model output
Flags a result whose closest precedent was itself produced by a model, such as a ChatGPT-assisted preprint.
Analyses done
paper: LaTeX analyst; lean: formal-proof analyst; family: combined paper and Lean analyst; trace: reasoning-trace analyst; novelty: literature and grading pass.

A · Logical form

Read from the innermost conclusion of the headline claim. Parameters that only index a family of witnesses count as an outer “for all”.

A1
Universal: a property holds for every object in a class (∀).
A2
Existential: some object exists, including counterexamples (∃).
A3
Quantitative or sharp: determines an exact value, formula or optimal rate.
A4
Characterization: an if-and-only-if or complete classification (⇔). The form of its hard direction is recorded too.
A5
Impossibility: non-existence, hardness, undecidability or independence (¬∃).

B · Relation to the posed problem

B1
Confirms the conjecture or open question in full.
B2
Refutes it.
B3
Partial: a special case, a conditional result, or an improved bound short of the target.
B4
Extends or generalizes a known result; no conjecture is settled.
Flags
“Full only with prior results” when cited theorems are needed to reach the headline. “Adds corollaries” when it also settles other questions.

C · Proof method

One primary label plus secondaries.

C1
Construction: an explicit object, gadget or counterexample. Primary whenever the witness is built.
C2
Induction or iteration: induction, descent, bootstrapping, iterative or multiscale schemes.
C3
Indirect: contradiction, minimal counterexample, compactness, probabilistic method, pigeonhole.
C4
Estimation chain: inequalities, energy or a-priori estimates, asymptotics.
C5
Reduction or transfer: a cited result invoked without being re-proved.
Computer-assisted
Set only when computation carries real search (SAT, enumeration, certified numerics), not for trivial decision procedures.
Induction strengthened by
Whether the induction hypothesis was strengthened, and by which standard template: add a parameter, carry an extra tracked quantity, uniform version, or all substructures. A templated strengthening caps at N2.

D · Novelty of machinery

A family takes the highest novelty grade among its extracted items.

D1
Recombination: every item is N0 or N1.
D2
Variants: the best item is N2.
D3
At least one item is an N3 eureka candidate. Cross-field transfer alone never gives D3.

E · Fields

Problem field
OpenAI's subject label for the family.
Method fields
Fields of theorems the proof actually invokes, each tagged essential, corollary-only or background-cited. Cited but unused work does not count.
Lean-directory field
Where the formalization lives. It often differs from both of the above.

F · Eureka type

Assigned to N3 items only.

F1
Extension definition: a new defined object or variable, like Cook's pigeonhole variables.
F2
Strengthened hypothesis or invariant.
F3
Auxiliary construction: a gadget built only to drive the proof.
F4
Potential or monovariant.
F5
Change of representation, including embedding into a larger structure.

N · Novelty grade of each lemma or definition

N0 Existing
It, or an equivalent, already exists in some field, including concepts transplanted from another field.
N1 Abbreviation
A renaming, special case or conjunction of concepts already in L.
N2 Variant
A close neighbour exists, including precedents the paper names itself, or a templated strengthening.
N3 Eureka candidate
Passes all four tests below.
Confirmed eureka step
N3, and adding it lets a prover resolve other theorems with much less budget. None is confirmed yet.

The four N3 tests

1 · No near-equivalent
Nearest-neighbour search over Mathlib and the literature in every field, then an equivalence check.
2 · Non-analytic
Does not occur, up to unfolding, as a subterm of the goal or of L. Syntactic size is irrelevant.
3 · Load-bearing
A substantial share of the proof's dependency graph rests on it.
4 · Discoverability
With the item hidden, models trained before the release rarely propose it, measured as a discovery rate over many samples.

Search dynamics in the reasoning traces

Each trace is coded like a SAT solver's conflict-driven search. Traces are OpenAI's curated summaries, so counts are lower bounds and lengths measure summary text, not compute.

Decision level
L1 is a top-level strategy such as prove vs disprove; L2 an approach within it; L3 and deeper are sub-approaches.
Conflict
An approach fails: a counterexample, a missing estimate, circularity, an invariant that is constant on the family, or no way to transport structure.
Learned lesson
What the model concluded from a failure. Lesson reuse is the share of lessons that later shape a choice.
Backtrack distance d
Levels jumped back after a failure. d = 1 tries a sibling approach; d ≥ 2 jumps further up the tree.
Insight-driven backtrack
An approach dropped not because it failed but because a new idea solved things more simply, like subsumption in a SAT solver. Retrieved ideas grade N0; constructed ones become novelty candidates.
Restart
Abandoning the whole strategy and returning to level 1, for example switching from disproving to proving.
Branch length
Words of trace spent on an abandoned branch before the backtrack.
Before final route
Share of the trace written before the successful route begins.
Conflict-driven final route
Whether the successful idea traces back to a learned lesson, or appears with no visible trigger.

Pivot classifications

Known technique
A standard method of the problem's own field.
Transfer
A concept or theorem imported from another field.
Known route
A cross-field connection already used in the literature for this kind of problem.
New bridge
A cross-field connection with no prior use for this problem type.
New concept
A constructed object or lemma with no visible precedent.
Pivot distance
Steps between the new idea and the nearest thing the model had just invoked.
Chain depth
How many other new items must exist before this one pays off.
